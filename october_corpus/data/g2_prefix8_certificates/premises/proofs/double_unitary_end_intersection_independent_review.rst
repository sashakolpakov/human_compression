The classification premise
--------------------------

I independently read Section 2, items (I)--(IV), and Theorem 2.1 of
Montanucci and Zini, arXiv:1804.03398v1, at
https://arxiv.org/html/1804.03398v1 . Their list attributes the underlying
classification to Mitchell and Hartley. For q=11 its only proper maximal
projective unitary subgroup with a factor 37 is the Singer normalizer of
order 111. Restoring the special unitary centre gives order 333, which is
odd. The proper subfield alternatives do not occur at this prime q.
This classification is an attributed premise, not a result newly proved
by our elementary argument.

The centre is handled correctly. For H0 inside SU3(11), a full projective
image gives H0 Z=SU3(11). Centrality and perfectness imply that every
commutator of SU3(11) lies in H0, so H0 is the entire special unitary
group. No complement to the centre or splitting of the cover is assumed.
The involution in H0 comes from restricting the quotient map of a Klein
four group to the index-two normal subgroup. It rules out the odd Singer
preimage when the projective image is proper.

The elementary deductions
-------------------------

The common eigenbasis argument is valid for distinct commuting unitary
involutions of determinant one in odd characteristic. Their positive
eigenlines are nondegenerate and orthogonal. Diagonal rescaling by the
nonzero coordinates of the third positive eigenvector need not be unitary.
Its Hermitian diagonal coefficients remain in the ground field, while that
vector acquires coordinates zero or one. The projection formula therefore
puts all three matrices over F11, preserving a nonsingular symmetric form.
An order-37 constituent needs degree six over F11, so this three-dimensional
subgroup cannot contain it. This excludes the special unitary group as an
entire end and leaves its full index-two normalizer.

The order bound treats semisimple and nonsemisimple elements separately.
The unitary eigenvalue orbits give divisors of 12, 120 and 111 for the
semisimple cases. A nontrivial unipotent part has order 11; a repeated
semisimple eigenvalue has norm one, giving the upper bound 132. In the
outer coset, the square has its characteristic polynomial over F11, hence
has the factor t-1 and a reciprocal quadratic factor. Distinct roots give
orders dividing 10 or 12; repeated roots give orders at most 22. The
outer element therefore has order at most 44. These bounds do not require
an exact-order computation on the retained candidates.

Finally, two full unitary normalizers have order k as displayed in the
argument. The product-set identity gives intersection size at least
k squared divided by the acquired ambient order, which is greater than
480. The common dihedral subgroup has order at most 264. Strict inequality
therefore proves the required intersection failure even if the two line
stabilizers coincide. Neither distinct fixed lines nor a pre-existing
rank-three intersection property is assumed.
