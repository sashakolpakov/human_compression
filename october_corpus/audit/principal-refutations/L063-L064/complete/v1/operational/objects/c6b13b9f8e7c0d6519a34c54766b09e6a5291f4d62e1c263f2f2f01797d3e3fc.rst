Source contract and equivalence
-------------------------------

The principal problem concerns q >= 4 and systems having a Borel or full
torus normalizer as one parabolic. It is not a classification with an
arbitrary first subgroup. The corrected theorem pays the source Theorem 1.1
and the geometry classifications in Propositions 5.3, 5.8, 5.12 and Tables
1, 5, 6, 7, including local action, exhaustion, class counts, graph
parameters, RPRI and the rank-two intersection property. The special
eleven-class E16 assertion in Remark 4.3 is separately proved. The q=2,3
boundaries are explicitly separated from the simple-group hypothesis.

The source defines conjugacy on unordered parabolic sets on printed page
181. Counting orbits with a fixed first subgroup initially gives ordered
systems. The proof and program correctly require an actual transporter
when the two subgroup types are conjugate, rather than declaring all
isomorphic ends interchangeable. The two ordered D12/D12 classes at each
of q=11 and q=13 merge into one unordered class. The source's value two is
therefore preserved as an ordered count and corrected for its own stated
unordered convention. Automorphism classes use the full Aut(G), not merely
inner automorphisms.

Other explicit corrections are the repeated split index on printed page
188, the omitted denominator two for the odd nonsplit normalizer in Table
3, and the q=17 D16 vertex count 153 rather than 136. The source's
unqualified prime-dihedral/reflection double-transitivity assertion is
replaced by the proved primitive cyclic-normal-image lemma: for a genuine
odd prime reflection action it works only at degree three. The rank-two
residue equivalence is proved directly; the source's stronger higher-rank
Boolean assertion is not imported. Unused numerical subgroup
multiplicities and the other local actions in Lemma 4.1/Table 4 are not

General mathematical audit
--------------------------

For actual right-coset systems, incident pairs are translates of the base
pair. Neighbours of H are indexed by H/(H intersect K); their stabilizer
orbits are exactly the corresponding double cosets. Two double cosets
therefore give double transitivity, positive local degree and primitivity
without an assumed faithful local action. A maximal parabolic and an
eligible second parabolic generate G. The residual weak-primitive and
connectedness conclusions, and the stabilizer-orbit classification with
one subgroup fixed, follow with the stated properness hypotheses.

The primitive-action lemma is applied only after passage to the faithful
image where needed. A nontrivial abelian normal subgroup is regular; a
cyclic one then has prime order. The stabilizer bound two limits the
dihedral local degree to two or three. The divisibility of the number of
other points by the stabilizer order is necessary in every application.

For the Borel, the scalar order (q-1)/gcd(2,q-1) exceeds the multiplicative
order of a proper subfield. Hence an invariant additive kernel is either
zero or the whole translation group. In the latter case a cyclic quotient
forces degree two and the point stabilizer contains a full characteristic
Sylow subgroup; the retained subgroup alternatives force the same Borel,
excluding a second parabolic. In the former case the translations are
faithful regular and the scalar complement must be transitive on their
nonzero elements. This forces q even and the full complement. Its eligible
overgroups are the two point Borels and its dihedral normalizer, with the
small A4/S3 cases explicitly accounted for. The stated geometric models
then give all uniform families. Sharp triple transitivity proves their arc
depths; for the point/pair incidence graph the two starting types have
depths four and three, so the common local depth is exactly three.

For a full torus normalizer D(2n), the faithful local action gives B=Cn or
B=D(2t) with n=2t or 3t. For n>2, a second cyclic/dihedral overgroup cannot
escape the same semisimple normalizer. The affine alternative has regular
translation image and the order equation n=p^e-1; the odd-characteristic
equations give no q>=4, and the even case is precisely the uniform Borel.
For noncyclic B, its characteristic rotation subgroup excludes a second
dihedral group, while the actual intersection with a normal translation
group excludes an affine second parabolic. This does not assume that a
quotient intersection equals an intersection of quotients.

The subfield alternatives use genuine maximality of B in the second
parabolic. The necessary index divisibility bounds odd PSL fields by four
and even PSL/PGL fields by six; the PGL2(5) indices fail the divisibility
test explicitly. The remaining S4/A5 stabilizers have t=3,4,5. Solving the
order equations, with n<=3 and t<=2 handled separately, gives exactly the
fourteen fields used in the certificate. Nonmaximal specified torus
parabolics at the small fields are retained and tested by the same actual
criterion. Thus the finite computation is a proved complete remainder, not
a sample extrapolated to all fields.

For the E16 auxiliary result, each subgroup fixes a unique projective
point, so conjugacy reduces to scalar multiplication on four-dimensional
F2-subspaces of F64. The scalar stabilizer together with zero is a field
whose degree divides both six and four. The F4-stable subspaces form one
orbit of length 21; the other 630 spaces form ten orbits of length 63.
This establishes the eleven classes and the two normalizer orders without
borrowing the source's numerical subgroup census.

Finite implementation and closure
---------------------------------

The GAP program constructs each named PSL2(q) and checks its order, then
uses complete conjugacy classes of subgroups and their actual conjugates.
Both torus signs are included. The unique selected dihedral class is
checked, and is the class supplied by the retained torus description.
Every proper second subgroup is tested using the literal intersection,
both double-coset conditions and actual maximality. Generation is also
checked. No order-only intersection or local-order substitute is used.

Normalizer and full automorphism-stabilizer orbits supply the two class
counts. Swap transports are actual group elements and form an involution
on the ordered classes. Four nontrivial literal transports are exported
and rechecked independently. One representative of each full
automorphism orbit suffices for graph invariants, since automorphisms
induce isomorphisms of these coset systems.

The incidence edges come from a full transversal of the actual
intersection; duplicate edges are rejected. The induced permutation
action is verified faithful. The arc routine successively takes point
stabilizers of a representative nonbacktracking arc, checking transitivity
on every permitted extension. Previous vertices may repeat, as required
for arcs; only immediate backtracking is forbidden. Reaching the safety
cap is an error, not a finite result. The separate standard-library
verifier checks the literal graph, degree/type consistency, connectedness,
girth and diameter, and the displayed inventory. Its independent girth
implementations and the damaged-output tests exclude several possible
parser or incomplete-result failures.

The wrapper rehashes all eleven author exports and thirty-three explicit
dependencies before output. The retained earlier graph failure and the
earlier wrong expected inventory size are preserved as failed history,
not successful execution premises. Current complete results have nineteen
representatives, not twenty. Fresh output may choose different equivalent
permutation representatives; the semantic table is identical. The fresh
