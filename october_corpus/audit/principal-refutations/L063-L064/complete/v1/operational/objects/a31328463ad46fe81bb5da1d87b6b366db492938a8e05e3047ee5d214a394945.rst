The normalizer computation
-------------------------

The characteristic C4 in D8 forces its normalizer to preserve the unique
cyclic torus. In D(4n), written with a rotation R of order 2n, a reflection
in G can be selected because the given D8 already supplies one. The index
two subgroup is generated by R squared and that reflection. All reflections
of the D8 therefore have even rotation exponents. Conjugation by powers of
R makes every such subgroup conjugate in E, while retaining its unique
rotation subgroup of order four. The condition for R^k to normalize it is
n/2 dividing 2k, equivalently d dividing k. This gives D16, contained in G
precisely for even d. The index-two orbit calculation then gives exactly
two G-classes for even d and one for odd d.

The incidence count of S4 overgroups is justified without assuming its
answer. A Sylow-two subgroup has three conjugates inside S4 and normalizer
D8. Transporters of S4 overgroups containing the fixed B can be adjusted
inside S4, so the relevant normalizer acts transitively with stabilizer B.
The two S4 classes and the D8 classes are both exchanged by the outer
coset when they split. Thus even d gives two overgroups in the same class,
whereas odd d gives one in each class. Every B is covered: there is one
E-class, and a Sylow-two subgroup of an S4 is one representative. Distinct
S4 overgroups intersect exactly in their common maximal subgroup B. The
chosen interchanger has its square in B because the normalizer quotient
has order two.

The graph maps and the full group
--------------------------------

The coset incidence graph is simple, connected and cubic. Connectedness
uses distinct maximal S4 subgroups generating the simple G. The G-action
is faithful because its kernel is normal in G and contained in a proper
vertex stabilizer. Conjugation by t gives the well-defined type map T;
its square is left multiplication by t squared because t squared lies in
both parabolics. In the even case the map C(gM_i)=g t^{-1} M_(1-i)
commutes with all left multiplications and squares to the identity. It is
nontrivial because it exchanges types. These computations check on actual
coset representatives, not only at the level of abstract group names.

Tutte's bound applies since this connected simple cubic graph is
arc-transitive under its full group A. It gives [A:G] at most four. The
core of G in A is normal in simple G; a trivial core would embed G into
S4, impossible for |G| at least168. Therefore G is normal in A. The
centralizer of G in A has order at most two: a type-preserving element
centralizing G is trivial on each self-normalizing coset orbit. A type
exchange centralizing G is possible only for conjugate stabilizers.

For even d, C is the nontrivial centralizer element. The image in Aut(G)
must preserve the sole S4 class represented by the two vertex types;
outer automorphisms cannot occur. The full group is consequently G x C2.
For odd d the centralizer is trivial, and the already constructed type
exchange extends G inside Aut(G)=PGL2(p) to the full group. Thus the exact
answer is PGL2(p). The two answers differ in their centers, so the even
cases really contradict the printed PGL2(p) identification.
