Lemma 1
-------
Every walk with labels avoiding j lifts to an actual walk in H_j by using
l to adjust its endpoints and each intermediate pair. Consequently a
selected cycle, including a two-edge parallel cycle, is impossible. An
actual h-edge within one selected tree could only follow the single selected
h edge, since a different selected label on its path would be bypassed.
All graph uses of an omission orbit refer to actual permutation orbits.

Proposition 2
-------------
If a noncommuting mark were represented by just one simple nonloop edge,
evenness in the row-preserving case, or the perfect-matching condition in
the row-interchanging case, would force a doubled edge, with all remaining
points fixed or paired by l respectively. It would commute with l. Thus an
R mark has at least two simple edges, so Lemma 1 forbids a connected F.
The forest count gives r<=m, and equality gives exactly two components.

Lemma 3
-------
Two distinct additional h-edges cross the component cut. Their two internal
tree paths cannot contain any selected label other than h. Thus one path
is empty and the other is exactly the selected h-edge ab, yielding the two
edges ax,bx. A third distinct crossing edge is impossible. Row-preserving
matchings cannot make a triangle; evenness leaves precisely two actual
transpositions, since multiplicities (2,2) commute with l. Row-interchanging
matchings have degree two on their nonisolated contracted vertices. The
only noncommuting remaining form is the triangle, with one edge of each
kind and h=l elsewhere. The written coordinate formula proves the opposite
three-cycles of lh on the two rows. Isolated vertices are correctly retained.

Proposition 4
-------------
Every selected C edge is a bridge after adjoining all actual R edges: an
alternate path would lift to its forbidden omission walk. Such an edge
cannot coincide with an R edge for the same reason. Adding the C bridges
therefore raises graph rank by |C|=m-2-|R|.

For one R mark, its graph rank is two, so the total graph rank is m-1 and
the graph is connected. In the block-preserving case its two edges form
a forest, and adjoining bridges gives a tree. This corollary, added after
the working note, follows completely from the same argument; no new
computation or independent group-action premise is needed.

For two R marks, graph rank is at most three. If the union still had rank
two, the connected-component partition already generated by either mark
would be unchanged by adding the other. Both would therefore occupy either
the same two disjoint edges or the same three vertices. In either case the
two-component F cut supplies the same unique internal unordered pair for
both selected R edges, contradicting Lemma 1. Hence rank(J)=3, and adjoining
the m-4 bridges gives connectedness. Mutual commutation of the two R marks
is not needed for this graph-rank argument.
