Distinct review: the remaining fixed-point budgets
==================================================

I read the complete new two-page proof and the actual required acquired
interfaces. This proof does not acquire the pending normalized-assembly theorem
as a premise. It starts with the same explicit ambient, natural-tail, head and
single-orbit assignment hypotheses and excludes f>=9 directly.

The full ambient interval inequality is 2(r-5)<=n-f-c, counting every nontrivial
H-orbit. Since H contains the nonidentity first tail mark, c>=1. Thus f+c<=11
leaves only f=9 with c<=2 and f=10,c=1. The actual head anchor x=b^sigma is
outside D,b, fixed by delta and by K. If H also fixed x, the eight-point set
D union {b,x} would be preserved by each of the head, tau, sigma and H,
contradicting ambient transitivity in degree at least thirteen. This proves
x lies in a nontrivial H-orbit, a needed extra argument.

For one nontrivial orbit, delta must preserve it; its fixed anchor makes its
commuting action trivial there. Only at most three additional H-fixed points
remain available for delta. Its odd involution is therefore literally one
transposition, so the acquired exterior-pair obstruction applies. For two
orbits the deficit sum is -1. Exchanging them costs at least two by transported
selected-colour bounds (including the explicit empty size-two case), and is
impossible. If both internal actions are trivial the same literal-pair
obstruction applies on the two extra fixed points.

A nontrivial preserved internal orbit W is free of size 2q and has deficit
at most -1, including empty assignments. It cannot be first, because the
other deficit would then be strictly negative. Thus it has deficit -1 and
the first orbit V has deficit zero. Delta must be trivial on V, and x lies
there. For q=1 oddness prevents a second transposition on the extra fixed
points, returning to the literal-pair obstruction. For q>=3 the actual row
separator applies with its seven required fixed points (it does not require
that there be only seven). Its nonterminal bound and the residual interval
support exceed n. No unproved blanket row or square-component claim is used.

For q=2, the sole W label j>=6 is omitted from the first assigned set. The
accepted fixed-anchor equality makes that first set an initial interval, so
j=r-1. I checked the two-pair argument directly: an eligible j-edge joins
distinct delta-pairs. Any other tail mark exchanging the pair vertices,
together with tau, would connect W in G_j, contrary to that edge's ambient
eligibility. K has the j pair exchange and is intransitive on W, so it has
two rows of size two and its pair-trivial elements fix W. Therefore rho_j
is a double transposition on W and all other marks of K fix W pointwise.

For Q=<rho_j>, the prefix through j-2 centralizes Q. Every Z_l=W^{G_<=l}
is therefore a union of nontrivial Q-pairs, disjoint from all nine actual
H-fixed points and also the distinct K-fixed x. If Z_4=W, prefix through4
and H both preserve W; transitivity excludes this, so |Z_4|>=6. At each
l through j-3 all later marks except j fix W and centralize that prefix,
hence fix Z_l. The prefix and Q preserve Z_l; it is proper because of the
ten excluded fixed points. Consequently rho_(l+1) must enlarge it, adding
at least a whole Q-pair. There are j-6 increments, giving |Z_(j-2)|>=2j-6.
The ten fixed points force n>=2j+4, contrary to j=r-1 and n=2j+3. The
endpoint j=6 is valid: the base Z_4 bound alone gives the contradiction.

The proof is sound under its stated assignment assumptions. It establishes a
second bounded exclusion, not whole L044 or a solution of the split assignment
and global attachment obligations. Combining it with a separately accepted
f>=9 theorem is logically possible but not a premise of this review.
