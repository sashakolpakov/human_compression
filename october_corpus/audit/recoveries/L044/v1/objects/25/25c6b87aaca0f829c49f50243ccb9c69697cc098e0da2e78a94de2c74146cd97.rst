The new point is the equality localized to a full tail orbit O: E_O=|O|.
A minimum selection has at most one cycle in each component, so this equality
forces every component in O to be unicyclic. The middle generator belongs to
the tail and preserves each tail orbit, hence preserves the localized equality.
After transporting a component and deleting its square, reinsertion cannot
meet a different unicyclic component; it must reconnect its four trees. Trees
in other orbits do not affect this argument. This establishes the needed
component partition and full-neighbour assertion without global absence of
trees.

For an ordinary slide, the localization and square-block arguments keep the
other selected edge of its bridge colour inside the same component. The edge
count and single-cycle property then preserve that component's vertex set.
If that other edge were on the chosen connector, the two paths on opposite
sides of its first bridge respectively contradict eligibility for the square
colour and for the bridge colour. Thus the remainder of the connector survives.
For a middle move the last full edge leaves O, so its colour is outside the
tail. It commutes with the middle index, which is at least two greater than
the tail boundary. The exit remains an actual exit and the connector shortens.
Both cases contradict minimality. Summing the resulting tree deficits proves
2d<=|U|-c and the stated rank corollary when c>=2.

to it, and this does not accept the broader literal source5.27 statement.
Every later application must prove the actual ambient hypotheses. Nor does
this review accept the suggested extension to an arbitrary middle interval.
No IP or parity premise is used by the proved combinatorial theorem.
