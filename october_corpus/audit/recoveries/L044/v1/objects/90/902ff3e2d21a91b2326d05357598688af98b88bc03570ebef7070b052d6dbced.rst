String commutation and paired orbit cuts alone do not imply rigidity
===================================================================

This bounded example isolates a missing hypothesis in a possible proof route.
It is not a counterexample to L044's C-group theorem or a conjecture solution.
Use zero-based generator labels in this note::

  rho0=(1,2)(3,4)(5,7)
  rho1=(2,6)(7,8)
  rho2=(1,3)(2,4)
  rho3=(1,5)(2,7)(6,8)

They generate S8 and satisfy all string commutations. Select edges 12,34 of
label0;26,78 of label1;13,24 of label2;15,68 of label3. Every selected edge
crosses two orbits of the subgroup omitting its generator. The selection is
connected, with eight vertices and eight edges, and has the unique alternating
square 1-2-4-3 with labels0,2. The full graph also contains the alternating
square 1-2-7-5 with labels0,3. It shares the selected label0 edge12, but exactly
one of its label3 edges (15) is selected. Edge27 is not selected.

Thus the corresponding rigidity conclusion cannot follow from string
commutation and paired orbit-crossing alone. The full intersection property
fails: the parabolics on labels012 and123 each have order720, their intersection
has order24, whereas the subgroup on labels12 has order12. Those two ordered
end-parabolic comparisons are the only failures among all256 subset pairs.
The first and last generators are odd. No claim about even or C-group actions
follows from this diagnostic.

Run GAP4.15.1 on check.g. It checks the exact group, commutations, omission
orbits, connected selected graph, actual second square and all subset
intersections. The successful quiet run is retained in execution.stdout.log;
execution.stderr.log is empty. An earlier syntax-error attempt is recorded in
scope.json only; it is not evidence of a mathematical result. This is a new
root-authored diagnostic and has not yet had a distinct review. No Lean
verification, historical priority, completed original or compression factor
is claimed. The source's Proposition4.13 is used solely to locate the attempted
stronger statement; its full hypotheses must not be discarded.
