Working mathematical review: contracted fracture graph
=====================================================

Verdict and limits
------------------
The snapshotted working argument is sound through the forest reduction,
the two local normal forms, and graph rank(J)=3. I found no gap or
counterexample in these elementary steps. This is a review of a working
note, not acceptance of a frozen manuscript or of Proposition 3.5 in full.
The equality exclusions remain with their authors. I have not used the

Precise standing hypotheses
--------------------------
For the local arguments, H is generated by the retained involutions in
the actual natural even permutation action on two blocks of size m;
H is transitive, every single-mark omission H_j is intransitive, and l
is a distinguished mark interchanging the blocks. The other labels are
indexed by the original string. For the empty-R exclusion, also state the
parent alternating string C-group, n=2m>=16, and the explicit smaller-degree
induction required by the acquired pair-block theorem. Degrees 12 and 14
are handled by the already acquired finite maxima, not by an implicit
extension of the m>=8 argument.

The source's physical pages 10--12 were read in full. Its claim that H acts
on the l-orbits is not a valid premise when an R mark breaks those pairs.
The new proof never uses such an action. Graph paths, not quotient group
actions, are the correct object here.

Empty R
-------
Inside the setwise stabilizer of the two blocks, write l as row interchange.
An element commuting with l has the same permutation on each row, followed
possibly by l, so the centralizer is diagonal S_m times <l>. The pair-action
kernel is exactly <l>. Actual IP gives <C> intersect <l>=1. Therefore <C>
and its pair image are marked-isomorphic, so IP and string relations really
do pass to that image. Its transitivity follows from transitivity of
H=<l,C>, because l fixes each pair. Its rank is r-2.

The complete acquired L047 theorem was reread: a proper transitive string
C-group of degree m has rank at most m/2, or the listed m/2+1 exceptions;
the only degree-eight exception has rank five. Hence r-2>=m-2 excludes all
proper transitive images for m>=10, and excludes the degree-eight exception
when m=8. The image is A_m or S_m and is primitive. Only at this point is the
pair system maximal, justifying the acquired pair-block theorem. This
argument is not a claim that an arbitrary preserved pair system is maximal.

The forest and the stronger path property
-----------------------------------------
Each H_j contains l, so each of its orbits is a union of entire l-pairs.
Transitivity supplies a j-edge between two such orbits. Every walk on
contracted pairs with labels avoiding j lifts to an actual walk with those
same labels and inserted l-edges: at each intermediate pair, insert l if
necessary to reach the endpoint of the next actual edge. Its endpoints can
likewise be chosen to be the endpoints of the selected j-edge. This is an
H_j walk, giving the required contradiction.

This proves that selected edges have no cycles, including two-edge cycles
of distinct labels. For an actual h-edge inside one selected tree, its
unique tree path cannot contain a selected j-edge with j!=h: the new edge
and the other path edges bypass that j-edge. Since only one h-edge was
selected, the path must be exactly that selected h-edge. Thus every other
contracted h-pair joins different selected components. Here h always means
a retained label other than l; say this explicitly in the manuscript.

A noncommuting h must join at least two distinct contracted pairs. If h is
row preserving, one pair supports at most two transpositions, evenness
forces both, and h then commutes with l. If h interchanges rows, it is a
perfect matching; one nonloop contracted pair is necessarily doubled and
all remaining pairs have h=l, again commuting. With R nonempty, the forest
cannot be connected. Thus r<=m, and at r=m it has exactly two components.

Two-component local support
---------------------------
Take selected h-edge ab. If two additional h-pairs cross the component cut,
orient them c--d and e--f with c,e in the first tree and d,f in the second.
Their two tree paths and these edges form a cycle, even when one tree path
has length zero. Every selected edge on the two paths must have label h by
the lift argument. Since the only selected h-edge is ab, the path in the
other tree has length zero and the path in its tree is exactly ab. The two
crossing pairs are consequently a--x and b--x. A third distinct crossing
pair cannot have this property relative to both preceding pairs. Therefore
the complete simple contracted h graph is a selected edge, that edge plus
one crossing edge, or their triangle with the opposite vertex x.

For row-preserving h, its graph is the union of the two row matchings. This
is a proper two-edge-colouring wherever the two edges are distinct; in
particular it has no odd cycle. A noncommuting h therefore has exactly two
simple edges, one selected and one crossing. Their multiplicities are at
most two. Evenness forces (1,1) or (2,2); the latter swaps entire l-pairs and
commutes with l. Thus precisely two actual transpositions remain. No other
transpositions are hidden inside pairs, since a row-preserving map cannot
interchange the two points of one pair.

For row-interchanging h, every pair either supports its internal h-edge,
or has two outgoing incidences. Correct the wording to say that the nonloop
multigraph is 2-regular ON ITS NONISOLATED VERTICES. The other vertices are
isolated after loops are deleted. One or two simple edges can only form
doubled disjoint edges, which commute with l. The remaining possibility is
the triangle with multiplicity one on all three edges, with h=l outside.
In coordinates h maps row zero by a permutation p and row one by p^-1;
on the triangle p is a 3-cycle. Hence lh is a 3-cycle in one row and its
inverse in the other, independently of the composition convention.

The two-neighbour graph
-----------------------
Every selected C-edge is a bridge of J union E_C: any alternate graph path
uses only R labels and the other selected C labels, and so lifts to the
forbidden omission walk. None duplicates an R edge, by the same argument.
Adjoining all m-4 such bridges raises graph rank by exactly m-4; the final
rank is at most m-1, so rank(J)<=3.

Each single R graph has rank two. If their union also had rank two, its
connected-component partition is already the partition generated by each
individual graph. Thus either both have the same two disjoint edges, or
both are supported on the same three vertices. In the first case the fixed
F-component cut makes the same unique edge internal, forcing both selected
labels onto it. In the second, the vertices cannot all be in one F component,
since each R graph has a crossing edge. A 2+1 colouring of three vertices
has precisely one internal unordered pair; each R graph must contain and
select it. Both conclusions contradict the forest's prohibition on two
selected labels on one unordered pair. Therefore rank(J)=3, and J union E_C
is connected. No use of a putative pair action, of the diagnostic census,
or even of the mutual commutation of the two R marks enters this graph-rank
argument.
