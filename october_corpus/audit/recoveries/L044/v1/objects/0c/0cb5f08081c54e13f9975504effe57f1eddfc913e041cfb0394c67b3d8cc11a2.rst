The decorated attachment lemma is valid with its stated hypotheses.
Conjugation by the kernel fixes all four attachment vertices and
centralizes the auxiliary restriction of a. Products of its conjugates
of a therefore cancel this restriction and give pure three-cycles.
They generate Alt(O plus u), including the two-point orbit case, where
this is Alt(3). Conjugation by b, then a, then b, and alternating support
gluing give Alt(O plus all four vertices). All conjugations preserve the
union of the main supports and the auxiliary set, so no auxiliary action
is introduced into these pure subgroups. Normality and transitivity
carry O through every kernel orbit on W; their common four-point support
then glues to the full pure alternating group. No IP of a projection is
assumed at any step.

For the global application, if a prefix omission were transitive on its
support X, every omitted-generator edge could be replaced by a path in
that omission. Adding the omitted generator to the global omission
could not change its orbits. This establishes the omission hypothesis
needed by the previously accepted Table 2 theorem.

For a row with H=Alt(X), all tail generators except the first centralize
H and fix X pointwise. The first one centralizes H_-; a point it moves
out of X must be fixed by H_-. The exact finite checks show either no
such point or exactly the one endpoint c. Its action on X minus c is in
the entire computed centralizer, so its restrictions to the four-point
attachment and the auxiliary set are controlled, not merely selected
examples of permissible actions.

Connectivity of the global coloured graph forces the tail to be
transitive on outside(X) plus c: the only exit from X is at c, so an
excursion into X can be deleted when connecting points in that set.
The kernel of the action on the four attachment vertices has at most
two equal-size orbits. If |X| is nine or ten, the outside-plus-endpoint
set has at least four or three vertices respectively, so none of these
orbits can be a singleton. For |X|=11 the computed attachment action is
trivial and that set has at least two vertices. Thus the uniform lemma
applies to all cases, including the nontrivial decoration in rows 6 and 7.

It gives a pure Alt(5) in the intersection of the prefix and the last
two prefix generators together with the tail. Marked IP would make
this intersection the last-pair group of order ten or twenty, an
impossibility. In the case-summary refinement, all auxiliary actions
are even and have order at most two. Global evenness therefore makes
the main action even. Since the pure alternating factor is already
present, any nontrivial auxiliary image splits off as an actual C2
factor, by multiplying by the inverse main component. This proves the
stated group shapes on their disjoint supports.
