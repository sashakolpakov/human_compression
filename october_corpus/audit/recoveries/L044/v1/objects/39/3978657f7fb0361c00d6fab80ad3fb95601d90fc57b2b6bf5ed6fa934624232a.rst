The saturated forest
--------------------

Omission-essentiality gives one eligible edge for each of the N-2 marks.
The selected edges form a forest: a cycle, including any parallel two-edge
cycle, would give an actual avoiding path across an eligible omission cut.
A forest on N vertices with N-2 edges has exactly two components, counting
isolated vertices. Each component is contained in an actual T-orbit; since
T is intransitive, the two components must be exactly its orbits.

For a selected edge j, all other selected edges lie in the actual omission
of t_j. Its two tree pieces are connected by those remaining edges and
cannot be joined by that omission, by eligibility of edge j. The omission
also cannot cross to the other actual T-orbit. Thus every t_i for i != j
preserves both pieces. For fixed i, the simultaneous partitions given by
all tree cuts except i have one two-vertex cell, the endpoints of i, and
all other cells singleton. Consequently t_i is precisely its selected
transposition. This also rules out any hidden action on the other tree.
If both tree components had edges, their nonempty sets of distinguished
transpositions would commute with one another and disconnect the prescribed
noncommutation diagram. One component is therefore a singleton and the
other supplies the full natural S_(N-1). No string-IP assumption is needed
for this auxiliary lemma.
