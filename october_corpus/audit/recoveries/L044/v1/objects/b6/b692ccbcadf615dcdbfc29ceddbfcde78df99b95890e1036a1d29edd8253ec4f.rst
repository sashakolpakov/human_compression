Paired central colours
----------------------

The early-colour calculation and the transitive-B exclusion give paired
colours3,4, and the complete reversal gives r-4,r-5. For a unique colour
5<=i<=r-6, the acquired actual orbit lemma makes the prefix-head orbit
fixed pointwise by every later mark. The reversed lemma applies because
the reversed index is also at least5 and gives the analogous assertion
on the right-head orbit. These orbits are distinct: if they coincided,
the left supported head would fix its own nontrivial support by the
reversed assertion. A unique eligible crossing gives exactly two
omission orbits, so the two sides are supported and transitive with
no shared labels. The retained later-unshared theorem then allows only
i=r-3 or i=r-1, outside the interval. Empty or overlapping boundary
ranges at the smallest ranks cause no unaccounted colour.

The actual odd prefix
---------------------

C is natural Alt(V), with degree n-5=2(r-3)+1 and rank p=r-3.
Its restriction is faithful and retains actual IP, rank, and every
adjacent noncommutation. If a C omission were transitive on V,
adjoining the full right head through its sole point v_R in V would
make the corresponding G omission transitive. Thus the local omissions
are intransitive. The two natural alternating groups have the stated
natural intersection, and ambient IP identifies its marking. The left
head, core transposition, exterior involution and distant fixed points
are unchanged. Every ambient G_i-eligible edge of a mark of C lies
in V and remains C_i-eligible, since C_i is a subgroup of G_i. No
reverse implication is used. These facts check every hypothesis of
the odd separator at rank p.

The separator is nonterminal in C, so its local suffix includes the
actual right crossing rho_(r-4), which moves v_R. Thus its fixed set
does not contain v_R. Each of the three remaining right-head marks
fixes V minus v_R pointwise. This verifies the transfer of the entire
local fixed set, including every later delta-fixed subset, to the
full ambient F=Fix(G_{>s}); it is not just a cardinality comparison.

The later support and the exterior orbit
---------------------------------------

Put d=r-s-1>=4. The reversed pure-prefix theorem applies to the actual
R=G_{>s}; its head orbit A has at least7 points and contains pure
Alt(A). The exact reversed parity-fibre formula identifies J=R intersect C.
It deletes the five nonexit head points and preserves every exterior
orbit. The resulting head orbit is still nontrivial: for |A|=7 its
two surviving points are exchanged by the literal crossing, and for
larger A its natural alternating subgroup is transitive. Consequently
J has support |U|-5 and exactly the same q nontrivial orbits as R.
The ambient interval theorem, with actual G eligibility, applies to
all d-3 marks of J and gives |U|>=2d-1+q. The transferred separator
gives |U|<=2d+1, hence q<=2 and |F|<=2s+2.

Delta centralizes R. Centralization of its pure Alt(A) fixes A
pointwise. If q=2, the sole other nontrivial orbit E is delta-invariant;
all remaining R-orbits are singletons. A nonidentity centralizing
involution on transitive E is fixed-point-free, and equality forces
|U|=2d+1. Tau agrees with delta on E because R fixes the left head
and beta. It belongs to every relevant ambient omission G_i.
Thus a selected eligible edge in E cannot join a delta-pair. A
cycle among its projected pair edges would lift, using other selected
marks and tau, to a path in G_i between the endpoints of its omitted
eligible edge. The projected graph is a forest, including exclusion
of duplicate projected edges, so |L|<=|E|/2-1.

For the complementary colours B, *every* ambient-eligible edge is in
the single proper J-orbit A': edges of J lie in its support, and B
has none in E. The selected-localization theorem permits arbitrary
B within the contiguous interval of J; it gives 2|B|<=|A'|-1.
Adding the bounds gives d-3<=d-7/2, impossible. Therefore delta
fixes all U pointwise. This checks the actual pair switch and the
confinement hypotheses that are essential to that argument.

Every witness route supplies the claimed fixed subset
-----------------------------------------------------

For two K_C-intransitive witnesses, both whole witness orbits are
pointwise delta-fixed. Equal endpoints supply 2s+1 fixed points.
Unequal endpoints with s>=j+1 supply the seven head points, the
whole shorter orbit of size at least s-3, and a truncated subset
of the longer orbit of size at least s-4: at least2s points.
For the crossing endpoint s=j+1 the shorter orbit and longer
left endpoint union each have size at least j-3, giving 2s-1
points together with the head. This includes j=5,s=6. The other
j=5 route uses the actual transported orbit; the complete proof
gives at least2s+2 points fixed by delta and by the later suffix.
These sets all lie in the local fixed set already transferred.
Thus in every such route delta is fixed on at least2s-1 points
of F and on all U. Its remaining support has size at most3.
Oddness makes it exactly one transposition. The acquired exterior-
pair obstruction is applied inside C, where its odd degree, rank,
natural tail and all required later ambient pairs have been checked.
It is not misapplied to the unpaired end colours of G.

For a K_C-transitive witness, the complete proof supplies disjoint
O,T of size N>=s-4, with s>=6, and eight further fixed head/anchor
points. T and the eight points are delta-fixed. If delta preserves
O, its restriction is that of tau in C_j. It preserves the unique
C_j-eligible edge; since its endpoints lie in distinct C_j-orbits,
it fixes each. Centralization of transitive K_C then fixes O
pointwise. At most2 points remain in F and delta is again the
forbidden transposition. Otherwise O^delta is a distinct K_C-orbit
in F, disjoint from T and the eight points. The bound |F|<=2s+2
leaves at most2 points for it; N>=s-4 and s>=6 force N=2,s=6.
The eight points and the three pairs exhaust F. Delta exchanges
two pairs by two transpositions, fixes the third and fixes U,
contradicting oddness. No restricted quotient IP or purity outside
an explicitly supported natural alternating subgroup enters here.
