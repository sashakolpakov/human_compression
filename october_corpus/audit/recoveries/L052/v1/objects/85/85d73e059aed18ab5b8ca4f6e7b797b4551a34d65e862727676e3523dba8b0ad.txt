Finite coset geometries in Highly symmetric hypertopes
Human Compression project
Abstract
We verify the three finite examples and all twenty-three table entries in Fernandes–
Leemans–Weiss, using their exact markings and presentations. The common coset criteria
are acquired from the earlier recovery ofHexagonal extensions of toroidal hypermaps; their
application here includes the actual flag and residue tests. All printed orders and regularity
assertions hold. One group name and both chiral flag-count captions require correction. The
completeness conjecture concerning the chiral list is not used or proved.

45 Scope, parameters and attribution
A minor domain qualification is needed in the source’s rank-three toroidal discussion. Its un-
restricted wording excludes (1,1), but one must also exclude the degenerate unit periods. For
example,
⟨x, y|x 3, y3,(x −1y)3, xy−1x⟩=⟨x|x 3⟩=C 3,
sincey=x 2. Its three pair-difference parabolics are all the whole group; its standard coset
system has one element of each type and is not thin. The usual admissible parameter domain is
therefore necessary when invoking the prior toroidal classification. We make no claim here that
a finite sampling proves that classification. Every period in Table 1 has norm at least three, and
every Table 2 period has norm at least four; all are checked separately.
The source attributes the regular classification of locally toroidal polytopes to McMullen–
Schulte, the rank-three chirality criterion to Breda D’Azevedo–Nedela, and the toroidal hypermap
families to Corn–Singerman. Those classification statements remain explicit prior inputs wherever
the general source recovery uses them. Our two finite tables are now verified positive examples
independently of their completeness. Problem 9.2 and the unnumbered completeness conjecture
remain separate questions. The old negative answers to Problems 9.3 and 9.4, including their
restricted Lean scope, are acquired in the general recovery and receive no new credit here.
The source presentations, examples and positive conclusions belong to the source authors and
their cited predecessors. The finite coset certificate theorem and its original implementation are
reused from the accepted L003 recovery. The present contribution consists of the literal L052
applications, independent permutation and incidence checks, exact named-group maps, and the
explicit corrections above. Named finite-group constructors, exact GAP presentation routines,
and the named CHEVIE root model are declared computational and identification premises. They
are not hidden inside a claim of Lean verification or new classification.
