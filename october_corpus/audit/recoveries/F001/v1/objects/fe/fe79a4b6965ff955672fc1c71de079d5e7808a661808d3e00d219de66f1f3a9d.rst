Independent calculations for the endpoint lemma
================================================

Working mathematical note, publication_recovery, 2026-10-13.
Not an accepted complete endpoint theorem. Work over an algebraic closure
of odd characteristic. A=<a,b>, h=ab, and D is a nonabelian dihedral
group centralizing A. The ambient form is nondegenerate in dimension5.
Every string mark lies in A, D, C(A) or C(D). An invariant subspace for
these four groups therefore contradicts full irreducibility.

The unipotent (2,2,1) case
-------------------------

Let h=I+N, N(v1)=u2, N(v2)=-u1, and N vanish on u1,u2,z.
The form has B(ui,vj)=delta_ij, B(z,z)=1 and other entries zero.
Since aNa=-N, the induced map on U=im N has determinant -1:
the nonzero alternating form represented by N is changed to its
negative, and in dimension2 its similitude multiplier is determinant.
Thus U has one a-positive and one a-negative line.

There is an a-stable isotropic dual plane: the two eigenspaces of
the orthogonal involution a are nondegenerate and perpendicular;
choose an isotropic dual to each image eigenline in its matching
eigenspace, adjusting by a multiple of that eigenline. Their mutual
pairing is zero. The perpendicular residual line is a-stable and
det(a)=1 forces its eigenvalue +1. Scaling the paired bases puts
a=diag(1,-1,1,-1,1), with N exactly as above.

To obtain the whole centralizer, M commuting with N preserves U;
commutation with a preserves each of its two lines. Write M u1=A u1.
Commutation with N and orthogonality then force

  M u2=A^-1 u2,                 M v2=A v2,
  M v1=-(A H^2/2)u1+A^-1 v1+H z,
  M z=-A H u1+z.

The possible sign on the last z term is +1 because det(M)=1.
There are no omitted cross terms: commuting with a separates u2,v2
from u1,v1,z, commuting with N fixes the image lines, and their
isotropic dual pairings fix the remaining coefficients. Conversely
the displayed M(A,H) satisfies all equations for every A nonzero,H.

With matrices acting on columns,

  M(A,H) M(B,J) = M(AB,J+H/B).

An involution has A=+1,H=0 or A=-1 with arbitrary H. In the latter
case its eigenvalue multiplicities are (-1)^4,(+1)^1, so it is a
negative reflection. Two noncommuting involutions necessarily have
distinct parameters H,J and their product is M(1,J-H). For t=J-H
nonzero its squared nilpotent image is exactly <u1>: explicitly
(M(1,t)-I)^2 v1=-t^2 u1 and its square vanishes elsewhere.
C(D) preserves this characteristic line. A and C(A) preserve it
by the explicit forms, as does D. Thus the complete string is
reducible. This unipotent case cannot occur in full Omega5.

The unipotent (3,1,1) case
-------------------------

Put B(e,f)=1, B(z,z)=1, N z=e, N f=-z, and N e=0.
Then h=I+N+N^2/2 on W=<e,z,f>, and h fixes Y=W-perp.
The line im N^2 has a-eigenvalue alpha in {+1,-1}. Choose f
in that same eigenspace pairing nontrivially with e; its N-chain
is nondegenerate and a-stable. Thus a has eigenvalues alpha,-alpha,
alpha on e,z,f. On Y it is an involution aY with det(aY)=-alpha.

Every element of C_SO(A) is of the following form:

  M e=c e,                      M z=c z,
  M f=c f+y-Q(y)/(2c)e,
  M u=T u-c B(y,T u)e            (u in Y),

where c=+/-1, T is orthogonal on Y, det(T)=c, T commutes with aY,
and y belongs to the alpha-eigenspace of aY. These equations follow
by first commuting with N and a, then imposing B(Me,Mf)=1,
B(Mf,Mf)=0, and B(Mf,Mu)=0. They conversely preserve the form.

For alpha=-1, aY=beta I. If beta=-1, both a and b already have
four negative eigenvalues. This is a surviving all-negative end,
not an exclusion and not a conclusion about the other endpoint.
If beta=+1, y=0. The centralizer acts as c I on W and as T on Y,
with c=det(T). A noncommuting involution pair in it acts irreducibly
on the nondegenerate plane Y: its two restrictions are noncommuting
orthogonal reflections and their product has reciprocal eigenvalues
different from +/-1. On W it acts by a scalar character. Hence
C(D) preserves the distinct two-dimensional irreducible constituent
Y. A,D,C(A),C(D) all preserve Y, contrary to full irreducibility.

For alpha=+1, aY has a positive line and a negative line, both
nondegenerate. In their basis T=diag(lambda,mu), lambda,mu=+/-1,
c=lambda mu, and y=t y_plus. Normalize the translation coordinate
to u=t/c. The group law becomes

  (lambda1,mu1,u1)(lambda2,mu2,u2)
    =(lambda1 lambda2,mu1 mu2,u1+mu1 u2).

This is one central C2 factor times the one-dimensional additive
group extended by inversion. An involution with mu=+1 has u=0
and is central. Noncommuting involutions therefore both have mu=-1
and different u. Their product has nonzero translation and possibly
a central sign. Its square is a nonzero pure translation (2 is
invertible), whose squared nilpotent image is <e>. Thus C(D)
preserves <e>, already preserved by A,D,C(A). This is reducible.

Other signed unit-primary cases
-------------------------------

1. Negative primary (2,2), positive line. The previous (2,2,1)
calculation has no H mixing, since the two primary spaces must be
preserved. Its centralizer is M(A,0), hence abelian. No D as above.

2. Negative primary (3,1), positive line. Use the three-chain W
with one residual line Y in the negative primary space, and a
separate positive primary line Z. Write aY=beta, aZ=gamma;
det(a)=1 gives alpha beta gamma=-1. The centralizer formula from
the three-chain remains valid with T=lambda on Y and an additional
sign mu=c lambda on Z. If beta differs from alpha, y=0 and this
centralizer is abelian. If beta=alpha, y is arbitrary on Y and
gamma=-1. With translation t/c its group law is again a central
C2 times the additive line extended by inversion, with action
lambda/c. Noncommuting involutions have a product whose square is
a nonzero pure translation with characteristic image line <e>.
The same common-line argument excludes full irreducibility.

3. Positive primary (3), negative scalar plane. Distinct primary
spaces forbid y mixing. The centralizer is c I_W on the three-chain
and T on the plane, det(T)=c, with T commuting with the reversing
involution's restriction there. If that restriction is a reflection,
the centralizer is abelian. If it is scalar, nonabelian D acts
irreducibly on the plane and scalarly on W. Its centralizer also
preserves the plane, giving a common proper invariant subspace.

These explicit computations are independently checked reasoning;
they still need to be incorporated into a full exhaustive primary-
space classification and an immutable author packet before theorem
credit. In particular the semisimple endpoint cases, nonunit primary
blocks, surviving negative ends, middle twists, higher ranks and
finite reflection-group alternatives are not settled by this note.
