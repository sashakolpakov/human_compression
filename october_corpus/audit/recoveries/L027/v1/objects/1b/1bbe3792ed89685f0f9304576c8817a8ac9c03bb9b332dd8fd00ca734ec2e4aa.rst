Even characteristic, rank six
-----------------------------

Write R=(tau12,tau13,tau14,tau15) for the first regular end.  Up to
duality its acquired normal form is
R0=I+E23, R1=I+a E31, R2=I+b E13, R3=I+E21, with ab nonzero.
The other end is S=(tau45,tau35,tau25,tau15).  Thus
S0=R1 R3=I+(e2+a e3)e1^T, S1=R0 R3=I+e2(e1^T+e3^T), S3=R3.
S0 and S3 have the same axis x1=0 and distinct centres.  Consequently
the other end has the dual acquired normal form: S0,S2 have centre
u=e2+a e3 and S1,S3 have centre e2.  Hence S2=I+u f for a nonzero
row f with f(u)=0.  Then sigma1=R3 S2=I+E21+u f and
sigma5=R2 R3=I+b E13+E21.  Their required distant commutation forces
ab f=0 by the first row of their difference, impossible.
This repairs the source's unjustified simultaneous fixed-point step:
the second end can preserve a line rather than a point.

Odd characteristic, rank six
----------------------------

Nondegenerate directly regular rank-four ends are only the acquired
Heisenberg marking [p,2p,p] and the S5 simplex marking [3,3,3] in
characteristic five.  Their common middle label forces the same kind
on both ends.  In the simplex case all five rotation orders are three;
the whole group is a quotient of A7, and neither PSL3(q) nor PSU3(q)
has order2520.  Their simplicity and standard orders exclude this.

In the Heisenberg normal form R, the other end has
S0=[[-1,0,0],[-1,1,0],[0,0,-1]], S1=diag(-1,1,-1),
S3=diag(-1,-1,1).  Its first two homologies have common centre e2.
The actual acquired Heisenberg normal form then forces S2,S3 to share
the axis z=0 (with dual orientation accounted for).  Therefore
S2=[[-1,0,u],[0,-1,v],[0,0,1]].  Commutation with S0 gives u=2v.
Now sigma1=R3 S2=I-u E13-v E23 and sigma5=R2 R3=I-E32.
Their required commutation gives u=v=0, making sigma1 trivial.

Preliminary general reductions to write fully
---------------------------------------------

If a rotation sigma_i=t has order two, let L and R be generated by
the rotations strictly to its left and right.  String rotation
relations make t normalize each side.  The only potentially non-distant
cross pair has indices i-1,i+1; (a t b)^2=1 and inversion by t imply
[a,b]=1.  IP+ gives L intersection R trivial, so H=L R is normal.
To prove H proper do not assume independence of the rotation generators:
if t=l r, the actual indexed IP+ gives r in <L,t> intersection R=1
and then t in L intersection <t>=1.  Endpoint cases use the same
singleton-index intersection.  Thus a simple group cannot have a
Schlaefli entry two.  Derived regular ends are consequently nondegenerate,
and their acquired regular rank bound four gives chiral rank at most six.

PSU3(2), the nonsimple exception, needs its own argument.  A characteristic
two unitary involution is a rank-one elation, with axis the Hermitian
polar of its centre.  For q=2 there is only one nonidentity unitary
elation at each centre: its trace-zero scalar parameter has one nonzero
value.  Distinct commuting elations require common centre or axis;
polarity makes these equivalent.  Thus no two distinct involutions
commute, and no regular string end of rank at least three exists.
This rules out chiral rank at least five, enough for the principal bound.
It does not recover the source's stronger historical computation of no
chiral markings at any rank in PSU3(2).
