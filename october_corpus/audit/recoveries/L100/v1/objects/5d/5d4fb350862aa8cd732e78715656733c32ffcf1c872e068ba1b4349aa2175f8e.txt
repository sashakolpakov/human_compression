Primitive residues in rank two
Human Compression project
Working proof, 14 October 2026
LetGbe finite. We use right cosets, with incidence given by nonempty intersection. Firmness
means that every panel lies in at least two chambers. For a rank-two geometry, residual weak
primitivity requires a primitiveG-action on at least one type and primitive actions of both
vertex stabilizers on their respective neighbours. Thus it includes conditions on the rank-one
residues. No double transitivity is assumed.
Theorem 1.All connected, firm, flag-transitive, residually weakly primitive rank-two geometries
forGsatisfying the intersection property are obtained by the following construction.
1. Choose one representativeAof each conjugacy class of maximal proper subgroups ofG.
2. Choose one representativeHof eachA-conjugacy class of maximal proper subgroups ofA.
3. Take every actual subgroupBwithH < B < G, retaining it precisely whenA∩B=H
andHis maximal inB.
4. Retain the pairs passing Lemma 2. Identify pairs under automorphisms ofGand inter-
change of their entries.
The final identification classifies the specifiedG-actions. Distinct resulting classes may require
a further comparison if one wishes to forget the specified action and classify abstract incidence
geometries.
Proof.Choose an incident pair in such a geometry. Its stabilizersA, Bidentify the geometry
with the incidence structure of their cosets, by flag transitivity. Interchange the types so thatA
is maximal inG, and conjugate to the chosen representative. The common stabilizerH=A∩B
is maximal in bothAandB: in a transitive permutation action, primitivity is equivalent to
maximality of a point stabilizer. Conjugating withinAnow putsHin the second list. The
resultingBis one of the actual overgroups required in the third step. This proves completeness
before the incidence test and equivalence comparison.
Conversely, the two proper containmentsH < A, Bgive firmness. SinceH < BandA∩B=
H, the subgroupBis not contained inA. Maximality ofAtherefore gives⟨A, B⟩=G, which
proves connectivity. Every incident pair of cosets contains a common element, so it is a translate
of (A, B); hence the action is flag-transitive. The three maximality conditions give precisely the
primitive actions required above. These are all the nonempty residues that need checking in
rank two. Lemma 2 supplies the intersection property. An automorphism carrying one pair to
another, possibly interchanging its entries, induces the corresponding incidence isomorphism.

Application toPSL(3,4)
The computation uses the degree-21 permutation representation supplied by GAP. It acquires
the complete maximal-subgroup algorithms and data forG, its maximal subgroups, and the
intermediate groups encountered during the overgroup search. These subgroup classifications
are retained inputs; the argument above does not reprove them. In particular, the routine for
intermediate subgroups must return actual subgroups, rather than only conjugacy representa-
tives.
There are nine conjugacy classes of maximal subgroups. Their local lists give 42 pairs (A, H).
The third step yields 60 pairs (A, B), of which 31 pass the incidence test. They give the following
seven classes. The source labels refer to the complete Halle preprint of Gottschalk and Leemans,
Geometries for the group PSL(3,4), Report 99–30 (1999), pp. 7–8; its later journal edition has
not been collated here.

Both local actions are doubly transitive in six of the seven classes. Class 2.2 has a primitive
action of degree 15 whose point stabilizer has order 24; it cannot be doubly transitive, since 14
does not divide 24. Its other local action, of degree 4, is doubly transitive. These are outputs of
the computation: the search includes every primitive local action and rejects pairs only by the
stated tests. The seven unordered pairs of parabolic orders are distinct. Since the two type sizes
are [G:A] and [G:B], the seven representatives are also inequivalent as abstract incidence
geometries, even allowing interchange of types. Explicit automorphisms identifying the other
surviving pairs are supplied separately as images of generators.
The coset correspondence and the stabilizer criterion for primitivity are classical. The present
argument organizes these inputs into the same finite construction used for doubly transitive
residues, with the incidence test extended to all local pairs. No claim of historical novelty for
this enumeration principle is made.
