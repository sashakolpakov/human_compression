=========================================

1. Backward propagation of the full leaf kernels
------------------------------------------------
Let H=<h0,...,h_(k-1)>=Sym(W), acting naturally, with an endpoint anchor a
moved by h0 and fixed by the later marks. Suppose adjoining the new pure
leaf t=(a c) before h0 gives a C-marking. Put P_j=<h0,...,h_(j-1)> and
X_j=a^P_j, with P0=1 and X0={a}. The acquired exact leaf theorem gives

 P_j intersect Sym(X_(j-1)) <= P_(j-1)       (1<=j<=k).

Since P_k=Sym(W), it contains Sym(X_k). Descend on j: if P_j contains
Sym(X_j), it contains Sym(X_(j-1)), so the j-th test puts that entire
symmetric group in P_(j-1). Thus every P_j contains Sym(X_j). In
particular P1 contains the pure transposition on X1={a,a^h0}. Since
P1=<h0> has order two, h0 itself is this pure transposition.
This does not invoke rank bounds or a finite classification. The same
proof applies if the full leaf tests were obtained from an odd external
one-point attaching group, using the acquired odd-attachment theorem.

2. The completed existence theorem supplies an internal PURE cut
----------------------------------------------------------------
Let a fractured natural symmetric C-marking of degree n>=5 and rank r
satisfy 2r>n+2. The accepted sufficient-split theorem gives an internal
perfect split. If pure, stop. Otherwise reverse the order if necessary
and write q=(a b) beta, with beta supported in B minus {b}; its prefix
L and suffix R are supported and transitive on A,B. Both L and
J=<q,R> are odd, by sufficiency. Write p=rank L, s=rank R. Thus
r=p+s+1, n=|A|+|B|, and

 (2p-|A|)+(2s-|B|)>0.

If 2s>|B|, the acquired actual-wing bound gives R=Sym(B), hence
beta belongs to R and J=Sym({a} union B). The original IP and odd
external L give the full leaf tests for J at a. Part 1 makes its
first mark q pure, contradicting the present nonpure case.

Therefore 2p>|A|. The same wing bound gives L=Sym(A). The odd
external J and original IP give the full leaf tests for L, oriented
from its endpoint a. Part 1 makes the last mark p0 of L pure,
say p0=(d a). All earlier L marks fix a, by the anchor property.
They are transitive on A minus {a}: its permutation graph is obtained
from the connected L graph by deleting its pendant vertex a. In the
whole marking, omission of p0 therefore has precisely the two orbits
A minus {a} and {a} union B. The latter is transitive under J.
The earlier and later label intervals are separately supported there.
Thus p0 is a pure perfect cut of the whole marking.

It is internal. Indeed 2p>|A| and transitivity give p>=2 (a one-mark
transitive nontrivial action has degree two). Hence its index p-1>=1;
q and the old nonempty suffix follow it. This proves internal pure-cut
existence. Degrees5 and6 are the already acquired simplex bases, with
the outer degree-six natural action replaced by its marked-isomorphic
ordinary representative when needed.

3. Exact geometry at two pure cuts
----------------------------------
Suppose i<j are perfect cuts and both marks are pure. Write their
prefix/suffix supports as A_i,B_i and A_j,B_j. The prefix at i is a
subgroup of the prefix at j. Since each is transitive on its support
and the second fixes B_j, we have A_i subset A_j. More explicitly the
pure i-mark and its left prefix have a connected graph meeting A_i,
so their support, which lies in A_j, includes A_i. Likewise B_j subset
B_i. Put W=A_j minus A_i=B_i minus B_j. Write the cuts
u=(a b), v=(c d), with a in A_i, b,c in W and d in B_j. If j=i+1,
W is a singleton and b=c, because the prefix at j is generated by the
prefix at i and the sole pure edge u.

For j>i+1 put M=<marks i+1,...,j-1>. These marks fix A_i and B_j
pointwise, hence are supported in W. They are transitive on W: the
suffix at i consists of M, v and the suffix at j; the latter is
transitive on B_j, and its only edge to W is the pure v-edge c d.
Consequently all of W must belong to the M-orbit of c. Similarly
u joins a to the M-orbit at b. Thus

 <u,M> = Sym({a} union W),
 <M,v> = Sym(W union {d}).

These are actual supported groups, by the pure-leaf factorization.
Their original label sets intersect exactly in M, so original IP gives

                         M=Sym(W).

The submarking (u,M) is C. It is a pure leaf attached to the first
anchor b of M: every M mark except its first fixes b by the string
relations, and transitivity makes its first mark move b. Part 1 therefore
makes that first mark pure. It is a perfect cut of the whole marking:
removing its pendant b from the connected M graph leaves its tail
transitive on W minus {b}; its prefix is the old prefix joined through
u to b, and its suffix is that tail joined through v to B_j. Each
interval is supported on the indicated disjoint side.

Repeating with this new pure perfect cut and j proves that every label
between i and j is a pure perfect cut. Thus the set of pure perfect-cut
indices is one consecutive interval. The argument uses no rank bound.
The W singleton case is handled separately as above; the transitive
rank-one M case is the same two-point leaf calculation.

4. Adjacent pure marks at the first internal pure cut
----------------------------------------------------
Let i be the least internal pure perfect cut in a natural symmetric
C-marking with 2r>n+2. Write its supported transitive wings L,R,
with degrees a,b and ranks p=i,s=r-i-1. We again have

                         (2p-a)+(2s-b)>0.

If 2p>a, then L=Sym(A) by the exact actual-wing theorem. The old
submarking consisting of L and the pure i-mark is a C pure-leaf
extension of L. Part 1 makes its last mark pure, and the same pendant
argument makes it a perfect cut of the whole marking. It is internal
because p>=2, contradicting the minimality of i.

Hence 2s>b. Then R=Sym(B), and Part 1 applied to the old pure i-mark
followed by R makes the first mark of R pure. The pendant argument
again makes it perfect globally. Since s>=2, its index i+1 is internal.
Thus the least internal pure cut always begins a pair of internal pure
perfect cuts. This proof already works at the strict domain threshold;
in particular it works at the required target threshold 2r>=n+4.

6. Choice inside the pure interval and reversal
------------------------------------------------
Any consecutive pure perfect cuts form a simple path. At each adjacent
pair their middle point is fixed by every other mark (as shown in the
literal geometry). Iterating, the pure interval is a path whose internal
vertices are fixed by all marks outside that interval. Its endpoints
are the only possible contacts with the two outside labelled subfamilies.
Subdividing ANY edge of this path gives, in its final ordered labels,
the same longer pure path between the same endpoints; all outside marks
have the same positions and permutations. The explicit conjugacy fixes
all points outside its internal vertices and matches path vertices in
order from the same first endpoint. It intertwines every generator.
Therefore E_i(X) and E_j(X) are ordered-conjugate for any two internal
pure cuts i,j. No unlabelled graph-isomorphism shortcut is used.

Global reversal sends the pure interval to the reflected interval and
sends subdivision at i to subdivision at r-1-i after reversal (the two
new edges are exchanged by their path-order identification). Since all
pure choices give the same ordered isomorphism class, E commutes with
reversal on ordered classes. Its inverse D therefore does too. Thus
the two inverse maps descend to the quotient by both isomorphism and
duality, including self-dual classes; no division by two is used.
