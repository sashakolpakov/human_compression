Structural argument
-------------------

At n=2r-3, a pure cut i has prefix surplus d(i)=2i-|A_i| and suffix
surplus 1-d(i). Consecutive pure cuts increase d by exactly one.
If the first cut had positive surplus, the actual-wing bound would make
its prefix a supported symmetric group. Its old pure-leaf extension
then pays all full kernel tests. Backward propagation makes the previous
mark pure, and deleting its pendant endpoint leaves the earlier prefix
transitive. This is an earlier perfect cut globally, contradicting
minimality. Positive surplus cannot occur at prefix rank one. Reversal
gives d>=1 at the last cut. The acquired consecutive pure interval
therefore has exactly one adjacent pair with surpluses 0,1.

The resulting supports are A,{c},B. Their ranks p,q and degrees 2p,2q
satisfy p+q=kappa+1; neither rank can vanish, since its support is
nonempty and its surplus is zero. Root anchoring follows from distant
commutation with the adjacent pure bridge. Original IP supplies C to
each wing and its pure-leaf extension. Fracture transfers: a transitive
wing omission would, with the two bridges and opposite transitive wing,
make the corresponding whole omission transitive. No projected IP or
faithfulness is substituted for this argument.

Conversely two literal rooted wings join through one bridge. The actual
one-bridge product identity and nested intersection rectangle in the
acquired contraction proof give IP of every crossing interval by the
end criterion. The noncrossing intervals and one-sided intervals come
from the wing or its valid pure-leaf extension. The supported
transitive wings and bridge generate the full symmetric group.
Pure subdivision yields the stated boundary marking and its balanced
pair. Thus this is a converse, not merely a positive construction.

Ordered point conjugacy preserves the unique pair and restricts to
rooted conjugacy on both wings. Reversal exchanges the root-oriented
wings; it does not separately identify a wing with its own reversal.
The global natural degrees are odd and at least seven, so the acquired
inner-automorphism argument applies. Degree five is the separately
acquired simplex. The formula uses unordered pairs of rooted classes,
including w_h(w_h+1)/2 on the diagonal. It never quotients degree-six
