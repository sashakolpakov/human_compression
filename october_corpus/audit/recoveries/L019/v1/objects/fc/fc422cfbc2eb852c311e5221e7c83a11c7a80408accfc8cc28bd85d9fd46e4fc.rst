Connectedness and the even-factor bound
--------------------------------------

For an anchor belonging to the first mark, all other diagram components
fix that point and commute with its component. The anchor's full orbit is
therefore already the orbit of its component. Transitivity makes that
component transitive; a commuting permutation fixing the anchor fixes the
whole domain. Thus no nonidentity mark can lie in another component.
This argument needs neither primitivity nor full alternating generation.

The unique-crossing result has the required generic opening hypotheses:
G is a transitive connected subgroup of Alt_d with every omission
intransitive. It is not restricted to G=Alt_d. Its explicit strictly
smaller-degree alternating rank premises are supplied by the complete
acquired principal theorem, whose induction is already closed. Thus its
use for a possibly proper even attaching group is legitimate.

If there is no unique eligible edge, every colour has at least two:
otherwise adjoining that mark to its omission cannot connect distinct
omission orbits. The paired-cut normal form gives 2s<=d; the equality
component proof supplies a connected one-square selection. The full ladder
proof requires d=2s>8, which holds here. I read its final actual-action
paragraph, not just its weaker partition conclusion. It explicitly fixes
all other points for each positive-index adjacent-pair swap and accounts
for every rho_0 edge. Every R_j point is moved both by rho_0 and a
positive-index mark. A leaf in L is moved by rho_1, and perhaps rho_0;
if it has a single moving colour, that is the interior label 1 because
s>=6. Reversal preserves the absence of endpoint anchors. This rules out
equality without assuming the even group primitive. This is the essential
new consequence of the acquired ladder description and it is sound.

-------------------

supported on C; adjoining E makes the suffix transitive on D union Y,
with just the common point a connecting the two transitive actions.

Parity and critical degrees
---------------------------

The acquired trivial-factor theorem is unconditional for the stated
fractured natural symmetric marking in the strict rank range. Orient its

Both supports are proper and meet in a; the actual string relations and

they generate S_n. If both are odd, the acquired odd-attachment corollary

corollary. No converse to either sufficient condition is claimed.

hypothesis and transport finishes. The exceptional group, its action and

When L is even and J odd, the actual-wing theorem gives R=Sym(B).
Consequently beta lies literally in R and q beta=(a b) is available;
R and this transposition generate Sym({a} union B). The nonsymmetric
wing bound applies to L itself and gives 2i<=e. With n=e+m-1 and
r=i+k, the strict parent inequality yields
2k>m+1+(e-2i). If 2k>m+2, the induction is on a strictly smaller
actual symmetric degree. Otherwise the integer 2k lies strictly above
m+1 and at most m+2, so it equals m+2, m is even, and e-2i<1.
Together with 2i<=e this forces e=2i. The new even-factor theorem

2r=n+3. No critical factor has been presumed soluble in this argument.
The usual independent-generator bound ensures any strict symmetric
factor to which the n>=5 inductive statement is applied has degree at
least five; the smaller degrees cannot meet 2k>m+2.
