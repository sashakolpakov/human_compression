Mathematical verification
-------------------------

1. The coset kernel is the intersection of the conjugates of P and Q, namely
   core_G(P intersection Q). Quotienting by it preserves the coset incidence
   graph. Components are indexed by the subgroup generated by P and Q. These
   statements distinguish the faithful quotient from the original acting
   group and do not silently assume connectivity.

2. For a fixed connected ordered bipartite graph X, with each part of size at
   least two, the classification is by conjugacy under A=Aut+(X) of all
   edge-transitive subgroups H. Vertex and edge stabilizers give the coset
   geometry. Conversely, a marked isomorphism gives the explicit well-defined
   maps h(b) -> phi(h)(b) and h(w) -> phi(h)(w); they preserve incidence and
   conjugate the actions. In the other direction, transitivity adjusts a
   conjugating graph automorphism to fix the selected edge. This proves the
   precise ordered classification, not classification up to abstract group
   isomorphism alone. The pointwise edge-stabilizer version has the same
   classes for the same reason.

3. Stability is exactly H=A in this finite context. The common cyclic factor
   construction gives infinitely many unfaithful realizations. The retained
   C4 times C2 squared example correctly separates marked isomorphism from
   equality of abstract groups and incidence graphs. The Mathieu construction
   supplies an actual automorphism and an actual conjugating element; it does
   not repeat the unsupported assertion that every choice of that element
   works. The fresh GAP replay checks the named subgroup structures, the
   orbit profiles that prohibit inner conjugacy, and the displayed marked
   isomorphism.

4. The normalizer formula for duality is valid. A type-preserving element
   centralizing H and fixing the selected edge fixes all vertices. A
   type-exchanging centralizer element fixing that edge would identify its
   two stabilizers; connectivity then forces the excluded one-edge case.
   Thus conjugation is faithful on the edge stabilizer in N_Aut(X)(H), so
   the minimum order of an exchanging permutation is the actual duality
   order. The manuscript's nondegeneracy hypothesis matters here.

5. For a cubic graph, subdivision vertices have degrees three and two, so
   every automorphism preserves their types and is uniquely induced by an
   automorphism of the original graph. Arc transitivity on the original
   graph is precisely edge transitivity on its subdivision. The stabilizers
   and the four actual actions used for Max have the same permutation
   actions as the four coset actions. The previously accepted action proof
   is acquired; the new review also compares literal results on small graphs.

6. In the bipartite cubic case, A has index two in the full automorphism
   group F. Every required subgroup H has K=H intersection A edge-transitive
   and H=<K,x>, with x in N_F(K) outside A and x squared in K. Conversely
   each such pair gives an arc-transitive H with exactly that intersection.
   Enumerating a transversal of K in its normalizer is therefore complete,
   followed by actual conjugacy in F. No split-extension hypothesis is used.

7. The four Max positions are the actions for B<P, B<Q, P<H and Q<H.
   Maximality is equivalent to primitivity by the complete blocks/intermediate
   subgroup correspondence; double transitivity takes precedence. Norm is
   tested in the stated full type-preserving automorphism group. The cubic
   prime-index argument and the classification of transitive subgroups of
   S4 establish the two missing-code observations without enumeration.

8. Maximal-subgroup descent is exhaustive. Given any edge-transitive K,
   take a maximal chain from the ambient group down to K. Every overgroup
   in the chain is edge-transitive. Conjugating at a retained node places
   its next step among that node's complete maximal subgroup representatives;
   conjugacy deduplication in the original ambient group cannot lose the
   class. Order divisibility and transitivity are necessary hereditary
   conditions. Each retained descent is strictly proper, proving termination.
   The arc variant is the same theorem on a different explicitly specified
   domain. Completeness of the library's maximal-subgroup and full graph
   automorphism routines remains an explicit computational premise.

Source scope and qualifications
-------------------------------

The source authors and editions are identified explicitly. The fixed arXiv
PDF has SHA256 511bf8f5355ae30cfd503c461b444fc81eaa2eb0373e86d9eb689e82c9131120.
The published citation is Mathematica Slovaca 64 (2014), 991--1006. The retained
publisher-text rendering, rather than an unavailable publisher PDF, supplies
the final subdivision obligation. Its absence from the older arXiv edition
does not remove it from this recovery.

The quartic historical set equality uses two stated borrowed premises:
historical class distinctness/invariants and Wilson--Potocnik's later coverage
of the previously known graph list. Inclusion plus equal cardinality then
identifies the sets of 397 graphs. It is not a classification of all possible
semisymmetric graphs, nor an identification of every old tag or ordered
colour choice. The three named exceptions are identified using invariant
uniqueness only after this set equality has been established.

The printed stabilizer cutoff below 100 excludes K4,4, which the source
itself lists. The replacement states the actual recovered scope, full
vertex stabilizer below 200, equivalently |Aut+(X)|/|X| below 100. The 388
literal lower-cutoff graphs plus K4,4 give 1353 markings; eight additional
graphs contribute 290, yielding 397 inputs and 1643 markings. The selected
ordering of the parts of current graph 128_055 is reversed for the printed
Max aggregate. This transfers three entries, changes no Norm count, and is
recorded separately from the original witnesses. No historical ordered-part
identification is claimed.

The cubic completeness premise is the cited primary Foster/Conder/Potocnik
catalogue classification: all 796 symmetric graphs through 2048 vertices
and all 43 semisymmetric graphs through 768. The present work reconstructs
their graph data and performs the subgroup classifications; it does not
claim to reprove the upstream graph census. Exactly 714 symmetric inputs
are bipartite, while all 796 are used for subdivisions.

The auxiliary source corrections are explicit: vertex-plus-edge transitivity
does not force arc transitivity for an arbitrary acting group; the disconnected
colour statement requires qualification; arc-transitive but not vertex-
transitive is not the intended semisymmetric definition; equal abstract
groups and graphs need not give marked isomorphism; the Mathieu witness uses
a specified conjugator; and the 110-vertex dihedral subgroups have verified
orders 24 and 12. These corrections do not expand the counted conjecture
scope. The final large-stabilizer question and the earlier cyclic-configuration
question remain unclaimed.
