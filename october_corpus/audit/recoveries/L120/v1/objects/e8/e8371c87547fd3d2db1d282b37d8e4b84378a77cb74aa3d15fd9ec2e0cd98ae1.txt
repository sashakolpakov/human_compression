Rank spectra, permutation orbits and almost simple groups
An attributed synthesis of the fixed classification survey
14 October 2026
We consider the twenty-one-page edition of Leemans’s survey [1], arXiv:1910.08843v2, 11
May 2020. It contains thirty-one numbered theorems, one corollary, six tables, one diagram
figure, fifteen problems and three conjectures. A survey has a different logical contract from
a classification paper: most of its theorems are statements of earlier results. We therefore
distinguish the proofs acquired in this project from the published classification theorems that
remain explicit inputs. This distinction applies to every row of the accompanying clause register.
Recovering this synthesis does not confer recovery credit on any other original paper.
Our companionThree elementary interfaces for a survey of string C-groupsproves the full
rank-three intersection theorem, the symmetric rank constructions with their exact corrected
types, and two counterexamples to the literal survey Theorem 5.7. Here we assemble those
results with the acquired uniform theories, record the exact numerical conventions, and give an
explicit disposition for every source claim and question. We use “acquired” only for a complete
proof with its recorded independent review and acceptance; it does not mean an unproved
statement inferred from a matching numerical answer.
1 Three principles and their actual hypotheses
For an ordered involution markingρ= (ρ 0, . . . , ρr−1) writeG I =⟨ρ i :i∈I⟩. Its intersection
property is
GI ∩G J =G I∩J (I, J⊆ {0, . . . , r−1}).
The string condition requires distant marks to commute. In all uses of a subgroup, the marking
is the actual subgroup marking. Passing to an orbit or a block action is a homomorphism and
does not, by itself, preserve the intersection property.
The following three acquired principles account for much of the survey. First, a faithful action
of a finite groupHis the disjoint union of coset actionsH/K, with conjugacy classes of stabilizers
and their multiplicities as complete invariants. Its kernel is the intersection of the corresponding
cores. Stabilizers of a partial marking act on possible next marks, so successive orbit selection
gives a complete census when every admissible extension is tested. The acquired finite census
proofs establish this induction, the full intersection test, and the boundr≤ ⌊log 2 |G|⌋used for
termination.
Second, the acquired restriction-kernel theorem controls adjoining a leaf to a permutation
marking. It tests the actual kernels of prefix restrictions, rather than assuming an extension
preserves intersections. For high-rank symmetric markings, the pure-cut theorem strengthens
this to an invertible subdivision operation, compatible with isomorphism and reversal. It proves
stability, not merely the construction of additional examples.

4 Suzuki groups, Ree groups and arbitrary diagrams
The acquired Suzuki argument gives a particularly short example of how subgroup structure
and orbit counting complement one another. The outer quotient of Aut(Sz(2 N )), for oddN, has
odd order. An involution-generated intermediate group therefore lies in the simple socle. The
acquired involution-centralizer and dihedral arguments give precisely string rank three. Normal-
izing two commuting endpoint involutions reduces the possible third marks to a field parameter
action. Exact subfield support, followed by field Moebius inversion, removes the proper subfield
groups. Orbit counting and duality give precisely the formula in survey Theorem 6.2. All Suzuki
subgroup and generation theorems used in that derivation remain explicit acquired inputs.
For small Ree groups, the complete acquired proof gives string rank three forR(3N ) with odd
N >1, excludes larger ranks and excludes the nonsimpleR(3). The odd outer quotient again
excludes proper almost simple extensions. The recovery replaces the source’s false normality
step and keeps the exact subgroup and fusion hypotheses; it does not transport the Suzuki proof
by analogy.
Removing the string condition changes the problem. We retain as published inputs the full
arbitrary-C-group classification of Suzuki groups by Connor–Leemans [7], including existence of
a nonstring marking, and the full PSL 2 /PGL 2 classification by Connor–Jambor–Leemans [8].

6 Questions, attribution and the recovery boundary
Every one of the eighteen numbered problems or conjectures has an individual record. Conjec-
ture 1 is answered by the acquired later stability theorem, with its original authorship retained.
General symmetric enumeration (Problem 1) is therefore partly answered, not entirely solved.
The construction and maximum-rank theorems for alternating groups do not enumerate them
(Problem 2).
The project already has internally reviewed written work on the odd-qmaximum-four con-
jecture for PSL 4(q) (Conjecture 2), and on part of Problem 7. Those exact earlier arguments,
6
checks, reviews and priority qualifications are linked as historical project results. They are not
assigned another conjecture credit here. Problem 7 includes field and graph extensions, whereas
the recorded diagonal interval results do not cover those extensions. The unitary companion
results are likewise not silently substituted for a linear-socle question. No claim of historical
priority follows from the earlier bounded literature searches.
Problems 3–6 concern complete extension spectra or enumeration and are not settled by
the linear-socle obstruction. Problem 8 is a Ree enumeration problem. Problem 9 asks for
maximal ranks of the characteristic-two classical groups; construction of the displayed rank
intervals alone does not prove maximality. Problem 10, the O’Nan rank-three computation, has
the published answer 6536 in the 2022 classification just cited; it is not a new result of this
project. Problem 11 asks for the wider remaining sporadic classification and is not settled by
those three named groups alone. Problems 12–15 concern arbitrary C-groups, whose questions
are not answered by string classifications. Conjecture 3, thatA 11 is the only finite simple group
with a gap in its rank spectrum, is not proved by certifying that one gap. These are dispositions
of the fixed source questions, not a new search asserting their present-day openness.
The new arguments in this survey integration include the companion’s complete cyclic-
normal intersection proof, the explicit counterexamples to the dimension-three and hypermap
assertions, and the complete finite classifications just stated. Contraction, orbit multiplicities,
pure-cut stability, supported symmetry groups and the completed censuses are reused with their
previous authorship and reviews. The published external classification premises are listed sepa-
rately. Neither citations nor finite agreement are treated as reproofs. Of the thirty-two numbered
theorem and corollary clauses, twenty-five have an acquired proof, correction or counterexample
route, with their stated prior inputs. The other seven—Theorems 3.1, 4.6, 5.1, 5.4, 5.6, 11.1 and
11.2—retain a published classification or exact construction. Their statements and attribution
are part of this synthesis; their proof cost is not silently transferred to a newly proved theorem.
The nine sporadic numerical cells have the same explicit status. No additional Lean claim or
numerical compression factor is made at this intake stage.
