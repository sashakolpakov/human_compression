# A uniform rank-four construction for the linear diagonal extension

10 October 2026. Private phase-sixteen proposed theorem. Two internal mathematical reviews found no gap after a corrected root index in §3; these are not external peer review. Earlier frozen artifacts are unchanged. Publication priority has not been established.

Let $q=p^e$ be odd. Denote by $L_2(q)$ the unique subgroup of $\operatorname{PGL}_4(q)$ containing $\operatorname{PSL}_4(q)$ with index two over that socle. Thus $L_2(q)=\operatorname{PGL}_4(q)$ when $q\equiv3\pmod4$; when $q\equiv1\pmod4$, it is the inverse image of the order-two subgroup of the cyclic determinant quotient of order four.

**Theorem.** For every odd prime power $q\ge3$, the group $L_2(q)$ has a rank-four string C-group representation of type $\{4,2p,2p\}$. Consequently its maximum rank is four, retaining the separate phase-nine upper bound. This note does not establish rank-three existence or the entire rank spectrum of this extension.

The construction uses a fixed small-field triangular parabolic and elementary root matrices. Neither maximal-subgroup classification nor a character-sum existence theorem is needed.

## 1. A one-parameter string marking

For $h\in\mathbb F_q^*$ define

$$r_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1&0\\0&0&0&1\\-h&0&0&-1\\0&-h&0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
r_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,1),$$
$$r_2=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0&0\\1&1&0&0\\-1&-2&-1&0\\0&1&1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
r_3=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,-1).$$

Assume

$$\mathbb F_p(h)=\mathbb F_q,\qquad \Delta=1+4h\ne0.$$

Write $\rho_i=[r_i]$ for the projective images. Direct identities give

$$r_0^2=-hI,\qquad r_1^2=r_2^2=r_3^2=I,$$
$$r_0r_2=r_2r_0,\qquad r_0r_3=-r_3r_0,\qquad r_1r_3=r_3r_1.$$

Thus the projective generators are involutions with the string relations. Their determinants are $h^2,-1,1,1$ respectively.

Set $T=r_0r_1$. Then

$$T^2=\begin{pmatrix}h&0&0&2\\0&-h&0&0\\0&0&h&0\\0&0&0&-h\end{pmatrix},\qquad T^4=h^2I,$$

so $[T]$ has order four. For $M=r_1r_2$ and $M=r_2r_3$, one has $(M^2-I)^3=0$ and $M^2\ne I$. Since $p$ is odd, $M^2$ has exact order $p$. The two eigenvalues $1,-1$ both occur in $M$, so its projective order is exactly $2p$. This proves the asserted type independently of generation or IP.

## 2. The triangular parabolic is exactly a small-field Borel subgroup

Let $U$ be the lower unitriangular group over $\mathbb F_p$ in dimension four. Let $D$ be the diagonal sign group whose second diagonal entry is one; it has order eight. We claim

$$H=\langle r_1,r_2,r_3\rangle=U\rtimes D.$$

Certainly $H\le U\rtimes D$, and its diagonal image is all of $D$. Put $K=H\cap U$. In $U/[U,U]\cong\mathbb F_p^3$, use the simple-root coordinates $(21,32,43)$. Writing $r_2=u d_2$ with $d_2=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,-1,1)$, the image of $u$ is $(-1,-2,-1)$.

The pure diagonal subgroup $D_0=\langle r_1,r_3\rangle$ has order four. Its three characters on these coordinates are respectively $(+,-),(-,+),(-,-)$, where the two signs give the actions of $r_1,r_3$. They are distinct and nontrivial. Commutators of $r_2$ with elements of $D_0$ lie in $K$; their images are, up to the invertible diagonal action, the differences between $u$ and its $D_0$ translates. The span of these differences contains all three coordinate lines: project onto each of the three nontrivial characters by averaging with its signs, dividing by four, which is invertible in odd characteristic. All three coordinates of $u$ are nonzero.

Thus $K$ maps onto $U/[U,U]$. Since $U$ has elementary abelian abelianization and is generated by its simple root groups, $\Phi(U)=[U,U]$. Burnside's basis theorem gives $K=U$. Recovering $d_2=u^{-1}r_2$ then gives $D\le H$, proving the claim. In particular

$$|H|=8p^6.$$

The projective action is injective on $H$: a scalar matrix in this triangular group has second diagonal entry one, so is the identity.

## 3. Elementary generation of the full linear socle

Write $x_{ij}(a)=I+aE_{ij}$. All lower root matrices $x_{ij}(a)$ with $i>j$ and $a\in\mathbb F_p$ belong to $H$. Moreover

$$x_{32}(1)r_0x_{32}(-1)=W_h
=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_2\\-hI_2&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

Conjugating $x_{32}(1)$ by $W_h$ supplies $x_{14}(-1/h)$. Set $a=-1/h$, so $\mathbb F_p(a)=\mathbb F_q$.

The elementary commutator identity for distinct indices supplies the simple upper roots. More explicitly, the ordered root pairs $(14,42)$, $(21,14)$, $(24,43)$ and $(31,14)$ give respectively $x_{12}(a)$, $x_{24}(a)$, $x_{23}(a)$ and $x_{34}(a)$. Signs in the commutator convention are immaterial because inverses are available.

All positive powers of $a$ can be supplied on every simple upper root. For root12, the sequence

$$x_{12}(a^j),x_{23}(a)\longmapsto x_{13}(a^{j+1})
\longmapsto x_{12}(a^{j+1})$$

uses a commutator followed by a commutator with $x_{32}(1)$. For root23, commutate $x_{23}(a^j)$ with $x_{34}(a)$ to reach $x_{24}(a^{j+1})$, then with $x_{43}(1)$ to return to root23. For root34, commutate $x_{23}(a)$ with $x_{34}(a^j)$ to reach $x_{24}(a^{j+1})$, then use $x_{32}(1)$ as the first commutator factor to return to root34. Hence addition and prime-field powers give every $x_{i,i+1}(c)$ with $c\in\mathbb F_p[a]=\mathbb F_q$. The positive powers span the full field because $a\ne0$; its minimal polynomial has nonzero constant term, so one is in that span as well.

Together with $x_{i+1,i}(1)$, the matrices $x_{i,i+1}(1)$ give the adjacent Weyl matrices. These transport every parameter to the lower simple roots. The standard elementary matrices therefore generate $\operatorname{SL}_4(q)$ inside $\langle r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3\rangle$.

The projective generated group is consequently the inverse image in $\operatorname{PGL}_4(q)$ of the subgroup generated by the determinant classes of $-1$ and $h^2$.

## 4. The common-form intersection has exactly 4p elements

Define the symmetric form

$$B=\begin{pmatrix}
-4&2&0&-1\\2&-1&0&-2h\\0&0&1+4h&0\\-1&-2h&0&h
\end{pmatrix}.$$

Its determinant is $\Delta^3\ne0$. Direct multiplication gives

$$r_0Br_0^T=-hB,\qquad r_1Br_1^T=B,\qquad r_2Br_2^T=B.$$

Thus the first projective maximal parabolic lies in the projective similitude stabilizer of $B$. The other maximal parabolic is the faithful projective image of $H$.

We calculate $H\cap\operatorname{GO}(B)$ explicitly. A matrix $g\in H$ is lower triangular over $\mathbb F_p$, with diagonal $(d_1,1,d_3,d_4)$, each $d_i\in\{1,-1\}$. If $gBg^T=\lambda B$, its 11 entry gives $\lambda=1$, because $B_{11}=-4\ne0$. Comparing the remaining entries gives precisely

$$d_4=1,\qquad g_{21}=(1-d_1)/2,$$
$$g_{32}=v,\quad g_{31}=v/2,\quad g_{43}=d_3v/2,$$
$$g_{42}=v^2/4,\qquad g_{41}=v^2/8+(d_1-1)/4,$$

where $v\in\mathbb F_p$ is arbitrary and $d_1,d_3$ are arbitrary signs. For completeness, the 24 entry gives $(1-d_4)\Delta/2=0$; the 34 entry gives $\Delta(d_3g_{43}-v/2)=0$; and the 44 entry reduces to $\Delta(v^2-4g_{42})/4=0$. These equations use only $\Delta\ne0$ and odd characteristic. Conversely substitution verifies every displayed matrix is an isometry. Thus the intersection has exactly $4p$ elements.

It contains $\langle r_1,r_2\rangle$, a dihedral group of order $4p$. Hence it equals that group. Projective lifting does not change this argument: multiplying a lift by a scalar preserves the property of being a similitude, and the $H$ lift of a projective element is unique. Therefore

$$\langle\rho_0,\rho_1,\rho_2\rangle\cap
\langle\rho_1,\rho_2,\rho_3\rangle
=\langle\rho_1,\rho_2\rangle.$$

## 5. The two rank-three meets

The projective group $\langle\rho_0,\rho_1\rangle$ is dihedral of order eight. Its elements have representatives $T^j r_1^\epsilon$, $0\le j<4$. For $j=1,2,3$ these matrices have a nonzero entry strictly above the diagonal: the displayed formula for $T^2$ has entry 14 equal to two, while $T,T^3$ have nonzero entries in row one above the diagonal. Right multiplication by $r_1$ cannot remove them. Thus its intersection with the lower triangular group is exactly $\langle\rho_1\rangle$. This proves the first triple's IP.

For the second triple, set $D_{12}=\langle r_1,r_2\rangle$ and $D_{23}=\langle r_2,r_3\rangle$ inside $H$. Their diagonal images are two-dimensional subspaces of $D$ meeting in $\langle d_2\rangle$. Their intersections with $U$ are the cyclic subgroups of order $p$ generated respectively by $(r_1r_2)^2$ and $(r_2r_3)^2$. In the simple-root coordinates of §2, the corresponding nonzero vectors are

$$(0,4,2),\qquad(-2,0,-2).$$

Their prime-field lines have zero intersection. Since each cyclic subgroup maps injectively to this elementary abelian quotient, $D_{12}\cap D_{23}\cap U=1$. Therefore $D_{12}\cap D_{23}$ injects into $\langle d_2\rangle$ and has at most two elements. It contains $r_2$, so equals $\langle r_2\rangle$. This proves the second triple's IP.

The standard recursive criterion now gives the full rank-four string C-group intersection property.

## 6. Parameter choice and exact diagonal class

For $q\ge5$, choose a primitive element $h\in\mathbb F_q^*$ different from $-1/4$. There are at least two primitive elements since $\varphi(q-1)\ge2$, and at most one is forbidden. Such an $h$ generates the whole field and is a nonsquare.

If $q\equiv3\pmod4$, the determinant quotient has order two and the class of $-1$ is its nonidentity element. If $q\equiv1\pmod4$, the quotient has order four and $h^2$ represents its unique element of order two, while $-1$ has even determinant class. In either case the projective generated group is exactly $L_2(q)$. For $q=3$, take $h=1$, for which $\Delta=2\ne0$; the class of $-1$ still gives the whole index-two quotient.

This proves uniform existence. The retained maximum-four theorem is applicable because these groups are diagonal linear extensions, not field or graph extensions. No claim about those other almost-simple cases or the rank-three spectrum is made.

Together with the retained odd-field rank-four existence theorem for the simple socle and the cyclic-quotient obstruction, this gives the following maximum-rank classification within the diagonal interval: if $\operatorname{PSL}_4(q)\le G\le\operatorname{PGL}_4(q)$ with odd $q$, then the maximum is four when $[G:\operatorname{PSL}_4(q)]$ is one or two; when the index is four there is no involution generating set. The latter case occurs only when $q\equiv1\pmod4$. This classification of maxima does not claim that every rank below four occurs.

## 7. Verification and source boundary

The source question is Leemans's [survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08843), Problem 7, §5.7: maximum string C-group rank for groups between PSL4(q) and its full automorphism group. The displayed construction handles the diagonal index-two subgroups. The survey's simple-socle rank-four theorem, attributed to [Brooksbank–Leemans (2016)](https://digitalcommons.bucknell.edu/fac_journ/1204/), is retained for the final diagonal-interval corollary, not as a source for this construction. The broader field and graph extensions remain outside the result. The separate [bounded source audit](phase16_linear_diagonal_status.md) does not establish publication priority.

The proof retains Burnside's basis theorem, standard unitriangular and elementary-matrix facts, and the recursive string-intersection criterion (McMullen–Schulte, *Abstract Regular Polytopes*, Proposition 2E16). It does not retain the orthogonal subgroup classification: the symmetric form is used solely to calculate a small intersection inside the explicitly known triangular parabolic. The maximum-rank conclusion retains [phase nine](phase9_linear_unitary.md) and its earlier bilinear-form alternative dependencies.

The [symbolic checker](phase16_linear_diagonal_symbolic.py) passed 89 exact rational-function identities, sign cases and character-projector checks. Its divisions are powers of two and $h$; the written intersection elimination also assumes $1+4h\ne0$. The [GAP checker](phase16_linear_diagonal_check.g) passed for $q=3,5,7,9$, explicitly constructing every one of the 16 marked subgroups and checking all 256 meets in each field. It uses the faithful action of the projective linear group on all projective points, with no group-recognition oracle. The generated projective orders were respectively $12130560$, $14508000000$, $4635182361600$ and $101519686195200$. These bounded computations corroborate the written proof and are not its all-field justification.
