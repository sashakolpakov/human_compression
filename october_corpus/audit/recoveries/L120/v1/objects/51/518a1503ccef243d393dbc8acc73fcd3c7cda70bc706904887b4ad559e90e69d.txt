Checking intersection properties on intervals
Human Compression project
This note records an exact implementation of the end-intersection criterion already used
in the acquired permutation enumeration [1]. The new verifier starts with a complete literal
tuple. It imports no prefix certificate from the enumeration and does not alter that search.
Lett= (t 1, . . . , tr) be a tuple of permutations, withr≥1. For 1≤a≤b≤r, write
Ha,b =⟨t a, . . . , tb⟩. The intersection property means that
⟨ti :i∈I⟩ ∩ ⟨tj :j∈J⟩=⟨t k :k∈I∩J⟩(I, J⊆ {1, . . . , r}).
Proposition 1.The tupletis a string C-marking if and only if its entries are distinct non-
identity involutions, its distant entries commute, and
Ha,b−1 ∩H a+1,b =H a+1,b−1 (1≤a < b−1≤r−1).(1)
For an accepted tuple, the last test uses exactly(r−1)(r−2)/2actual subgroup intersections.
Proof.Necessity follows by applying the full intersection property to the two displayed intervals.
Distinctness follows by applying it to distinct singleton index sets. The string relations and
involution requirements are part of the definition.
For sufficiency, induct on the length of a contiguous interval. A single nonidentity involution
gives the rank-one marking. Two distinct involutions give the rank-two marking: their order-
two subgroups have trivial intersection, which is the only nontrivial intersection condition in
that rank. This includes the case where the two involutions commute. For an interval of length
at least three, both end omissions are string C-markings by the induction hypothesis. Their
actual intersection is their common middle subgroup by (1). The acquired end-intersection
criterion [1, Section 1] now gives the intersection property on that whole interval. Its string
relations are restrictions of the originally checked relations. This proves the induction and
hence the result for [1, r]. There arer−ℓ+ 1 intervals of lengthℓ, so the number of tests isPr
ℓ=3(r−ℓ+ 1) = (r−1)(r−2)/2.
The routine constructs the actual subgroupsH a,b in increasing interval length. All three
groups in each comparison have therefore already been constructed. It tests equality of sub-
groups, rather than equality of their orders or the intersection of projected images. The full
generating-group order is a separate check in an application; the proposition applies equally to
proper subgroups and to disconnected string diagrams. It makes no classification or counting
assertion.
Controls and scope
The independent comparison routine constructs every subgroup associated with an arbitrary
index subset. It compares the two decisions on all ordered tuples of one through four involu-
tions ofS 4, including tuples with repeated entries and tuples failing string commutation. No
tuple is removed from that comparison by the new criterion. Five explicit invalid inputs also
cover the empty tuple, the identity, an element of order three, a repeated mark and a distant
noncommuting pair.

The retained completeS 8,S 10 andS 11 positive lists are read directly from their accepted
full-subset certificates. The new routine checks every tuple, including its actual symmetric
generating group. The extraction is compared literally against those frozen certificates; the
underlying enumeration is not repeated. These controls test the implementation. The propo-
sition, with its acquired criterion, justifies the replacement of exhaustive subset checks in later
uses. Permutation arithmetic and exact subgroup intersections in GAP remain explicit com-
putational infrastructure.
The end-intersection criterion is borrowed, and its original provenance is retained through [1].
This component only reorganizes its evaluation and supplies a separate verifier. It establishes
no additional survey cell, whole-original recovery, open conjecture or compression rate. Any
application must separately pay completeness, target identification, the source’s isomorphism
convention and the exact numerical output.
