# Primary-source status audit for the unitary rank-four family

Audit date: 10 October 2026. This is a bounded literature check. It neither certifies first priority nor establishes that an unlocated result was open immediately before this work. The companion JSON records thirty search queries, primary URLs, source dates, reading scope and local checksums. No third-party full text is part of this original audit.

**Finding.** The inspected primary sources establish rank-three existence for every odd \(\operatorname{PSU}_4(q)\), \(q\ge5\), and exclude \(\operatorname{PSU}_4(3)\) at every rank. No earlier theorem giving rank-four existence for all odd \(q\equiv1\pmod4\) was located. No named conjecture specifically asserting that unitary rank-four family was located either. The phase-thirteen theorem should therefore be described as a proposed uniform existence result extending the ranges found in these sources, with publication priority unverified. It must not be advertised as a solution of the survey's similarly worded **linear** conjecture.

## Exact question and theorem boundaries

In the introduction to [Leemans's survey, arXiv:1910.08843v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08843v2), the three general research questions concern the attainable ranks, the largest attainable rank, and enumeration of string C-group representations of a group. The first two provide the precise program to which the new unitary construction contributes. This is a general program rather than a numbered unitary conjecture. The version was submitted on 19 October 2019 and revised on 11 May 2020; the local filename `survey2021.pdf` is not used to infer its version date.

Section 5.7 of that survey states the following distinct assertions:

| Source identifier | Mathematical content | Relation to this unitary theorem |
|---|---|---|
| Theorem 5.8, citing Brooksbank–Leemans Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 4.5 | \(\operatorname{PSL}_4(q)\) has a rank-four string C-group representation for every odd prime power \(q\); it has none at any rank when \(q\) is even. | A theorem about the linear family. |
| Conjecture 2 | The maximum string C-group rank of \(\operatorname{PSL}_4(q)\), for odd \(q\), equals four. | An upper-bound question for the linear family, not unitary existence. |
| Problem 7 | Determine the maximum rank for every group \(G\) between \(\operatorname{PSL}_4(q)\) and its full automorphism group. | Also linear; includes extensions beyond the simple socle. |

The [primary repository record for Brooksbank–Leemans, *Polytopes of large rank for PSL(4,q)*](https://digitalcommons.bucknell.edu/fac_journ/1204/) confirms the linear rank-four construction, published in *Journal of Algebra* 452 (2016), 390–400. Its abstract and metadata were freshly read; this audit does not claim a new reading of its entire proof. Changing PSL to PSU in any of these statements would be an incorrect source attribution.

## What the later unitary source actually settles

[Leemans–Vandenschrick, *On simple groups that admit a string C-group representation*](https://repozitorij.upr.si/IzpisGradiva.php?id=22370&lang=eng&print=), Theorem 1.1, classifies the nonabelian finite simple groups admitting at least one string C-group representation, without fixing its rank. Its exception list contains \(\operatorname{PSU}_4(3)\) and the even-field \(\operatorname{PSU}_4(q)\), and does not contain odd \(\operatorname{PSU}_4(q)\) with \(q\ge5\). The paragraph immediately after the theorem expressly retains rank-three existence for every group outside the list from Nuzhin, Mazurov and the corrections of Jones–Macaj. Thus this source supplies rank three for our unitary groups; it does not supply rank four or their maximum rank.

The first two pages, including the full theorem and following paragraph, were reread from the retained [primary author manuscript](https://dipot.ulb.ac.be/dspace/bitstream/2013/397490/3/simpleGroups6.pdf). Its journal dates are placeholders. The primary publisher repository instead records online publication **24 November 2025**, journal year **2026**, volume 9(1), article P1.07, DOI [10.26493/2590-9770.1329.6f5](https://doi.org/10.26493/2590-9770.1329.6f5), and repository deposit **5 January 2026**. The manuscript URL currently redirects unsuccessfully in the browser; the saved primary text and separately accessible indexed primary metadata are the actual evidence used. Publication, issue and deposit dates have not been conflated.

## Nearby constructions checked for overlap

* [Brooksbank, arXiv:1912.05512](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05512), submitted 11 December 2019: the introduction, Figure 2, Theorem 1.1 and the initial orthogonal-group definitions were read. The principal rank spectrum is \(\{3,4,5\}\) for \(\operatorname{PSp}_4(q)\), except \(q=3\). Figure 2 lists then-known infinite families with rank at least four and contains the odd PSL4 family, but no PSU4 family. This is evidence about that paper's scope, not proof of current absence.
* [Brooksbank–Leemans, *Rank reduction of string C-group representations*, arXiv:1812.01055](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01055), submitted 3 December 2018 and revised 3 May 2019: Theorems 1.4 and 4.1 and the surrounding construction were read. Theorem 4.1 produces an index-two subgroup of \(O(d,\mathbb F_p)\), \(p\ge5\) prime, in every rank from three to \(d\). The constructed group contains generating reflections. In dimension six their determinant remains nontrivial after quotienting by scalar matrices, so this does not identify the resulting projective group with the simple group \(P\Omega^-_6(p)\cong\operatorname{PSU}_4(p)\). That last exclusion is our elementary inference from the specified generators; we do not assume that arbitrary orthogonal constructions pass to the simple derived group.
* [Cameron–Herman–Leemans, *String C-groups with real Schur index 2*](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/26879/1/210414jpaaCHL_final.pdf), arXiv:2201.01313 submitted 4 January 2022: the first two pages were read. The unitary examples used there have rank three, and the concrete smallest example is PSU5(3), of type \(\{5,5\}\). The character-theoretic question resolved there is different.
* The [author-hosted regular-polytope atlas](https://leemans.dimitri.web.ulb.be/polytopes/index.html), freshly inspected on the audit date, has a small-group scope with later explicit additions. Its unitary table gives no odd PSU4 example. In particular it does not cover PSU4(5), whose order is 14,742,000,000. Absence from that table supplies no nonexistence result. The older Auckland mirror was also inspected.
* The publisher's summary of [McMullen–Schulte, *Abstract Regular Polytopes*, Chapter 9](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/abstract-regular-polytopes/unitary-groups-and-hermitian-forms/A060A0AD6ECE91CFE7A00EF74873AFDA) explicitly concerns Hermitian forms over the complex numbers. It is not a finite-field PSU4 existence theorem. Only the summary and metadata, not the full chapter, were read. The print year is 2002 and online chapter date 20 August 2009.

Search matches about chiral polytopes, projective unitary groups of dimension three, generalized quadrangles, fusion systems and complex reflection groups were not counted as regular rank-four existence results. Secondary and generated aggregation pages were not used as evidence of prior theorems. Direct opening of one guessed author-catalogue URL failed; this audit does not claim exhaustive inspection of the author's current catalogue or all forward citations.

## Precise status of the proposed result

The frozen [phase-thirteen theorem](phase13_unitary_sieve.md) constructs rank four for **every odd prime power \(q\equiv1\pmod4\), \(q\ge5\)**. Its type is \(\{4,q^2-1,2p\}\) for \(q>5\), with the separately certified type \(\{4,26,10\}\) for \(q=5\). It includes characteristic three through the earlier \(q=9^e\) theorem, and uses the elliptic sieve plus explicit field certificates for the other characteristics. This is stronger than the published rank-three existence statement located above.

The conclusion **maximum rank four** additionally depends on our separately proved phase-nine upper bound. Existence alone does not establish a maximum. Together with published rank-three existence, those two original results give rank spectrum \(\{3,4\}\) in this congruence class. No rank-four existence claim for \(q\equiv3\pmod4\), \(q\ge7\), or for almost-simple extensions is made here. The source-backed nonexistence for \(q=3\) remains separate.

Thirty searches were executed on 10 October 2026 using exact and variant group notation, rank-four and regular-polytope terminology, orthogonal-minus-six terminology, recent-year terms and primary-domain restrictions. No matching earlier uniform theorem or named unitary rank-four conjecture was located. These are reproducible search limits, **not a declaration of exhaustive novelty**. The defensible label is “proposed uniform rank-four existence theorem; internally reviewed and computationally certified; priority unverified.”
