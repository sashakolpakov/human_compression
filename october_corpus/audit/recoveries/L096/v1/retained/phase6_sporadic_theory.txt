# One panel enumeration for J1, M11 and M12

This phase extends the phase-five J2 engine from locally two-transitive panel actions to arbitrary primitive panel actions. It independently enumerates firm, flag-transitive, residually connected, residually weakly primitive coset geometries satisfying the rank-two intersection condition `(IP)2`. The group, its complete subgroup catalogue, and an optional local two-transitivity predicate are parameters. No published geometry list is an enumeration input.

The arithmetic outputs are recorded in the separate group certificates. These results depend on the completeness of the imported TomLib subgroup catalogues and GAP's exact finite-group algorithms. They are not a new classification of the sporadic groups or of their subgroups.

The completed unrestricted censuses, under **Aut(G) and arbitrary type permutation**, are:

| Group | Rank 2 | Rank 3 | Rank 4 | Rank 5 | All ranks at least 6 | Borel anchors | Verified witnesses |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| J1 | 22 | 8 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 40 | 32 |
| M11 | 6 | 18 | 12 | 4 | 0 | 39 | 40 |
| M12 | 13 | 21 | 20 | 1 | 0 | 147 | 55 |

In particular, the broader M12 class has rank exactly five. This independently recovers L124's stated rank-at-least-six nonexistence result, together with the rank conclusion reported in L105, conditional on the subgroup catalogue. The M11 result is a new reproduction of a precisely defined finite census here; it is not being relabelled as L119. Neither the counts nor the rank bounds are extrapolated from a chosen search cutoff: all 226 Borel anchors terminated with an empty level. Search times were about 224 seconds, 12 seconds and 159 seconds for J1, M11 and M12, respectively; exact run times and input hashes are in the certificates.

## 2. The uniform recognition theorem

Let G be a finite group, B a subgroup, and let P_i properly contain B, with B maximal in P_i. Set K_S to the subgroup generated by B and all P_i with i in S; K_empty=B. A panel family defines a geometry of the stated class, with group K_I and Borel B, exactly when:

1. `K_S intersect K_T = K_(S intersect T)` for every S,T.
2. Each nonempty K_S has a deletion K_(S minus {i}) maximal in K_S. For singletons this is the initial panel condition.
3. For every |S| at least three, the deletion parabolics in K_S satisfy typed Helly: every pairwise incident partial flag of their cosets has a common group element.
4. Each two-panel coset geometry is a partial linear space or a digon.

Necessity follows by taking the standard residues of a flag-transitive geometry. Residual connectedness gives the panel-generation equalities. Weak primitivity is exactly maximality of at least one deletion stabilizer, also for actions with nontrivial kernel.

For sufficiency, typed Helly makes the coset incidence system a flag-transitive geometry. The meet condition identifies every standard residue with the corresponding panel subfamily. Generation by its deletion parabolics proves connectedness of every residue of rank at least two. Proper panel extensions prove firmness. Condition 2 proves RWPRI in every residue, and condition 4 proves `(IP)2`. The same argument applies at every finite rank. There is no assumption of simple connectedness or faithful residue actions.

The three intersection notions remain distinct: the subgroup meet condition in item 1, the rank-two incidence condition `(IP)2` in item 4, and the shadow intersection property studied in L096.

## 3. The essential change from phase five

All maximal inclusions B < P are now eligible panels. Previously only the inclusions with `|B\P/B|=2` were eligible. The optional `REQUIRE_2T` switch retains the narrower class, but the default is false.

For a two-panel pair P,Q with intersection B, put K=<P,Q>. The digon case is exactly `|P||Q|=|K||B|`. Otherwise form the Q-cosets incident with the base P-coset:

`L = {Qh : h in P}`.

For each representative x of a nonidentity B-double coset in Q, compare L with `Lx={Qhx:h in P}`. The partial-linear-space condition is exactly `|L intersect Lx|=1` for all such x. Indeed, any two P-cosets with a common Q-neighbor can be moved to P and Px with x in Q. B-double cosets describe all such pairs up to their stabilizer, and Q is already one common neighbor. A second common neighbor is precisely a four-cycle. Absence of four-cycles is symmetric between the two types. This full test is necessary without local two-transitivity; checking one x would be unsound.

## 4. Exhaustion by one maximal residue

Import one representative of each G-conjugacy class of subgroups. For each representative K, compute its K-conjugacy classes of maximal subgroups H. These inclusions are retained input, not inferred from subgroup orders.

For fixed B, transport each matching inclusion H < K to B < K^c and take its orbit under N_G(B). This yields every actual maximal overgroup of B. The identical transporter construction yields every maximal overgroup of a partial join H. It uses exact subgroup conjugacy; order tests only prune impossible transporters.

Classify panel tuples under N_G(B) and arbitrary permutation of types. Given an accepted tuple with join H, extend by each panel P outside H lying inside some maximal overgroup K of H. Then <H,P>=K. The final case K=G is included. Pair tests and the recognition theorem decide acceptance.

Completeness follows because every positive-rank RWPRI geometry has at least one maximal deletion residue. Its lower-rank panel tuple therefore appears inductively; a transporter in N_G(B) puts that deletion into the stored representative, and the new panel is one of the tested extensions. It is unnecessary, and generally false, that every deletion is maximal. Deduplication only identifies tuples by exact successive transporters and stabilizers, with all type permutations considered.

Every extension strictly enlarges the join. Consequently the rank is bounded by Omega([G:B]), the number of prime factors with multiplicity. The implementation computes one further level and **requires it to be empty**. A terminal empty level is also closed under all further extensions. Each Borel class receives its own terminal checkpoint; an incomplete anchor cannot be reported as an exhausted census.

## 5. Equivalence, verification and numerical checks

The search quotient is ambient conjugacy plus type permutation. A final exact transport-and-stabilizer computation fuses these classes under the full automorphism group of G plus type permutation. Generated witnesses consist of factual permutations and subgroup data produced by this search, not copied supplementary programs.

The independent verifier starts from the maximal parabolics, intersects them to obtain every flag stabilizer, and checks firmness, RWPRI, residual connectedness, all rank-two double-coset tests, and typed Helly directly. It also checks the panel reconstruction and exact equality with the named TomLib group. It does not reuse search acceptance flags. It reports `(2T)1` separately rather than requiring it for the broader problem.

For comparison with the 2005 table, the fused representatives satisfying `(2T)1` are filtered. For each such geometry, every type subset of every higher-rank representative is tested for exact Aut(G)/type equivalence. A class is removed as a truncation only after finding such a witness. These restrictions are post-processing and play no role in exhaustion.

## 6. Reproduction and retained cost

Required software: Python 3, GAP, and GAP's TomLib package. The runner discovers GAP from the `GAP` environment variable or PATH. Run from any directory:

```
python3 research/leemans/phase6_sporadic_run.py J1
python3 research/leemans/phase6_sporadic_finish.py J1
python3 research/leemans/phase6_sporadic_build_certificate.py J1
```

Replace J1 by M11 or M12. Add `--resume` to the search after an interruption. The runner checks the exact search-source hash and all completed checkpoint hashes before reusing them; it records an in-progress run before starting GAP, so interruption cannot overwrite a previous log silently. Intermediate rank checkpoints are diagnostic; terminal Borel checkpoints are the exhaustion evidence.

This is a substantive common enumeration and verification proof, with one implementation and one source-axiom checklist for all three groups. It removes group-specific geometry enumeration arguments once the subgroup input is available. It retains the cost of the subgroup classifications, finite transporter/coset computations, and the old papers' separate geometric constructions. Lack of access to a paper body is not converted into a claim that every theorem or diagram in that paper was reproduced.

## 7. Post-processing conventions and the unresolved M12 table difference

The following are counts of locally two-transitive representatives that are **not** truncations. Two conventions are computed explicitly: higher geometries also locally two-transitive, or higher geometries from the whole broader RWPRI class.

| Group | Higher geometries restricted to 2T, ranks 2–5 | Higher geometries from the broader class, ranks 2–5 |
|---|---|---|
| J1 | (11,1,2,0) | (8,1,2,0) |
| M11 | (0,9,11,0) | (0,9,5,0) |
| M12 | (5,10,17,1) | (4,10,17,1) |

L091 pp. 231–233 were read visually, including Table 1 and its explanatory text. The class introduced in §1 and summarized in §3 includes local two-transitivity. The class-restricted convention is therefore the natural reading, and its J1 and M11 rows agree exactly. The wording of the truncation sentence alone is broader, so both computed conventions are retained.

For M12, the printed rank-three entry is 11; the class-restricted calculation gives 10. The certificate records this comparison as a mismatch. This is not advertised as a correction to the paper: the original article/supplement's precise equivalence conventions and the historical selection of representatives require separate verification. In particular, M12 has outer automorphisms, whereas J1 and M11 do not. None of these numerical comparisons are needed for the complete enumeration or the rank bound.

The supplementary `phase6_sporadic_truncations.py` and `.g` record every source representative and type subset witnessing a truncation under each convention. Their reports bind exact source and witness hashes. They do not alter the core certificates' equivalence relation or modify search inputs to fit the old table.

## 8. A separate check against the 2004 M12 archive

The original Cambridge supplement to Jacobs–Leemans (L096) was compared only **after** completing all independent searches. `phase6_sporadic_archive_compare.py --archive PATH` accepts an explicit external archive and parses only a narrow grammar of permutation/subgroup/list data; it never executes the Magma program. The archive and its third-party source files are not redistributed.

All 34 raw rank-three, 36 raw rank-four and one rank-five records pass the independently checked broader axioms. The source ambient group is identified with the named M12 group by an exact isomorphism. Every record is matched to exactly one independently generated Aut(M12)/type class. The rank-four and rank-five lists cover all 20 and one classes, respectively.

The raw rank-three list and its selected 20-entry sublist both cover 20 of the 21 generated classes. The absent class is certificate witness **18**, with Borel order 6, parabolic orders `(24,660,660)` and panel orders `(60,12,12)`. It is not locally two-transitive. This is an explicit omission from the inspected archive version, not a claim that the inaccessible L105 article omitted the geometry. The archive includes other non-2T geometries, so it cannot be treated as a pure locally-two-transitive dataset. Its omission is separate from the rank-three 2T summary-table discrepancy.

The comparison report records archive/member hashes, every source-to-census correspondence, and the missing class explicitly. A passing comparison means all source records have been verified and matched; it does not mean each archived list equals the complete census.
