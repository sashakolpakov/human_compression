N012: principal source contract and routes to the complete proofs
================================================================

Status and scope
----------------

This audit maps the principal results of Irina Markovskaya's 2025 thesis,
``Linear groups over finite fields and over the ring of integers as
automorphism groups of regular polytopes of rank 3``. The fixed edition
has 80 pages and names Ya. N. Nuzhin as supervisor. Its PDF SHA256 is
03548648ed9e2b0c521ef70656da857633e831f01f82bbe220a83972285b13eb.

There are twelve distinct numbered principal theorems or corollaries,
including the three consequences on pages 69–70. Both classification
tables have six rows. Repeated theorem statements in the introduction
and chapter openings are the same claims. The numbered principal list
agrees with the main-result descriptions on pages 5–8 and the conclusion
on page 76. Chapter 1 and the preliminary sections supply definitions,
borrowed criteria and supporting lemmas; they are not additional main
classifications. This distinction does not remove any principal claim.

The audit grants no admission. The integral, N011, central-quotient and
finite-H components already have distinct reviews and root acceptance.
The three-page closing component Q is frozen but is undergoing its own
distinct review. It is recorded as a candidate route, not used here as
an accepted premise. A whole-source review and portable registration
must still bind the complete acquired proofs and the correction below.

Exact source domains
--------------------

Throughout the classifications, n is at least two. This is the intended
domain fixed by the source tables and all its case analyses. Some source
theorem sentences omit that qualification. They cannot be asserted
literally for n=1: the projective group is trivial, and GL_1(2) and GL_1(3)
are admissible. The final wrapper should state n>=2 once and retain the
omission as an explicit source qualification.

For finite groups q is a prime power. The new classifications in Chapter
4 assume q odd. Its sentence about characteristic two before Theorem
4.2.1 attributes that subject to earlier work; it is not an additional
principal theorem proved in the thesis.

The source definition on page 11 allows repetitions among the three
involutions and includes the trivial group. Thus admissibility is weaker
than a rank-three string C-marking. In particular S3 is admissible but
cannot have three distinct involutions with a commuting pair. No
intersection property can be inferred merely from admissibility or from
reducing an integral generating triple.

Write H_n(q) for the determinant-{1,-1} subgroup, Z1 for its full centre,
Z2 for Z(SL_n(q)), and Q_n(q)=H_n(q)/Z2. Q is precisely the source's
PGL_n^{±1}(q), as explicitly defined on pages 7, 10 and 54. It is not in
general the quotient by Z1. Every statement and route in the ledger
preserves that distinction.

The centre correction and other proof qualifications
----------------------------------------------------

Put d=gcd(n,q-1) and e=(q-1)/d. The accepted Z theorem proves

* if e is even, |Z1:Z2|=2, Q=PSL_n(q) times C2, and H/Z1=PSL_n(q);
* if e is odd, Z1=Z2 and H/Z1=Q embeds in PGL_n(q) as the index-two
  extension of PSL_n(q) with complement represented by
  diag(-1,1,...,1).

Odd n always belongs to the first case; even n may belong to either.
At n=6,q=5 the scalar 2I has determinant -1, with |Z1|=4 and |Z2|=2.
Thus the universal even-dimension equality of the centres on page 69
is false. Consequently the structural assertion in Corollary 4.2.1
cannot be admitted unchanged. Its existence and exact type [4p,n]
remain valid in both cases by H's full-scalar-quotient theorem. The
whole admission must expose this corrected numbered claim.

There are also proof-level qualifications which do not change the
principal existence classifications:

* The displayed beta on page 72 squares to diag(-1,-1,1,1), not a
  scalar. Its image is not an involution in odd characteristic. This
  defect was already recorded in phase 22. Z replaces the witness
  using the acquired journal matrices and explicitly proves their
  generation argument works for every odd q.
* The seven-dimensional cancellation on page 67 needs the square
  recorded and proved in H. The original formula is not a premise.
* The page 39 argument that a nonmonomial matrix automatically has
  no nonidentity monomial powers does not establish an intersection
  property. H supplies the full calculation. The page 38 monomial
  sentence likewise omits the identity and the monomial generator
  from its stated exclusions; the replacement proves the precise
  set {I,C}.
* The page 71 negative n=3 paragraph ends with wording that would
  affirm admissibility despite its preceding quotient obstruction.
  Its theorem statement and the negative argument agree; the ledger
  records n=3 as excluded.
* The cyclic-normal-subgroup criterion cannot be applied to Q when
  Q has a central C2. The source's short deduction of Corollary 4.3.1
  needs the actual central lift or quotient intersection argument.
* The criterion's adjacent product orders must exceed two. In the
  applications with no nontrivial cyclic normal subgroup, an adjacent
  commuting pair would make an endpoint generator central. Thus the
  missing order condition follows there. The S4 case is explicit;
  S3 is excluded directly. No blanket assertion about all dihedral
  groups or all projective groups is needed.

Supporting lemmas and attribution
--------------------------------

The ledger records ten groups of supporting source lemmas. The shared
root-parameter proof supplies the propagation, field recognition,
primitive-square, symmetric-square and Scott arguments actually used.
L supplies determinant sections, projective determinant quotients and
the finite general-linear classifications. I supplies complete integral
words and root generation. Z supplies the centre calculation, central
lift, low-dimensional obstruction and corrected four-dimensional
quotient. H supplies every remaining finite determinant-sign marking,
its product orders and its scalar-quotient intersections.

The rank-two Dickson/Levchuk assertions on page 33 are supporting
borrowed statements. They are not premises of the replacement root
proof or of Q's route through simple/projective classifications. This
audit does not claim a new proof of every background lemma quoted by
the thesis. The actual external inputs remain the explicitly stated
simple-linear admissibility classification, Sjerve–Cherkassoff's
PGL_2(q) result, Conder–Oliveros and the polytope correspondence.
Their complete external proof and software costs are unknown, not zero.

The source's main conclusions remain attributed to Markovskaya and her
coauthors as the thesis specifies. Pages 6–8 attribute Chapter 2 jointly
with the supervisor, the difficult n=5,6 triples to the author, the
Chapter 3 negative cases jointly with the supervisor, its explicit
positive triples to the author, and Chapter 4 to the author. The new
project contribution is the common proof organization, completed root
and intersection arguments, actual centre correction, and deductions
that reuse those proofs. No priority claim over all earlier literature
is made by this audit. There is no new open-conjecture credit here.

Acyclic assembly and remaining work
----------------------------------

The proof order is: explicit external inputs; I and U; L; Z; H; Q;
the final whole-source assembly. I retains only the specified finite
PSL_3(2)/PSL_4(2) exclusions and does not depend on the finite GL
classification that later uses I. The old N011 text is left immutable:
the final wrapper substitutes the complete I proof for its previously
retained integral-generation input. Z uses L's four-dimensional
generation proof, not the integral branch. H uses L's six-dimensional
identities and Z's low-dimensional obstruction. Q then assembles the
small quotients and all finite polytope conclusions.

The closing Q packet must first obtain distinct acceptance and root
adoption. A whole-source wrapper can then route all twelve claims and
twelve table rows, explicitly label corrected Corollary 4.2.1, retain
the external inputs and full companion proofs, and undergo its own
review. Portable evidence and public registration follow that review.
