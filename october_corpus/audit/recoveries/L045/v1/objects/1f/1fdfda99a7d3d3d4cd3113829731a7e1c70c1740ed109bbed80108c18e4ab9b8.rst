Identifying the inverting class through the flag variety
=======================================================

Status: proposed substantive review amendment; not an independent review
of its own mathematical contribution. Author: /root/l044_resume. Root is
the author of the surrounding exceptional-group manuscript. A separate
reader must check this addition and its classification interface.

Lemma
-----

Let H be a connected reductive algebraic group over an algebraically
closed field. Let T be a maximal torus and B a Borel containing T. Suppose
n in N_H(T) acts as minus one on T. Then

  dim C_H(n) <= dim H/B = N,

where N is the number of positive roots of H.

Proof. The Weyl action of n sends every positive root to its negative,
so n B n^{-1}=B^{-}, the opposite Borel. Therefore the stabilizer in
C_H(n) of the point B of H/B is contained in B intersect B^{-}=T.
Within T, centralizing n is exactly the equation t=t^{-1}; hence this
stabilizer is T[2]. This is a finite group scheme, even in characteristic
two, and has dimension zero. The orbit dimension formula gives

  dim(C_H(n).B)=dim C_H(n)-dim T[2]=dim C_H(n).

This orbit is a locally closed subvariety of H/B, proving the claim.
The argument uses dimensions of algebraic groups and orbits, not dimensions
of fixed spaces in the Lie algebra; no smoothness assertion for T[2] or
separability of the orbit map is required.

Application and complete retained inventory
-------------------------------------------

For an ordinary Steinberg endomorphism with field parameter q, the degree
of the uniform fixed-centralizer order polynomial equals the algebraic
centralizer dimension. For a Suzuki/Ree endomorphism whose normalized
linear scaling is sqrt(q), it is half that dimension. These are the
usual order formulas for fixed points of connected unipotent/reductive
centralizers; finite component groups do not affect the degree. The
uniform classified centralizer types, rather than an isolated numerical
order, supply this interface.

The following lists include every relevant rational involution class from
Burness--Thomas Proposition2.3 and Table5, physicalpp8,10, with their
Aschbacher--Seitz structural identifications (and1978 corrections). E7 in
odd characteristic also uses Remark2.4: both rational versions of each
A7 and T1E6 algebraic class occur in the inner-diagonal group, although
only one of each lies in the simple subgroup. Equal dimensions below may
therefore correspond to distinct rational classes in the same algebraic
class.

* E8, odd: 120 (D8),136 (A1 E7); N=120.
* E8, even:190 (A1),156 (A1^2),136 (A1^3),120 (A1^4); N=120.
* E7, odd:69 (A1 D6),63 (A7.2, either rational form),
  79 (T1 E6.2, either rational form); N=63.
* E7, even:99 (A1),81 (A1^2),79 ((A1^3)^(1)),
  69 ((A1^3)^(2)),63 (A1^4); N=63.
* F4, odd:24 (A1 C3),36 (B4); N=24.
* F4, even:36 (A1),36 (short A1),30 ((short A1)^2),
  24 (A1 short A1); N=24.
* G2, odd:6 (A1 A1); N=6.
* G2, even:8 (A1),6 (short A1); N=6.
* Triality 3D4, odd:12 (three SL2 factors permuted by triality,
  together with its fixed SL2 factor); N=12. The finite centralizer
  order |SL2(q)||SL2(q^3)| has degree12, as it must for ordinary Frobenius.
* Triality 3D4, even:18 (A1: q^9 SL2(q^3)),12
  (A1^3: q^9 SL2(q)); N=12. These are the complete triality-stable
  rational involution classes; unrelated D4 classes are not silently
  assumed to descend to this rational group.
* Large Ree 2F4: degree15 for q^10 |2B2(q)|, degree12 for
  q^9 |SL2(q)|. The algebraic dimensions are respectively30 and24;
  N=24. The selected class is A1 short A1.
* Small Ree 2G2: its unique rational involution class has centralizer
  |SL2(q)|, of degree3 and hence algebraic dimension6; N=6.
* Suzuki 2B2: its unique rational involution class has centralizer q^2,
  of degree2 and hence algebraic dimension4; N=4.

Thus in every row there is precisely one possible algebraic involution
class satisfying the flag-variety bound, and its finite centralizer type
is exactly the one used in exceptional.tex Table2. In E7 the two possible
rational A7 forms both satisfy the same subsequent numerical upper bound;
no assertion that the reverser lies in the simple subgroup is needed.
For all other listed types the inner-diagonal group equals the simple
finite group. E6 is deliberately absent from this argument: the accepted
odd torus-normalizer proof rules out inner-diagonal involutory reversers,
and the outer classes are bounded using LLS Proposition1.1 instead.

The lemma does not reprove the classification of involutions. It supplies
the missing identification within that explicitly retained classification,
without borrowing the target source's assertion that the inverting
representative has the selected centralizer. No computational re-enumeration
or change to the final arithmetic polynomials is needed.
