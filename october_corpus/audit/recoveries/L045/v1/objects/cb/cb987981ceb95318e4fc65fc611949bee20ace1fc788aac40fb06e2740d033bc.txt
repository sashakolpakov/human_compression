Chiral generating pairs in finite simple groups:
a complete assembly of the positive theorem
Human Compression project
Abstract
We assemble a proof of the positive existence theorem of Leemans and Liebeck for finite
nonabelian simple groups. The proof combines explicit alternating generators, finite word
certificates, and involution-counting arguments with all parameter ranges accounted for. The
classification and geometric reconstruction theorems used as antecedents are stated explicitly.
Several auxiliary assertions in the original proof are replaced by corrected arguments, without
changing its positive conclusion. No new existence theorem or classification is claimed.
The full proofs and certificates used below are retained in their exact component editions. The
accepted general orthogonal counting argument and the separately acceptedD +

1 The exact theorem and its geometric meaning
The fixed source is arXiv:1603.07713v3, whose running header is dated 18 May 2016 and whose
internal date is 6 November 2018. Its numbered principal theorem is the following positive
statement [1].
Theorem 1(Leemans–Liebeck).LetGbe a finite nonabelian simple group which is not isomor-
phic toA 7,PSL 2(q),PSL 3(q)orPSU 3(q). There arex, t∈Gsuch that
G=⟨x, t⟩,|t|= 2,∄α∈Aut(G) :x α =x −1, t α =t.
The source asks only that noinvolutoryautomorphism have the specified action. These
formulations are equivalent for a generating pair: the square of such an automorphism fixes
both generators and hence is the identity. The identity itself cannot invertx, since|x|>2.
Indeed, a group generated by two involutions is dihedral, whereasGis nonabelian simple. The
automorphism group in the theorem is the full Aut(G); restricting to inner automorphisms would
not suffice.
Lemma 2.Every pair in Theorem 1 satisfies the rank-three rotation intersection conditions and
yields an abstract chiral polyhedron with full automorphism groupG.
Proof.Sety=x −1t. Thenxy=t, so (xy) 2 = 1, andG=⟨x, y⟩. An element of⟨x⟩∩⟨y⟩commutes
with both generators, and therefore is central inG. This intersection is trivial. Neitherxnory
has order at most two: otherwiseGwould be cyclic or generated by two involutions. Thus the
orders are at least three.
We now invoke the retained Schulte–Weiss rank-three reconstruction and regularity crite-
rion [2], in the form used in the source: these rotation data reconstruct a directly regular or
chiral polyhedron; the directly regular alternative is precisely the existence of the reversing au-
tomorphism in Theorem 1. Its absence gives chirality, and the reconstructed full automorphism
group isG.

Corollary 3.Every group in Theorem 1 is the full automorphism group of an abstract chiral
polyhedron.
This uses geometric reconstruction as an explicit antecedent, rather than claiming a new
construction theorem for abstract polytopes. Equivalence of marked rotation data means a group
isomorphism carrying the distinguished generators to their counterparts. No bijection between
literal labelled pairsand unmarked polyhedra is asserted here. In particular, no enumerative
count is deduced by forgetting orientation, identifying enantiomorphs, or dividing the number of
pairs by an unstated symmetry factor.

5 Completion and source boundaries
Proof of Theorem 1.By the retained classification of finite simple groups, every group under the
hypothesis is alternating, sporadic or of Lie type, with the standard small identifications described
above. The alternating proof covers all eligible alternating groups. The two disjoint sporadic
rosters cover all twenty-six sporadics. The finite list, exceptional component and three classical
routes cover every eligible Lie parameter. Each route establishes the same full-automorphism
generating-pair conditions, either by Lemma 4 or Lemma 5. There is no assumption that an
auxiliary table is itself a theorem about an unverified new element. This proves the group-
theoretic assertion; Lemma 2 proves Corollary 3.
The manuscript establishes thispositivetheorem. On source page 3, nonexistence for PSL 2(q)
is attributed to Macbeath and Singerman, and nonexistence forA 7 to the earlier atlas compu-
tation. These are historical antecedents, not deductions of our positive construction. The cor-
responding PSL 3(q),PSU 3(q) statement is explicitly announced there as forthcoming work [19],
not proved in the fixed source. We do not promote that announcement into a proved converse.
The source’s introductory classification of regular polyhedron groups, and its final almost-simple
Suzuki and PSL 2-socle statements, likewise remain attributed antecedents. The general almost-
simple extension is a question, not an additional conclusion.
The Section 6 counts forM 11, M12, M22, J1, J2, the cited all-types O’Nan enumeration, and
example types not realized by our replacement witnesses are auxiliary historical statements. Our
theorem requires their existence conclusion only. No new exhaustive map count, faithfulness of
an unrelated historical census, or claim to recover every ancillary numerical example is made. In
particular the O’Nan existence argument does not import the disputed total from the separate
L046 source.
Several source proof interfaces have been replaced: the omitted normalizer term is covered;
outer inverters are not assumed to have one class; small field hypotheses are respected; the
symplectic unitary overgroup is included; orthogonal scalar losses and triality multiplicities are
