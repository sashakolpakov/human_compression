We identify this class before using its centralizer. Let $\mathbf B$
be a Borel subgroup containing $\mathbf T$, and let $N$ be the number
of positive roots. Since $n$ inverts $\mathbf T$, it interchanges
$\mathbf B$ and its opposite $\mathbf B^-$. Consequently
\[
 C_{\mathbf G}(n)\cap\mathbf B
 \subseteq\mathbf B\cap\mathbf B^-=\mathbf T,
 \qquad C_{\mathbf G}(n)\cap\mathbf B=\mathbf T[2].
\]
The last group scheme is finite, including in characteristic two.
The orbit of $\mathbf B$ under $C_{\mathbf G}(n)$ in the flag variety
$\mathbf G/\mathbf B$ therefore has dimension $\dim C_{\mathbf G}(n)$.
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{flagdimension}
                  \dim C_{\mathbf G}(n)\le N.
\end{equation}
This argument uses the dimension of an orbit and its stabilizer;
it requires neither a smooth stabilizer nor a separable orbit map.

The involution classification remains a retained input. The complete
lists in~\cite[Proposition~2.3, Table~5 and Remark~2.4]{BT}, with the
centralizer structures of~\cite[Sections~13--18]{AS} and the
corrections~\cite{ASerr}, give the following dimension inventory.
Repeated entries may denote different rational classes.
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|l|l}
\text{type}&N&\text{odd characteristic}&\text{even characteristic}\\\hline
E_8&120&120,136&190,156,136,120\\
E_7&63&69,63,79&99,81,79,69,63\\
F_4&24&24,36&36,36,30,24\\
G_2&6&6&8,6\\
{}^3D_4&12&12&18,12\\
{}^2F_4&24&--&30,24\\
{}^2G_2&6&6&--\\
{}^2B_2&4&--&4
\end{array}
\]
For ordinary Frobenius endomorphisms these dimensions are the degrees
of the classified centralizer order polynomials. For Suzuki--Ree
endomorphisms the normalized scaling is $\sqrt q$, so the dimensions
are twice those degrees: the two ${}^2F_4$ degrees are $15,12$,
and the ${}^2G_2$ and ${}^2B_2$ degrees are $3$ and $2$.
These degrees come from the uniform algebraic centralizer structures,
not an isolated finite group order. In the triality case the listed
classes are precisely those descending to ${}^3D_4(q)$.
In odd-characteristic $E_7$, both rational forms of the algebraic
$A_7.2$ and $\mathbf T_1E_6.2$ classes must be allowed in $J$;
Remark~2.4 of~\cite{BT} records this distinction.
In each row only the class used in Table~\ref{centralizers} satisfies
\eqref{flagdimension}. The two rational $A_7$ forms have the same
subsequent upper bound. This proves the required identification without
assuming that the reverser lies in the simple subgroup.
