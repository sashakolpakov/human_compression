Order 63 in the exceptional q=5 overgroups
=========================================

Scope and source
----------------

This is a bounded working contribution to the D4+ cover argument, not a
review or a whole-source admission.  Bamberg--Penttila, ``Overgroups of
cyclic Sylow subgroups of linear groups'', author preprint, Theorem 3.1,
physical pages 3--4, gives the alternatives for d=8, e=6, q=5.  In the
nearly simple alternatives the projective group lies between S and
Aut(S).  The exceptional candidates relevant here have socles A7, A8,
A9, A10, PSL2(7), PSL2(8), PSL3(4), P Omega8+(2), Sp6(2), and Sz(8).
The two symplectic-type alternatives on page 3 have a normal 2-group
with quotient a subgroup of Sp6(2) or O6+(2).  Scalar factors at q=5
also have 2-power order.  These are the exact retained classification
inputs; the arguments below do not replace the overgroup theorem.

None of these projective alternatives contains an element of order 63.
In particular, knowing only that 7 divides the group order would lose
the useful obstruction: the entire order 63 must be retained.

The small classical groups
--------------------------

For 7 <= n <= 10, Aut(An)=Sn.  A permutation of order 63 must have
cycle lengths whose least common multiple is divisible by both 9 and 7.
Either one cycle already has length divisible by 63, or distinct cycles
use at least 9+7=16 points.  Neither is possible here.

The order of Aut(PSL2(7)) is 336, which is not divisible by 9.  The order
of Aut(Sz(8)) is 3 * 8^2 * (8^2+1) * (8-1) = 87360; its 3-part is
again only 3.  Lagrange's theorem excludes 63 in both cases.

The outer automorphism group of PSL2(8) has order 3.  If x in its full
automorphism group had order 63, then x^3 would be an inner element of
order 21.  Every odd-order element of SL2(8)=PSL2(8) is semisimple.  Its
eigenvalues are either in F8, of order dividing 7, or are an inverse
pair in F64, of order dividing 9.  Thus order 21 is impossible.

The outer automorphism group of PSL3(4) has order 12, with odd part 3.
Again an element of order 63 would have its cube inside PSL3(4), of
order 21.  We give the elementary projective eigenvalue calculation,
so that no element-spectrum table is required.  An odd-order projective
element has an odd-order lift to SL3(4), hence a semisimple lift.  (For
any lift the 2-part would have to lie in the scalar kernel of order 3,
so that 2-part is trivial.)  The rational eigenspace partitions are:

* 1+1+1: all eigenvalues lie in F4*, so the projective order divides 3.
* 2+1: write the quadratic eigenvalues as a,a^4 in F16*, with third
  eigenvalue a^-5.  The fifth power of the matrix is scalar, since
  a^20=a^5 and a^-25=a^5 modulo a^15=1.  Its projective order divides 5.
* 3: an eigenvalue a in F64 has determinant a^21=1.  The seventh powers
  of a,a^4,a^16 agree, so the seventh matrix power is scalar.  Its
  projective order divides 7.

These cases also include repeated eigenvalues.  None allows order 21.

The characteristic-two form argument
------------------------------------

We prove that Sp6(2) and O8+(2) have no element of order 21.  A fortiori
they have no element of order 63, whose cube would have order 21.

An element of odd order in characteristic two is semisimple: its minimal
polynomial divides the separable polynomial X^m-1.  For an isometry of
a nondegenerate bilinear form, its nontrivial irreducible constituents
occur together with their reciprocal constituents, with equal
multiplicities.  Indeed B(gv,gw)=B(v,w) pairs an eigenvalue a only with
a^-1; scalar extension and descent give the asserted pairing.

For a primitive seventh root, its Frobenius orbit over F2 is
{a,a^2,a^4}.  The inverse orbit is distinct, because -1 is not a power
of 2 modulo 7.  A nondegenerate isometry module carrying order 7 thus
uses at least six dimensions.  For a primitive twenty-first root the
Frobenius orbit has length six, and the inverse orbit is again distinct:
the powers of 2 modulo 21 are 1,2,4,8,16,11, none equal to -1.  Such a
constituent would require twelve dimensions and is impossible in either
space under consideration.

Consequently an order-21 isometry in dimension at most eight must get
its factors 7 and 3 from different constituents.  The 7-part consumes
a paired six-space; a nontrivial 3-part needs the irreducible two-space
with minimal polynomial X^2+X+1.  This already excludes dimension six.

In dimension eight, these are exactly the two nondegenerate summands.
Each irreducible three-dimensional half of the 7-space is totally
singular for the quadratic form.  Its polar form vanishes by the
reciprocal pairing, and its quadratic form is therefore a linear
functional over F2; invariance under the irreducible order-7 action
forces that functional to vanish.  Hence this six-space has plus type.
On the remaining two-space, order 3 permutes all three nonzero vectors
transitively.  An invariant nondegenerate quadratic form has the same
value on them, which must be 1; the value 0 would make its polar form
zero.  This plane has minus type.  The orthogonal sum consequently has
minus type, contradicting O8+(2).  This proves the assertion.

Sp6(2) has no outer automorphisms.  For P Omega8+(2), the scalar centre
is trivial and its outer automorphism group is S3 (orthogonal conjugation
and triality).  Thus the cube of a hypothetical order-63 automorphism
would lie in Omega8+(2), with order 21.  The preceding O8+(2) argument
excludes it.  This use of the classical outer automorphism description
is explicit; it is not a fresh proof of triality.

Finally O6+(2) embeds into Sp6(2) by taking the polar form of its
nondegenerate quadratic form.  It therefore has no element of order 63
either.  If K is a normal 2-subgroup and x has odd order 63, then
<x> intersects K trivially.  The image of x in the quotient still has
order 63.  This disposes of both symplectic-type alternatives, including
their additional scalar factors.
