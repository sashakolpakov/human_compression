Lemma 3.No element of the orthogonal groupO +
8 (2)has order63.
Proof.Such an element would be semisimple. An eigenvalue whose order is divisible by 63 has
degree at least six, and an eigenvalue of order 63 has degree six. Its irreducible polynomial is not
self-reciprocal: no power of 2 is−1 modulo 63. A second, distinct dual constituent would require
twelve dimensions. If no eigenvalue has order divisible by 63, separate constituents must supply
the factors nine and seven. Order nine requires six dimensions, since ord 9(2) = 6. A constituent
supplying the factor seven costs at least six dimensions: if its degree is three, its eigenvalue has
order seven and a distinct dual constituent is required; otherwise its degree is at least six (this
includes eigenvalue order 21). The two contributions are distinct, since otherwise an eigenvalue
would have order divisible by 63. Again at least twelve dimensions are necessary.

Scope and attribution.This is an application of an explicitly retained complete subgroup
classification, not a new classification. It corrects the missing arbitrary-element interface in the
use of Leemans–Liebeck Table 4; it does not infer that that source’s multiplicities or projective
torus orders are correct. The triality analysis deciding whichB 3 subgroups contain a particularx,
the inverter bounds and the final generating-pair inequality are separate components. No whole-
original admission, measurement or new question is asserted here. The torus construction and the
surrounding orthogonal program were authored by the root agent; this cover specialization and its
two-field-structure argument were authored by publication recovery. The parent independently
obtained the relative-field orientation argument in a separate orthogonal manuscript. The peer

retained as a contribution, not as an independent review of this component.
