\section{Decision and boundary}

This review accepts the specified four-application contract, subject to the exact
artifact binding in the accompanying JSON record. The common paper and the four
applications contain complete written arguments for that contract, with the
retained mathematical inputs, corrected historical assertions and excluded
auxiliary assertions stated explicitly. The two required finite programs are
part of the proof of the Janko classification. The acceptance does not assert
that every sentence of every source is recovered literally, that the whole
volume is formalized in Lean, or that the rewritten arguments have historical
priority. It makes no numerical compression claim and adds no source or question
credit.

The historical boundary consists of the 35 previously accepted contract rows
for L088, N013 and L108, and the five previously accepted F014 clauses. These
include qualified and excluded rows; ``40 rows'' does not mean 40 newly proved
theorems. The F014 higher-rank conjecture belongs to its separate admitted
question packet and is not a result of this volume.

I read the complete common paper, all four applications, both required GAP
programs and the three Lean modules. I read the complete available primary
bodies for the four target sources. The L108 representation is the archived
authored browser text of its seven printed pages, not an original PDF. Eleven
critical pages of the three available source PDFs were also inspected visually.
All 31 rendered pages of the six scientific/code documents were inspected. The
complete primary and artifact inventory is recorded separately rather than
redistributed with this review.

\section{The common geometric arguments}

The distinction between subgroup intersection and flag completion is preserved
throughout. The coset-recognition proposition uses both the intersection
property and typed coset Helly. A common coset representative completes a flag;
the intersection property identifies its stabilizer. The panel and residual
connectivity arguments then give a thin, residually connected geometry. The
separate chamber-gallery argument proves that the full automorphism group acts
freely on chambers. Consequently a chamber-transitive subgroup is the full
group. None of these assertions is obtained by treating an arbitrary nonstring
C-group as automatically defining a geometry.

The anchored Helly criterion is correct: right translation puts a previously
intersecting family at the base parabolics, and its remaining coset is represented
in a selected parabolic. The displayed product conditions are exactly the
remaining pairwise intersections. In the finite counting form the represented
cosets of the common subgroup already form a subset of the tested intersection;
equality of their cardinalities therefore proves equality of those sets.

For endpoint conjugation, the proof lists every possible marked subgroup.
Old--old and conjugated--conjugated intersections follow from the original
intersection property. Mixed intersections use precisely the stated overlap
identity. The only remaining intersections are between the groups containing
both endpoint marks; the index-two dichotomy reduces these to intersection
closure of the support family. Finiteness of the index set is now stated
explicitly where the least-support conclusion uses it. No finite-group
assumption is needed. The central-twist lemma preserves generation by recovering
both commuting factors from their odd-order product, and it does not claim
automatic preservation of the geometric conditions.

The graph-halving construction is a literal incidence construction. Higher
objects retain a specified component in the bipartite double cover. At a fixed
lifted vertex its residue is identified with the original residue. For a flag
containing only higher objects the common shadow is either one component or
both components of the lifted shadow graph; the precise parity condition is
therefore necessary and sufficient for residual connectivity. The proof extends
flags to chambers before deriving connectivity and does not identify a
truncation with a residue without proof. Simplicity of the original graph makes
an edge recoverable from its endpoints. This also supplies the faithful-action
adapter and the endpoint-overlap condition. For lattice polytopes, the relevant
shadows are nested, so the parity hypothesis is verified rather than omitted.

\section{Binary modules and the inverse construction}

For an arbitrary nonempty vertex set, the augmentation module uses finitely
supported vectors. Edge differences along a path give the difference between
its endpoints. The closed-walk relations make the reverse assignment independent
of the chosen path, and the two compositions fix the edge generators and the
augmentation basis. This proves the graph presentation even for infinite vertex
sets; it does not require an unmentioned finite-dimensional argument.

The recognition theorem first constructs a homomorphism from the augmentation
module using its basis. The covariance formula on that basis extends to the
whole module. Multiplication then verifies directly that
\[
        \Psi(w,h)=T(w)j(h)
\]
is a homomorphism from the semidirect product. Generation is an explicit
hypothesis for surjectivity and for the second inverse identity. The conclusion
is not supplied as a hypothesis: component maps and their stated values under
the proposed forward map are checked separately.

I checked the cyclic displacement calculations with integer indices. The
commutativity proof, both conjugation formulas, inversion by the reflection and
the period reduction are valid. With $T_i=t_{-i}$, the formulas become exactly
the augmentation-basis covariance for rotation and reflection. The relation
$y=t_1x^{-1}$ supplies generation. The inverse maps consequently prove equality
with the presented group, not merely the existence of a finite quotient. The
earlier finite-order upper-bound argument is no longer a required step. The
displacement argument remains attributed to the source; standard augmentation
and semidirect-product algebra is not described as a new invention.

As a separate finite diagnostic, I reconstructed the nonabelian example
$S_3=C_3\rtimes C_2$ from explicit permutations and checked all 36 products and
both inverse compositions. Further controls remove generation, covariance, or
either component-inverse condition. These controls expose the need for the
hypotheses; they are not substitutes for the general proof.

\section{The four source applications}

\paragraph{The first Janko group.}
The exact source presentation is identified by a completed coset enumeration
with index 1463 over a subgroup of order at most 120, together with a quotient
of order 175560. A valid finite permutation action alone would not establish
the upper bound; the written proof and program correctly distinguish them.
The transformed markings satisfy the required intersections and typed Helly
conditions. The six multisets of maximal-parabolic orders are distinct, which
separates geometries even when type permutations are allowed. The two failed
operations have the asserted nonthin panels. The figure-label transposition is
kept separate from the definitions of the geometries.

The census proof retains the complete subgroup catalogue and exact algorithms,
not the historical geometry census. Every rank-$k$ residue of a target geometry
lies inside some maximal proper subgroup, and its distinguished involutions
occur in the local enumeration. Appending every involution outside the local
generated subgroup is therefore exhaustive. The local group is not incorrectly
required to equal its containing maximal subgroup. The rank-five stage retains
proper subgroup targets too, so it excludes higher-rank geometries by taking a
rank-five residue. The conjugacy key is justified using actual conjugators and
centralizers; no assertion that every automorphism of $J_1$ is inner is needed.

My fresh execution reproduced six constructions, 1536 marked intersections,
96 typed-Helly equalities, 100016 rank-four extensions and 21352 rank-five
extensions. It found six rank-four classes and no rank-five classes. The complete
class artifact agrees byte-for-byte with the expected certificate. The two
negative geometric controls also reproduced their exact panel failures.

\paragraph{Generalized cubes.}
Danzer's construction, polytopality and original full automorphism group remain
retained inputs. The shared graph-generation theorem identifies the even
translation subgroup. The dual convention is handled explicitly: the exponent
in the source counts facets of its input. The polygon counts, orientation and
self-duality are proved, with Euler characteristic stated as a retained standard
input.

The local-sphericity classification checks actual maximal residues, not merely
their diagrams. The source lattice hypothesis excludes digonal sections. The
finite Coxeter list leaves the stated simplex, cube and exceptional cases;
the remaining $F_4$ possibility is excluded by an affine proper fork. Conversely
the residues split into the proved $D_j$ factor and the relevant spherical
section. The exact toroidal identification is established by a Gray-map
face-poset bijection, the distinguished affine reflections and the actual
translation lattice $4\mathbb Z^r$. The rank-three square-map basis is checked
separately. A group-order coincidence is not used as a substitute for these
geometric identifications.

The convention from Ens, selected printed pages 70--75, is an inherited retained
notation input. No stored primary PDF or text is available for that reading,
so this review does not claim to have freshly checked those pages. The explicit
lattice theorem in the application is nevertheless proved independently of an
Ens classification. The comparison with every example in the source's
reference [9] and its unused semiregular identification remain excluded.
The rank-three limitation of the source's nonsphericity remark is stated.

\paragraph{Petrials.}
The new faces are actual pairs of vertex and edge sets, with equal pairs
identified. The faithful-action theorem therefore gives the exact natural
kernel, the core of the indicated order-two stabilizer. Residual connectivity
is proved by replacing paths through old faces; it is not inferred from a
diagram. The thinness obstruction treats separately whether an edge-changing
involution preserves the Petrie face. The connected degree-two residue argument
and the rotation/reflection calculation remain valid for infinite residues.
After thinness has been proved, the action on new chambers and panel indices
gives faithfulness. The second Petrial recovers old face shadows, and their
distinctness is established explicitly. The $C_2^3$ and $C_2\times S_3$
counterexamples correctly qualify the unconditional embedding and length
assertions. No finite atlas or sampled family is used to prove the theorem.

\paragraph{Equal adjacent parameters.}
For every $n\geq3$, the proposed model contains the pure dihedral factor and
one edge translation, so shared graph generation gives the whole group. The
complement-twist automorphism interchanges the endpoints, identifies both
dihedral intersections, and supplies the marking reversal. The remaining
maximal intersection is checked by translation coordinates. The proof then
derives all subgroup intersections in rank three. The augmentation-basis
adapter matches the source's actual matrices and distinguished translation.
The universal presentation uses the full range $1\leq k<n$, and the common
inverse construction proves injectivity. The $n=2$ specialization uses its
separate $C_2^3$ construction. The literal inverse-action multiplication in the
source is correctly flagged as inconsistent with the stated left action.
The earlier polygon construction receives attribution; no new existence or
historical-priority claim is made.

\section{Formal verification and remaining costs}

Fresh compilation checked all three supplied modules: nine reported
declarations in \texttt{SemidirectRecognition}, five in
\texttt{BinaryDihedralDisplacements}, and two in
\texttt{EndpointDoublingIntersections}. Every axiom report contains only the
standard Lean axioms. The new module constructs the actual semidirect lift,
proves surjectivity from subgroup generation, establishes both inverse
identities and constructs the multiplicative equivalence. The reused modules
prove their stated displacement and fixed-overlap identities.

The augmentation basis, the cyclic covariance instance, all source-specific
incidence arguments, the GAP census and the source-to-proof correspondence
remain written or computational obligations. They are not consequences of
compilation alone. Missing pinned dependency objects were rebuilt privately;
the remaining pinned package objects were trusted, not all recompiled. The
retained catalogue and algorithm interfaces are likewise explicit mathematical
dependencies.

The common paper is a full topic-local acquisition. Both complete mathematical
GAP files are included once in the program appendix. They must not disappear
from a later cost comparison. Operational drivers, formal validation and audit
records are separately inventoried. The original L108 PDF is unavailable, so
no four-source PDF-length numerator or complete-volume factor is authorized by
this review. There is no claim about unmeasured graphic, historical program or
retained external proof costs.
