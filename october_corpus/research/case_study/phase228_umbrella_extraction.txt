Supported extensions and finite two-group quotients
Abstract
We study marked groups through the intersections of subgroups generated by subsets of their markers.
A construction over a group with deletion maps attaches local extensions whose kernels are invisible on
proper local subgroups. We prove generation of the full compatible product, compute every parabolic
kernel and obtain the intersection property. The same coordinates give local recognition, deletion maps,
lower-central identities, separator factorizations and transfer of the flag property for coset geometries. For
finite two-groups whose noncommuting diagram is a forest, we identify a canonical class-two quotient
and a finite, exhaustive parameter space of marked quotients of any prescribed excess order. Explicit
affine square groups supply local models of every required two-power order. The paper includes the
supporting quotient, Frattini and amalgam arguments so that subsequent applications can cite complete
statements with specified hypotheses.
Introduction
A presentation by involutions does not by itself determine a regular polytope or hypertope. The dis-
tinguished generators must have the prescribed pair orders, their generated subgroups must intersect
correctly, and a geometric conclusion may require a further common-representative condition on cosets.
Establishing these properties separately for every presentation can obscure the part of the proof that is
independent of its numerical parameters.
The main construction here retains an already understood marked quotient and records the extra relations
in separate kernel coordinates. Each attached group is required to agree with the quotient on every proper
part of its support. This hypothesis lets us isolate its whole kernel, even when one support contains
another. Once the kernels have been isolated, all marked subgroup intersections and orders follow from
a single coordinate calculation. The geometric assertion uses an additional proved transfer of typed coset
Helly; it is not inferred from the intersection property alone.
A second branch identifies the quotient which every finite two-group marking on a forest must have. The
proof uses the intersection property in the original group, a descending lower-central argument and the
separation of a forest at an edge. It then reduces classification at any fixed excess order to normal sub-
groups of one finite nilpotent quotient. The explicit square groups in Section 7 provide a complementary
construction: their orders vary, while their proper marked subgroups remain unchanged.
The immediate application in the companion paper Prescribed rank, type and two-power order is the ex-
istence theorem of Hou, Feng and Leemans [HFL20]. Other applications require additional arguments,
including recognition of complete source presentations, bounds on cyclic cores, finite census computa-
tions or geometric assembly. Those arguments are not consequences of the words “fiber product” alone
and are not claimed here.
This paper reorganizes existing complete proofs in the project’s reviewed umbrella and two-group
manuscript. It makes their hypotheses independent of that manuscript’s section numbering. The
original existence and classification results retain their source attribution. We make no claim of
historical priority for the general arguments and no claim to solve an open conjecture.

3. Coordinate kernels and incidence geometries
An incidence system has a finite type set, objects carrying types, and a symmetric incidence relation;
distinct objects of one type are not incident. A flag is a set of pairwise incident objects. A chamber has
one object of each type. A geometry is an incidence system in which every flag extends to a chamber.
The residue of a flag consists of the objects of the remaining types incident with that flag, with induced
incidence. The geometry is residually connected when the incidence graph of every residue of rank at
least two, including the empty-flag residue, is connected. It is thin when every panel, a flag missing just
one type, lies in exactly two chambers. A regular hypertope is a thin, residually connected geometry
admitting a chamber-transitive type-preserving automorphism group.

Proposition UA.Geometry (coset recognition and faithful chamber action). Suppose the marking
of 𝐺 consists of nonidentity involutions, has IP, and has typed coset Helly for its maximal parabol-

preserving automorphism group, acting regularly on chambers. More generally, the full type-preserving
automorphism group of any thin, residually connected geometry of finite rank acts freely on chambers.
A chamber-transitive subgroup is consequently the full group.
Proof. In the coset system, a common coset point extends every flag to a chamber by choosing the coset
of each missing type through that point. Right translation sends every such flag to its base flag. For a
base flag of type set 𝑆, IP gives stabilizer 𝐺𝐼∖𝑆 . In particular the full chamber stabilizer is 1, while a
panel stabilizer is a group generated by one nonidentity involution and has order two. There are therefore
exactly two chambers through each panel.
Consider a residue with remaining type set 𝑇 , where |𝑇 | ≥ 2 . Its stabilizer is 𝐺𝑇 , and its maximal object
stabilizers are 𝐺𝑇 ∖{𝑖}, 𝑖 ∈ 𝑇 . Every marker indexed by 𝑇 lies in at least one of these proper parabolics.
A word in those subgroups supplies a walk between their translated base objects in the residue incidence
graph; all objects are translates of base objects and all base objects lie in a common flag. The residue is
therefore connected.
For the general assertion, first observe that every residually connected finite-rank geometry has a con-
nected chamber graph. This follows by induction on rank: chambers through a fixed object are connected
through galleries in its residue, and a path in the incidence graph, extending each consecutive incident
pair to a chamber, connects arbitrary chambers through these subgraphs. The rank-one case holds be-
cause all chambers share the empty panel. In a thin geometry an automorphism fixing a chamber fixes its
unique neighbor across each panel. It therefore fixes every chamber along every gallery, and hence every
object. The chamber action is free. If a subgroup is chamber-transitive, any full automorphism agrees on
a base chamber with one subgroup element; their quotient fixes that chamber and is the identity. Apply
this to the coset geometry to obtain the stated full automorphism group. □

10. Retained inputs, applications and attribution
The proofs use the elementary subgroup, quotient and isomorphism theorems. The further classical in-
puts are explicitly limited: Burnside’s basis theorem for finite 𝑝-groups; their nilpotence; generation of
the lower-central graded Lie ring by degree one and its multilinear commutator rules; and the precise
amalgam normal-form theorem stated in Section 9. The Frattini, forest, finite-quotient and torsion de-
ductions are proved in full above. No finite group classification, atlas census or numerical search result
supplies a premise to these theorems.
For applications to abstract regular polytopes we retain the following standard correspondence [MS02,
Section 2E]. A group generated by an ordered list of nonidentity involutions, with the string commutation
rules and IP, is the full automorphism group of a regular abstract polytope with that distinguished mark-
ing. Its rank is the length of the list, its type consists of the adjacent product orders, and its number of
flags is the group order. A generator-preserving epimorphism between such groups induces a rank- and
adjacency-preserving covering of their associated polytopes. These are geometric input theorems, not
new conclusions of the present paper. By contrast, the coset-geometric recognition and coordinate-kernel
Helly statements needed for the hypertope branch are proved in Section 3 with their full hypotheses.
The supported-extension construction supplies a common proof interface for the two-power constructions
studied in [HFL20, HFL19a, HFL19b, CFHS25]. Its use does not identify a compatible product with a
universal presentation without a further order or normal-form argument. The forest quotient gives the
14
canonical group underlying the main quotient and cover conclusions of [HS26], and the finite-kernel
parameter space gives the exhaustive part of the order-1024 reconstruction associated with [GLP18].
Their exact source statements, literal corrections, numerical certificates and named-object identifications
belong to the separate applications.
The elementary kernel, image and deletion proofs were already part of the reviewed proof library. The
supported-kernel induction abstracts the recurring extension construction; the forest quotient and affine
models were already proved in the reviewed two-group manuscript. We have retained those proofs and
placed them with the shared theorems they support. The new work in this edition is the closed topic
interface, explicit definitions, and the local clarifications recorded in the provenance ledger. Neither this
reorganization nor the use of a language model establishes historical priority. Original source results and
borrowed geometric theorems keep their attribution.
Existing Lean certificates cover declared components of the kernel, supported-extension and Helly argu-
ments. They do not presently formalize this whole paper: in particular, the full forest quotient proof, the
affine model and the geometric recognition/application interfaces are not all connected in Lean. Their
written proofs above remain part of the mathematical obligation. The accompanying certificate scope
distinguishes inherited checked declarations from any freshly checked application components.
