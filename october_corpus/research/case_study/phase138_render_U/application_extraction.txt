Marked groups, kernels and intersections
Abstract
Many constructions of incidence geometries require the same two tasks: control the intersections of
subgroups generated by selected markers, and identify the permutation group acting on the resulting
objects. We give a common treatment in which a quotient supplies the visible geometry and its kernel
records the remaining intersection obstructions. The main construction assembles local extensions over
specified sets of markers. Injectivity on every proper local parabolic is enough to isolate the entire
local kernels, even when their supports are nested. It follows that the full fiber product is generated by
the prescribed markers and has an exact kernel profile on every parabolic. We derive local recognition,
deletion maps, lower-central identities and separator factorizations from the same proof. Exact coordinate
profiles also transfer typed coset Helly; with the necessary involutory local markings this gives regular
geometries. A finite nilpotent quotient and an invariant subgroup ladder then give a complete parameter
space for marked finite two-group quotients with a prescribed class-two quotient. Further results treat
exact quotient intersections, comparison through one exceptional parabolic, faithful actions on retained
objects, relative generation and Frattini coordinates. These results are intended to be cited with explicit
hypothesis bindings; local presentations, geometric identifications and finite classifications remain the
responsibility of their applications.
1. Markings, images and kernels
A marked group is a group 𝐺 together with a finite indexed generating family (𝑟𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼. Except where
stated, the markers need not be involutions or distinct. For 𝑆 ⊆ 𝐼 , put
𝐺𝑆 = ⟨𝑟 𝑖 ∶ 𝑖 ∈ 𝑆⟩, 𝐺 ∅ = 1.
We call these subgroups parabolics, retaining their index sets even when two of them happen to coincide.
The marking has the intersection property, or IP, if
𝐺𝑆 ∩ 𝐺𝑇 = 𝐺 𝑆∩𝑇 (𝑆, 𝑇 ⊆ 𝐼). (1.1)
A marked homomorphism sends each source marker to the corresponding target marker. Its image on
𝐺𝑆 is therefore the target parabolic with index set 𝑆.
All subgroup products below are products of sets unless a subgroup assertion is made. We write𝛾1(𝐺) =
𝐺 and 𝛾𝑗+1(𝐺) = [𝛾 𝑗(𝐺), 𝐺]. We use the elementary correspondence and isomorphism theorems for
groups. In the finite 𝑝-group arguments we retain Burnside’s basis theorem, the nilpotence of finite 𝑝-
groups, and the usual multilinear commutator description of the lower-central graded Lie ring. Precisely,
its degree-one part generates the graded Lie ring, and degree 𝑗 is generated by left-normed commutators
of weight 𝑗 in any generating set. In Section 8 we explicitly retain the normal-form theorem for an
amalgamated free product. None of these classical inputs is presented as a result originating in this
manuscript.

7. How the statements are used
The coordinate-kernel theorem U.SK.Helly additionally requires exact kernel profiles and base IP to
move pairwise intersections to a common fiber. Its geometric corollary requires actual involutory local
markers, as well as a regular base geometry. These obligations are checked for the three dihedral pair
attachments in Module A.
The application interface is mathematical rather than merely notational. An application of U.SK supplies
the marked base, every actual deletion, each local marked epimorphism, and injectivity on every proper
local parabolic. Its order formula then needs only the orders of the base and local kernels. Its universal-
presentation claim, if any, still requires a presentation map and an independent sharp bound such as
U.Order. An application of U.Nilpotent must first identify the canonical class-two quotient of every
target group. An application of U.Compare must prove injectivity on the whole protected product set and
exact membership in the exceptional image. An application of U.Action supplies the actual encoding
and retained surjection, not an assumed equality of kernels.
These requirements permit the same complete proof to be cited several times without repeating its induc-
tion or quotient argument. They also identify the portions that cannot be omitted from an application:
local models, presentation recognition, geometric realization, source-specific parameter ranges, and any

Fix injective homomorphisms 𝑖𝐴 ∶ 𝐶 → 𝐴 and 𝑖𝐵 ∶ 𝐶 → 𝐵 . Let 𝐴 ∗ 𝐵 be the free product and let

We use the amalgam normal-form theorem in the following precise form: the canonical maps of 𝐴
and 𝐵 into 𝑃 are injective; an alternating reduced word with factors outside the common copy of 𝐶
has nonzero reduced length and is nonidentity; every element is conjugate either into a factor or to a
cyclically reduced alternating word of length at least two, whose positive powers remain reduced with
proportionally increasing length. This is a retained classical theorem.

infinite. If 𝐴 and 𝐵 are finitely generated, then so is 𝑃 .

The torsion assertion for subgroups is immediate. For an extension 1 → 𝐾 → 𝐸 → 𝑅 → 1 , with
