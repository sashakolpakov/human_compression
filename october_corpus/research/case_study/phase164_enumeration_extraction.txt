Module D: Marked actions, field orbits and geometry enumeration
Abstract
We classify regular polytopes and coset geometries by actions on marked configurations. Orbit multiplic-
ities describe commuting involutions in symmetric and alternating groups; normalized field coordinates
describe Suzuki polyhedra; and panel subgroups describe finite geometries with nontrivial chamber sta-
bilizer. In each case, classification of an extension reduces to a stabilizer acting on an explicit fiber.
We derive the all-degree commuting-pair formulas, the exceptional six-point polyhedra, the complete
Suzuki counting formulas, and the residually weakly primitive 𝐽2 census. A second finite application
gives the 81 rank-two and 431 rank-three primitive geometries of 𝑀22 and recovers their geometric shad-
ows; the finer labels in the source table remain a separate comparison obligation. The arguments use the
action and intersection results of the companion manuscript Marked groups, kernels and intersections .
Classical finite-group structure and the complete finite certificates are specified separately.
1. Markings, equivalence and geometric recognition
Counting configurations is useful only after their equivalence and admissibility conditions have been
identified. A pair of commuting involutions is a faithful action of a marked four-group. Completing
such a pair to a regular polyhedron requires a third involution, generation and the subgroup intersection
property. A family of panel subgroups gives a different type of marking: its chamber stabilizer need not
be trivial, and subgroup intersections alone do not ensure flag transitivity. These distinctions explain the
separate local recognition arguments below.

4.3. Intersection property and the rank restriction
The first and middle involutions in a normalized triple do not commute; neither do the middle and last, by
their different fixed points. The dihedral group generated by either adjacent pair has odd rotation order.
Indeed, if that order had 2-part at least four, its dihedral Sylow 2-subgroup would have noncommuting
involutions, impossible inside a Suzuki root group. If the rotation order had 2-part exactly two, its central
involution would centralize both generators, placing both in one root group, whose involutions commute.
Rotation order two is also excluded by noncommutation.
Suppose these two odd-rotation dihedral subgroups intersect beyond their common middle involution.
Their intersection then contains a nontrivial odd-order rotation 𝑥. Both rotation subgroups lie in the
unique maximal torus 𝐶𝑆𝑑
(𝑥). Each adjacent dihedral subgroup normalizes that torus, so all three
generators lie in its proper normalizer. This contradicts the generation theorem. Their intersection is
consequently exactly the order-two group generated by the middle involution.
The outer four-group intersects either odd-rotation dihedral subgroup in exactly the appropriate endpoint
subgroup, by order divisibility. Together these statements give every rank-three subgroup-intersection
identity. Every normalized triple is therefore a nondegenerate string C-group for its generated subfield
group. Its adjacent product orders are at least three.
There is no string C-group representation of 𝑆𝑁 of rank at least four. In such a representation, 𝑟0 com-
mutes with every 𝑟𝑗 for 𝑗 ≥ 2 . These involutions lie in the center of one root group and therefore
commute with each other. In particular 𝑟3 commutes with all 𝑟𝑗 for 𝑗 ≥ 2 , as well as with 𝑟0, 𝑟1 by the
string relations. This would be a nontrivial central element of 𝑆𝑁 . Ranks one and two cannot generate

The action may be replaced by its actual permutation image on ℳ: each image element has an automor-

has order 887 040, equal to the order of the separately computed full automorphism group. Counting
normalizer elements as distinct automorphisms would be unjustified; taking the image gives the correct
action.
