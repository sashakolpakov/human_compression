General theorem
---------------

Let G be a centerless group. Fix a,c in G and a finite subgroup
T <= C_G(a) intersect C_G(c). Let T act on possible b in G by conjugation.
Suppose the orbit of b has exact size w<|T|. Orbit-stabilizer gives
|C_T(b)|=|T|/w>1. Choose a nonidentity t in that stabilizer. It commutes
with a,c because t lies in T, and with b by the stabilizer condition.
It therefore centralizes both ab and bc, as well as <a,b,c>.

If <ab,bc>=G, then t centralizes G, contradicting Z(G)=1. Consequently
<ab,bc> is proper in G. The same argument also rules out <a,b,c>=G.
For every x in T the rotations associated to b^x are the conjugates by x
of the rotations associated to b, so this nongeneration property holds on
the entire exact T-orbit. Exactness of T as the FULL common centralizer
is unnecessary: subgroup containment, finite order and exact orbit size
already suffice. No particular nonidentity stabilizer element must be found
or supplied to use this existential finite-group proof.

This is a nongeneration theorem. It does not prove an intersection property,
classify automorphism/reversal orbits, or provide a constructive marking.
The converse is false: a free orbit need not generate G. No free row is
discharged by this family.
