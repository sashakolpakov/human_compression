Commutation constraints and affine matrix witnesses
===================================================

This note develops a uniform obstruction to completing a partial group
marking. It uses commutants, generated matrix algebras and rank-nullity,
all standard parts of representation theory and linear algebra. Root
developed this formulation after the certified branch 621 commutant
example. Final independently suggested a related algebra construction
while studying its centre argument. Earlier group, matrix-bridge and
centralizer acquisitions retain their attribution. No historical priority
claim is made for the mathematics in this note.

The note is written theory. No new matrix solve, family exclusion,
manuscript treatment, Lean proof or compression measurement is claimed.

1. A proper subgroup from one endomorphism
------------------------------------------

Let F be a field, V a finite-dimensional F-vector space, E=End_F(V),
and rho:G -> GL(V) a representation. For a subset T of E write

             T'={X in E : XY=YX for every Y in T}.

Thus T' is its commutant. The commutant is an F-subalgebra and hence an
F-linear subspace. For a
subalgebra A write A''=(A')'. Always A is contained in A'': its elements
commute with every element that commutes with A. Equality is not assumed.
In particular no semisimplicity hypothesis is being silently imposed.

Suppose a set of group elements generates P<=G. If X commutes with the
image of every generator of P, it commutes with rho(P). This follows
from XM=MX and XN=NX implying XMN=MNX, and from XM=MX implying
XM^-1=M^-1X when M is invertible. If some acquired g in G satisfies
Xrho(g)!=rho(g)X, then g is not in P and P is proper.

The matrix X may be singular, and rho need not be faithful. What is
needed is a genuine representation of the same group, together with an
escape element actually belonging to that group. The certificate uses
an endomorphism, rather than asserting that it is a group element.

2. Unknown generators constrained to commutants
------------------------------------------------

Fix some positions I in a prospective marking, with known group elements
a_i and matrices A_i=rho(a_i). For each unknown position j, let S_j be
the known positions with which its generator is required to commute.
Put

        A(S_j)=F[A_i : i in S_j],
        W={A_i : i in I}' intersect intersection_j A(S_j)''.

The notation F[...] means the unital F-subalgebra generated by the listed
matrices. An empty list generates the scalar algebra F*1.

Theorem 1. If X belongs to W and fails to commute with an acquired
rho(g), then every completion of the fixed marking satisfying the stated
commutations generates a proper subgroup of G.

Proof. The matrix Y_j of any unknown generator commutes with each A_i,
i in S_j. It therefore commutes with every word in those matrices and
with every F-linear combination of words. Thus Y_j belongs to A(S_j)'.
Since X belongs to A(S_j)'', it commutes with Y_j. By the first factor
in the definition of W it also commutes with every fixed matrix A_i.
Section 1 now excludes g from the generated subgroup. This works for
every simultaneous choice of the unknown matrices. Further conditions,
such as involution orders or the intersection property, only restrict
the possible completions and do not weaken the exclusion.

This theorem requires no full group centralizers and no enumeration of
possible unknown generators. A useful sufficient subspace is obtained
by replacing each A(S_j)'' with the smaller algebra A(S_j). Either
space is finite-dimensional. A failure to find an escape in the chosen
space is inconclusive about the existence of a generating completion.

If S_j is empty, A(S_j)'=E and A(S_j)''=F*1. Indeed a matrix commuting
with all matrix units is scalar. Thus the criterion correctly reduces
to scalar witnesses when an unknown generator is wholly unconstrained
by the fixed positions. Such witnesses cannot escape a group generator.

3. One test for string markings of every rank at least five
-----------------------------------------------------------

Let r>=5. A string marking is an ordered sequence t_0,...,t_(r-1)
of involutions with [t_i,t_j]=1 whenever |i-j|>=2. Fix

          a=t_0, b=t_1, e=t_(r-1),
          A=rho(a), B=rho(b), E_0=rho(e).

Here E_0 is one matrix; the surrounding endomorphism algebra is still E.
The fixed far commutations give [A,E_0]=[B,E_0]=0. Define

          P=F[A,E_0], Q=F[A,B],
          W_*= {A,B,E_0}' intersect P'' intersect Q''.

Corollary 2. An escape witness in W_* excludes every string completion
of this fixed triple in every rank r>=5.

Proof. The unknown t_2 commutes with a and e, so its matrix lies in P'.
The unknown t_(r-2) commutes with a and b, so its matrix lies in Q'.
When there are further unknown positions, they commute with all three
fixed generators. Put R=F[A,B,E_0]. Since P and Q are subalgebras of R,
we have R' contained in P' and Q', hence P'' and Q'' contained in R''.
Thus a member of W_* also commutes with every remaining unknown matrix
in R'. Theorem 1 applies. For r=5 the two displayed unknown positions
are simply positions 2 and 3, so there is no further case.

The corollary does not assert that ranks or group representations with
different fixed triples have been classified. The same acquired triple
and the same genuine group representation must be retained in an actual
application.

There is a particularly small sufficient space. Since A and E_0 are
commuting involutions, every word in them reduces to one of

                         1, A, E_0, AE_0.

Therefore P has dimension at most four over F, in every characteristic.
If

                   X in P intersect Q,       XB=BX,             (2)

then X commutes with A and E_0 because P is commutative, and satisfies
the sufficient-algebra version of Corollary 2. The four displayed
matrices need not be independent. Membership in Q and equation (2)
are linear conditions on their coefficients. An actual application must
verify them. Abelianity of a group centralizer alone does not establish
these matrix identities.

4. A certificate using words instead of a second centralizer
-------------------------------------------------------------

The following intermediate version is useful when a full K=C_G(a,e)
and its generating matrices K_1,...,K_s have already been acquired.
It needs no full H=C_G(a,b) acquisition. Seek

       X in F[A,B] intersect {A,B,E_0,K_1,...,K_s}'.              (3)

Every completing c belongs to K, so the explicit K-basis commutations
make its matrix commute with X. Every completing d commutes with a
and b, so it commutes with the entire algebra F[A,B], including X.
The three fixed matrices commute with X by (3). An original-generator
escape again proves properness for all such completions.

A positive certificate for membership in F[A,B] consists simply of
coefficients and matrix words whose linear combination is X. The checker
can multiply those words and verify the equality; a claim that they
span the whole algebra is unnecessary. A producer can find a complete
word basis by starting with 1 and adjoining independent products of
current basis words with A and B. At most (dim V)^2 basis vectors are
retained. When every such product is already in the span, the span is
a unital right-invariant algebra containing A and B, and hence is
exactly F[A,B]. The existence search and the supplied positive proof
have different obligations.

For example, since A and B are involutions, AB+BA commutes with A and
B. It also commutes with E_0, which commutes with A and B. Every matrix
centralizing A and B commutes with AB+BA. Its commutations with the
actual K-basis and its escape from the original group still require
checking. It may be scalar, zero, or otherwise unsuitable.

The earlier group-theoretic half-turn is another special case. If a,b
are involutions and ab has finite even order m, z=(ab)^(m/2) is a
nonidentity involution central in <a,b>. If K=C_G(a,e) is abelian and
e commutes with a and b, then z belongs to K, centralizes K and all of
C_G(a,b), and centralizes the fixed marks. In a centerless group this
excludes every rank-five completion. The order m, full K equality and
abelianity must be acquired for the actual prefix; they are not assumed
from a list of candidate jobs.

5. Complete commutant certificates from two rank bounds
-------------------------------------------------------

Put n=dim V and m=n^2. A commutant of finitely many given matrices is
the kernel of the explicitly known linear map

               L: E -> E^s,   Z |-> (ZM_i-M_iZ)_i.

After choosing matrix-entry coordinates this is a k-by-m matrix, with
k=s*m. A proposed complete kernel basis can be verified without trusting
a producer's elimination history. Let Q be an m-by-d matrix. Check:

* LQ=0;
* one d-by-d minor of Q is invertible;
* one (m-d)-by-(m-d) minor of L is invertible.

Distinct in-range row and column indices must be supplied for the minors.
An inverse matrix is a finite certificate of invertibility, verified by
matrix multiplication over the stated field. Empty zero-by-zero minors
have their usual determinant-one convention when d=0 or m-d=0.

The second condition gives dim ker L>=d; the third gives rank L>=m-d.
Rank-nullity forces equality in both. Thus Q is a complete basis of the
commutant. Checking that X commutes with every basis matrix now proves
membership in the double commutant. An incomplete list of commuting
matrices would not suffice: completeness is exactly what the two rank
bounds establish.

These certificates concern finite-dimensional linear maps. They do not
identify a named finite group or prove equality with a group centralizer.
Theorem 1 deliberately avoids those additional group acquisitions.

6. The affine witness spaces
----------------------------

All defining conditions for W are homogeneous linear equations over F.
For a chosen acquired generator G_l=rho(g_l) and entry (u,v), put

                 ell(X)=(XG_l-G_lX)_(u,v).

An escape with ell(X)!=0 can be rescaled to have ell(X)=1. Hence one
can search the finite family of affine systems

                         X in W, ell(X)=1.                     (4)

If a system has one solution X_0, all its solutions are exactly
X_0+(W intersect ker ell). Translation by the additive group
W intersect ker ell is free and transitive. This is a torsor in the
ordinary precise sense. The assertion follows directly by subtracting
two solutions and adding a homogeneous solution to a fixed one.

For a fixed representation and acquired ambient generating set, at most
|generators|*n^2 entry systems are needed. No field-size or tuple-count
factor appears in the number of linear equations. Solving these systems
is a way to find certificates; the positive certificate remains the
literal X, the proved commutant memberships and one actual failed entry.

7. Permutation matrices
------------------------

For a finite action on a set Omega, the commutant of its permutation
matrices has the familiar orbital basis. A matrix commutes with every
permutation matrix exactly when its entries are constant on each orbit
of the simultaneous action on Omega x Omega. Indeed conjugating a matrix
by a permutation simultaneously permutes its row and column indices.
The indicator matrices of these disjoint pair orbits form a basis,
over every field: their supports are disjoint, so they are independent,
and every invariant matrix is their linear combination.

This proves the same method applies to permutation representations. It
does not make a large square matrix automatically inexpensive. A smaller
faithful or otherwise suitable representation can make the difference
between an impractical pair-orbit calculation and a small linear system.
The original representation bridge and its proof are common material
whose cost must be retained in any later compression comparison.

The reusable mathematical work is the commutation theorem and its
certificate interface. An application still owes exact fixed matrices,
their relation to the original group and source marking, an acquired
escape witness, and the claimed original result. None is supplied by
the general theorem alone.
