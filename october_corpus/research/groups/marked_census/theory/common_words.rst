Lemma 1: one common nonisotropic pure line rejects generation
------------------------------------------------------------

Suppose 0!=v in V, N(0,v)!=0, and every one of a,b,c,e preserves the
line ell=<v>. Then H<G. In particular this marking fails the original
whole-quartet generation predicate, independently of its IP outcome.

Proof. Since each generator preserves ell, every word does, so
H<=G_ell. In the natural split Cayley action the full stabilizer of a
nonisotropic pure line is SL3(11):2 if -N(0,v) is a square, or SU3(11):2
otherwise. With q=11 their orders and the ambient order are

  k_S=2 q^3(q^3-1)(q^2-1),
  k_U=2 q^3(q^3+1)(q^2-1),
  g=q^6(q^6-1)(q^2-1).

Consequently k_S/g=2/[q^3(q^3+1)]<1 and
k_U/g=2/[q^3(q^3-1)]<1. Either stabilizer is proper. This proves the claim.
These are the full natural SL3 and SU3 groups, not projective quotients.
No subgroup-order computation or maximal-overgroup search is involved.

gives lambda^2=1, hence lambda is1 or-1 in F11. Thus the complete exact
preservation test can be the seven-coordinate equality vh=v or vh=-v;
testing only a fixed vector would incorrectly exclude the outer sign.

Lemma 2: a common word outside the dihedral middle
--------------------------------------------------

This lemma holds in ANY group. Suppose b^2=c^2=1 and put r=bc. If actual
words w_L in(a,b,c) and w_R in(b,c,e) have the same value Z and

  Z r != r Z,             Z r != r^-1 Z,

then Z belongs to L intersection R but not to D=<b,c>. Hence the required
mutual IP equality L intersection R=D fails.

Proof. Since c=br and b r b=r^-1, every D-word reduces to r^i or r^i b,
with i an integer. A rotation d=r^i satisfies dr=rd. A reflected word
d=r^i b satisfies dr=r^-1 d. The two displayed inequalities therefore
exclude BOTH forms, so Z is not in D. Its two genuine word representations
prove membership in the two ends. This proves strict excess over D.

The second inequality is specifically Zbc!=(cb)Z, not a second ordinary
commutator and not Zbc!=Zcb. The direction is material in right-row
coordinates. Both inequalities are sufficient, but not necessary:
an outside element may centralize or invert r, and a failure to find this
certificate proves nothing.

No exact order of r, adjacent lower bound, end properness, natural Cayley
classification, centralizer enumeration or subgroup membership algorithm
is needed. The normal form permits coincident forms and nonfaithful
dihedral presentations. If r=1, the first inequality is impossible, so this
criterion cannot fire. If r has order2, the two inequalities coincide;
every D-element commutes with r and one noncommutation excludes D. These
degeneracies never create a false positive. The argument also works for
infinite-order r, although the actual intake is finite.

In matrices, first evaluate BOTH supplied end words independently and
check whole-matrix equality. Lifting that equality to one original group
element requires the paid faithful bridge (or an independent genuine
original word relation). A common element of two image subgroups alone
does not prove an original intersection violation under a noninjective map.
Once common original membership is established, the unequal image products
already suffice to exclude original D-membership.
