A certificate census for five marked families in G2(11)
======================================================

This package completes a partition of 16,414 original indexed positions in
five acquired families of rank four markings over F11. Relative to the
original domain and group premises below, 14 positions are string C-group
markings of G2(11), and 16,400 are obstructed. These are positions in the
original orbit lists, not newly established isomorphism classes.

The second coordinate of a family identifier is its rank. The valid original

Reproduce the arithmetic
------------------------

With Python 3.10 or later on Linux or macOS, run from the repository root::

    sh october_corpus/research/groups/marked_census/run_checks.sh

The standard library Python checker uses this directory and its frozen
sibling ``shared_obstructions``. For an isolated copy, place both complete
directories as siblings under one directory. No original workspace paths,
group software, network access or installed Python packages are required.

The driver runs 16 fresh interpreters sequentially: the unchanged baseline,
the final 48 obstructions, and each of the 14 valid markings. It stops on the
first failure. It creates no runtime files and prints 16 stage receipts
followed by one final receipt only if every command succeeds. A single case
can be checked from this directory with::

    python3 -I -B -S check.py --case 15_366

Each arithmetic invocation checks 125 seconds and 128 MiB sampled peak RSS,
with bounded file reads and matrix products. The baseline retains its own
unchanged limits. These are process checks rather than kernel quotas or a
speed guarantee. There is no asserted global limit of 125 seconds for the driver.
The fixed number of invocations bounds the total work. An isolated copy
passed all 16 stages in 51.69 seconds, with a sampled peak of 103,956,480
bytes for the entire supervised process tree, within an outer 125-second /
128 MiB guard. The exact 17 receipts are retained in ``verification.json``.
Timings vary by machine; this observed bound for the whole run is not a speed
guarantee. Earlier source calculations are pinned in ``provenance.json``.

What the checker proves
------------------------

Write the ordered marks as (a,b,c,e). A valid marking generates the intended
ambient group, consists of nonidentity involutions, has ac=ca,ae=ea,be=eb,
and satisfies H_J intersect H_K=H_(J intersect K) for every index pair,
where H_J is generated by the marks indexed by J and H_empty={I}.

The sibling baseline directly checks 16,352 obstruction certificates and
retains 62 unresolved positions. This package checks that these exact 62
positions split into 48 further obstructions and 14 valid markings. Older
receipts call successful obstruction certificates "positive"; that usage
does not mean valid string C-groups.

The 48 new obstructions use 39 pairs of fully recognized quadratic line
stabilizer ends, three literal common words outside the exact middle
subgroup, and six cases with one fully recognized end. Recognition checks
the acquired canonical norm and its negative squareclass, a word in that end
whose supplied power has exact prime order 19 or 37, and the required distinct
commuting involutions. For two recognized ends the bound from the product
set exceeds the exact middle order. With one recognized end, a supplied word in the opposite end fixes
its line but is outside the exact middle subgroup. A common line alone is
never treated as full stabilizer recognition.

Every valid marking has two directly checked words in all four marks yielding
elements of orders 19 and 37. The borrowed complete maximal subgroup list
then gives generation of G2(11). Its intersection property uses one of:

* Ten complete finite comparisons: reconstruct both larger endpoint tables
  from word trees, enumerate the other proper distinguished subgroups,
  check generator closure and every one of 105 distinct pairs of proper subsets.
* Three invariant line bounds: contain one endpoint in a line stabilizer K
  and check H_J intersect K=H_(J intersect M) for every relevant finite H_J.
  Word membership supplies the reverse inclusion. Neither the exact large
  endpoint order nor completeness of a line search is assumed.
* One conjugation action, at original index [15,3126]: reconstruct 1,320
  states and their actual word representatives, verify all 3,960 exact
  Schreier loops lie in the middle subgroup of order 20, and independently
  check both rank three ends. The included proof of the classical rank four
  end criterion then gives the full intersection property. The conjugation
  action need not be faithful.

Only arrays of parents and generator labels are retained for the larger tables.
Matrices, inverse transversals and orbit states are regenerated as actual
word products and compared with the hashes of the complete original tables.
Every closure edge is checked. Identical arrays are stored once: 35 tree
files occupy 4,683,480 bytes. No full matrix table copies are needed.

Premises, attribution and scope
--------------------------------

The following facts remain explicitly borrowed: completeness of the original
indexed domains and orbits; the orbit automorphisms preserving fixed marks;
the catalogue/frame correspondence and the faithful matrix representation
of the original group; membership in the natural split Cayley group G2(11); the complete
maximal subgroup classifications and natural quadratic line stabilizer
descriptions. The canonical norm is the acquired normalization, not an
arbitrary invariant form. Exact source identities are in ``provenance.json``.
The checker does not reacquire or reprove those structural facts.

The mathematical sources in ``theory/`` preserve the detailed end recognition,
word and stabilizer arguments. The stabilizer note includes complete proofs
of Schreier's telescoping argument and the rank four end criterion, so its
book citation is attribution rather than an additional unproved premise.
The conjugation IP step uses no classification; its separate generation
certificate still uses the borrowed G2(11) classification.

The classification attributions include Kleidman for G2(q), Gill's account
of the classical linear subgroup lists, and the unitary classification
identified in the retained note. The general criteria are classical; no
historical novelty is claimed. The sources cite Schreier, "Die Untergruppen
der freien Gruppen"(1927), DOI 10.1007/BF02952517, and McMullen--Schulte,
Abstract Regular Polytopes(2002), Proposition 2E16. Kleidman's classification
appeared in Journal of Algebra 117(1988),30--71,
DOI 10.1016/0021-8693(88)90239-6.

The baseline credits the independent arithmetic work of
``registration_reuse_review``, ``q11_batch_source_review`` and
``readme_structure_audit``. The added direct checkers and portable adaptation
are by ``q11_batch_source_review``; ``artifact_audit`` supplied the reviewed
stabilizer proof. These identifiers document artifact ancestry separately
from scholarly attribution. New work here assembles and checks explicit
certificates; it does not claim those classical theorems as new ideas.

Completeness is relative to precisely the five displayed acquired lists.
It is not a classification of the whole F004 problem or a recovery of its
whole manuscript, and does not verify the even-characteristic conclusions
of the paper tracked under that project label. No open conjecture, Lean
formalization, research compression ratio or whole-manuscript claim follows
from this package.
