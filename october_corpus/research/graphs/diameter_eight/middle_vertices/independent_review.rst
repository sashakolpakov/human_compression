The written proof
-----------------

The fixed eight-edge root path is geodesic in every permitted completion.
The saturated neighborhoods A, B and C have fifteen distinct vertices. Their
union I is independent: within a neighborhood an edge is a triangle, and each
cross-neighborhood edge would shorten the fixed root path. No bipartiteness of
the full completion is assumed. Regularity therefore gives exactly five edges
in F=G[V\I], and the three saturated centers are isolated in F.

The sets L and R mean adjacency to A and C in the full completion. They are
disjoint by the root-distance condition; B_L and B_R are disjoint for the same
reason. The fixed paths give 21 in B_L and 25 in B_R. If a vertex v meets both
sides, a hypothetical A- or C-neighbor gives a root walk of length six. For an
F-neighbor u of v the corresponding walk has length seven. Thus v and every
F-neighbor of v lie in M, whose I-neighborhoods lie entirely in B.

Write d=d_F(v). Two distinct B-neighbors imply d<=3. Each F-neighbor u has a
B-neighborhood disjoint from that of v, by triangle-freeness, so
d_F(u)+d>=5. The d incident edges at v and at least d(4-d) further edges are
distinct: two neighbors of v cannot be adjacent, while repeated outside
endpoints do not identify edges. Consequently |F|>=d(5-d). For d=2 or 3 this
is six, contradicting |F|=5. The same counting proves the stated general
k>=5 result d in {0,1}, with d<=k-2 and |F|=k. This uses standard elementary
graph counting and geodesic arguments; no field-priority claim is made.
