Put $E_n=\{v\in\mathbb F_2^n:\sum_i v_i=0\}$ and
$W_n=E_n\rtimes S_n$, with the natural permutation action on coordinates.
We retain the classical identification $W(D_n)=W_n$. In the signed action
on $\{\pm1,\ldots,\pm n\}$, $v_i=1$ exchanges $i$ and $-i$.
The two retained symmetric-group inputs are stated precisely below.
An independent family means that no member belongs to the subgroup
generated by the others; it need not have the intersection property.

\begin{lemma}[The module and relative generation]\label{module}
For $n\ge3$, every $S_n$-invariant subspace of $E_n$ is either zero,
the constant line (when $n$ is even), or $E_n$.
If an independent family generates $W_n$, and $L$ is any inclusion-minimal
subfamily whose images generate $S_n$, at most one member lies outside $L$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If an invariant subspace contains a nonconstant vector $v$, choose
$i,j$ with $v_i\ne v_j$. Then $v+(i\ j)v=e_i+e_j$ belongs to it.
Conjugating gives every vector of weight two, which spans $E_n$.
The only nonzero constant vector is $\lambda=(1,\ldots,1)$, and
it belongs to $E_n$ exactly when $n$ is even.

For each generator $g$ outside $L$, choose a word $w_g$ in $L$
with the same image in $S_n$ and set $v_g=g w_g^{-1}\in E_n$.
The new list $L,(v_g)$ generates the same group. It remains independent
with respect to the entries $v_g$: if one belonged to the subgroup
generated by all other entries, then so would the corresponding $g$.
Let $K_g$ be generated by $L$ and all $v_h$ with $h\ne g$.
Its image in $S_n$ is full. Conjugation of $E_n$ by $K_g$ is consequently
the full $S_n$ action, since $E_n$ is abelian. If $v_g$ belonged to
the $S_n$-span of the other $v_h$, it would belong to $K_g$, a contradiction.
Thus the $v_g$ are independent under generation of $S_n$-submodules.
There can be at most one: a nonconstant one spans all of $E_n$, and
two nonzero constant ones would be equal. Zero entries are impossible.
\end{proof}

This is the normal-module normalization already used in the project's
common theory \emph{Forest coordinates and relative generation} and its
transitive-rank supplement. The proof is repeated here to make the
stronger consequence for this particular module explicit.

The retained Whiston bound says that an independent generating family
of $S_n$ has at most $n-1$ members. The acquired extremal string result
says that an independent generating family of $n-1$ involutions in
$S_n$ which satisfies the string commutations is a Coxeter path, up to
an automorphism of $S_n$, for $n\ge5$. For $n\ge7$ it follows from the
common Cameron--Cara classification: the sign modification of a tree
has a generator of order three, and the line graph of an ordinary
transposition tree satisfying the string condition must be a path.
The acquired complete $S_5,S_6$ certificate gives the two remaining
degrees, including the outer image of the path in $S_6$.
Neither input is inferred from a projection having the intersection property.

\begin{theorem}\label{bound}
Every independent generating family of $W_n$, $n\ge5$, has at most $n$
members. If $n$ is even, every such family of involutions satisfying
the string commutations has at most $n-1$ members.
In particular these bounds apply to every string C-marking, connected
or disconnected.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $L$ as in Lemma~\ref{module}. Its images are an independent
generating family of $S_n$, so $|L|\le n-1$. The lemma gives total
length at most $|L|+1\le n$.

Suppose $n$ is even and a string family has length $n$. Equality
forces $|L|=n-1$. Its images are nonidentity involutions and retain
the string commutations after the other position is deleted.
By the acquired extremal result they form a Coxeter path.
Every neighboring pair in this path fails to commute. Thus their
original positions were consecutive as well; the one omitted position
is an endpoint of the original string. Reverse that string if needed
and call its first, omitted generator $x$.

The element $x$ commutes with the last $n-2$ members of $L$.
Their images generate an endpoint-omission subgroup $Q\cong S_{n-1}$
of the Coxeter path. Its centralizer in $S_n$ is trivial, so $x$
has trivial image and is a nonzero vector in $E_n$. Commutation says
that this vector is constant on every $Q$-orbit. For the natural
path these orbits have sizes $1,n-1$. Since $n-1$ is odd, even weight
makes the two constants equal. For the outer $S_6$ path the endpoint
subgroup is transitive on six letters, so its invariant vectors are
also constant. These assertions for the two degree-six markings
follow directly from the explicit acquired permutation representatives.
Hence $x=\lambda$ is central in $W_n$.

Put $H=\gen L$. Since $H$ maps onto $S_n$, $H\cap E_n$ is an
$S_n$-submodule. Independence excludes $\lambda\in H$, while
Lemma~\ref{module} shows that every nonzero submodule contains
$\lambda$ when $n$ is even. Therefore $H\cap E_n=0$.
But $W_n=\gen{H,\lambda}=H\gen\lambda$ would then have kernel
$\gen\lambda$ over $S_n$, whereas its kernel $E_n$ has dimension
$n-1>1$. This contradiction proves the second assertion.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{bound} replaces the upper-rank argument in Chen--Rowley,
\emph{Abstract Regular Polytopes of Finite Irreducible Coxeter Groups},
EJC~32(4) (2025), P4.18, Theorem~12 and the upper bounds in
Theorems~16 and~21. It uses no transitive half-degree theorem, and
eliminates the source's separate case in which a minimal quotient
family has length $n-2$: the module normalization rules out that
case at once. Its retained Whiston and extremal inputs, including
the complete small certificate, remain mathematical dependencies.
