\section{A complete and small matrix boundary}
Put $Q=q^3$, $F=\mathbb F_Q$, $k=\mathbb F_q$ and
$J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
We use the actual automorphism $\sigma:X\mapsto X^{(q)}$.
The paid uniform proof in \emph{Absolute flags and their moving lines}
establishes full completion in both characteristics and the exact fixed
absolute. Projective-linear descent conjugates any source marking's
triality to $\sigma$ or $\sigma^{-1}$. Replacing it by its inverse does not
change either absolute. Its fixed middle involution can be conjugated to
the projective image of $J$ over $k$. In odd characteristic its determinant-one
lift is itself fixed by $\sigma$: a negative lift would remain negative after
three applications. The rational canonical form of this trace-zero matrix
gives the asserted conjugation. In characteristic two all nonidentity
unipotents are conjugate over the perfect field $k$.

Choose the unique sign of the lift $R$ of the first endpoint for which
\[
 \det R=1,\quad \operatorname{tr}R=0,\qquad
                     R^{\sigma^2}=RR^\sigma.               \tag{1}
\]
In odd characteristic the two lifts change the sign of the left side and
not of the right side, so exactly one works. In characteristic two there
is no sign choice. Thus every source marking occurs among the solutions
of (1), with $a=\overline R$, $b=\overline J$, $c=\overline{R^\sigma}$.

We retain in full the accepted three-page L023 matrix-equation proof.
It proves that the odd-characteristic solutions form one free
$\PGL_2(k)$ conjugacy orbit, of size $q(q^2-1)$, and that the nonscalar
even solutions consist of a $k$-rational fixed point and a nonzero
trace-zero parameter, of size $(q+1)(q^2-1)$. These are written exhaustion
theorems, not inferred from a successful search. The present program checks
its literal odd seed against (1), enumerates that complete orbit, and
enumerates all the even parameters. It then removes repetitions under
the projective centralizer of $J$ over $k$ and coordinate Frobenius.
Both operations preserve the resulting moving graph. Consequently this
reduction can establish nonexistence of a claimed graph as well as produce
witnesses. It is used exhaustively for $q=7$; the other table entries below
need only their displayed witnesses.

The five fields are given in the program by these explicit moduli; $z$
is the residue of the indeterminate:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
q&p&\text{modulus over }\mathbb F_p\\\hline
2&2&z^3+z+1\\
4&2&z^6+z^4+z^3+z+1\\
5&5&z^3+3z+3\\
7&7&z^3+6z^2+4\\
9&3&z^6+2z^4+z^2+2z+2
\end{array}
\]
The program checks that successive powers of $z$ visit every nonzero
residue once and return to one. This also proves that every nonzero
residue is a unit, hence that the quotient ring is a field. No irreducibility
or primitive-polynomial database is an unchecked premise.

\section{Exact construction and finite trust boundary}
Let $H_0=\gen{R,J}$, $V=\gen{R,R^\sigma}$ in the determinant-one matrix
group, including its scalar center, and let $H=\mathrm{SL}_2(k)$.
The uniform fixed-absolute theorem proves that all absolute right cosets
of $H_0$ are among
\[
                   H_0h,\quad H_0R^\sigma h\qquad(h\in H). \tag{2}
\]
The program forms each whole coset, identifies duplicates by its actual
matrices, and enumerates all original edges at these vertices. An edge
represented by $Vx$ has other endpoint $H_0R^\sigma x$ and is fixed
exactly when $x^\sigma x^{-1}\in V$. It is retained as moving precisely
when its two endpoints belong to (2) and it is not fixed. Distinct edge
cosets are checked against their endpoint pairs, so parallel edges cannot
silently disappear. Isolated vertices are then removed exactly as specified
in the source. The matrices, whole cosets, adjacency lists and edge-fixedness
tests are exact finite-field operations.

For every displayed witness, and all seven $q=7$ representatives,
generation of $\PSL_2(Q)$ can also be read from the marked period $m$.
Usually $m=(Q-1)/\gcd(2,Q-1)$ or $(Q+1)/\gcd(2,Q-1)$, so the two-generator
dihedral is a full torus normalizer. The retained Dickson classification
makes it maximal here. The generated group is triality-stable and contains
a Klein four subgroup cycled by the triality. It cannot be dihedral:
an odd rotation subgroup admits no Klein four, whereas an even one makes
each Klein four contain the characteristic central involution, contradicting
that cycle. The single remaining $q=7$ period is $86$. Its prime factor
$43$ excludes the Borel, subfield and exceptional maximal subgroups of
$\PSL_2(343)$ by their orders; the remaining possible dihedral containment
is excluded by the same central-involution argument. This is a named use
of Dickson's classification, not a new subgroup classification.

Finally these generating markings have no marked duality. If an automorphism
fixed $b$ and interchanged $a,c$, it and the displayed triality would induce
the faithful $S_3$ on the three nonidentity elements of $V$. Since the markings
generate, the corresponding relations hold as actual automorphisms of $G$.
Their commutator is the noninner triality or its inverse. This is impossible
in the retained abelian outer automorphism group of $\PSL_2(Q)$.
Thus every displayed witness meets source condition (5), not merely the
involution and generation conditions.

The full required programs are \texttt{finite\_graphs.cpp} and
\texttt{graph\_certificate.py}; the literal automorphism generators are
required data. The first produces the complete relevant marking lists and
coset graphs. The second checks adjacency, distances, components and full
automorphism orders. These are finite mathematical premises. Their successful
termination and ordinary exact arithmetic are trusted; no kernel-checked
exhaustion or formally verified compiler is claimed. A separate GAP field,
group and coset reconstruction agrees with all selected graphs and all seven
$q=7$ cases; it is a cross-check, not a substitute for the written exhaustion.

\section{Full automorphism certificates}
For a graph, partition vertices by any stated intrinsic invariants, then
repeatedly refine each cell by the numbers of neighbors in every current
cell. Every automorphism preserves this partition. Individualize a vertex
in a nonsingleton cell of size $b$ and refine again. The stabilizer index
is at most $b$. If this process ends in singletons, the product of these
cell sizes is an upper bound on the full automorphism group. This argument
does not assume that a refinement cell is an actual orbit.

The certificate verifies literal permutation generators against every
edge and closes the small generated groups exactly. Matching upper and
lower bounds prove fullness. For the connected cubic 90-vertex graph,
the initial invariant is the number of five-cycles through a vertex: its
cells have sizes 30 and 60. All other initial partitions are indiscrete.
The certificate's stabilizer-index bounds are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|r}
\text{graph}&\text{refinement factors}&|\operatorname{Aut}|\\\hline
6\text{-vertex prism}&6\cdot2&12\\
90\text{-vertex fixed--moving union}&30\cdot2\cdot2&120\\
30\text{-vertex moving}&30\cdot4\cdot2&240\\
60\text{-vertex moving}&60\cdot2&120\\
168\text{-vertex moving}&168\cdot2^{29}&90\,194\,313\,216\\
180\text{-vertex moving}&180\cdot2\cdot4&1440
\end{array}
\]
For the large order no enormous permutation closure is required. Every
vertex lies in a unique triangle; the 56 triangles occur in 28 intrinsic
pairs with identical neighborhoods in their triangle quotient. Each pair
supports a literal six-point interchange fixing every other vertex.
The certificate verifies all 28 interchanges. Their disjoint supports give
an elementary abelian group of order $2^{28}$ in the kernel of the action
on the 28 six-point blocks. The supplied graph automorphisms induce a
group of order 336 on those blocks, checked by direct closure. Thus the
full group has order at least $2^{28}\cdot336$, exactly the refinement
upper bound. In particular it is not isomorphic to $\PSL_2(7)$.

For each claimed $C_2\times A_5$ identification the certificate finds a
center of order two and a derived subgroup of order 60, with trivial
intersection. The derived subgroup acts faithfully on its five Klein four
subgroups, identifying it with $A_5$. Their orders prove the direct product.
For the 240-group it additionally finds an involution extending the derived
subgroup to a subgroup of order 120, disjoint from the center and acting
faithfully on the same five Klein groups. This subgroup is $S_5$, proving
the direct product $C_2\times S_5$. The finite group identifications therefore
do not rest on a database's abstract-name output.

The 60-vertex graph is vertex-transitive with full group $C_2\times A_5$;
the 30-vertex graph is arc-transitive with full group $C_2\times S_5$.
The latter's incidence point and line eccentricities are 7 and 8, and

two-edge paths, and its moving graph is the source's triangular prism.
There is one generating marking class under semilinear conjugacy. This
is the precise conjugacy convention verified here; it is not a claim of
one ordered inner-conjugacy class.

The $q=4$ paragraph conflates two graphs. Its moving graph really is
$15K_2\sqcup12C_5$, with degrees one and two, and 75 edges. Its full
automorphism group has order
\[
                        2^{15}\,15!\;10^{12}\,12!,
\]
by permuting and independently acting on its connected components.
It is disconnected, so has no finite diameter. On the same 90 absolute
vertices, \emph{adding the 60 fixed edges} gives a connected cubic graph
with 135 edges, girth five, diameter seven and full group $C_2\times A_5$.
Thus the later properties in the printed paragraph are recovered for the
fixed--moving union, not falsely attributed to the moving graph.

For $q=7$, all 336 normalized solutions of (1) reduce to seven classes
under the certified graph-preserving operations. Each generates the full

\[
84K_2,\quad\varnothing,\quad42C_4,\quad
\text{the connected 168-vertex graph},\quad21C_{12},\quad
42D,\quad84P_3,                                             \tag{3}
\]
where $D$ is the six-vertex tree obtained by joining the centers of two

does not occur for any marking satisfying the source hypotheses. This
is an exhaustive negative conclusion from (1)--(3), not a failure to find
a witness in a sample. The 168-vertex degree-four graph has the stated
connectedness, girth and diameter, but its asserted full automorphism
group is corrected by the exact certificate above. These two source
claims are therefore explicitly refuted in their literal stated scope.
