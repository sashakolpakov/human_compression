\begin{proposition}[Checking a coset graph]
Let $X$ be a connected graph with one adjacency $x\mapsto x^i$ of each
colour $i$ at each vertex, and choose $x_0\in X$. Choose permutations
$g_x\in W$ by following a breadth-first traversal from $g_{x_0}=1$.
Suppose that
\[
 |X|\,|N|=|W|,
 \qquad g_xr_i g_{x^i}^{-1}\in N\quad(x\in X,\ 0\le i<d).
\]
Then $x\mapsto Ng_x$ is an isomorphism from the coloured graph $X$ to
the right coset graph of $N$ in $W$ with the given generators.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The displayed membership is exactly the equality
$Ng_xr_i=Ng_{x^i}$. Thus the map preserves every adjacency and sends
$x_0$ to $N$. Every element of $W$ is a word in the marked generators.
Following that word from $x_0$ shows that every coset occurs in the image.
The cardinality equality makes this surjective map bijective.
\end{proof}
The checker enumerates $N$ from its literal generators and verifies its
claimed order. It constructs every $g_x$ as a word in the original marks
and checks every displayed equality. The value of $|W|$ is acquired from
the preceding exact group calculation. An abstract database identifier
is not used in this argument.

\begin{proposition}[Reusing a verified flag graph]
In a connected graph with specified colours and one adjacency of each
colour, an isomorphism is determined by the image of one vertex. Label
the vertices in breadth-first order, always exploring colours in their
specified order, and record the resulting adjacency array. Two rooted
graphs have the same array if and only if the traversal orders give a
colour-preserving isomorphism between them.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An isomorphism must send each path from the root to the path with the
same colours from its image. Consequently it preserves the traversal
order and recorded array. Conversely equality of the arrays makes the
bijection between the two traversal orders preserve every coloured edge.
\end{proof}
Each of the 56 acquired chiral flag graphs has exactly two automorphism
orbits, represented by a chosen vertex and one of its adjacent vertices.
The 112 corresponding arrays therefore suffice for comparison with
every possible root image. Every successful comparison also supplies
the full vertex bijection, and every edge of that bijection is checked.
The acquired polytope axioms, chirality and group identifications then
transfer through this explicit isomorphism.

For an unmatched graph, omit colour $i$ to obtain its prospective
rank-$i$ faces as connected components. In an actual polytope these
are precisely the faces containing the flags in those components.
Two particular necessary conditions suffice for all unmatched graphs
in this calculation. First, each interval between ranks $i-1$ and $i+1$
has exactly two intermediate faces, with the least and greatest faces
included at the boundary ranks. Second, flags containing the same fixed
chain are connected by adjacencies that change none of those faces.
The first condition is the diamond axiom; the second is strong flag
connectedness. A failure is recorded by the actual face labels and
representative flags, or by two flags in distinct allowed components
with the same fixed faces. These are necessary conditions in every
rank; passing them alone is not used as a positive certificate.
