Let $G=\HS$, of order $44\,352\,000$, in the acquired faithful
permutation action on $\Omega=\{1,\ldots,100\}$. Let $N$ be the
acquired permutation group inducing the complete $\Aut(G)$, of order
$88\,704\,000$, with $C_N(G)=1$. We use the fixed representatives
in \texttt{full\_fusion03/result.g}, their already established geometry
and flag-transitivity properties, and their actual parabolic subgroups
$P_1,\ldots,P_r$. Identifiers below refer to that file, not to historical
diagram numbers. Automorphisms preserve every type; correlations may
permute types. Put $B=\bigcap_iP_i$ and $A=\Aut(G)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{main}
Under the acquired finite-group inputs and the exact graph-automorphism

\section*{Restriction to point shadows}
For each type $a$ of size $100$, the actual subgroup $P_a$ fixes exactly
one point $u_a\in\Omega$. Its order equals $|G|/100$, so it is exactly
$G_{u_a}$. Thus $P_ag\mapsto u_a^g$ is a bijection from that type to
$\Omega$. For the base object of type $j$, its shadow on type $a$ is
$u_a^{P_j}$. This follows directly from right-coset incidence:
the incident objects of type $a$ are $P_ap$ for $p\in P_j$.

\begin{lemma}\label{faithful}
Let a transitive type have base stabilizer $P$. An equivariant encoding
of its objects is injective if and only if the stabilizer in $G$ of its
base encoding is $P$. Once certain types are pointwise fixed by an
automorphism, so are all objects encoded injectively by their shadows
or by their sets of neighbours in those types.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An equivariant encoding is the map from $P\backslash G$ to the orbit
of its base value. Its fibres have size $[G_{\mathrm{value}}:P]$.
An automorphism fixing the encoded types fixes every such set or tuple
of their objects, and therefore every object having a unique encoding.
This also proves the iterative assertion.
\end{proof}

The literal calculation checks that every $100$-object type is determined
by its shadow on any other such type, including itself. For all other
types start with the ordered tuple $(u_a^{P_j})_a$. Its stabilizer is
the intersection of the set stabilizers. Fifteen families already have
every object determined this way. In the others, adjoin sets of encoded
neighbours from already identified types. If type $k$ is identified,
the neighbours of the base type-$j$ object are precisely the orbit of
its base type-$k$ encoding under $P_j$. The program checks the orbit
length $[P_j:P_j\cap P_k]$, then intersects the current encoding
stabilizer with its set stabilizer. Only a type whose resulting
stabilizer equals its actual $P_j$ is admitted for use in later steps.
There are $98$ types in total. All are identified. Representative $40$
needs two successive neighbour steps; no unproved direct-shadow
injectivity is used there. Lemma~\ref{faithful} proves faithful
restriction of the full typed automorphism group to each of the $38$
possible $100$-object anchors.

\section*{The intrinsic graph bound}
The orbits of a point stabilizer on $\Omega$ have sizes $1,22,77$.
Transport its orbit of size $22$ to obtain a simple undirected graph
$\Delta$ of degree $22$. The two unordered pair orbits have sizes
$1100$ and $3850$. These assertions are checked in the actual action.
For a type-$j$ shadow $S=u_a^{P_j}$ let $e$ be the number of edges of
$\Delta$ inside $S$, and put $f=\binom{|S|}{2}-e$. Double counting
pairs and incident objects gives the two common-incidence counts
\[
 \frac{[G:P_j]e}{1100},\qquad
 \frac{[G:P_j]f}{3850}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
This formula counts objects, so repeated point shadows cause no error.
For every anchor at least one type has unequal counts in (1).
Consequently the vector of common-incidence counts over all types
partitions the distinct point pairs into exactly these two orbits.
An arbitrary correlation permutes the coordinates of this vector and
maps an anchor to another $100$-object type. The two pair relations
have different valencies, so the degree-$22$ relation is preserved.
Thus every correlation induces an isomorphism of the corresponding
copies of $\Delta$.

The complete nauty computation for this single $100$-vertex graph gives
order $88\,704\,000$. Every returned generator and every acquired
generator of $N$ is checked on all adjacencies. Hence the full graph
group is exactly $N$. The literal permutation calculation also checks
$N'=G$; in particular $G$ is characteristic in the graph group.
For any correlation $c$ and any $g\in G$, its induced point action
therefore makes $cgc^{-1}$ equal on the target anchor to some $h\in G$.
The typed automorphism $h^{-1}cgc^{-1}$ is trivial on that anchor.
Faithfulness from Lemma~\ref{faithful} makes it trivial everywhere.
We have proved that every full correlation normalizes the specified $G$.

\section*{Exact lifts and the group structures}
Write $S$ and $T$ for the ordered and setwise stabilizers, respectively,
of $(P_1,\ldots,P_r)$ in the complete group $N=\Aut(G)$.
An element $t\in T$ with $P_i^t=P_{\pi(i)}$ acts by
\[
             P_i g\longmapsto P_{\pi(i)}g^t .                \tag{2}
\]
This is well defined and preserves incidence in both directions.
It fixes the base chamber setwise. Conversely, a correlation normalizing
$G$ can first be moved by $G$ so that it fixes that chamber. Its induced
automorphism of $G$ then belongs to $T$; faithfulness of the action and
the coset description give (2). The ordered analogue gives $S$.
Consequently
\[
 |\Aut(\Gamma)|=|G:B||S|,\qquad
 |\Cor(\Gamma)|=|G:B||T|.                                  \tag{3}
\]
The complete type-lift calculation tests every type permutation, using
subgroup orders and orders of pairwise intersections only to reject impossible cases.
For each remaining permutation it successively transports actual
subgroups in $N$, replacing the transporter group by the stabilizer
of each target already placed. The transporter-coset induction proves
that this loses no solution. Every positive witness is checked on the
whole ordered family. The group generated by $S$ and these witnesses
has order $|S|$ times the number of successful type permutations, and
stabilizes the family. It is therefore exactly $T$.

For completeness, let $C=C_{\Cor(\Gamma)}(G)$. Centralizing correlations
are the maps obtained from inner elements $t\in T\cap G$ in (2),
followed by the inverse right action of $t$. Their kernel is $B$, and
all centralizing correlations arise this way by moving the image chamber
back. Thus $C\cong (T\cap G)/B$ (an opposite-group convention gives the
same isomorphism). Direct calculations give $S\cap G=B$, so $C$ meets
the typed automorphism group trivially. The quotient above is always
trivial or of order two; its actual quotient generators are retained.
The conjugation image of $\Cor(\Gamma)$ in $\Aut(G)$ has order
$|\Cor(\Gamma)|/|C|$, hence is $G$ or $A$.

If $C=1$, this identifies the full group with that image. If $|C|=2$,
it is central in the full group, and in every such row the typed
automorphism group has order $|\Cor(\Gamma)|/2$. Their trivial
intersection and full product order give the direct product asserted
in Theorem~\ref{main}. In particular the factor $C_2$ is not inferred
merely from a doubled cardinality. The exceptional row $67$ has
typed group $G$, trivial $C$, and full conjugation image $A$.

\paragraph{Inputs, attribution and remaining scope.}
The acquired complete HS action, family enumeration, and its exact
Aut/type quotient are explicit premises. GAP's finite permutation
and subgroup algorithms and nauty's complete graph-automorphism
algorithm are retained computational inputs. The scripts retain the
literal shadow encodings, positive type lifts and every rejected type
permutation; no subgroup or geometry census is repeated.
Small literal incidence controls test restriction, iterative shadows,
type interchanges and a nonfaithful duplicate-shadow example.
The shadow and transporter arguments are elementary and overlap the
accepted common incidence theory. The historical L096 naming treatment
retained the source's full typed-group assertion; it is not an upper-bound
premise here. This component supplies a new check of the required bounds,
not a novelty claim for the original results.

Representatives $14,15,35,36$ have separate acquired full-group proofs.
The remaining $58$ representatives are outside the present result.
All $86$ source-diagram identifications and the global source synthesis
remain separate. The source is D.~Leemans, \emph{The residually weakly
primitive geometries of HS}, Australasian Journal of Combinatorics
33 (2005), 231--236, together with the explicitly retained 6 October
2003 supplementary diagrams. No whole-source, measurement, formalization
or new-question credit is claimed by this component.
