\begin{proposition}
The displayed geometries 3.1, 3.2 and 4.1 are residually primitive
for the specified $G$ action: every nonempty type of every residue
carries a primitive action of its flag stabilizer.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $I=\{1,\ldots,r\}$, $H_J=\bigcap_{j\in J}P_j$, with
$H_\varnothing=G$. Flag transitivity, acquired for each literal
geometry, transports every flag of type $J$ to the base flag. For
$i\notin J$, its incident objects of type $i$ form the transitive
$H_J$-set with point stabilizer $H_{J\cup\{i\}}$. A transitive coset
action is primitive exactly when this stabilizer is a maximal proper
subgroup. The statement does not require the action to be faithful:
subgroups between a point stabilizer and the acting group correspond
to blocks through its base point, and quotienting by the action kernel
does not change that interval.

For each required inclusion $L=H_{J\cup\{i\}}<H=H_J$, the certificate
retains an index $a$ in the acquired 589-class roster and $x\in G$ with
$H^x=K_a$. It also retains an actual maximal-inclusion row $(K_a,M)$
and $y\in K_a$ with $(L^x)^y=M$. Both equalities, both element
memberships, and proper containment are verified literally. Since
$y$ normalizes $K_a$, conjugation by $xy$ identifies $(H,L)$ with
$(K_a,M)$. The acquired maximality of $M$ in $K_a$ proves the result.
The checker enumerates every proper $J\subset I$ and every $i\notin J$,
including $J=\varnothing$ and rank-one residues. Hence there are
$r2^{r-1}$ inclusions: twelve each for representatives 36 and 35
(diagrams 3.1 and 3.2), and thirty-two for representative 77 (4.1).
All fifty-six have positive literal transports. This pays the all-type
claim, which is stronger than the acquired weakly primitive condition.
\end{proof}
