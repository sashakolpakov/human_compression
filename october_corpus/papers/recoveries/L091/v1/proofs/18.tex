Let $G=\mathrm{HS}$ in the acquired faithful action of degree $100$.
We retain its complete automorphism group $N$, of order $88\,704\,000$,
and the accepted list of coset geometries. Representatives below refer
to that list. The source labels refer to the supplementary diagrams
accompanying Leemans, \emph{The residually weakly primitive geometries
of HS}, Australasian Journal of Combinatorics 33 (2005), 231--236.
Their identification with the acquired representatives is separately reviewed.

The assertion uses the acquired finite group model and the complete

\begin{lemma}
Let a flag-transitive coset geometry have a faithful specified group
$G$, with parabolics $P_i$. Select all types whose cardinalities belong
to a fixed set. Suppose every object is determined uniquely by the
tuple of its shadows on the selected types. Suppose the induced
incidence system on those types has full correlation group $D$, and
the image of $G$ in $D$ is characteristic. Then every correlation
of the original geometry normalizes its specified $G$ action.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The selected types form an intrinsic collection, so a correlation $c$
restricts to a correlation of the induced system. For $g\in G$,
characteristicity gives $h\in G$ whose restriction agrees with that
of $cgc^{-1}$. The typed automorphism $h^{-1}cgc^{-1}$ fixes all
selected objects. It fixes the shadow tuple of every remaining object
and hence, by uniqueness, fixes that object. Thus $cgc^{-1}=h$.
\end{proof}
The argument is the same restriction argument used in the separately
reviewed component for the geometries having a type of size $100$.
Here we select every type of size $176$ or $1100$. This selection,
including repeated cardinalities, is checked against the actual subgroup
indices. No type is selected by an arbitrary historical label.

\paragraph{Literal coset and shadow identifications.}
Write $X$ for the union of the selected types. The certificate gives
permutations on $X$ for the ordered generators of the actual $G$.
Their homomorphism is checked and is bijective onto its image $E$.
For each selected type the specified base vertex has stabilizer exactly
the image of $P_i$, and its orbit is the full type. Thus
$P_i g\mapsto b_i^g$ is an actual coset bijection.
For every ordered pair of selected types, the neighbours of $b_i$
are checked to be precisely $b_j^{P_i}$. Every generator preserves
every incidence and nonincidence row. Equivariance therefore proves
the full incidence identification, not only agreement of local degrees.

The shadow of the base object of an arbitrary type $j$ is the tuple
$(b_i^{P_j})_i$. Its setwise tuple stabilizer in $E$ is checked to be
exactly the image of $P_j$, in all nineteen types of these five geometries.
The orbit map then has fibres of size one. This proves the uniqueness
required by the lemma, without constructing all objects of the larger types.

\paragraph{The complete upper bounds.}
The graph used for the induced incidence system has one marker for
each selected type, joined to all objects of that type. Objects are
coloured by their type cardinality. Markers have separate colours,
also grouped by cardinality. A graph automorphism therefore permutes
whole types of equal cardinality; conversely every correlation of the
induced system extends uniquely to the markers. In particular, repeated
types are not artificially distinguished by separate colours.

For representatives $34,48,51,61,62$ the graph sizes, including markers,
are respectively $2379,1278,2379,1278,1278$. Complete nauty computations
give orders $88\,704\,000$ in the first case and $44\,352\,000$ in
the other four. All returned generators and all specified generators
of $G$ are checked on every coloured adjacency row. The group generated
by the returned permutations has the reported order and has derived
subgroup exactly $E$. Thus $E$ is characteristic in each full graph
group, and the lemma applies.

\paragraph{From normalizers to full groups.}
We now use the previously reviewed coset normalizer argument. Put
$B=\bigcap_iP_i$; let $S$ and $T$ be the ordered and setwise family
stabilizers in $N=\Aut(G)$. Once every full correlation normalizes $G$,
flag transitivity and the base chamber give
\[
 |\Aut(\Gamma)|=|G:B||S|,\qquad
 |\Cor(\Gamma)|=|G:B||T|.
\]
The calculation checks every type permutation. It discards a permutation
only when subgroup orders, intersection orders, or a complete successive
subgroup transporter proves it impossible. Every surviving transporter
is checked on the entire family. This is exactly the already reviewed
method, applied to five new literal families.

The resulting values are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{crrrr}\toprule
Representative&$|B|$&$|S|$&$|T|$&$|(T\cap G)/B|$\\\midrule
34&720&720&1440&2\\
48&4&4&4&1\\
51&6&6&6&1\\
61&8&8&8&1\\
62&8&8&8&1\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In every row $S\cap G=B$. Consequently the typed group is exactly
the specified $G$. For row $34$, the centralizer of $G$ in the full
correlation group is $(T\cap G)/B$, of order two. It is central and
meets the typed group trivially. Their product has the full order,
proving $\Cor(\Gamma)\cong G\times C_2$. The other four full groups
already have order $|G|$ and hence equal $G$.

\paragraph{Verification and scope.}
The verifier checks actual stabilizers, base incidences and all graph
generators; it does not repeat an ambient subgroup or geometry census.


The argument reuses elementary coset, shadow and normalizer ideas already
established in the project. The new contribution here is their explicit
application and the finite identifications. GAP's exact finite group
algorithms and nauty's completeness are computational premises; no Lean
verification, novelty, conjecture resolution or whole-manuscript
completion is asserted. The other source obligations remain separate.
