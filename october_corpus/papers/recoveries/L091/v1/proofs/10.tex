\begin{lemma}[Literal generator-edge transport]
Let $M=\langle s_1,\ldots,s_t\rangle$ and $U=\langle u_1,\ldots,u_v\rangle$
be finite permutation groups. Suppose a map $f:M\longrightarrow U$ is a
bijection, $f(1)=1$, and for fixed $h_i\in U$ it satisfies
$f(xs_i)=f(x)h_i$ for every $x\in M$ and every $i$. Then $f$ is an
isomorphism carrying $s_i$ to $h_i$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}

groups are finite, inverse powers can also be replaced by positive powers.
Induction on a word for $y$ gives $f(xy)=f(x)f(y)$. Thus $f$ is a bijective
homomorphism. This is the acquired marked-coordinate transport argument,
here applied to the small literal subgroup closures.
\end{proof}
