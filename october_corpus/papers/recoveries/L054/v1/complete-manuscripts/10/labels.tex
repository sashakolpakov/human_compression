\begin{lemma}
Let $H=\langle x,y\rangle$ have order $27$, and put
$z=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$. Suppose $z$ is central of order $3$, $x^3,y^3\in
\langle z\rangle$, and $Z(H)=\langle z\rangle$. Then $H$ is extraspecial.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The quotient by $Z=\langle z\rangle$ is generated by two commuting
order-dividing-three elements and has order nine, so it is $C_3^2$.
Since $z$ is a commutator and this quotient is abelian, $H'=Z$.
Every maximal subgroup contains $Z$. Indeed, a subgroup of order nine
not containing $Z$ would have product with $Z$ equal to $H$; that subgroup
is abelian, and $Z$ is central, contradicting $H'\ne1$. A subgroup of
order three not containing $Z$ extends to its proper order-nine product
with $Z$, so cannot be maximal. Consequently the maximal subgroups are
exactly the inverse images of the four lines in $H/Z$. Their intersection
is $Z$, proving $\Phi(H)=Z$. The elementary fact that groups of order
$p^2$ are abelian follows from the class equation and the fact that a group
with cyclic central quotient is abelian.
\end{proof}
