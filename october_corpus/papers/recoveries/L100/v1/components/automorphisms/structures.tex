This note identifies the groups attached to the 29 geometries in the fixed
22-page Halle preprint of Gottschalk and Leemans \cite{GL}. It acquires the
complete literal coset models, their proved source-label identifications, and
their complete palette automorphism groups. It does not repeat the ambient
subgroup or geometry census or the GRAPE/nauty computation. The completeness
of the latter groups remains an explicit acquired computational premise.
The journal edition has not been independently collated.

\paragraph{Notation and acquired actions.}
Put $G=\PSL_3(4)$, in the retained 21-point action of order 20160.
Its two actual generators, all its elements, and their actions on every
coset model are acquired literally. Each palette graph has the objects and
one additional vertex per type; objects and palette vertices have separate
colours. Its full colour-preserving automorphisms are precisely $\Cor(\Gamma)$;
the subgroup fixing every palette vertex is $\Aut(\Gamma)$. These statements
use the accepted palette lemma, rather than the uncoloured incidence graph.
Both groups contain the faithful embedded $G$.

On coordinates over $\mathbb F_4$, let $\delta$ be conjugation by
$\operatorname{diag}(\omega,1,1)$, $\sigma$ the field map $a\mapsto a^2$,
and $\tau$ the inverse-transpose map induced by ordinary projective polarity.
The explicit coordinate-to-retained-point bijection is acquired. In particular
$\tau$ uses the bilinear dot product, not a Hermitian polarity.
The twelve maps $\delta^i\sigma^j\tau^k$ form an actual subgroup
\[
 O=\langle\delta,\sigma,\tau\rangle\cong S_3\times C_2,
 \qquad 0\leq i<3,\quad 0\leq j,k<2.
\]
Indeed, both involutions invert $\delta$ and commute with each other.
The certificate checks the complete multiplication table on the two
generators of $G$. It checks that all 241920 maps obtained by following one
of these twelve maps by conjugation by an element of $G$ have distinct
generator-image pairs. Thus $O$ intersects the inner group trivially.
The separately acquired complete computation $|\Aut(G)|=241920$ identifies
this explicit subgroup with all of $\Aut(G)$; the order is not inferred
from the geometry table.

Define the following precise names:
\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 \Sigma=G\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle,&
 T=G\rtimes\langle\tau\rangle,&
 U=G\rtimes\langle\sigma\tau\rangle,\\
 \Gamma=G\rtimes\langle\delta,\sigma\rangle,&
 V=G\rtimes\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle,& A=\Aut(G).
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{1}
\]
Here $\Sigma$ is the field-extension meaning of $P\Sigma L_3(4)$,
explicitly documented by the primary Magma handbook \cite{Magma},
$\Gamma=P\Gamma L_3(4)$, and $V$ supplies the explicit meaning of the
source's $P\Sigma L_3(4):2$ in this table. Outer subgroups conjugate by
$O$ give the same names, with an actual conjugating element recorded.

\begin{lemma}
For every saved typed or correlation group $H$, the certificate determines
its conjugation image in $\Aut(G)$, its kernel, and an explicit splitting.
It proves $H\cong G\rtimes E$ or $C_2\times(G\rtimes E)$, for the
recorded subgroup $E\leq O$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose the smallest object type. Traverse the already known generators
simultaneously on $G$ and on this type. The 20160 restricted permutations
are distinct, so this action is faithful. For each actual palette generator
$q$ and each generator $g$ of $G$, look up the restriction of $q^{-1}gq$.
Then reconstruct the resulting element's action using its saved generator
word and check equality on \emph{every} object and palette vertex.
Consequently every $q$ normalizes the embedded $G$; restricted equality
alone is never used as the conclusion. Its two generator images have a
unique decomposition into an inner map and one of the twelve coordinate
maps. The small quotient multiplication table determines $E$ exactly.

The conjugation image contains all inner maps and projects onto $E$,
so it is the entire preimage $G\rtimes E$, of order $20160|E|$.
The acquired complete order of $H$ therefore gives its kernel order,
which is one or two in these 58 instances. This use of order follows
the explicit image identification; it does not identify a group by order.

Choose short words in the actual generators lifting every element of $E$.
Correct each lift by its exact inner factor, giving $q_e$ whose conjugation
on $G$ is the specified coordinate map $e$. All products
$q_e q_f q_{ef}^{-1}$ are checked literally. Discrepancies between the
original generators and these normalized lifts are also checked. In the
kernel-two cases their nonidentity values exhibit a single permutation $z$.
The certificate verifies $z^2=1$, $z\ne1$, and commutation with every
generator of $H$ and $G$. These relations detect the whole kernel: otherwise
the normalized lifts together with $G$ would already express every original
generator with trivial kernel, contradicting the acquired order.

Write the multiplication discrepancies as $z^{c(e,f)}$. Solve the finite
binary system
\[
 b(e)+b(f)+b(ef)=c(e,f)\pmod2.
\]
The certificate then checks all products of the actual corrected permutations
$s_e=q_ez^{b(e)}$, including $s_1=1$. Thus these permutations form an
actual homomorphic section. The subgroup generated by $G$ and the $s_e$
has the faithful conjugation image $G\rtimes E$ and has trivial intersection
with $\langle z\rangle$. Together they generate $H$, proving the direct
product assertion. In particular a central kernel of size two is never
assumed to split without this section certificate.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Two precise name corrections.}
The preprint prints $P\Sigma L_3(4)$ for both $\Aut(3.5)$ on page 9 and
$\Cor(4.11)$ on page 12, as it also does for groups of field type elsewhere.
With the explicit field convention in (1), the first two names require
replacement by $U$ and $T$, respectively. This is not merely a relabelling
of abstractly isomorphic groups. The acquired 20160-element list contains
315 involutions of $G$. For an involutory coordinate automorphism $a$,
the involutions in its outer coset correspond exactly to
\[
 \{h\in G:h\,a(h)=1\}.
\]
Literal tests over this complete finite set give 120, 280 and 336 for
$a=\sigma,\sigma\tau,\tau$, respectively. Thus $\Sigma,U,T$ have
435, 595 and 651 involutions and are pairwise nonisomorphic. Even if one
leaves the source's ATLAS-notation convention uninterpreted, its identical
printed name cannot denote all three groups consistently. The unqualified
$L_3(4):2$ at 2.4 is instead refined to $U$, not declared erroneous.
All group orders remain unchanged, and these auxiliary name corrections
do not refute the principal geometry classification.
