This note specializes the previously accepted Topic D arguments: anchored
Helly, panel recognition, subgroup transport and maximal augmentation.
The printed augmentation theorem there imposes
local two-transitivity. Here that extra restriction is removed explicitly;
primitivity alone is used. The exact general double-coset test already present
in the phase-six and phase-seven programs is retained. No new enumeration of
the $\operatorname{PSL}(3,4)$ geometries is asserted in this note.

All cosets below are right cosets, with incidence defined by nonempty
intersection. Inverting every group element converts the source's left-coset
convention to this convention and preserves incidence. Geometries are firm
when each panel has at least two objects, residually connected, and
flag-transitive under the specified acting group. RWPRI means that in each
positive-rank residue the specified residue stabilizer acts primitively on
at least one type. The rank-two axiom $\ip$ means that each rank-two residue
is a partial linear space or a generalized digon. A local action may have a
kernel; subgroup maximality still characterizes its primitivity.

\begin{theorem}[Broad primitive panel recognition]
Fix a finite group $G$, $B\leq G$ and distinct subgroups $P_i>B$ indexed by
a finite set $I$. Put $K_\varnothing=B$ and
$K_S=\langle B,P_i:i\in S\rangle$. The family defines a firm,
flag-transitive, residually connected RWPRI $\ip$ geometry for its acting
group $K_I$, with chamber stabilizer $B$, precisely when:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K_S\cap K_T=K_{S\cap T}$ for all $S,T\subseteq I$;
\item for every nonempty $S$, some $K_{S\setminus\{i\}}$ is maximal
proper in $K_S$;
\item for every $|S|\geq3$, the deletion parabolics
$(K_{S\setminus\{i\}})_{i\in S}$ have typed coset Helly;
\item for every two-element $S$, the corresponding coset graph is a
partial linear space or a generalized digon.
\end{enumerate}
Conversely every geometry with these properties arises from its actual
panel stabilizers in this way.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first condition, together with proper panels and the top Helly condition,
is the accepted panel recognition theorem. It identifies the stabilizer of the complementary
base flag with $K_S$, and identifies each residue with the corresponding
coset geometry inside $K_S$. The singleton cases of the second condition
give proper maximal inclusions $B<P_i$ and hence firmness. All other cases
of that condition give exactly RWPRI, by the block--subgroup correspondence
for a transitive coset action; its kernel does not affect that correspondence.
The fourth condition gives exactly $\ip$ in the identified residues.

For necessity, use actual flag stabilizers. Their intersections give the
first condition. The stabilizer of a residue is generated by its panel
stabilizers: in rank two this is the coset-path connectedness argument; in
higher rank, connectedness and induction first give generation by the
maximal parabolics, then by their panel stabilizers. Equivalently one can
lift an incidence path to adjacent chambers, using the already connected
lower-rank residues. This retains the common chamber stabilizer $B$ at every
step. Flag transitivity gives typed Helly, and every residue inherits all
four conditions. Thus no local two-transitivity premise is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The general rank-two test]
Let $B=P\cap Q$ and $K=\langle P,Q\rangle$. The coset graph is a digon
if and only if $|P||Q|=|K||B|$. Otherwise it is a partial linear space if
and only if, for every nontrivial double coset $BxB$ of $B$ in $Q$,
\[
 \left|\{Qh:h\in P\}\cap\{Qhx:h\in P\}\right|=1.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The neighbours of $P$ are the $Qh$ with $h\in P$; their number is
$[P:B]$. This equals the entire number $[K:Q]$ of opposite objects
precisely in the displayed digon case. Otherwise a quadrangle exists
precisely when two distinct $P$-type objects have two common neighbours.
Translate one common neighbour to $Q$, and then translate one of the two
objects to $P$ by an element of $Q$. The other is $Px$ for $x\in Q\setminus B$.
The remaining stabilizer of the base incident pair is $B$, and its orbits
on these objects are the double cosets $B\backslash Q/B$. The two neighbour
sets are the displayed sets. They already share $Q$, so the required bound
of at most one is exactly equality with one. This treats every double
coset, without a two-transitivity shortcut.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete primitive augmentation]
Assume representatives of every $G$-conjugacy class of subgroups are supplied,
together with representatives of every $K$-conjugacy class of maximal
subgroups for each representative $K$. For each Borel representative $B$,
let $\mathcal P(B)$ contain \emph{all} actual overgroups $P$ in $G$ with
$B$ maximal proper in $P$. Apply the phase-seven augmentation with
\texttt{REQUIRE\_2T=false}, its general rank-two test, and all four recognition
conditions. If every Borel run reaches its certified empty next level,
the outputs with join $G$ are exactly the requested geometries, represented
as subgroup families, modulo inner conjugacy and permutation of types.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
UD.Transport enumerates all maximal overgroups of any given subgroup from
the retained inclusion data. There is no local-action filter in
$\mathcal P(B)$. In a target of rank $r$, RWPRI at the top supplies a maximal
deletion $H$ in the target join $K$. Its rank-$(r-1)$ panel family is a
residue satisfying the same conditions, so induction reaches it. Its missing
panel $P$ lies in $K$ but not in $H$; since $H$ is maximal, adjoining $P$
has join exactly $K$. Every such maximal overgroup and every such panel is
tried. The pointwise stabilizer of the old tuple in the image of $N_G(B)$
acts on these extensions; taking its orbits loses no family. Using this
possibly smaller stabilizer instead of the setwise stabilizer only leaves
duplicates. The program's final successive-transporter test and type
permutations remove exactly those duplicates. Its order and pair-join
signatures are necessary invariants, never sufficient equivalence tests.
Conversely each accepted family passes the exact recognition conditions.

The intersection condition forces every new panel to enlarge the join:
$P_i\leq K_S$ for $i\notin S$ would imply $P_i=P_i\cap K_S=B$.
Consequently the rank is at most $\Omega([G:B])$, counting prime factors
with multiplicity. The extra next level must therefore be empty. A timeout,
missing Borel, unfinished anchor, or numeric cap before this terminal test
is not an exhaustion certificate. Finally every Borel is conjugate to an
input representative, and conjugating a family also conjugates its Borel.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact implementation boundary.}
The retained engine is \path{phase7_sporadic_search.g}.
Its seven top-level function definitions, from \texttt{IsMaxPair} through
\texttt{RunBorel}, and its maximal-inclusion initializer are extracted
byte-for-byte; the legacy file is unchanged. The only new interface supplies the complete explicit group and
subgroup representatives and fixes the broad-mode flag. \texttt{FlagTransitive}
uses the anchored Helly test: for each distinguished parabolic it intersects
the sets of cosets meeting the other base parabolics and compares their
cardinality with the contained set of cosets meeting their intersection.
All subfamilies of size at least three are checked. \texttt{ValidPanels}
checks every subgroup intersection and every maximal deletion.
\texttt{RankTwoAdmissible} additionally checks the two-deletion RWPRI condition.
\texttt{IsMaxPair} is called only for known inclusions. Prime index is an
exact sufficient shortcut; all other branches use maximal-subgroup
transport or the actual primitive coset action. The union of maximal
subgroup orders is only a necessary pruning test.

\paragraph{L100 retained inputs and equivalence.}
The installed TomLib 1.2.11 table named \texttt{L3(4)} has 95 subgroup classes.
Its underlying degree 21 permutation group has order 20160 but is not literally
the retained named action. A separately checked homomorphism given by images
of its two generators is bijective onto the retained group. Transporting
the complete representative and inclusion lists by that map preserves all
conditions. TomLib's complete subgroup classification and GAP's exact
maximal-subgroup, transporter and group algorithms remain named retained
premises, not conclusions of the small controls. No claim is based merely
on equal orders or names.

The first census would produce inner-conjugacy/type classes. A later exact
$\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ fusion would classify the specified-action objects
up to automorphism of $G$ and type permutation. This alone is not asserted
to classify bare incidence geometries: an arbitrary incidence isomorphism
need not normalize the specified subgroup $G$. An additional faithful
incidence-isomorphism comparison or a proved uniqueness-of-$G$ argument
would be required for that assertion. Full automorphism and correlation
groups, displayed kernel/group names, all remaining constructions, and the
whole source contract are separate obligations.
