## 1. The construction and its provenance

Let $I$ be a finite set of cardinality $r\ge2$, let $F=F(s_i:i\in I)$ be the free group on this basis, and define
\[
F_i=\langle s_j:j\ne i\rangle,
\qquad F_J=\bigcap_{j\in J}F_j.
\]
The objects of type $i$ are the right cosets $F_i g$. Objects of distinct types are incident when the cosets intersect. Denote this incidence system by $\Gamma_I$. Use the left permutation convention for the natural action:
\[
R_g(F_i h)=F_i h g^{-1}.
\tag{F1}
\]
Thus $g\mapsto R_g$ is a homomorphism.

The construction and its flag-transitivity assertion are Theorem 7.3 of Leemans–Stokes–Tranchida, *Groups represented by incidence geometries*, arXiv:2506.10464v1 [L126]. The chosen subgroups of an ambient free group appear in their Theorems 7.5–7.6. The general coset intersection criterion is classical, quoted there from Buekenhout–Cohen and already retained in the project's typed-Helly umbrella slice. We supply the missing reduced-word argument and then determine the complete type image and the normalizer of (F1). No priority claim is made for the elementary free-group arguments.

The distinction between three groups is essential: the specified group acting on the construction, the full correlation group, and the full automorphism group of the abstract free group. An embedding of a finite subgroup of the last group does not establish a weak representation of that entire group.

## 2. Reduced words and the Helly condition

A standard free factor generated by a subset of the basis consists exactly of the elements whose reduced words use only that subset. In particular
\[
F_J=\langle s_k:k\in I\setminus J\rangle.
\tag{F2}
\]
This includes $F_\varnothing=F$ and $F_I=1$.

For a reduced word $w$ and $i\in I$, let $P_i(w)$ be the prefix ending at its last occurrence of $s_i$ or $s_i^{-1}$; set $P_i(w)$ to the empty word if there is no such occurrence.

**Lemma R2.Prefix [SHARED].** For $i\notin J$,
\[
w\in F_JF_i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
P_i(w)\text{ uses no letter indexed by }J.
\tag{F3}
\]

**Proof.** If $w=ab$ with $a\in F_J$ and $b\in F_i$, reduce the concatenation of their reduced words. Cancellation takes place across their common boundary. The remaining word is a prefix of the reduced word of $a$ followed by a suffix of that of $b$, with no further cancellation. The second part has no occurrence indexed by $i$, so the prefix ending at the last such occurrence lies entirely in the first part. It avoids $J$. This also covers complete cancellation of the first part, when the prefix is empty. Conversely, if the specified prefix avoids $J$, split $w$ immediately after its last $i$-occurrence. The prefix belongs to $F_J$, and the suffix has no $i$-occurrence and belongs to $F_i$. $\square$

**Corollary R2.Product [APPLICATION].** For every $J\subseteq I$ and $i\notin J$,
\[
F_JF_i=\bigcap_{j\in J}F_jF_i.
\tag{F4}
\]

**Proof.** By (F3), membership in the right side says that $P_i(w)$ avoids each $j\in J$, which says exactly that it avoids $J$. The empty intersection causes no exception. $\square$

The classical coset Helly criterion identifies (F4) with flag-transitivity of the natural action on $\Gamma_I$. A direct geometric proof makes the mechanism visible. Consider the Cayley tree with edges joining $g$ to $s_i^{\pm1}g$. Every right coset of a standard free factor is a convex subtree, because the unique reduced path between its vertices uses only its allowed generators. A finite family of pairwise intersecting subtrees of a tree has a common vertex. For example, root the tree and select, among the nearest-to-root vertices of the subtrees, one at maximal depth. For any other subtree, take a point in its intersection with the selected subtree. Both nearest vertices lie on the root path to that point; the deeper one lies in both subtrees. It therefore lies in every member of the family. This proves that every flag of $\Gamma_I$ consists of cosets with a common representative. Right translation sends that representative to the identity and the flag to the standard flag of its type. In particular every flag extends to a chamber.

**Theorem R2.Geometry [APPLICATION].** The incidence system $\Gamma_I$ is a flag-transitive, residually connected geometry. The natural group $R(F)$ acts regularly on chambers.

**Proof.** The common-representative argument proves geometry and flag-transitivity. The stabilizer of the standard chamber is $\bigcap_i F_i=1$ by (F2). Thus the chamber action is regular and the natural action is faithful.

Translate an arbitrary flag to the standard flag of some type $J$. Its chambers are uniquely indexed by $F_J$, and its remaining objects identify with the cosets of
\[
F_J\cap F_i=F_{J\cup\{i\}},\qquad i\notin J,
\]
inside $F_J$. To verify this identification, intersect each incident coset with $F_J$; it is nonempty by the common-representative property and is a coset of the displayed intersection subgroup. Distinct right cosets remain distinct after nonempty intersection.

The residue is consequently the same free-basis construction on the remaining basis $\{s_i:i\notin J\}$. If at least two types remain, its chamber graph is connected: chambers labeled $h$ and $s_i^m h$ share all objects except possibly type $i$, and the remaining basis elements generate $F_J$. Each chamber is a clique with at least two vertices, and consecutive chambers have a common object. The union of this connected family of cliques is the entire residue incidence graph. Thus every residue of rank at least two is connected. $\square$

This proof also yields exactly the generation condition quoted as Theorem 7.4 in the source:
\[
F_J=\left\langle F_{J\cup\{i\}}:i\in I\setminus J\right\rangle
\quad\text{when }|I\setminus J|\ge2.
\tag{F5}
\]
Each remaining basis letter belongs to one of those factors by choosing a different remaining index.

**Proof correction [CORRECTION].** Membership in $F_jF_i$ does not imply that the entire reduced word avoids $s_j$. Its second factor is allowed to contain that letter. Theorem 7.3's printed proof uses this implication. Formula (F3), rather than avoidance by the whole word, supplies the valid common argument.

## 3. The full type image

Let $C=C(\Gamma_I)$ and $K=K(\Gamma_I)$ denote the full correlation and type-preserving groups. An automorphism $\alpha$ of $F$ satisfying $\alpha(F_i)=F_{\sigma(i)}$ induces the correlation
\[
F_i g\longmapsto F_{\sigma(i)}\alpha(g).
\tag{F6}
\]
The formula is well-defined because equality of cosets is preserved by $\alpha$, and incidence is preserved because $\alpha$ preserves nonempty intersection.

**Theorem R2.TypeImage [SHARED].** The action of the full correlation group on the palette has image $\operatorname{Sym}(I)$. Thus
\[
C/K\cong\operatorname{Sym}(I).
\tag{F7}
\]

**Proof.** Every permutation $\sigma$ of the free basis extends to an automorphism of $F$ by the universal property of the free group, and it sends $F_i$ to $F_{\sigma(i)}$. Formula (F6) therefore realizes every palette permutation. The kernel of the palette action is precisely $K$ by definition. $\square$

There is no claim here that (F7) determines $K$ or the entire group $C$. The natural subgroup $R(F)$ need not be the full type-preserving group.

The Lean file `BasisPermutation.lean` verifies the generic basis-permutation automorphism, its faithful dependence on the permutation, and the equation transporting the subgroup closure generated by every complementary basis. This is the formal group-theoretic step in the proof of (F7). The coset quotient construction and the inference of the full type quotient are written proofs, not part of that file's formal scope.

## 4. The chamber normalizer

Write
\[
W_I=C_2^I\rtimes\operatorname{Sym}(I)
\]
for the signed basis permutations. Each maps $s_i$ to $s_{\sigma(i)}^{\epsilon_i}$ with $\epsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}$ and acts by (F6).

**Theorem R2.Normalizer [SHARED].** The normalizer of the natural chamber-regular group is
\[
N_C(R(F))=R(F)\rtimes W_I.
\tag{F8}
\]
The signed basis subgroup is precisely the stabilizer of the standard chamber inside this normalizer.

**Proof.** If $c$ normalizes $R(F)$, compose $c$ with the unique natural translation taking its image of the standard chamber back to that chamber. It suffices to consider a normalizing correlation $d$ fixing the standard chamber. Conjugation by $d$ induces an automorphism $\alpha$ of $F$, since $R:F\to R(F)$ is an isomorphism. Let $\sigma$ be the palette permutation of $d$.

The pointwise stabilizer in $R(F)$ of the standard chamber with type $i$ deleted is
\[
R\!\left(\bigcap_{j\ne i}F_j\right)=R(\langle s_i\rangle).
\]
Since $d$ fixes the chamber as a set and permutes its types by $\sigma$, it sends this stabilizer to $R(\langle s_{\sigma(i)}\rangle)$. Thus $\alpha$ maps each infinite cyclic subgroup $\langle s_i\rangle$ onto $\langle s_{\sigma(i)}\rangle$. An isomorphism of infinite cyclic groups sends a generator to a generator, so $\alpha(s_i)=s_{\sigma(i)}^{\pm1}$. Hence $\alpha\in W_I$.

A normalizing correlation fixing the standard chamber is determined by $\alpha$. Indeed every chamber is its unique translate by an element of $R(F)$, so its image is forced by conjugation; every object belongs to a chamber. Conversely every signed basis permutation acts by (F6), fixes the standard chamber, and normalizes the natural group. The chamber-regular subgroup has trivial intersection with that stabilizer, and every normalizer element has the unique factorization already described. This proves (F8). $\square$

In particular, the geometry gives a weak representation of the specified pair
\[
(F,\ F\rtimes W_I)
\]
when the inner group is written first as in the source's pair notation. This is a positive statement about compatible injections into $(K,C)$; it is not a representation of the full abstract group $F$ in the source's special sense.

**Corollary R2.NaturalObstruction [CORRECTION].** For $r\ge2$, there is no injective homomorphism
\[
\operatorname{Aut}F\longrightarrow C(\Gamma_I)
\]
whose restriction to $\operatorname{Inn}F\cong F$ is the natural embedding $R$.

**Proof.** The inner subgroup is normal, so the image of such a homomorphism would lie in $N_C(R(F))$. Injectivity, and equality of the image of the inner subgroup with $R(F)$, would induce an injection
\[
\operatorname{Out}F\hookrightarrow N_C(R(F))/R(F)\cong W_I.
\]
The right side is finite. The left side is infinite: for distinct basis elements $s_1,s_2$, the automorphisms $s_1\mapsto s_1s_2^m$, fixing the other basis elements, induce distinct transformations of the abelianization $\mathbb Z^r$, whereas inner automorphisms act trivially there. This is a contradiction. $\square$

The corollary fixes the natural embedding used in the construction. It does not rule out every conceivable weak embedding with a different image of $\operatorname{Inn}F$. The source's finite-rank definition separately rules out an *exact* representation of a nonabelian free group: its outer quotient would have to embed into the finite palette permutation group. That observation is already made in [L126, §7].

## 5. The source's conjugate families

Let $F_n=F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ with $n\ge2$, and put
\[
b_{j,\epsilon,i}=x_j^\epsilon x_i x_j^{-\epsilon},
\qquad j\ne i,\quad\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}.
\tag{F9}
\]
There are $r=2n(n-1)$ displayed words. Let $G$ be the subgroup they generate.

**Lemma R2.Core [APPLICATION].** The words (F9) freely generate $G$.

**Proof.** Construct a finite labeled graph with one central vertex $o$ and $2n$ outer vertices $v_{j,\epsilon}$. Join $o$ to $v_{j,\epsilon}$ by an oriented edge labeled $x_j^\epsilon$. At $v_{j,\epsilon}$ put a loop labeled $x_i$ for every $i\ne j$. Map this graph to the rose labeled $x_1,\ldots,x_n$.

At each vertex, all outgoing oriented labels are different: at $o$ they are the $2n$ signed letters; at $v_{j,\epsilon}$ the edge back has label $x_j^{-\epsilon}$, and the loop directions have labels $x_i^{\pm1}$ for $i\ne j$. Thus a reduced edge path maps to a reduced word. A nonempty reduced closed path cannot map to the identity in $F_n$, so the induced homomorphism on fundamental groups is injective. The spokes form a spanning tree. The associated free basis consists of a spoke, one outer loop, and the reverse spoke, giving exactly (F9). Equivalently the graph rank is
\[
\bigl(2n+2n(n-1)\bigr)-(1+2n)+1=2n(n-1).
\]
This proves the assertion. $\square$

The finite graph is the core relevant to the displayed finite generating set. Attaching loops at every integer vertex of entire coordinate axes, as a literal reading of the subsequent source description suggests, would instead give infinitely many independent loops. No such enlarged graph is used here. Trees may be attached to the finite core to obtain the subgroup covering without altering its fundamental group.

**Theorem R2.SourceFamily [APPLICATION].** Form the source coset system on $G$ with one maximal standard parabolic omitting each word (F9). Then it is flag-transitive and residually connected, $G$ acts faithfully and regularly on chambers, and its full type quotient is
\[
\operatorname{Sym}(2n(n-1)).
\tag{F10}
\]
The signed permutation group $C_2^n\rtimes S_n$ of the ambient basis acts faithfully by correlations. Its action is a specified subgroup, not the complete type image.

**Proof.** Lemma R2.Core identifies $G$ with the free group on the displayed basis. Theorem R2.Geometry and Theorem R2.TypeImage apply. An ambient signed permutation $x_k\mapsto x_{p(k)}^{\delta_k}$ sends
\[
b_{j,\epsilon,i}\longmapsto
b_{p(j),\epsilon\delta_j,p(i)}^{\delta_i}.
\tag{F11}
\]
It therefore permutes the omitted-generator parabolics and acts by (F6). Its palette action is faithful: fixing all indices $(j,\epsilon,i)$ forces $p$ to fix every symbol and forces every $\delta_j=1$, since for each $j$ another index $i$ exists. $\square$

For $n=2$, the four words are precisely those printed before Theorem 7.5. The specified ambient group acts on their four types as the square group of order eight. But an arbitrary three-cycle of the four free generators also acts by a correlation, and such a permutation is not in that square group. The full quotient in (F10) is $S_4$ of order twenty-four. Thus the quotient assertion in Theorem 7.5 is false; it is not repaired merely by giving a more detailed proof of the action of the chosen subgroup.

The geometric conclusions and the chosen finite actions of Theorems 7.5–7.6 are recovered. Their final inference of a weak representation of $G$ does not follow from those actions and fails for the natural inner embedding by Corollary R2.NaturalObstruction. The complete normalizer formula provides a positive replacement with exact scope.

## 6. Verification and source contract

The word checker independently tests factor membership by trying every cut of a reduced word, then compares it with the last-occurrence rule and the product identity. It covers all 23,437 reduced words of length at most six in rank three and all 22,409 of length at most five in rank four. These are bounded checks, not an infinite-word proof. It checks the folded finite cores for ambient ranks two through six and the signed palette actions for ranks two through four. Replacing the last occurrence by the first is a negative control and must be rejected.

The Lean certificate has three declarations: the action on each free generator, transport of every complementary generated subgroup, and faithfulness of the basis-permutation map. It uses the pinned Lean 4.32.2 and mathlib checkout. Its axiom reports contain only the standard imported foundations, with no new axioms or proof holes. The normal-form, tree-Helly, residual-connectivity, normalizer, and obstruction proofs above remain written proofs. The replay records this formal boundary explicitly.

The source's free-basis construction is retained; the product-identity proof, complete palette calculation, and normalizer criterion are the proof work of this application. The classical universal property and uniqueness of free-group reduced words remain inputs. The general Artin–Tits extension mentioned without proof in [L126] is outside this contract. No claim concerning all finite subgroups of $\operatorname{Out}F_n$, maximum finite subgroup orders, or arbitrary lifts to $\operatorname{Aut}F_n$ is needed.

Within the representation topic, the prefix/Helly and normalizer arguments are shared acquisitions followed by the short substitution (F9). For another topic the full required shared slice is charged again. No matched compression ratio, whole-paper replacement, or open-conjecture solution is inferred from these corrections.
