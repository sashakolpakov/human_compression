\section{Source contract and certificates}
\label{sec:scope}
The source is D\'iaz Ramos--Molinier--Viruel, arXiv:2511.02410v2,
16 December 2025. The full finite normal-pair theorem, the ordered-arrow
construction, chamber completion, cleaning and the recursive symmetric family
belong to those authors. Their proof acknowledges Frucht and K\"onig's
classical graph-representation constructions. Those historical representation
theorems are not unproved premises of the explicit constructions here.

The common paper and this application recover source Theorems 3.1, 4.1, 4.2
and A. They also recover the complete intended statements of Examples 5.1
and 5.2 with the stated corrections. All local recognition, extension,
triangle-free, minimum-degree, connectedness and faithful-action arguments
are supplied in the common paper and this application. The full
ordered-pair proof and finite normal-pair existence theorem are shared
in the common paper; neither application supplies an unpaid premise
to the other. The three qualifications are the actual chamber degrees,
the completed-hexagon type kernel, and the degree-one recursive base path.
No optimality assertion about all completion hypotheses or all possible
graph sizes is inferred. The family is asserted as $(S_n,A_n)$; no universal
identification of that pair with $(\Aut(A_n),\operatorname{Inn}(A_n))$ is
needed or claimed. The source's Question 2.4 is answered by its own
Theorem A; this recovery is not a newly solved conjecture.

The generic formal file for the common theorem proves the restriction and
extension isomorphism for actual permutation subgroups and an embedded
vertex set. It does not verify the gadgets that discharge its hypotheses.
The unchanged \texttt{CompletedHexagonCounterexample.lean} gives four
reported certificates: preservation of adjacency and color by
\eqref{eq:reflection}, its order two, and the absence of a nontrivial
involution in $C_3$. These are not a Lean computation of the full graph
automorphism group or a formal proof for all finite normal pairs.

The two complete GAP programs in the appendix check the two hexagon groups,
all 25 properly colored graphs on at most three labeled vertices under
cleaning and completion, seven normal-pair examples, four completed pair
examples, and the recursive family for $2\leq n\leq5$. They include exact
restriction actions rather than comparing only group orders. They are
bounded validation, since the general constructions and both exceptional
group identifications have full written proofs in the common paper
and this application. The full programs
remain available; no diagnostic code is removed. Their drivers and proof
infrastructure are recorded separately. There is no numerical compression
claim in this edition.
