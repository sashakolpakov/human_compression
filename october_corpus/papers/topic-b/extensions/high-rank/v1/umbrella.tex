\begin{abstract}
We combine bounds on maximum irredundant generating length with an
exact attachment theorem for permutation groups. Normal-module and
simple-factor arguments give a uniform bound for every transitive
imprimitive group of degree at least nine. Primitive groups require an
explicit classical classification input and a bounded exact catalogue
calculation. A separate finite-core interface proves all subgroup
intersections after attaching an arbitrary tree. Finally we give a
point-stabilizer proof of the tree presentation of a symmetric group
and a general change-of-marking rule. The companion application derives
the complete high-rank symmetric-group classification, its geometries
and its presentations, with an explicit correction to a printed
branching clause. All retained hypotheses and the limited formal scope
are recorded here.
\end{abstract}

A marking is a finite indexed generating family of distinct nonidentity
involutions. It is a \emph{C-group marking} if, for every pair of subsets
$S,R$ of its index set,
\[
 \langle\rho_i:i\in S\rangle\cap\langle\rho_i:i\in R\rangle
 =\langle\rho_i:i\in S\cap R\rangle.
\]
In particular every subfamily is independent: a generator cannot lie
in the subgroup generated by the others. Its rank is the number of
indices. The results on irredundant generation below apply to arbitrary
finite groups, not only to involution markings. All trees and underlying
permutation sets in the attachment and presentation sections are finite.

\section{Attribution, retained inputs and formal scope}
The normal-quotient inequality is Cameron--Cara's Theorem 1.1, not a
new result. Its full proof and the elementary subdirect-product lemma
are included. Forest transpositions, tree presentations and changes of
generators are classical mechanisms; the source application and its
historical attribution are identified in the companion paper. The
module bounds and the finite-core interface are the shared arguments
used here to reorganize that recovery. We make no historical priority
claim for them.

Two substantial classification inputs remain external. Whiston's
independent-family bound for symmetric groups, including its equality
clause, is retained from [W], as stated and used in the genuine authored
paper [CC, \S\S1--2]. The original [W] full text has not been obtained
for this packet. The companion also retains the three possibilities
for a one-generator omission from a maximum family, stated in the
proof of [CC, Theorem 2.1]. Mar\'oti's original Theorem 1.1 [M] is
retained with both containments in its product-action case. These
results depend on finite-group classification; their proofs are not
replaced by the elementary arguments here.

The entire \texttt{primitive\_bound.g} is a required finite premise:
it uses the complete GAP primitive catalogue through degree 25 and
exact maximal-subgroup, socle and quotient algorithms. Its exception
lists, all special identifications and integer inequalities are explicit
above. Completeness of that catalogue and correctness of those group
algorithms are retained computational inputs. This is not an exhaustive
search over C-group markings. The companion's core and local-obstruction
programs are finite validation of fully written arguments, with their
roles kept distinct in the complete program appendix.

The eight declarations in \texttt{IrredundantNormalization.lean}
prove the actual normalization leg of Lemma~\ref{lem:quotient}:
quotient-kernel membership, preservation of generated subgroup and
independence of the normalized extra elements, first under base-group
conjugation and then under all conjugations for an abelian normal
kernel. The latter theorem explicitly assumes $G=N\langle T\rangle$
and internal commutativity of $N$. It does not assume the desired
independence conclusion. The Lean file does not prove the cardinal
bounds, irreducibility consequences, Scott's lemma, the primitive
classification, finite catalogue completeness, the attachment theorem
or any source-specific geometry. These remain the written and declared
computational obligations. Fresh compilation reports standard Lean
axioms only; cached pinned library objects are part of the trusted
formal environment.
