\section{What is shared and what is retained}
The common paper proves the complete elementary coset interface, the
residual-maximality induction and its geometric adapter, the positive
weighted-forest construction, and the two-block overgroup interval.
The forest includes both subgroup intersections and a common
representative for every partial flag. It is used with unit weights,
one contracted edge of weight two, and equal leaf weights. These are
actual applications to specified subgroups, not a diagrammatic analogy.
The Frattini ladder, the normal-quotient bound, and graph reconstruction
are local proofs in the relevant papers. No application needs the proof
of another application.

B1 retains the 1997 classification and uniqueness theorem of Buekenhout,
Cara and Dehon in the exact form recalled by the 1998 source. B2 retains
Whiston's independent-family bound and the separately stated omission
consequence, including the latter's dependence on the classification of
finite simple groups. Cayley's formula and the innerness of automorphisms
of $S_n$ for $n\geq7$ are retained classical facts. B3 retains the
elementary-abelian Frattini quotient of a finite $p$-group; its infinite
maximal-overgroup argument uses Zorn's lemma. B4 proves the required
Kneser filtration, eigenvalue bounds and equality eigenspace; only
elementary real symmetric spectral theory and linear algebra are used
without proof. These external costs remain explicit and unmeasured.

\section{Source editions and qualifications}
B1 uses the complete available authored browser text. Its mathematical
formula and diagram extraction is imperfect, and the original PDF has
not been acquired. Its source PDF cost is unknown. B1 remains part of
the proposed four-application volume; it is not silently removed from
the denominator or assigned an invented numerator. The source phrase
``this is the case'' is interpreted precisely as the criterion for
failure of PRI, rather than for QPRI alone.

B2 uses the twelve-page author manuscript. Its extremal classification
assumes $n\geq7$ and retains three distinct counting conventions. Numerical
group bounds are asserted for finite groups. Small-degree discussions
and unnumbered general direct-product questions are outside this contract.

B3 uses the nine-page published source. The unrestricted infinite converse
of its generating-set Theorem 2.1 is false; the paper includes both the
relative finite-generation correction and its explicit counterexample.
The infinite geometric containment theorem remains fully proved for
finite-rank firm RWPRI geometries. The $p$-group classification requires
finite groups and applies to every prime. Its count is of indexed
subgroup families, not automorphism orbits. The residue Frattini condition
is necessary, not sufficient. The quoted Gasch\"utz theorem and the older
primitive-group reduction are outside the target clauses and are not
used in their proofs.

B4 uses the ten-page institutional revised manuscript, whose title and
numbering differ from other editions. The source map binds Theorem 1.2
and Corollaries 1.3, 1.4, 4.8 and 5.1. The three retained corrections are
the panel order $\binom{n-k}{k}-1$, the thin case $k=1,n=3$, and the full
locally Desargues graph group $S_7\times S_3$. The latter includes an
uncolored type-reconstruction proof. No identity with an unacquired
publisher edition is presumed.

\section{What Lean verifies}
The file \texttt{formal/ResidualGeneration.lean} works with actual
subgroups of an arbitrary group. For a finite set $S$ of unfixed types,
it defines the intersection of the stabilizers outside $S$ and the
subgroup generated by the common stabilizer and the chosen elements in
$S$. Off-diagonal membership, exclusion from one's own stabilizer, and
a maximal next intersection imply equality of those subgroups by strong
induction. The six reported declarations certify containment, that
equality, the intersection formula, its generated-subgroup counterpart,
irredundancy, and generation of the whole group for a finite type set.
The desired equality is a conclusion, not a hypothesis.

The geometric panel choices and residual-maximality bridge are written
proofs in UB.Coset, UB.Flags and UB.Generation. The forest construction,
Whiston and B97 inputs, Frattini theorem, spectral argument, and four
source applications are not formalized by this module. The checker
freshly compiles the actual module against pinned Lean 4.32.2 and mathlib,
reports only the standard axioms, rejects three missing-hypothesis proof
edits, and checks three literal finite countermodels plus an altered
model control. These examples establish the necessity of the stated
hypotheses in the displayed cases; they do not replace any uniform proof.

\section{Replay and accounting boundary}
From the packet directory run
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
python3 -O check.py --out /fresh/absolute/path
\end{quote}
The \texttt{--lean} and \texttt{--packages} options relocate the pinned
toolchain and dependencies. With an interpreter providing \texttt{pypdf},
\texttt{--render} performs two XeLaTeX passes for all five scientific
papers and this reader, checks diagnostics, and compares every page's
extracted text with its authoritative PDF. The optional
\texttt{--verify-primary --sources-root /private/study} checks the
private source and prior-review bindings. Primary texts and images are
not distributed in the packet. Cached dependency objects are part of
the stated Lean trust boundary; no claim is made that mathlib was rebuilt.

No mathematical computation is required by the written uniform proofs.
The portable checker is validation infrastructure, whose cost is recorded
separately from the scientific papers if measured later. The complete
common paper is paid within this topic even where arguments overlap
another topic. This packet makes no numerical factor, full-visible-text
comparison, or claim of a sawtooth pattern. The current mathematical
edition remains an author candidate pending independent review.
