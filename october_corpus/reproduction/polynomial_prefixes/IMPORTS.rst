Borrowed premises and attribution
================================

The original manuscript is Malcolm Hoong Wai Chen, Abstract regular
polytopes of G2(q) in even characteristic, arXiv:2609.25004v1,
21 September 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25004v1. Its Table 2,
p. 11, reports 105 rank-four classes at q = 11 up to isomorphism and
duality. These ten resolved prefix jobs are partial census obligations;
they do not complete the positive classification or the original paper.

The matrices belong to the full split group G = Aut(C) isomorphic to
G2(11), in its faithful natural action on the pure seven-space of a split
Cayley algebra over F_11. The acquired original-frame and mark transfer
identifies the displayed a, e, b with labels 1, 4, 2 in the original
rank-four jobs. Their membership in G is imported. Preserving a quadratic
form alone would not identify this group. PROVENANCE.json supplies the
original export, frame and full-model bridge digests, together with the
existing public prefix provenance. These provenance files are not inputs
opened by the finite replay.

The imported maximal cover at this field has the two parabolics, the
involution-centralizer type, the full split and nonsplit quadratic-line
stabilizers, and the four types 2^3.PSL3(2), G2(2), PGL2(11), J1. A
parabolic here is the full stabilizer in G of the specified isotropic
pure line or null two-space. The A2 extensions are full setwise line
stabilizers, not their index-two vector stabilizers. The centralizer type
is the actual C_G(t) for a nonidentity involution t. These full geometric
identifications are essential to the polynomial argument.

The complete cover and field restrictions are borrowed from P. B.
Kleidman, J. Algebra 117 (1988), 30-71, DOI
https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(88)90239-6, and H. N. Nguyen,
arXiv:0910.4994v1, proof of Theorem 4.4, printed pp. 10-11 (the complete
list before the degree-based exclusions). The geometric descriptions are
in R. A. Wilson, The Finite Simple Groups, retained preliminary edition,
Sections 4.2.5-4.2.6 and Table 4.1, printed pp. 105-109. The published
edition numbers the Cayley chapter as Section 4.3.
https://webspace.maths.qmul.ac.uk/r.a.wilson/TFSG.html

For 2^3.PSL3(2) and G2(2), the prime 11 does not divide the group order.
In PGL2(11), semisimple orders divide 10 or 12 and unipotent orders are 11.
For J1 the imported maximal orders under divisibility are
{6, 7, 10, 11, 15, 19}; the source is Li--Moghaddamfar--Vasil'ev--Wang,
arXiv:2312.07907v1, Lemma 3.9(2), printed p. 6. These facts exclude
orders 33 and 55 from all four exceptional types. A group-order argument
alone would not exclude those orders from J1.

Polynomial invariance, the elementary commutant argument, Cayley--Hamilton
and the C-group intersection-property obstruction are standard facts.
The present contribution is their common use with the explicit finite
identities and the retained classification. There is no historical
novelty claim. The imported classification, model and transfer costs
remain part of any later compression audit.
