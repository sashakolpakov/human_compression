Local completeness
------------------

For fixed side-port order, a sorted list of masks is exactly a multiset of
side vertices and solves the column-margin equations. Every such multiset
occurs once in the count recursion. The actual ordinal is the recursion's
deterministic multiplicity order; it need not be the usual lexical order of
mask lists. Interval rank skipping uses exact subdomain cardinalities, and
does not assume an expected graph count.

At size5 every endpoint row omits one side vertex, and the multiset of those
five omissions is forced by the mask population counts. At size6 the rows
omit pairs. Five unordered loopless omission edges, with repetitions, are
exactly a multigraph with the required six degrees. Every one occurs in the
11,628 nondecreasing5-tuples of the15 possible pairs. For any fixed degree
sequence at most945 possibilities occur: label its10 stubs, and each such
multigraph is represented by at least one of the9*7*5*3*1=945 pairings.
Discarding degree>4 in the global omission index is valid because the allowed
mask widths bound every side omission degree by4.

Ports are first grouped in the fixed nonincreasing missing-multiplicity
order. All permutations inside equal-multiplicity groups are considered.
After applying a port permutation, sorting masks removes every permutation
of unequal masks. The remaining side action is precisely the permutations
within equal-mask classes. A zero mask has omission degree0, so its vertices
never occur in an omission edge. Omitting its internal permutations leaves
both the mask list and omission list unchanged; it loses no local orbit.
The sorted omission list removes endpoint-row permutations. Hence the
least local pair is a complete local invariant, not a heuristic invariant.

The equal-multiplicity port maps extend by permuting the matching Xi classes
of the same size and fixing X0,z. Thus independent local minimizations extend
through the middle. Rejecting a mask profile which is not the least in its
port orbit, and then retaining precisely the least omission representatives,
loses no unmarked side object and emits one representative per local orbit.

Intrinsic center choices
------------------------

In the center color, distinct core vertices are at distance2. Every endpoint
false twin is at distance4 from EVERY core vertex. Every other side vertex
is within distance2 of some core vertex. Consequently every pair in this
color other than opposite endpoint-twin classes has distance at most6:
for a twin and a side vertex use that side's own nearby core; for two side
vertices use their two cores and the intervening distance2. Opposite
endpoint twins retain distance8. Thus there is exactly one diametral
unordered twin-class pair in this color. Applying the same normal-form
theorem from an eight-geodesic in the other color gives at most one there.
Connected bipartiteness ensures an ordinary isomorphism can only preserve
or interchange these two colors.

For a fixed pair at distance8, simultaneous distance4 vertices are exactly
the core. Its twin classes are X0 and the nonempty Xi, at most1+u<=4.
Conversely a vertex at distance4 from each endpoint lies on an actual
eight-geodesic: concatenate shortest4-paths; an overlap producing a shorter
walk would contradict endpoint distance8. The source may therefore use
each such midpoint as the normal-form center. Replacing a chosen vertex
by another member of its twin class changes no code, because a twin swap
is an automorphism fixing every other vertex.

These facts prove the at-most2 endpoint pairs and at-most8 complete center
choices used by ordinary_code. They do not rely on a supplied marked root,
nor merely on three centers being pairwise at least4 apart.
