\begin{document}\maketitle
We prove a positive lower bound using explicit permutations and relation
words. The finite search which found them is not a premise of this
lower bound. The named group is the acquired degree-forty model of
$\mathrm{PSp}_4(3)\cong\mathrm{PSU}_4(2)$, of order $25920$; its
identification is retained from the reviewed subgroup atlas. In
particular, it is not identified by its order alone.

We use the already acquired geometric recognition theorem of
Schulte and Weiss, in the prefix coordinates of our reviewed manuscript.
At rank three the tuple $(1,s,t)$ represents a chiral marking when
\[
 \langle s,t\rangle=G,\quad |t|=2,\quad |s|,|s^{-1}t|>2,
\]
the three cyclic subgroups $\langle s\rangle$, $\langle t\rangle$,
$\langle s^{-1}t\rangle$ intersect pairwise trivially, and no
automorphism simultaneously inverts $s$ and $t$.
The cyclic subgroups are precisely the three nontrivial proper
difference subgroups. The remaining difference subgroups are $1$ and
$G$, so these tests imply the full intersection property. We count
ordered markings modulo $\Aut(G)$, retaining the ordered type and
distinguishing mirror markings. This convention is explicit throughout.

\begin{lemma}\label{transport}
Suppose $s,t$ and $s',t'$ each generate the same finite permutation
group $G$. Start with the proposed image $1\mapsto1$, and propagate
images by the directed edges $x\mapsto xs$ and $x\mapsto xt$,
using target edges $y\mapsto ys'$ and $y\mapsto yt'$. If propagation
is consistent on every edge, it defines the unique automorphism
sending $(s,t)$ to $(s',t')$. If it is inconsistent, there is an
explicit word $w$ with $w(s,t)=1$ and $w(s',t')\ne1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every element is a positive word in $s,t$, since inverses are positive
powers in a finite group. The directed graph is therefore connected
from the identity. Consistency means that the proposed image is
independent of the chosen positive word: follow the letters of any
such word along the verified edges. Consequently concatenating words
shows that the resulting function preserves multiplication. It is
surjective because $s',t'$ generate $G$, hence bijective by finiteness.
Its generator images make it unique.

For an inconsistent edge, let $u$ be the recorded word reaching its
initial vertex and let $v$ be the recorded word reaching its final
vertex. If the edge is labelled $z\in\{s,t\}$, the word
$uzv^{-1}$ evaluates to the identity in the source. The two conflicting
target images show that it does not evaluate to the identity in the
target. This gives the claimed obstruction, which can be checked by
permutation multiplication without repeating the propagation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
The supplied permutations give at least 46 distinct ordered chiral
marking classes of rank three in $\mathrm{PSU}_4(2)$. Their ordered
types and multiplicities are as follows.
\end{proposition}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cr@{\qquad}cr@{\qquad}cr@{\qquad}cr}
\toprule Type&Count&Type&Count&Type&Count&Type&Count\\\midrule
$(4,9)$&2&$(5,9)$&6&$(9,4)$&2&$(12,4)$&2\\
$(4,12)$&2&$(5,12)$&2&$(9,5)$&6&$(12,5)$&2\\
$(6,9)$&2&$(9,6)$&2&$(9,12)$&4&$(12,9)$&4\\
$(6,12)$&2&$(9,9)$&4&$(12,6)$&2&$(12,12)$&2\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{proof}
The certificate contains 92 literal tuples $(1,s,t)$ on forty points.
The validator first closes the acquired ambient generators under
permutation multiplication and obtains 25920 elements. For each tuple
it verifies membership, the displayed order conditions, the three
cyclic intersections and equality of its generated subgroup with the
ambient group. These are finite set equalities on actual permutations.

Apply Lemma~\ref{transport} with target $(s^{-1},t^{-1})$ to each
tuple. Every propagation yields a relation obstruction. The retained
92 words therefore exclude every required reflection. The acquired
recognition theorem now supplies a chiral polytope marking in each case.

To distinguish the markings, process them in their retained order.
Compare a tuple with the existing class representatives of its same
ordered type using Lemma~\ref{transport}; different types cannot be
related by an automorphism. A successful propagation assigns the tuple
to that representative. If all comparisons fail, retain a new
representative. Induction shows that every new representative is
inequivalent to all earlier ones, and every assigned tuple is equivalent
to its recorded representative. The completed comparisons give 46
classes, each containing two of the supplied tuples. There are 46
successful transports, each checking 25920 vertices and 51840 directed
edges, and 118 unsuccessful transports with explicit relation witnesses.
The classes have precisely the sixteen distributions in the table.
Neither a computed outer automorphism order nor completeness of the
search is needed for this argument.
\end{proof}

For example, the first marking has type $(12,9)$ and is given by

 &\quad(25,35)(26,33)(27,39)(28,36)(29,34)(30,40)(31,37).
\end{align*}
One reflection obstruction is
\[
 w(s,t)=s^2t s^5t s t^{-1}s^{-1}t^{-1}s^{-3}t^{-1}=1,
 \qquad w(s^{-1},t^{-1})\ne1.
\]
Both statements are checked by direct multiplication with permutations
acting on the right. The complete certificate supplies the same form
of witness for every proposed reflection and every rejected comparison.

\paragraph{Source comparison and attribution.}
Hartley, Hubard and Leemans, \emph{Two atlases of abstract chiral
polytopes for small groups}, Ars Mathematica Contemporanea 5 (2012),
371--382, print 26 in the chiral column of the $\mathrm{PSU}(4,2)$
row of Table~6, on page~381. Section~4 classifies the ordered generator
tuples up to isomorphism. Section~6 explicitly counts the two
enantiomorphic forms separately, and halves its overall total only
when these are subsequently identified. Thus the printed column uses
the same convention as the proposition: its value 26 is contradicted
by the literal lower bound 46. This comparison does not identify duals.
The mirror formula in Section~3 becomes $(s^{-1},t)$ in our prefix
coordinates, exactly the reflection tested above. The broader search
reports the same count 46, but its exhaustion is not used in this
refutation of the printed value.

The geometric reconstruction and named-group identification are
borrowed, with the attributions retained in the acquired manuscripts.
The present contribution is the explicit finite collection and its
verification by permutation closure and relation words. This verifies
a positive lower bound in ordinary Python, not a Lean formalization
or a new general reconstruction theorem. No compression factor or
completion of the whole source follows from this component alone.
