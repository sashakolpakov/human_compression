Original contract and native reading
------------------------------------

The fixed source is Dimitri Leemans, The residually weakly primitive
pre-geometries of the Suzuki simple groups, Note di Matematica20(1)
(2000/2001),1--20, 223966 bytes, SHA256

I read all twenty pages in the retained text, and directly viewed native
pages4,5,15,16,17,18. These include the exact subgroup definition, the
whole-lattice instruction, every one of the eleven constructions, and
Theorem5. The paper explicitly distinguishes its pregeometry definition
from geometric RWPRI and explicitly postpones flag-transitivity questions.

On page4, for every proper subset J of types, at least one remaining
G_(J union {i}) must be maximal in G_J. The example proves the stronger
property that every one-step inclusion is maximal. There is no additional
geometry, chamber-transitivity, Lang-descent or coset-Helly hypothesis in
the stated classification. Using that weaker source contract is therefore
legitimate; importing a stronger unproved geometric conclusion is unnecessary.

Page15 asks the reader to respect the entire intended intersection lattice
of each construction. The example supplies its entire lattice. This instruction
does not allow dropping arbitrary types from the eleven listed families.
Page16 specifically notes that Construction2 is a truncation of Construction1;
this local observation is not an additional all-truncations clause in Theorem5.

Construction11 on page18 factors the odd field degree using pairwise distinct
primes, and literally allows alpha in {2,...,m-1}. Thus m counts distinct prime
divisors, not prime factors with multiplicity. For3465=3^2*5*7*11, m=4 and the
listed maximum rank is3. The rank4 example is outside that range. Constructions
1--10 each have a soluble maximal parabolic: their displayed groups include
a dihedral factor, an affine soluble factor, a cyclic odd group extended by C4,
or the mixed root-group factor. I checked this for both cases of Construction5
and the lower parameter endpoints of Constructions7--10 as well. In the
example all four maximal parabolics are nonabelian simple. Neither a type
permutation nor an ambient automorphism identifies these subgroup systems.

The word maximal in maximal parabolic here refers to omitting one type; it
does not mean that every such subgroup in every source construction is maximal
in G. The review uses simplicity/solubility to distinguish the actual listed
parabolics, not that terminological ambiguity.

Proof audit
-----------

The complete classical input is explicit: the faithful Suzuki matrix model
and its two Bruhat cells, the simplicity of Sz(2^d) for odd d>=3, and maximality
of a simple subfield subgroup of prime relative degree. The source's Table1
records the latter maximal-subgroup family. No completeness of the source's
geometry/pregeometry list is assumed to construct the counterexample. No
new verification of the full classical Suzuki classification is claimed.

I multiplied the actual displayed matrices to check the extraction formulas.
For g=U(a,b)h(t), g22=t, g21/g11=a and g42/t=b. For the open cell
g=U(a,b)h(t)wU(c,f), g14=t^(theta+1), g13/g14=c, g12/g14=f,
g24/g14=a and g34/g14+a^(theta+1)=b. The last addition is the correct
characteristic-two subtraction. The closed cell has g14=0 and the open cell
has nonzero g14, so no cell ambiguity occurs. All divisions are by nonzero
torus coordinates, even if some root parameters vanish.

For odd ambient exponent n, theta+1 is coprime to2^n-1: a common odd
divisor would give both2^(n+1)=1 and2^n=1. Hence raising g14 to the inverse
power recovers t inside any coefficient subfield. The ambient theta restricts
to the standard Suzuki exponent on each odd-degree subfield. The exact
coefficient-field subgroup is consequently the standard S_d in both Bruhat
cells. Combining this with finite-field intersection proves
S_a intersect S_b = S_gcd(a,b), including the possible degree-one intersection.
This does not assert anything about arbitrarily conjugated subfield embeddings.

For distinct selected primes p_i, the standard intersections are exactly
G_J=S_(n/product_{j in J}p_j). Their degrees are distinct by unique
factorization, and the strictly increasing Suzuki order formula distinguishes
the groups. If the bottom exponent d is greater than1, it is at least3 since
all degrees are odd. Every one-step smaller group is therefore simple, and
the ratio of field degrees is exactly the selected prime. Each of the32
rank-four cube edges is a proper maximal inclusion by the retained theorem.
This pays even the last four steps to the bottom S3; the exceptional S1
boundary is never used in the counterexample.

For n3465 the parabolic exponents1155,693,495,315 and bottom3 are correct.
The sixteen intersections are all distinct. The system has four types, and
the verified subgroup condition is precisely the source's RWPRI condition.
All relevant index statements are analytic; constructing the enormous group
or checking cosets by enumeration would add no completeness argument.

Since all four parabolics are simple, the only potentially matching literal
family is Construction11, whose rank is too small. Theorem5 states that all
rank-at-least-four RWPRI pregeometries are given by Constructions1--11, so
this omitted system refutes that fixed exhaustive assertion. Allowing an
amended rank range or suitable truncations can repair this particular omission;
the manuscript says so explicitly and does not claim such an amendment gives
a complete classification.
