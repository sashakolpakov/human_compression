# Coset certificates checked by Lean

Lean now verifies the first two stages of the label-eleven upper-bound argument:

| Presented group | Formal conclusion |
|---|---|
| Two involutions with product of order dividing 11 | The group has at most 22 elements. |
| The exact rank-three presentation, including all ten triple-power relators | The subgroup generated by the first two involutions has finite index at most 1,024. |

The second certificate contains **3,072 proved transitions on 1,024 names**. The [full manuscript](research/case_study/phase219_coset_certificates.md) explains the deduction system and its interface to the actual presented group. The [independent review](research/case_study/phase226_coset_review.md) and [publication record](provenance/phase118_review.md) state exactly what has been checked.

## Why the certificate proves an upper bound

Assign a representative word to each name. Interpret a name as the actual right coset of that word. Every edge must be derived from a literal representative-word definition, a subgroup loop, a reversed or concatenated proved path, or a defining relator with one missing edge. Once every name has an outgoing proved edge for every generator, every group word leads to a named coset. This proves that the names cover all cosets, and hence bounds their number.

The general Lean file proves this argument for an arbitrary subgroup, including a proof that the coset space is finite. It defines the universal group as the free group modulo the normal closure of exactly the generator squares and supplied relators. There is no assumed finite group order or external claim that the names exhaust the cosets.

The two small dihedral certificates use a proved executable trace checker. The larger certificate contains individual Lean deductions and an array carrying the proof of every row. A further theorem identifies its literal relator list with the source formula. All generated deductions are checked by Lean's kernel; the generator and the original coset table are not trusted proof oracles.

## Files, reproduction and limits

- [CosetUpperCertificate.lean](research/case_study/formal/CosetUpperCertificate.lean): the inference rules, their soundness, the executable checker and the presented-group index theorem.
- [CosetUpperExamples.lean](research/case_study/formal/CosetUpperExamples.lean): the dihedral certificates and four proved rejection examples.
- [LabelElevenRankThree.lean](research/case_study/formal/LabelElevenRankThree.lean): the complete rank-three certificate and literal source-relator identity.

Use Lean 4.32.2, the pinned package revisions, and a new output directory:

```sh
python3 -B -O research/case_study/phase219_check.py --out /tmp/NEW-coset-proof --lean LEAN --packages PACKAGES
```

The replay regenerates both concrete source files byte for byte, compiles all three files, checks 25 selected axiom reports and rejects two changes that remove essential checks. The four small rejection examples are themselves Lean theorems. No `native_decide` is used. The compiler, kernel and cached dependency objects retain their stated trust roles.

Rank-four and rank-five coset certificates still use the separate exact checker. The concrete dihedral-to-facet map needed to compose the rank-three
group-order bound 22,528 is also not yet formalized. The large matrix-group
calculations and the [complete label-eleven refutation](LABEL_ELEVEN_OBSTRUCTION.md) are not fully formalized in Lean. This package strengthens verification of that existing result; it does not add a solved question or a recovered source manuscript.

Classical coset enumeration supplies the deduction method; Li–Zhang supplies the source presentation; Mathlib supplies the free-group, quotient and finite-cardinality infrastructure. The contribution here is the formal inference system, its soundness proof and the concrete certificates. The roughly one-megabyte generated proof is a real part of the package's cost. No compression ratio is assigned to it.
