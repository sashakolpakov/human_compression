# Infinitely many finite minimal covers in rank four

**Every finite rank-four polytope whose distinguished monodromy group is
not a string C-group has infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic finite
minimal regular polytopal covers.** This extends the earlier five-family
results to every finite rank-four polytope satisfying that condition.

The [complete seven-page proof](research/case_study/phase256_two_stage_rank_four.pdf)
has a [source text](research/case_study/phase256_two_stage_rank_four.md),
[independent mathematical review](research/case_study/phase273_rank_four_review.md)
and [root review](research/case_study/phase274_root_review.md). The frozen
author text predates review and retains its candidate label; these separate
reviews accept its exact theorem. Historical priority remains unverified.

The stronger group theorem states: if a finite group G is generated by
four distinct nonidentity string involutions, with the first outside the
last three-generator subgroup and the last outside the first, then every
prime p>4|G| admits a finite marked string C-group cover T → G whose kernel
is a p-group. No intersection property is assumed for G.

The Cayley complex with the three commuting-square types is a surface with
boundary. Its first homology cover repairs both rank-three ends. A middle
boundary class outside an end is transverse to that end's homology image.
Translating a path by this class avoids both chosen ends: each visited
vertex excludes at most two field elements, and p>4|G| suffices for the
required spanning-tree loops. An odd loop also preserves nonorientability
where needed. Filling the middle boundary circles and taking a second
homology cover then gives the complete intersection property.

A finite minimal regular quotient still covering the original polytope
has a nontrivial p-group kernel over its monodromy. Different primes give
different group orders, proving infinitude. This is a ratio of group
orders over monodromy; it need not equal the ratio of flag counts over
the original polytope.
