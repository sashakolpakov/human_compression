\section{The common arguments and their applications}
A connected graph on a set $\Omega$ gives generators $e_u+e_v$ for the even
finite-support binary module $E(\Omega)$. Products around closed walks give the
relations. Choosing paths from a base vertex constructs an inverse to this
presentation. If a group $H$ acts on the graph, its conjugates of a suitable set
of edge differences consequently generate $E(\Omega)\rtimes H$.
This is the common generation argument used by the generalized cubes and the
polyhedra with equal adjacent parameters. The cube application still retains
Danzer's construction and its full automorphism-group theorem.

For the latter polyhedra, the separate recognition theorem constructs the inverse
$(v,h)\mapsto T(v)j(h)$ from actual translation and group homomorphisms.
Covariance makes this map multiplicative, and the explicit generation hypothesis
makes it onto. Cyclic displacement commutativity, attributed to Li--Zhang and
proved in full, verifies the local hypothesis. The application obtains the
universal presentation from two inverse maps. Its proof therefore does not use
the historical finite-order upper bound. The group intersection property, the
matrix change of basis and the polytope correspondence remain explicit local
obligations. The standard last correspondence is a retained theorem.

The geometric operation arguments have a different common starting point.
Typed coset Helly supplies flag completion, while marked subgroup intersections
supply the flag stabilizers. Their roles are distinct. The retained-object
lemma computes the kernel of an operation's action from an equivariant encoding
of its objects. Graph halving and geometric Petrials both use that lemma.
Its use for Petrials can give a nontrivial kernel, so faithfulness is proved
under the later thinness hypothesis rather than presumed at the outset.

Endpoint replacement is governed by an explicit mixed-parabolic intersection
and a least-support condition on a finite index set. In the odd-order case the
new markers recover the old group. The Janko application applies this criterion
to its actual subgroup intersections, then checks typed Helly separately.
The complete classification uses the extension-orbit lemma on proper residues.
Its two full GAP programs remain mathematical dependencies: one checks the
literal constructions and their presentation, and one exhausts the extension
census. The operational driver is not a third mathematical program.
