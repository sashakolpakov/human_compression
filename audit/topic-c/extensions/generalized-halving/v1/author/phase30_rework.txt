## A finite affine construction and its hypotheses

Let M be a finite, thin, residually connected rank-three incidence geometry with types called vertices, edges and faces. Let K act regularly on its chambers. Assume the following concrete map conditions: every edge has two distinct incident vertices and two distinct incident faces; every incident vertex-face pair has exactly two common edges; and an edge common to a vertex and a face makes that vertex-face pair incident. These conditions hold for the seed below and are checked directly, rather than inferred merely from subgroup IP.

Write $\Omega$ for the edge set. For a seed vertex v let $B_v$ be its incident-edge set, and for a seed face f let $D_f$ be its incident-edge set. Let C be a K-invariant binary linear code in $\F_2^\Omega$, and put $N=\F_2^\Omega/C$. Write $q_e$ for the image of the coordinate vector at e. For any block B among the $B_v$ and $D_f$ put $W_B=\operatorname{span}\{q_e:e\in B\}$. Require:

1. All $q_e$ are nonzero and distinct.
2. For every block B, $q_e\in W_B$ if and only if $e\in B$.
3. For every incident seed vertex-face pair (v,f),
   $W_{B_v}\cap W_{D_f}=\operatorname{span}\{q_e:e\in B_v\cap D_f\}$.

The quotient columns span N by construction. No assumption about intersections for nonincident seed vertex-face pairs is needed.

Define a typed incidence system $\Gamma$ as follows. Its type-0 objects are vectors $n\in N$. Its type-1 objects are pairs $(e,n+\langle q_e\rangle)$, identified with the graph edge $\{n,n+q_e\}$. Its type-2 objects are pairs $(v,n+W_{B_v})$; its type-3 objects are pairs $(f,n+W_{D_f})$. The seed labels v and f remain part of each object, even if two affine subspaces happen to coincide.

Vertex-edge incidence is endpoint containment. A vertex is incident with a higher object exactly when it belongs to its affine subspace. An edge (e,L) is incident with (v,S), respectively (f,T), exactly when $e\in B_v$ and $L\subseteq S$, respectively $e\in D_f$ and $L\subseteq T$. Finally, (v,S) and (f,T) are incident exactly when **v and f are incident in the seed and $S\cap T\ne\varnothing$**. Distinct objects of the same type are not incident.

### Affine construction theorem

Under these hypotheses $\Gamma$ is a finite regular hypertope, with regular group G=$N\rtimes K$. It satisfies (B1) and (B2) at types {0,1}.

Here regular hypertope means a thin, residually connected geometry with a type-preserving group acting regularly on chambers. In fact G is its full type-preserving automorphism group.

**Geometry and chambers.** A chamber is uniquely specified by a vector n and a seed chamber (v,e,f): its four objects are n, $(e,n+\langle q_e\rangle)$, $(v,n+W_{B_v})$, and $(f,n+W_{D_f})$. Every flag extends to such a chamber. For a flag containing a vertex, use its vector and extend the prescribed seed flag in M. For a flag containing an edge but no vertex, choose either endpoint; all prescribed higher affine subspaces contain this endpoint, and its prescribed seed data form a flag. A flag containing only an incident higher pair has a common vector by definition and two possible common seed edges. A single higher object contains a vector and has an incident seed chamber. The empty flag also extends. This proves the geometry axiom, with no coset-Helly assumption.

**Thinness.** A panel missing type 0 has the two endpoints of its edge as completions. A panel missing type 1 has its fixed vector and incident seed vertex-face pair, hence exactly their two common edge labels. They give distinct edges by hypothesis 1. A panel missing type 2 has the two possible seed vertices at its prescribed edge-face pair; each determines exactly one affine coset through the fixed vector. The dual argument treats type 3. Thus every panel has two completions.

**Residual connectivity.** The residue at a vector n is exactly M, with types reordered as edges, vertices, faces: each seed higher label has a unique affine coset through n. Thus all flags containing a vertex have the required connected residues. At an edge, the residue consists of its two endpoints, its two seed vertices, and its two seed faces, with every cross-type pair incident. It is the join of three two-point sets; flags containing an edge therefore also have connected residues whenever their corank is at least two.

The residue at a single higher object (v,S) contains the connected Cayley graph on the affine space S with directions $\{q_e:e\in B_v\}$. Every remaining higher object in this residue contains a common vector with S by the definition of incidence, and hence attaches to this connected lower truncation. The same holds for a type-3 object. For an incident pair (v,S),(f,T), its residue is precisely the vertex-edge graph on the affine intersection $S\cap T$ with directions $\{q_e:e\in B_v\cap D_f\}$. Hypothesis 3 makes it connected. Finally, the global lower graph is connected because all $q_e$ span N, and every higher object contains lower vertices. These cases exhaust all flags of corank at least two in rank four.

**Regularity.** Translation by N and the induced K-action on N and on the seed labels preserve incidence. The chamber parametrization above shows that $N\rtimes K$ acts freely and transitively: translate the vector, then use the regular action of K on seed chambers. A thin, residually connected geometry of finite rank is chamber connected (induct on rank inside residues, using connected incidence graphs). An automorphism fixing a chamber fixes each adjacent chamber by thinness and hence every chamber. Therefore any type-preserving automorphism is the unique element of G with its prescribed action on a base chamber.

**The two leaf conditions.** Hypothesis 1 makes the lower graph simple, which is (B1). An edge with endpoints in S=n+W_B has direction $q_e\in W_B$; hypothesis 2 says exactly that $e\in B$, and then it is incident with the higher object by definition. This is (B2).

## The seed and the binary quotient

Use permutations of {1,…,7}:

$$c=(1\,2)(3\,4)(6\,7),\quad b=(1\,5)(2\,3)(6\,7),\quad d=(1\,3)(2\,4)(6\,7).$$

Let $K=\langle c,b,d\rangle$. The exact checker obtains |K|=120, |cb|=|bd|=5, cd=dc, and all 64 subgroup-IP meets. The permutation group is $A_5\times C_2$, although that name is not needed in the proof. Take the seed vertex, edge and face objects to be the left cosets of $\langle b,d\rangle$, $\langle c,d\rangle$ and $\langle c,b\rangle$, with incidence defined by nonempty intersection. The checker enumerates this incidence system itself: 12 vertices, 30 edges, 12 faces, 355 flags and 120 chambers; every flag extends to a chamber, every panel has size two, every residue of corank at least two is connected, and K is regular on chambers. Thus this is an actual regular map of type {5,5}.

Each vertex-star and each face-boundary contains five edges. There are 60 incident vertex-face pairs, each with exactly two common edges; nonincident pairs have no common edge. Define C to be the span of the characteristic vectors of these 24 five-edge blocks in $\F_2^{30}$. This definition is K-invariant because K permutes the two block families; the checker also verifies invariance under c,b,d explicitly.

Exact binary row reduction gives dim C=17, dim N=13, and the following complete small certificate:

- 30 nonzero, pairwise distinct quotient columns;
- 24 block spans of dimension four;
- all 720 column-in-block-span membership tests satisfy hypothesis 2;
- all 60 incident block-span intersections equal their two-column spans, of dimension two.

The concrete permutation marking, coset labels, code basis, quotient columns and block lists are included in the checker output. These are finite mathematical data. Neither the code computation nor its certificate substitutes for the global geometry proof in the affine construction theorem above.

Each five-column block has sum zero and rank four. Its only nonempty linear dependence is therefore the sum of all five columns. In particular its affine Cayley graph has a simple five-cycle and is nonbipartite. At an incident higher pair, the common shadow has two independent directions and hence is a square.

## Distinguished involutions and sizes

Choose the seed base chamber at the identity cosets, and let $e_0$=$\langle c,d\rangle$. Let a be translation by $q_{e_0}$, and regard b,c,d as elements of the complement K in G=$N\rtimes K$. The chamber-adjacent involutions of $\Gamma$, in type order, are (a,b,c,d). Indeed a exchanges the endpoints of its affine edge, b exchanges the two seed edges at the prescribed vertex-face pair, c changes the seed vertex, and d changes the seed face.

The edge stabilizer $\langle c,d\rangle$ fixes $q_{e_0}$, so a commutes with c,d. The two columns $q_{e_0}$,$q_{be_0}$ are distinct, and $(ab)^2$ is their nonzero sum, a translation of order two. Thus

$$|ab|=4,\quad |bc|=|bd|=5,\quad |ac|=|ad|=|cd|=2.$$ 

The diagram is a star with centre b and labels 4,5,5. In particular a is a nondegenerate leaf of the required kind. Conjugates of a by K give all column translations, so these four involutions generate G. This marking has actual subgroup IP because it is the distinguished marking of the regular hypertope just proved, not because an arbitrary involutory marking is assumed to define a geometry.

The object and chamber counts are

$$|N|=8192,\qquad |G|=983040,$$

$$\text{object counts}=(8192,122880,6144,6144),\qquad\text{chambers}=983040.$$ 

The standard maximal parabolics have orders 120,8,160,160. For example a higher-object stabilizer is its 16-element translation space extended by its 10-element seed stabilizer. The base edge stabilizer has order eight. An incident pair of higher objects has a square residue, with eight chambers; hence there are 983040/8=122880 such pairs.

## The geometric failure after halving

The global lower graph is nonbipartite, since each higher shadow already contains a five-cycle. All individual higher shadows are nonbipartite, so Construction 3.6 of the inspected source does not split any higher object by parity. Its two new lower types are the two labelled copies of N, with incidence (n,0)∼(m,1) precisely when n,m were adjacent in the original lower graph. Incidence with each unsplit higher object is affine containment in either copy. Higher-higher incidence is unchanged here.

Fix any incident pair of higher objects. Its original common shadow is a square. Its new rank-two residue is the canonical bipartite double cover of that square, namely two disjoint squares. Thus the new incidence geometry is not residually connected. There are exactly 122880 such failed residues. This is a literal local description, not an inference from a numerical graph test. The original affine construction has already established geometry, thinness, residual connectivity, regularity, (B1), (B2), and the leaf diagram independently.

For clarity, the transformed incidence system is still a geometry and is thin: a flag of higher objects has a common original vector, and, for a higher pair, a common adjacent original pair; each transformed flag extends using the original chamber description. The diagonal action of G on both labelled lower copies is regular on transformed chambers, again indexed by oriented original edges and their two seed higher objects. The rank-two residues above have more than one chamber-connected component; a free and transitive action on the global chambers does not repair those disconnected residues.

## A direct subgroup-IP failure of the halved marking

Put $a^{\prime}=aba$ and $H=\langle a^{\prime},b,c,d\rangle$. In the local group $U_c=\langle a,b,c\rangle$, the subgroup $\langle b,c\rangle$ is dihedral of order ten and is transitive on the five edges of the base face. The translation $a^{\prime}b$ is $q_{e_0}$+$q_{be_0}$. Consider the orbit of the unordered pair {$e_0$,b $e_0$} under this transitive degree-five action. Its graph is connected: all components have the same size dividing five, and the existence of an edge rules out singleton components. Pair differences along a connected graph span all even sums of its coordinate vectors. Thus the conjugates of $a^{\prime}b$ span the even sums of the five face columns. Since the sum of all five columns is zero, these even sums already span the whole four-dimensional space. Consequently $a\in\langle a^{\prime},b,c\rangle$ and

$$\langle a\prime,b,c\rangle=\langle a,b,c\rangle,\qquad |\langle a,b,c\rangle|=160.$$ 

The same argument with $\langle b,d\rangle$ and the base vertex-star gives

$$\langle a\prime,b,d\rangle=\langle a,b,d\rangle,\qquad |\langle a,b,d\rangle|=160.$$ 

In particular H=G. Actual IP for the original regular marking gives

$$\langle a\prime,b,c\rangle\cap\langle a\prime,b,d\rangle=\langle a,b\rangle,\qquad |\langle a,b\rangle|=8.$$ 

But $a^{\prime}$ and b are distinct commuting involutions, since $(ab)^4=1$, and $\langle a^{\prime},b\rangle$ has order four. It does not contain a: in the dihedral group $\langle a,b\rangle$ of order eight, the subgroup $\langle aba,b\rangle$ consists of the elements having an even number of occurrences of a. Thus a is an explicit witness to the failed meet. The final checker also constructs both local semidirect groups and this strict intersection directly.

This obstruction is precisely the nonprincipal recovery family from the general halving criterion: a is recovered after adjoining c or d separately, but not before either is adjoined. Here the original incidence system is an actual regular hypertope, unlike the earlier S₆ pregeometry separator.

## Source and dependency boundary of the regular example

The inspected primary is Piedade–Tranchida, arXiv:2405.19050v1 (29 May 2024). Its exact input identity is recorded with this assembly. The source's broad Theorem A and its residual-connectivity assertion in Proposition 3.13 / Theorem 3.18 fail on this example, now even with regular thin input. In §4, the sentence immediately before Theorem 4.4 says the resulting object must be a regular hypertope; this claimed consequence also fails, as does the assertion that the halved distinguished generators must form a C-group.

The literal sentences of Theorem B and Theorem 4.4 assert a coset-isomorphism, a narrower statement. This note does **not** claim to refute that isomorphism. Indeed equality of the local new and old higher parabolics is compatible with the transformed geometry still having its asserted coset description while failing residual connectivity. We do not conflate those assertions.

The revised journal article, Combinatorica 45 (2025), article 10, DOI 10.1007/s00493-024-00134-y, has a later revision date. Its full revised body was not available in the bounded source audit. No claim about that body, absence of a subsequent correction, or novelty is made here.

The mathematical construction uses elementary permutation-group closure, binary linear algebra, the explicitly proved affine construction theorem, and the elementary chamber-connectivity property of finite-rank thin residually connected geometries. It uses no subgroup catalogue, no presumed quotient-IP theorem, and no full enumeration of the 983040-element group or the global incidence system. The retained parity terminology and source identification are separate from the direct square-residue and local-subgroup proofs above.

## The complete finite certificate

The original checker `phase30_affine_map_check.py` produces `phase30_affine_map_check.json`. It uses Python's standard library only. The whole program and its saved exact output accompany this paper. The replay command is recorded in the assembly guide.

The completed check enumerates all 355 seed flags, their extensions and residues, all 64 seed subgroup meets, all 720 column memberships, all 60 literal four-element span intersections, 72 code-generator and 51 code-basis invariance tests, and the two old and two changed local semidirect groups of order 160. It verifies all six marked product orders and the explicit excess-intersection witness a. It also constructs the eight-vertex doubled square and its two four-vertex components. The JSON includes both 30-coordinate quotient representatives and compressed 13-bit columns, all block labels, a basis of the code, and the induced generator action on the quotient.

The independent parent checker `phase30_regular_halving_root_check.py` uses $A_5\times C_2$ product coordinates instead of seven-point permutations and separately checks the same seed geometry, binary hypotheses and local subgroup failure. Neither script enumerates the whole 983040-element group or its global incidence system. Global geometric conclusions and their derived counts depend on the affine construction and local recovery proofs above. The earlier exploratory probe is excluded from the final authored export manifest.
