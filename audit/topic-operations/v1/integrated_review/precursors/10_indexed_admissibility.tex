Let $\alpha=(A,(A_i)_{i\in I})$ be a coset geometry of finite rank.
Write $A_J=\bigcap_{j\in J}A_j$, $A_\varnothing=A$,
$H=A_I$, and $R_i=A_{I\setminus\{i\}}$.
This note relates the unlabelled admissibility condition in
Piedade--Tranchida, arXiv:2505.24662v1, Definition 3.30, to the indexed
condition used in our note \emph{Coset Helly properties in an HNN extension}.
The distinction matters when a geometry is not firm.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:boolean}
Suppose $R_i>H$ for every $i\in I$. Then
\[
 A_J\leq A_K\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K\subseteq J.
\]
Consequently the lattice of standard parabolics, ordered by inclusion,
is the reverse Boolean lattice on $I$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $K\subseteq J$, the inclusion follows from the definition of an
intersection. Conversely, suppose $k\in K\setminus J$. Then
$R_k\leq A_J$, because every member of $J$ differs from $k$.
If $A_J\leq A_K$, it follows that $R_k\leq A_k$, whence
$R_k=R_k\cap A_k=H$, a contradiction. This proves the equivalence,
including injectivity of $J\mapsto A_J$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:firm}
Suppose $\alpha$ is flag-transitive and firm. Let
$\phi:A_J\longrightarrow A_K$ be an isomorphism inducing an isomorphism
of the unlabelled lattices of standard parabolics contained in its domain
and codomain. Then there is a unique bijection
$f:I\setminus J\longrightarrow I\setminus K$ such that
\[
 \phi(A_{J\cup X})=A_{K\cup f(X)}
 \qquad(X\subseteq I\setminus J).
\]
Thus the indexed hypothesis of our HNN theorem follows from the source's
admissibility condition for firm flag-transitive inputs.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Flag transitivity identifies the chambers on a standard panel of missing
type $i$ with $R_i/H$. Firmness therefore implies $[R_i:H]\geq2$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:boolean} applies. The standard parabolics contained in
$A_J$ are precisely the $A_{J\cup X}$, and their coatoms are
$A_{J\cup\{i\}}$ for $i\notin J$. The isomorphism permutes coatoms
bijectively, giving $f$. Since it preserves intersections, intersecting
these coatom equalities proves the formula for every $X$, including
$X=\varnothing$.

For completeness, form the equivalence classes $s$ of the arrows
$i\mapsto f(i)$ on $I$. Bijection of the domain and range gives
$f(s\setminus J)=s\setminus K$. Hence
\[
 \phi(A_s\cap A_J)
 =\bigcap_{i\in s\setminus J}\phi(A_{J\cup\{i\}})
 =\bigcap_{j\in s\setminus K}A_{K\cup\{j\}}
 =A_s\cap A_K.
\]
Both displayed intersections of images are taken inside $A_K$; if
their index sets are empty they mean $A_K$, in agreement with
$\phi(A_J)=A_K$. Every $A_s$ is therefore compatible. These are precisely the
subgroups required by the local coset Helly theorem. Its flag-transitivity
conclusion follows; its residual-connectivity conclusion also requires
residual connectivity of the input, as stated there.
\end{proof}

\section*{Why an unlabelled lattice is not sufficient in general}
Let $A$ be the additive group of $\mathbf F_2^3$, with basis
$e_1,e_2,e_3$, and take four indexed subgroups
\[
 A_1=\langle e_2,e_3\rangle,\quad
 A_2=\langle e_1,e_3\rangle,\quad
 A_3=\langle e_1,e_2\rangle,\quad
 A_4=\langle e_3\rangle=A_1\cap A_2.
\]
This coset geometry is flag-transitive. Indeed, each coset prescribes
values of some coordinates. Pairwise intersecting cosets have no
conflicting coordinate prescriptions, so all of them have a common
representative. Every such flag extends to a chamber by that representative.
The geometry is not firm: $H=0$ and, for example, $R_1=0$.

Put $J=K=\varnothing$, so both associated subgroups are $A$, and let
$\phi$ cyclically permute the basis,
$e_1\mapsto e_2\mapsto e_3\mapsto e_1$.
The unlabelled standard parabolics are exactly the eight coordinate
subspaces. Thus $\phi$ preserves their whole lattice and is admissible
under the literal Definition 3.30. Its action on the three planes is
$A_1\mapsto A_2\mapsto A_3\mapsto A_1$. The image
$\phi(A_4)=\langle e_1\rangle$ is a standard parabolic but is not one
of the four individually indexed subgroups. Neither is
$\phi^{-1}(A_4)=\langle e_2\rangle$.
The equivalence classes in Definition 3.31 are consequently
$\{1,2,3\}$ and $\{4\}$.

For $s=\{4\}$ the compatibility asserted in the proof of Lemma 3.32
fails:
\[
 \phi(A_s\cap A)=\langle e_1\rangle
 \ne\langle e_3\rangle=A_s\cap A.
\]
The resulting intersection formula on source page 29 is false as well.
In the HNN extension
\[
 G=\langle A,t\mid t^{-1}vt=\phi(v)\ (v\in A)\rangle
\]
one has $G_s=\langle t,e_3\rangle=G$, since conjugating $e_3$ by
$t$ and $t^2$ supplies $e_1$ and $e_2$. Also $F=J\setminus K$ is
empty, so $G_t=A_F=A$. It follows that
\[
 G_t\cap G_s=A,\qquad A_{F\cup s}=A_4=\langle e_3\rangle,
\]
whose orders are eight and two. Normal forms ensure that $A$ embeds
in this HNN extension, so these orders do not collapse.

This example refutes the claimed compatibility and the displayed
intersection formula in their stated generality. It does not refute
the existence of the abstract HNN extension, nor does it show that
$G_s$ cannot be a special subgroup with some larger base subgroup.
It also does not disprove the source's broad flag-transitivity conclusion:
the particular output just constructed is still flag-transitive. Its
maximal parabolics are $\langle t\rangle,G,A$; the $G$-coset is a
single element incident with everything, and $A\langle t\rangle=G$
shows that every pair of the other types meets and has a common
representative.
The firm case is covered by Corollary~\ref{cor:firm}; more general
inputs require explicit indexed compatibility or a separately proved
replacement construction.
