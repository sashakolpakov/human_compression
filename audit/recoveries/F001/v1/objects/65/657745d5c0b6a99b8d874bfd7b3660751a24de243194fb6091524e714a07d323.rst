Exact classification interface
-------------------------------
Monson's Theorem 4.1 applies to a finite irreducible group generated by exactly
dim(V) reflections, in odd characteristic, preserving a nonzero bilinear form.
Its proof explicitly excludes the deleted symmetric module and the exceptional
five-dimensional modular E6 group because they need one more reflection than
the dimension. Table 3, together with its dimension and characteristic entries,
leaves in dimension five only orthogonal type or the faithful standard modular
A5, B5 and D5 representations. The A5 entry requires p not dividing six.
The general classification behind this specialization remains an external
input and is not replaced by the brief course proof.

The manuscript deliberately allows an independently chosen defining field
q_0 of the same characteristic. This avoids reading the q in the notes as
an unjustified assertion that every group remains maximal over an arbitrary
coefficient field. Orthogonal-type entries contain Omega_5(q_0), preserve a
nondegenerate form defined over that field, and contain a reflection outside
that subgroup. The stated order formula agrees with the source table.
The permitted q_0=3 case is included in the subsequent order comparison.

Gram path and absolute irreducibility
--------------------------------------
For distinct orthogonal reflections, commutation is equivalent to perpendicular
root lines. The string Gram matrix is therefore tridiagonal with all adjacent
off-diagonal entries nonzero. Its kernel has dimension at most one, and any
nonzero kernel vector has both end coordinates nonzero, by the row recurrence
from either end. Rank at most five gives at most six roots. At six roots the
Gram rank is exactly five, so they span V and the Gram kernel is exactly their
linear relation space. Omitting either endpoint leaves a basis.

An invariant subspace of either end group either is fixed pointwise by all
five reflections or contains one of their roots. The first option puts it in
the perpendicular of a basis and gives zero. In the second, nonzero adjacent
pairings propagate membership to every basis root. This proof survives scalar
extension and establishes the absolute irreducibility actually needed later.
It does not appeal to a characteristic-zero irreducibility criterion.

Field of definition and the size argument
-----------------------------------------
After classification conjugacy, an orthogonal end group preserves a form B_0
over F_(q_0). Absolute irreducibility makes its original nondegenerate invariant
form a scalar multiple of B_0. The negative eigenspace of each of the five
end reflections is defined over F_(q_0). The first four independent root lines
span a field-defined four-space. The sixth root line is its one-dimensional
perpendicular, since the sixth reflection commutes with the first four and has
a distinct line. It is nonsingular because it is an actual reflection root.
Thus the sixth reflection also has coefficients over F_(q_0) and preserves B_0.
This proves containment of the whole ordinary six-reflection group in O_5(q_0),
without assuming q_0 is an actual subfield of the original matrix entries.

The determinant twist pi(g)=det(g)g is a homomorphism with kernel {I,-I} in
odd dimension five. On an ordinary reflection it is its negative. The end
group has order at least 2 omega(q_0), so its signed image inside Omega_5(q)
has order at least omega(q_0). Strict monotonicity gives q_0<=q. If q_0<q,
the same odd prime characteristic gives q>=3q_0; the displayed order formula
then gives omega(q)>4 omega(q_0), contradicting containment of the full signed
image in O_5(q_0). Equality q_0=q makes the signed end image the whole group.
This step treats possible extension fields by actual orders, not an unproved
field-minimality assertion.

Finite end types and their overlap
----------------------------------
For A5, p does not divide six. In the five-dimensional standard summand of the
six-point permutation representation, an involution's negative eigenspace has
dimension equal to its number of transposed pairs. Hence its reflections are
exactly the transpositions, and noncommuting adjacent reflections have product
order three. No other involution class becomes a reflection in this reduction.

For B5 in every odd characteristic, a reflection is either a single coordinate
sign change or a signed coordinate transposition. Generating its S5 permutation
image needs at least four long reflections, whereas generating B5 requires a
short reflection. Five generators therefore give exactly four long roots whose
coordinate edges form a tree and one short root. An internal short root would
have two neighbors with different tree edges incident to the same coordinate.
Those long roots are not perpendicular, contradicting their distance two in
the string. Thus the short root is at an endpoint. Its adjacent product has
order four and the remaining adjacent products have order three.

For D5 all noncommuting reflection pairs have product order three. A string
of five such generators would be a quotient of the finite Coxeter A5=S6,
whose order 720 is smaller than 1920. It cannot generate D5. Therefore each
proper finite end has bond sequence 3333 or 4333, up to reversal. Overlap of
the two length-four bond sequences forces the middle three of the full five
bonds to be three; only the outside bonds may be four.

Four Gram possibilities and the final contradiction
---------------------------------------------------
After normalizing root norms over an algebraic closure, the square pairing
for an order-three product is 1/4 and for order four is 1/2. The two-reflection
trace formula is valid in odd characteristic; in characteristic three an
order-three restriction is unipotent with trace 2=-1, giving the same value.
The tridiagonal determinant recurrence gives the four displayed patterns.
Their denominators are powers of two, so reduction in any odd characteristic
is legitimate. Six roots force determinant zero. Exactly one outside four is
impossible. All bonds three force characteristic seven, and the ordinary group
is a quotient of A6=S7 of order at most 5040, too small for the required full
Omega_5(q).

For both outside bonds four, the six displayed coordinate roots have exactly
the same squared pairings. Successive sign choices along the path identify
all their Gram entries. The first five normalized roots have determinant 1/16,
nonzero in every odd characteristic. The isometry determined by those five
roots also maps the sixth, since its pairings with the basis determine it.
All six reflections are thus conjugate to signed permutation matrices and
generate a group of order at most 3840, again too small. This is a uniform
matrix argument, not a numerical extrapolation from a few finite fields.

Consequently a connected six-term negative-reflection string generating the
full group has a full end group. Actual IP excludes that in a C-marking:
a missing generator cannot belong to the subgroup on the other generators.
Simplicity makes a C-marking of the full group connected, since disconnected
nonempty components would give commuting nontrivial normal subgroups. Together
with the Gram bound this proves rank at most five in the specified class.
