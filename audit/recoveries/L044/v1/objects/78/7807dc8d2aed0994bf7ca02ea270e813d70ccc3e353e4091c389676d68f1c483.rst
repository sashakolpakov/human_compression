The minimum left endpoint union
-------------------------------
For h>=1, one anchored left action has active length h. The acquired lower
bound and |C|=h+2 force a left orbit A_0 of size h+1 and a singleton b.
The reversed proper-prefix bound gives |P|>=h, where P=a^{L'}, and the
surviving crossing joins P to b inside a single M-orbit. Thus C minus that
orbit has at most one point. If an R-trivial M-orbit met P or b, R would fix
both anchors, then their whole L-orbits by commutation. Those become the two
actual LR-omission sides and exhaust W, contrary to the stipulated active R.
If such an orbit meets the possible remaining point z, R fixes z^L=A_0.
The actual side is consequently A_0. Its only exit under the crossing is at a,
which lies in the different orbit containing P and b. The orbit of z therefore
stays within A_0 minus P and is a singleton. This proves the prohibition in
Lemma 1 without assuming that an M-orbit is itself contained in C.

For a fixed z in C, the crossing excludes both anchors, so z belongs to A_0
minus P. The same commutation makes A_0 an actual side, and equality gives
P=A_0 minus z. On the other side L fixes b and commutes with R, so the side
is b^R. The surviving left factor, crossing and right factor join the entire
complement of z. R is nontrivial only on that complement. For h=0, M=R;
a fixed endpoint is a singleton actual side and the other is the nontrivial
transitive R-orbit. Both assertions cover this short case directly.

One additional point and the shorter witness
--------------------------------------------
For h>=2 and |C|=h+3 the two possible left orbit sizes are (h+1,2) and
(h+2,1). In the former case the two-point orbit is exchanged by the nearest
left mark, which survives the first omission. All farther left marks fix
both points; commutation with R makes them fix the whole corresponding actual
side. Hence an M-fixed z belongs to the large left orbit, which R fixes
pointwise. Its surviving left orbit has all h remaining points, and the
nearest left mark together with R connects the opposite side. There is one
fixed point and a complementary orbit with nontrivial R-action.

In the (h+2,1) case the opposite actual side is the nontrivial transitive
R-orbit. Its M-orbit also contains the at least h points of P. At most two
points remain, one already the fixed z. There can be no second nontrivial
M-orbit; all remaining fixed points are in C. The short h=1 case is written
separately: C consists of two L-pairs, and a fixed point makes one whole pair
R-fixed. The opposite side has one or two R-orbits permuted by L. Each meets
its L-pair and none can be a singleton, since that would force R to be trivial
everywhere. The crossing joins one of these nontrivial R-orbits to the other
endpoint; any internal crossing edges can only merge existing orbits. Thus
every nontrivial M-orbit meets C and has nontrivial R-action, with its only
fixed point in C. This proves the complete stated Lemma 2, including h=1.

When the shorter witness ends at j and has minimum size h+2, its right
factor is trivial. The joined surviving anchor orbits already contain at
least h+1 points in one first-omission orbit. The assumed intransitivity
forces exactly one additional fixed point. No classification of the marks
or quotient intersection property is required in Lemma 3.

Nonterminal shift and exact distributions
-----------------------------------------
If s=j+1 were terminal, y=s and the two complete witnesses together with the
seven head points occupy at least n-3 points fixed by delta. An odd involution
on at most three remaining points is a literal transposition. The acquired
exterior-pair obstruction applies because its actual head, natural suffix,
degree and ambient-pair hypotheses are all retained. Hence s is nonterminal.
The seven head points, O_1 and C are pairwise disjoint subsets of F=Fix(G_{>s}).
Their lower sizes are 7,h+2,h+2. Failure of the required 2j+3 bound leaves
precisely the three distributions listed, with at most one extra point.
Sigma centralizes K and the suffix, so sends actual K-orbits to actual
K-orbits and preserves F. This supplies the exact orbit comparisons.

When both lower bounds are equalities and h>=1, the shorter witness consists
of a singleton z_1 and a J-trivial orbit T_1 of size h+1>=2. Lemma 1 excludes
its image meeting C; the fixed head and at most one extra point cannot host
it either. Thus T_1 is preserved. The actual head anchor cannot equal z_1,
or the head and shorter witness form a proper invariant union. If the anchor
lies in C, Lemma 1 gives a singleton and a J-nontrivial complementary orbit
T_2 on O_2. If T_2 moved, its at least two points in C would have to map into
F outside O_2, excluding J-trivial T_1 and all K-fixed points. At most the
one extra point remains, contradicting injectivity. Hence T_2 is preserved.
The K-fixed points in F now complete an invariant union. An extra K-fixed
point is explicitly H-fixed and delta-fixed: rho_5 preserves F and its other
constituents, while delta fixes those constituents pointwise. Adjoining that
point therefore preserves the required generator-by-generator invariance.
If the head anchor is instead the extra point, z_1 cannot be sigma-fixed;
its image is a K-fixed point of C. Applying Lemma 1 there gives the same
preserved complementary orbit and the same contradiction.

When O_1 has the extra point, h=0 is impossible for a transitive involution
on three points. Every nontrivial K-orbit in O_1 is J-trivial and, by Lemma 1,
its image stays in O_1. Either the head anchor is in C or failure of invariance
of the head with O_1 sends one of its fixed points into C. Lemma 1 then yields
a single J-nontrivial complementary orbit on O_2. A point of it in C cannot
map outside O_2, where all nontrivial K-orbits meeting F are J-trivial. The
K-fixed points of F finish the same invariant union.

When C has the extra point, h=0 is again impossible. The joined set E of
surviving anchor orbits has at least h+1 points and belongs to a J-nontrivial
K-orbit, since otherwise J would fix both anchors and hence all of O_2.
For h>=2, a moved T_1 would have to fit into C minus E, of size at most two,
although |T_1|>=3. For h=1 its only possible image is the two other endpoints
of the two L-pairs. J-triviality there and commutation with L would also fix
the original anchors and make J trivial on O_2. Thus T_1 is preserved in
both cases. The actual head anchor lies in C, so Lemma 2 applies. Every
nontrivial K-orbit on O_2 meets C and has nontrivial J-action; its image cannot
leave O_2. All K-fixed points on O_2 are in C. The complete invariant union
is therefore justified, not inferred from a partial collection of points.

Colour five and the actual copied orbit
---------------------------------------
Here O_1 is a two-point H-orbit fixed by K and C is the two crossing endpoints.
Only the first distribution is possible. Delta fixes F, including its possible
extra point. Any extra K-fixed point is H-fixed because rho_5 preserves F,
O_1, the head and C. The proof considers all locations of the actual anchor.
If it lies in O_1, the other point cannot map within the head and O_1; if it
maps to the extra point, adjoining that H-fixed, delta-fixed point already
gives a forbidden invariant union. If the anchor is the extra point, O_1
cannot be sigma-invariant. These cases, and the immediate anchor-in-C case,
produce an actual K-fixed endpoint w in C.

Lemma 1 gives exactly {w} and a transitive K-orbit T in O_2. If T were
preserved by sigma, the head, both witnesses and the extra point when K-fixed
would again be invariant. Thus V=T^sigma is a genuinely distinct actual
K-orbit. The image of the other endpoint in C lies in V intersect F; it
cannot lie in the fixed head, O_1 or O_2. It must be the extra point q.
Delta fixes q and centralizes K, hence fixes all of V. This is an actual
orbit-copy argument with distinctness paid; no transported second witness
is merely presumed new. All four sets, namely the head, O_1, O_2 and V, are
disjoint and fixed after y. Their total size is at least 2y+2. If y were
terminal, delta would have at most one point left, impossible. Thus y is
nonterminal and supplies the separator. This completes the claimed theorem.
