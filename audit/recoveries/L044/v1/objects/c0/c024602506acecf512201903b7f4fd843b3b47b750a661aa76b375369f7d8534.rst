New orbit and transport arguments
--------------------------------
The active labels in Lemma2 are the nonidentity restrictions of the tail marks
of H to its actual orbit O. Components of their noncommutation graph generate
commuting factors. A component not containing j centralizes rho_j on O and
lies in the actual G_j. It maps the unique ambient-eligible j-edge in O to
itself. Because G_j preserves each of its own point orbits, it cannot exchange
the two endpoints, so fixes both. A factor of a transitive commuting product
fixing one point fixes all points. This contradicts a nonidentity factor,
proving the diagram connected. The string relation then makes its active
labels an interval containing j. No projected IP is invoked.

For Lemma3, a K-intransitive witness orbit necessarily has nontrivial rho5,
so its active interval starts at5. A K-transitive orbit already starting at5
also satisfies the conclusion. Suppose instead its first active index m>=7.
The complete whole-K-orbit transport gives T=O^p inside a K-intransitive
H-orbit W, preserving the entire marked K-action. Since rho6 fixes T, the
later group L=G_>=7 is transitive on T. Rho5 centralizes L, so its image of T
is an L-orbit. If different, that image is disjoint and carries a second
ambient-eligible j-edge in W; here j>=7 ensures the needed commutation.
This violates the at-most-one-per-orbit hypothesis. Otherwise rho5 preserves
T and its unique witness, fixing its endpoints because rho5 lies in G_j.
Centralization with transitive L then fixes all of T. Hence H preserves T,
forcing W=T and contradicting K-intransitivity. Only the first label6 remains.

The final construction correctly prefers an orbit with two ambient witnesses
whenever one exists. A pair, or a sole witness, is transported by the same
whole-orbit bijection. A colour requiring transport exceeds5, so that
bijection centralizes the mark and belongs to its ambient omission. Distinct
retained edges remain distinct and ambient eligible. All target orbits are
K-intransitive. If every target had a local pair, Lemma1 would contradict the
standing assumptions. Hence some target has exactly one local eligible edge.
The actual full restricted action is transitive; collapsing its local omission
orbits leaves a connected graph with exactly one crossing edge, therefore
exactly two omission orbits.

That edge is the retained ambient witness. If any H-orbit had two ambient
edges of this colour, the preference would have retained and transported two,
contradicting local uniqueness in the target. Thus every H-orbit has at most
one ambient edge of this colour. The normalized target starts at5. The standing
global pair ensures a second witness on a distinct H-orbit, to which the
active-interval and first-label lemmas apply. Its first label is5 or6. The
proof correctly does not transport that second witness and assume its image
is distinct, nor claim local uniqueness or K-intransitivity there.
