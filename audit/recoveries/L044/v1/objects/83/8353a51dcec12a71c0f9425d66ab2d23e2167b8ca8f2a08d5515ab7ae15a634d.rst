Distinct review of neighbours of a square
========================================

Accept all four local conclusions under the exact explicit input: a connected
selection of two eligible edges of each colour, n=2r, with one alternating
square as its unique cycle. Eligibility refers to actual omission orbits.
The degree conclusion requires n>8. Root read the entire new three-page
manuscript and its scope, bindings and contribution statement independently
of the author's discussion with publication_recovery. Earlier normal-form
and connectedness results are acquired from their retained acceptances;
this review does not rereview their complete proofs or the source paper.

Deleting either square colour yields exactly two connected components.
Eligibility keeps them in distinct actual omission orbits, so they are the
orbits themselves. Intersecting the two partitions gives the four root
trees. Every other generator consequently preserves each root tree. An
eligible edge in such a tree needs that colour on its selected tree path.
This justifies counting selected colours after commuting transport, without
assuming that the transported edge is already selected.

The extremal normalization is valid for each fixed admissible square.
If a transported parallel connector is missing, its tree path contains the
other selected connector of that colour. Removing that edge and inserting
the missing one reconnects precisely the same two components and preserves
the unique square. The removed edge cannot be incident with the square:
its tree has only one square vertex, whose unique edge of that colour is
the inserted edge. The incident-edge objective therefore strictly increases.
This proves the claimed normalization, not a property of every selection.

For any still missing actual neighbour, the first selected path colour
must occur twice on the path by eligibility. Both selected edges of that
colour would then occupy the same root tree, so that colour commutes with
neither square mark and must be the common middle index. Transporting the
missing neighbour by a commuting square mark either contradicts the proved
parallel-edge property or requires both middle edges in a different root
tree. This establishes the full-neighbour assertion.

The two-branch argument uses actual eligible transported edges and tree
cuts, not a visual inference. Its four vertices are distinct because a
collision would identify two different-colour edges and violate eligibility.
The transported opposite vertex would have to belong to two disjoint
branches. This contradiction gives the local degree restriction except
for four consecutive labels, after a permitted move in the middle case.

For those four labels, the manuscript explicitly displays eight distinct
vertices and all eight selected edges. For the possible outside colour
a-1 at q, its second edge is in the t-tree while the h-edge has its second
edge in the p-tree; the branch lemma applies. The b+1 edge at s is the
symmetric case. These cases cannot be silently omitted. All other outside
colours fix the square and commute with the two attachment marks, hence
fix the four attachment vertices. Both selected edges of each inside
colour are already in the displayed core. The core is therefore an entire
selected component, contradicting connectedness when n>8. It is not
necessary to assert that every actual inside-colour edge stays in the core.
