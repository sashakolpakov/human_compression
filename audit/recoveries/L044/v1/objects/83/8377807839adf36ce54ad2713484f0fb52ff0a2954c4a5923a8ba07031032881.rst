Proof assessment
----------------

The square-block argument follows directly from a forbidden one-colour cut
cycle. In long-cycle reduction, allowing either one or two missing completing
edges is necessary and sufficient. Every removed edge lies on the old long
cycle; every added edge is in the new complete square and hence on no other
cycle. The number of long cycles therefore decreases, rather than merely
changing a convenient drawing of one cycle.

For incident squares, the proposed new corner lies outside the old component:
any path to it would need both square colours, which a simple path from their
common cut vertex cannot do. Deleting a square edge and joining the new
component destroys one cycle without creating another.

For ordinary transport the case split is complete. When the second connector
edge lies outside the chosen path, either another square disappears or the
remaining path survives with shorter distance. If both connector edges lie on
the shortest path, the first is a bridge. Indeed, if it belonged to a square,
the path would use both opposite connector edges of that square; traversing
those requires the three-edge arc and can be shortened across its remaining
edge. This contradicts shortestness. The component argument then forces the
new corner outside the entire old component; the corrected first-edge removal
joins three components by a tree and strictly decreases the square count.
This supplies the elementary shortest-path justification implicit in the text;
no additional group-theoretic hypothesis is needed.

The global middle-colour exchange is valid even when transported vertices
collide with old square vertices. All other selected colours commute with the
middle involution; their images are actual edges and retain eligibility.
For the middle colour the edge is preserved setwise. Inserting the original
square after transporting the rest cannot create a further cycle by the
square-block lemma. Transporting the connector after its first edge starts
back on the original square and shortens the path. No fresh-vertex assumption,
nonrepeated connector assumption, parity or intersection property is used.

The finite minimization is legitimate: first minimize the square count among
selections with only square cycles, then the distance of a pair in one component.
Each possible first connector colour contradicts one of those minima. Counting
edges in the resulting tree/unicyclic components yields exactly the stated
bound and equality condition. The defect formula remains valid before the
one-square-per-component reduction because all square cycles are edge-disjoint.

The unique-crossing lemma contracts maximal-parabolic orbits to a connected
one-edge graph. A distant generator preserves each endpoint orbit and the
unique crossing edge, hence fixes both endpoints. For the no-gap lemma, words
omitting an intermediate colour factor into two commuting colour intervals;
the distant interval fixes the initial point, and the target generator then
fixes its image under the other interval. This is the required contradiction.
