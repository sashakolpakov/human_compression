Distinct review of two moving singleton-prefix orbits
======================================================

Accepted under the literal orbit decomposition and stated critical singleton
hypotheses. I read the complete proof, scope and actual acquired singleton

The support Y is proper since g<r and |Y|<=2g+1<=2r-1. The anchor P-orbit C
has at least g+1>=5 points, contains a,b, and is a union of H-orbits together
with those fixed points. It therefore contains exactly one of the two moving
H-orbits; containing both would make P transitive on Y. The other orbit E is
itself an actual P-orbit. This justifies rho1 preserving C,E without an
assumption about an orbit projection.

Since rho0 centralizes H, its restriction to a preserved transitive orbit is
either identity or fixed-point-free. Exchanging the two moving orbits or acting
freely on both makes its support all of Y. Rho1 preserves Y and every later
mark centralizes rho0, so this support would be globally invariant. Acting
trivially on both would leave the odd transposition (ab). Acting freely on
the orbit V inside C and trivially on E makes its support exactly C, giving
the same invariant-support contradiction. Thus only free action on E and
identity on V remains. The exchange case could also be excluded immediately
from P-invariance of C,E, but its included support argument is valid.

Strict growth implies x=b^rho1 is a new point in V. On {a,b,x}, the first
marks are (ab),(bx); they preserve this exact three-point orbit. Since G0 is
literally the alternating point stabilizer and P1 is even, ambient IP gives
(P1)_a=P1 intersection G0=<rho1>. Orbit-stabilizer yields the actual order
six of P1, not just the order of its image on these three points. Hence the
relation (rho0 rho1)^3=1 holds also on the exterior action. On V minus x,
rho0 is identity and rho1 preserves the set, so its order divides both two
and three; it is identity there. Outside Y both marks are already identity.

All P1-orbits inside E have sizes dividing six. Freeness of rho0 there
excludes the odd sizes one and three, leaving only two and six. Consequently
E is exactly the union of every P1-orbit of those sizes in the whole ambient
set. The remaining nontrivial orbit has size three; all others are singletons.
Every mark of index at least three centralizes P1 and must preserve that union.
Rho0,rho1 preserve E as a P-orbit, and rho2 preserves E as an H-orbit. Thus E
is invariant under the full transitive group, the required contradiction.
This argument covers arbitrary additional fixed points and uses full actual
IP only at the explicit stabilizer step.
