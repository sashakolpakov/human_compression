Distinct review of the singleton prefix orbit budget
=====================================================

Accepted as a bounded reduction, with no claim that its three residual rows
are eliminated. I read the complete two-page proof and the actual acquired
singleton-prefix, ambient-interval and all-edge confinement proofs. In
particular, the interval theorem genuinely handles an arbitrary contiguous
interval with ambient eligibility, not just a terminal interval. Its proof
checks commuting exit generators on either side. The confinement lemma requires
every eligible edge to stay in the specified invariant set; the application
here proves exactly that premise.

H=G_{2,...,g-1} fixes both a and b because each mark fixes a and commutes with
rho0. Its support lies in the actual support Y of P. Thus the acquired interval
bound gives 2(g-2)<=|Y|-f-c, and hence f+c<=5. H is intransitive on the ambient
set since it fixes a,b; its interval is nonempty. Strict singleton growth gives
|C|>=g+1>=5. No faithfulness of a restriction or projected IP enters this step.

For c=1 the bound gives f<=4, so C meets and contains the single nontrivial
H-orbit U. Since P is intransitive on its moved support, there is a nontrivial
P-orbit outside C; it uses at least two H-fixed points in addition to a,b.
Therefore f=4 and those remaining points form the exterior pair E. Equality
in the budget forces |Y|=2g+1 and |U|=2g-3, odd. A centralizing involution on
an odd transitive orbit is pointwise trivial: its fixed point propagates under
H. This makes rho0=(ab)(uv). The actual P-orbit E is preserved by rho1, while
rho2 fixes it; strict growth makes rho1 send b into U. All conditions of the
literal pair lemma hold.

I checked that pair lemma beyond a mere comparison of orbit sizes. P1 can
have additional two-point orbits, but rho0 acts trivially on every such orbit
outside E. E is therefore the unique two-point P1-orbit with nontrivial rho0
action. An element centralizing both first marks preserves this distinguishing
action, so all later marks preserve E in arbitrary ambient degree. This gives
the required contradiction to transitivity, including extra fixed points.

For an H-pair internally exchanged by rho0, no edge of any relevant colour
on it is ambient-eligible: rho0 already joins the endpoints in that colour's
actual omission. The H-fixed points have no edges of these colours. Hence all
eligible edges lie in the union V of the other nontrivial H-orbits. V is an
actual invariant proper subset and its actual H-orbit count is c-1. The
confinement bound is 2(g-2)<=|Y|-f-c-1. Together with f,c>=2 it forces f=c=2,
|Y|=2g+1, and a unique odd V of size 2g-3>=5. Since this size differs from two,
rho0 preserves and fixes V pointwise. The anchor orbit meets V and contains
it; meeting E too would make P transitive on Y. Thus E is again the actual
exterior P-orbit, and the same literal invariant-pair contradiction applies.

The three displayed rows follow from integer arithmetic on f+c<=5, f,c>=2
and the support bounds; they are necessary possibilities, not constructed
examples or claimed exclusions. Since the fixed set inside Y now has size two
or three, rho0 acts there exactly as (ab), and has odd sign on the nontrivial
orbits. On a preserved transitive orbit a nonidentity commuting involution is
free, with odd sign precisely for size 2q with q odd; the q=1 case was excluded.
Exchanging two equal-size orbits has sign (-1)^m, so an odd contribution uses
odd size m>=3. More than one contribution may occur; the proof requires only
that one odd contribution of the stated forms exists.
