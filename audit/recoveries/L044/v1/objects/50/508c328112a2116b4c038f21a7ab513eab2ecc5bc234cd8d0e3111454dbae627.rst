Distinct review of the symmetric central kernel
==============================================

I read all four pages, the complete new finite engine and guarded
checker, and the actual acquired fracture-forest, irredundant-generation,
corrected primitive-string, actual pair-centralizer and faithful-lift

The hypotheses are exactly the residual case of the accepted faithful
component: even m>=8, rank m, B on marks1 through m-2 with kernel the
constant flip z, H with full even flip kernel, and one extra independent
mark at the opposite end. The projected marks are independent because
the family L was chosen inclusion-minimal for the block image. They
need not inherit IP, and the new proof never gives them that property.
The two full retained rank bounds apply to independent generating
families, not just C-markings: the B5 imprimitive theorem states maximum
irredundant length <=q-4 for q>=9; the corrected primitive theorem
states the same bound for independent involutory strings in primitive

I checked the changed forest hypothesis directly against the complete
proof of the acquired Lemma forest in B5 section12. Every step uses
only one eligible selected edge per omission and the matching property
of edges of one label. A selected cycle would bypass a selected edge
without its label. An extra edge within a selected tree would similarly
bypass a differently labelled selected edge. Hence every extra crosses
the two components. The paths between two disjoint extras cannot be
built using only their two labels, by the matching condition. Extras
therefore intersect, and two nonparallel ones force exactly the unique
alternating square. The proof forbidding a third extra uses the same
selected paths and matching condition. None of this invokes IP. The
ambient C-group hypothesis in that older section is stronger than the
hypotheses actually used in this lemma, so its literal proof applies
under the new intransitive-omission assumption.

The new string argument then avoids the older IP-based eliminations.
For q>=6, any decomposition of the noncommutation graph would express
Sq as a product of commuting nontrivial normal subgroups, impossible
because every such normal subgroup contains Aq and its centralizer is
trivial. The noncommutation graph is therefore the full path. With
parallel extra edges, every connector incident to the common edge tau
is a single transposition adjacent in this graph to every repeated
label. Three repeated labels are impossible. For two, they must be
nonadjacent vertices with a unique common neighbour k. Their other
transpositions are disjoint, since the repeated marks commute. The
unique connector k detaches the two-vertex edge tau from the rest of
the underlying tree. The path between the other two transpositions
uses only distinct single labels and avoids k. It gives a second path
between the repeated labels, contradicting the noncommutation path.

For one repeated label, two or more intervening single edges would
again make a cycle. Its two edges and one connector give the four-point
path. Any edge at a middle point yields a triangle with the core marks.
At an outer point it neighbours the double mark; there can be only one
such attachment because that mark already has the connector as a
neighbour. An attached branch of degree three would also give a
triangle. This leaves precisely the canonical core and one path.
In the square alternative the two core marks commute, and any exiting
single edge neighbours both. There is at most one exit, whose outside
endpoint cannot have another edge without raising its noncommutation
degree to three. The entire graph then has only five points. Thus this
alternative is excluded at every q>=6, as claimed. The two possible
orders of the final noncommutation path are exactly reversal.

For m>=10, a projected maximal omission is a proper group generated
independently by m-3 marks. The two retained bounds exclude every
transitive group except Am. An interior omission cannot generate Am:
its two nonempty strings commute and would be nontrivial commuting
normal subgroups of that simple group. At the first end, the actual
lifted subgroup T would have primitive Am block image, so the acquired
pair-centralizer lemma would force rho0 to preserve the pairs. Its
regular prime-dihedral exception cannot apply. At the opposite end,
the extra mark is already in H, hence already preserves the pairs.
Its block image centralizes Am and is trivial; its flip vector is
constant. It would be identity or z, both in B, contradicting actual
independence. These endpoint arguments concern actual permutations and
do not presume that a block-breaking permutation acts on the blocks.

The concluding lift argument is independent of the forest computation.
The whole H centralizes z. If rho0 did as well, the natural A(2m) would
centralize z, contrary to its trivial natural centralizer. Since rho0
centralizes T, this proves z is absent from T. The known kernel of B
then makes projection of T faithful. At the tail orientation, the
acquired one-fixed-pair lemma applies directly. At the core orientation
T projects isomorphically to S2 x Sb, b=m-2 even. The explicit cubing
identity separates the small transposition, so this is the actual full
product, not just a subdirect claim.

The pure Ab preimage in T is perfect. On the four points over the small
two blocks its image lies in the abelian flip group, hence is trivial.
It fixes no point in the other2b points because its block action is
transitive. Its fixed set is therefore exactly those four points, and
rho0 preserves it. On the complement, the actual pair-centralizer
lemma applies to the induced T action with primitive Sb block image.
The block action of rho0 is trivial, and its flip vector is constant.
Both possibilities are even because b is even. Thus rho0 is even on
the four-point fixed set as well. Faithfulness of T makes the pure S2
preimage an involution; its restriction exchanges the two small pairs
and is a double transposition. Its even centralizer is V4, which
preserves every partition into two pairs. Hence rho0 preserves the
original pairs everywhere, contradicting the parent alternating group.
No splitting of B or quotient IP is assumed in this reasoning.
