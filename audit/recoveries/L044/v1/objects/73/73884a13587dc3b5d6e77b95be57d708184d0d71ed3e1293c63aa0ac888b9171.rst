Explicit alternating group construction
---------------------------------------

On T={b,a0,a0',a1,a1'}, the two matchings of s and q1 form the path
b--a0--a1--a1'--a0'. Their product is a five-cycle. Outside T, q1 is
trivial and s has order dividing two, so c=(s q1)^2 is a pure five-cycle.
Conjugation by q1 inverts c; q1 fixes b. Thus the generated pure group D
is the dihedral group of order ten on T. Its five reflections have distinct
fixed points. The reflection d fixing a1 moves a1' to a different z in T,
and fixes the two points with index 2.

The transposition (a1 a2) occurs in both q2 and q2^d and cancels. Their
remaining transpositions are (a1' a2') and (z a2'), giving the stated pure
three-cycle. Conjugating by c rotates the two distinct points a1',z through
T, so their unordered pairs form a connected five-cycle: every nonzero step
modulo five generates that cyclic group. The extra point a2' stays fixed.

Three-cycles through one extra point and the edges of a connected graph
generate the alternating group on that extra point and the graph vertices.
For completeness, the initial overlap of two three-point supports is valid
as well: (x u v) and (x v w) generate A4 on their four distinct points;
conjugation produces the remaining three-cycles. More generally, the same
common-two-point argument in the acquired overlap proof supplies (x y z)
for each z in the union, so it applies to supports of size at least three.
A spanning-tree induction gives the six-point alternating group claimed.
Its q2-conjugate loses a1 and gains a2, so its support meets the first in
exactly five points and their union has seven points. Subsequent q_i replace
the last old pair by the next pair in the conjugate support; intersections
have size at least five. Their unions successively contain both points of
each pair. The external involution f was removed in the very first square
and does not contaminate any of these pure subgroups.

Application and its exact range
------------------------------

The two faithful natural row actions are identified equivariantly by the
specified shared restriction v. If a pair-image omission were transitive,
the ambient omission, which also contains rho_g, would have A as one orbit.
The omitted generator is supported on A and could then merge no ambient
orbits, a contradiction. Choosing one omission-crossing edge per pair mark
therefore gives a forest. Its t-1 edges span a tree on t vertices. Each
omission has exactly the two components of its edge deletion as its orbits.
Each other omission cut is preserved by the remaining mark; the common
refinement singles out exactly the two endpoints of that mark's edge.
Thus its pair image is that transposition. String commutation forces the
tree to be a path with successive labels. The fixed anchor identifies its
first endpoint. Equivariance of the two faithful rows then gives the literal
doubled transposition marking in the displayed formula.

For t at least three, omitting q1 leaves exact fixed set C union {a,a'}.
The crossing centralizes this subgroup, swaps a with b, and has just one
edge across C and A. It must therefore fix a' and preserve the other paired
points. For t at least four, both remaining orbits carry the natural
S_(t-1) action of degree at least three. Their individual centralizers are
trivial. There is exactly one equivariant bijection between them, because
composing two such bijections gives an individual centralizer. Consequently
the crossing is either the identity there or the complete row swap. The
packet appropriately does not assert that dichotomy for t=2 or 3.

If the complete row swap occurs, the first lemma applies with f supported
on C minus b. It yields pure Alt(A union {b}) in the suffix starting at g+1.
The shared mark moves b: otherwise both it and P_(g-1) would fix b, contrary
to the transitivity of P_g on C. Its image c is another point of C. Since
rho_g preserves A, conjugating and overlapping yields pure
Alt(A union {b,c}) in the suffix starting at g.

The exterior image of P_g is generated by v. Adjoining the crossing attaches
a to the actual head orbit and v attaches a'; the remaining paired points
are invariant under both the old prefix and the crossing. Thus the new head
orbit is exactly C union {a,a'}. The complete acquired prefix theorem puts

both contain pure A4 on {a,a',b,c}. Actual ambient intersection gives their
intersection as the subgroup generated by rho_g and s. A group generated
by two involutions is dihedral; all odd-order elements lie in its cyclic
rotation subgroup. It cannot contain A4, which has noncommuting elements of
order three. This establishes the contradiction also for the explicitly
