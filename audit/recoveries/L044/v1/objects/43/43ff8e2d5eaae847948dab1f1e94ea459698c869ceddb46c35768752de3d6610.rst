Distinct review: the normalized odd-degree assembly
===================================================

I read the complete new two-page proof and its precise acquired interfaces.
The statement retains actual ambient eligibility, the natural alternating tail,
the six-point supported head, full IP, and the existence of one orbit containing
two eligible edges of each tail colour. Whole-orbit normalization supplies only
intransitivity of K on the orbits with nonempty assignments. No split assignment
is inferred from a local fracture graph.

The seven-fixed-point budget is exhaustive. Its sum is -2 because there are
r-5 assigned colours and 2r-6 nonfixed points. All nonfirst deficits are strictly
negative, while the first deficit is nonpositive. An exchanged pair of orbits
can be transported by the actual tau in every relevant ambient omission.
Combining their disjoint assigned label sets on one orbit gives the stated
-ceil(m/2) bound. Orbits of size two or three cannot contain two disjoint edges
of an involution, so their assignments are empty. Odd exchanges cost at least
three. Even exchanges cost at least two and have even permutation sign; because
delta fixes all seven fixed points, its odd sign requires an additional odd
internal action costing at least one more. Thus all nontrivial orbits are
preserved, and an internal orbit of size 2q with odd q exists.

The head anchor belongs to a different nontrivial orbit: it is outside D,b,
which are exactly the fixed points here, and is fixed by delta. I checked the
actual row-separator theorem, including its terminal equality exclusion. Its
hypotheses require the seven specified fixed points, not exactly seven, and
no parity restriction on q. For any nonfirst saturated orbit with q>=3 it gives
a nonterminal t with at least 2t+3 fixed points of the remaining tail. The
interval bound on that nonempty tail needs support at least 2(r-1-t)+1. These
disjoint counts exceed n by one.

For an odd internal orbit with q>=3, an empty assignment is impossible. In the
nonfirst case, exclusion of saturation leaves deficit -2; all other deficits
must vanish, leaving exactly the first even orbit. Its two first-colour edges
force size at least four. This is exactly the accepted nonfirst deficit-two
packet, not a stronger unstated result. In the first case, deficit -2 leaves no
anchor orbit. Deficit -1 leaves either one even orbit or two odd orbits, with
no other partition of the half-integral strictly negative deficits possible.
The first alternative is the accepted complete first-internal theorem,
including its two-point case. In the two-odd-orbit alternative both assignments
are nonempty. Internal pair-path equality makes the first assigned labels an
initial interval. Every later pair image on that internal orbit is trivial,
so any later edge there is already joined by tau in its ambient omission.
Hence all eligible edges of the complementary interval are confined to the
two odd orbits. Their normalized K actions are intransitive, and the selected
tail is a subgroup of K, giving at least four local orbits. The complete
localization inequality then contradicts the sum of the two deficits.

For q=1 the internal pair has no assigned colour. If delta has no other
internal action it is a literal transposition, with every hypothesis of the
accepted exterior-pair obstruction present. Otherwise a second internal orbit
has deficit exactly -1. A second two-point orbit would give two transpositions
and delta even; any further action would exceed the budget. A larger second
orbit has nonempty assignment. If first, it leaves no anchor orbit. If
nonfirst, all remaining deficit is zero, so exactly one even first orbit remains.
The saturated row exclusion handles size at least six. At size four the exact
E2,W4,V budget satisfies the accepted fixed-anchor corollary, including its
specified delta action. These cases exhaust the remaining budget.

The acquired eight-fixed-point exclusion then yields f>=9. This is a bounded
conditional theorem and a local inequality at x=4. It proves neither the
single-orbit assignment hypothesis nor a global attachment statement.
