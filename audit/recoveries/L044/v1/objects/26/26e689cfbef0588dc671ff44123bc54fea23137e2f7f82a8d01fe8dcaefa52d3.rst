Connected four-pair core with a row-swapping neighbour
=====================================================

Working proof, not yet a frozen reviewed theorem. No whole L044 credit.
Hypotheses are those of the accepted common contracted-forest component
at equality r=m, m even>=8, with R={r,s} on either side of l. In addition
assume the full contracted R graph J is connected on exactly four pairs Y.
At least one R mark interchanges the original binary rows. The reduction
to this four-pair case is root's separate obligation; it is not assumed
proved here. Smaller alternating degrees are assumed exactly as in the
accepted pair-block theorem.

1. Extra C edges lie in Y
------------------------
J together with one selected edge per C mark is connected, and each such
edge is a bridge, by the common proof. Collapse J to one vertex: the
selected edges form a tree on that vertex and the outside pairs. No
selected C edge has both endpoints in Y. An additional edge of a C mark
cannot have a selected edge of another label on a connecting path, since
it would bypass that eligible edge. If its own selected edge occurs,
matching on actual pair indices forbids that edge from being the first or
last edge of the path. Both ends would then be in J, impossible for a
bridge. Thus every additional C edge lies in Y. The local support lemma
and the matching condition give at most one such additional edge.

2. Two canonical actual forms
-----------------------------
Write points a0,a1,... with l interchanging rows. Reverse the string if
necessary so that r swaps rows. Normalize its three-cycle to p=(a b c),
so r sends x0 to p(x)1 and x1 to p^-1(x)0, and equals l elsewhere.
Commutation of s with r and the connected four-pair hypothesis leave:

M: s=(b0 d0)(c1 d1), preserving rows;
S: s=Swap((b d c)), interchanging rows and equal to l elsewhere.

For M, conjugation by r exchanges the two rows, so the two transpositions
of s must be one in each row; if its first-row transposition is t, the
other is t^p. Their union with the p triangle has four vertices, forcing
t to join d to an element of {a,b,c}. Rotate p to obtain t=(b d).
For S, the support of the three-cycle q of s shares two vertices with p.
Rotate p so it is {b,c,d}. The equation r*s=s*r is
p*q^-1=q*p^-1, and direct evaluation of the two possible orientations
leaves exactly q=(b d c).

Put z=diag((b c))*l. This is a fixed-point-free involution swapping rows,
equal to l outside Y. Direct substitution shows it commutes with r,l,s
in both forms. It need not belong to H (and does not in the local S form).

3. Classifying a C mark that does not centralize z
------------------------------------------------
Any C mark g has form diag(pi)*l^chi and commutes with at least one of r,s.
Its projected permutation is its selected edge e, multiplied by either
the identity or one extra transposition tau in Y. All selected edges
have at least one endpoint outside Y.

If g commutes with r, its selected edge is either (d x), or (x y) with
x,y outside Y. Commutation gives tau=identity when chi=0, and one of
(a b),(a c),(b c) when chi=1. Indeed on {a,b,c}, pi centralizes p when
chi=0 and inverts p when chi=1. Only tau=(a b),(a c) fail to commute
with z.

In form M, if g commutes with s, its selected edge is (a x) or outside.
Commuting with the two row transpositions forces (tau,chi) to be (1,0)
or ((b c),1), both centralizing z.

In form S, commuting with s gives the analogous list with {b,c,d};
the failures are tau=(b d),(c d), chi=1. Reverse r,s and rename
(a',b',c',d')=(d,c,b,a) to obtain exactly the earlier bad case.
Conjugation by z fixes r,l,s and exchanges the two bad transpositions
(a b),(a c). It therefore suffices in either form to handle

                 g=diag(e*(a b))*l,

where e=(d x) or e=(x y), with x,y outside Y.

4. Form M: two uniform permutation identities violate IP
-------------------------------------------------------
Set s'=l*s*l and t=l*g. For e=(d x), put

        w=(t*s')^2*(s*t)^2*s*(s'*t)^2*s*s'*s.

For e=(x y), put

        w=(l*(s*g)^2*s)^2.

In both cases direct permutation multiplication gives
w=(a0 b0 c0)(a1 c1 b1)=r*l, fixing every other point. These are identities
on the displayed points; the global l factors cancel outside them.
Thus r=w*l belongs to <l,s,g>, contradicting actual IP for the singleton
mark r and the disjoint marked set {l,s,g}. The bad g is impossible.
The preliminary GAP diagnostics suggested these words, but the displayed
identities themselves, verified on each displayed point, are the uniform
proof. No catalogue or enumeration is a premise.

5. Form S: the l-omission becomes transitive
-----------------------------------------
The marks r,s,g, with g as above, connect all eight core points by the walk

 a0 --r-- b1 --s-- c0 --r-- a1 --g-- b0
    --r-- c1 --s-- d0 --r-- d1.

Every outside l-pair is already contained in an orbit of <r>, since r
equals l there. Consequently every orbit of H_l=<r,s,C> is a union of
whole l-pairs, and the orbit containing Y contains all four core pairs.
The connected graph J plus the selected C bridges now joins these orbits:
all J edges lie inside that core orbit, and each selected C edge is an
actual edge of H_l. Hence H_l is transitive, contradicting the standing
double-omission hypothesis. Thus every C mark centralizes z in form S too.

6. Maximality of the new pairs, then the acquired pair-block theorem
------------------------------------------------------------------
All H centralize z and preserve the original binary partition. In
coordinates matching the rows via z this places H inside diag(S_m)x<z>.
Its image P on the z-pairs is transitive. If all m-1 projected marks are
independent, the acquired extremal independent-generator theorem gives
P=S_m. Otherwise delete one projected redundant label j: H_j still maps
onto P, but is intransitive on 2m points. Its two actual orbits are sections
of the new pairs; the projection is faithful, supplying an actual string
C-marking of P of rankm-2. The complete L047 bound and degree8 boundary
force P=S_m or A_m. Thus the z-pair system is maximal. The accepted
pair-block theorem excludes the parent alternating marking with its exact
smaller-degree induction. No membership z in H was needed.
