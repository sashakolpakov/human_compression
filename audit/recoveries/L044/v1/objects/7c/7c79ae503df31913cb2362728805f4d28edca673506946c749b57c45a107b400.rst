Anchored action on pairs
------------------------

Choose one edge joining different omission orbits for each induced pair mark.
Any cycle would contain an alternative path avoiding one of its labels,
contradicting the choice of that edge. There are t-1 edges on t vertices, so the
forest is a tree. Removing the selected edge of label i leaves two components
connected by all the other selected edges. These are exactly the two orbits of
the corresponding omission: joining them would contradict intransitivity.
For every other label j, mark i preserves each side of cut j. The common
refinement of those cuts has singletons except for the two ends of edge i;
thus the induced mark i is exactly the transposition across that edge.
Nonadjacent labels commute and their edges are distinct, so their edges are
disjoint. Three edges incident with one vertex would give three pairwise
consecutive labels, which is impossible. The tree is a path and its labels
occur successively. Since marks 2 through t-1 fix the anchor pair, that pair is
its endpoint at label 1. Those marks induce its full S_(t-1) stabilizer.

The pair kernel N cannot be trivial. Otherwise Q maps isomorphically onto S_t,
and the subgroup generated by marks 2 through t-1 has order (t-1)! while fixing
the actual anchor point. Its point orbit would have size at most t, contrary
to transitivity on 2t points. The kernel is an S_t-invariant nonzero subspace
of the even-weight flip space E. If it contains a vector other than zero and
the all-ones vector, swapping an inside coordinate with an outside coordinate
and adding yields a vector of weight two. Its conjugates span E. Consequently
N is E or the line spanned by all ones. For even t both possibilities contain
the full flip v. For odd t the all-ones vector is not in E, so N=E and the
order of Q equals that of the full even centralizer. This proves the claimed
literal equality with that centralizer. No projected intersection property is
assumed in this argument.
