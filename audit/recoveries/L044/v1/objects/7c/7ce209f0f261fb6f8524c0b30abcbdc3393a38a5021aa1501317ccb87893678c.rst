The orbit reduction
-------------------

The natural Alt(C), |C|>=4, has trivial centralizer on C, and C cannot be
exchanged with a fixed-point orbit of that pure subgroup. Therefore s
fixes C pointwise. It moves z to a J-fixed point a. A double transposition
t can join at most three J-orbits, and P is transitive on X, so the orbit
partition consists of C, {a}, and at most one further orbit. If the latter
were absent or a singleton, s would be a nonidentity even involution on
at most three points, impossible. Thus there is exactly one nontrivial
exterior orbit E. Commutation implies s preserves it, and its exterior
delta is a free odd involution on that transitive J-orbit.

Connecting the three orbits uses both edges of t; neither can be internal,
and both cannot meet a. This gives exactly forms I and II in the paper.
The actual intersection <s,t> intersect P=<t> applies to every element
of <s,t> fixing z because P is the full natural alternating group on X.

a as well as z, so cannot be t, forcing m=1. In form II with f=e', the
fifth power does the same. If f is not e', the fourth power fixes z but
has the displayed three-cycle on {f,f^delta,c}; it is neither identity
nor the involution t. These arguments handle all odd m without a finite
search. They force |E|=2.
