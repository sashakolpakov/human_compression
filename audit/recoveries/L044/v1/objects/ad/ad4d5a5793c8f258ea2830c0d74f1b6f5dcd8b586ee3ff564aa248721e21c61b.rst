Distinct mathematical review of the large-block argument
========================================================

omission of a natural alternating string C-group, with a maximal block
system having both parameters greater than two, forces the asserted
half-degree rank bound for n at least12. The complete four-page proof

Acquired block decomposition
----------------------------
The subgroup H inherits the intersection property on its distinguished
marks, including across the missing label. Minimality of L makes its
block images independent. If its image diagram is connected, the labels
of L form a literal interval, and only its two neighbouring labels can
belong to R. D centralizes the primitive block image, whose centralizer
is trivial: a nontrivial primitive centralizer would be transitive and
would make the image regular of prime degree; odd-prime cyclic groups
cannot be generated by involutions. Faithfulness of D on each block
follows by transporting a pointwise identity through B. Thus the acquired
independent-family bounds apply to actual faithful permutation groups.
The exclusion of rank(H)=k+m is precisely the already proved L047
argument. In the disconnected case the retained bound on rank(H) has
been increased by one, correctly giving the parent-rank bound used here.

Equality and interval argument
------------------------------
Since H has r-1 marks, equality r=k+m leaves exactly the three displayed
triples of cardinalities. When |C|=k-1, faithful restriction and Whiston
give the diagonal S_k. The centralizer of this group inside the block
stabilizer is the consistent S_m, so B is faithful on blocks. If both
neighbours of L belong to R, the omitted label is not adjacent to L.
The full centralizer of B is the permutation group of its identical
rows, because the primitive row action has trivial centralizer. It fixes
each original block. This excludes the second equality pattern.

For the first pattern B and D are the two symmetric factors. The C
diagram is connected: its commuting components would otherwise give
a direct product decomposition of S_k, using IP. A lone R mark must
be adjacent to both C and L. If it centralized D, it would lie in B,
contradicting independence. Therefore the two intervals and the one
intervening mark occupy every H label consecutively. The omitted label
is at an end and centralizes at least one factor. Centralizing D
permutes its orbit blocks; centralizing B fixes them. Either possibility
contradicts the primitive parent action. The remaining pattern has
|C|=k-2, |L|=m-1, |R|=2 and makes the omitted mark centralize B.

Centralizer lemmas
------------------
The binary-module lemma is valid for any vector space V. A nonconstant
vector plus a transposition image gives one nonzero value at exactly two
coordinates. Symmetric conjugates and a partition into pairs produce
the nonzero constant vector when m is even. This uses neither simplicity
of a module nor an unjustified claim about arbitrary subspaces.

For three-point blocks, coordinates identify the centralizer of the
diagonal transposition with C2 wr S_m. A nonzero block kernel contains
that transposition by the preceding lemma. If the kernel is zero, the
unique lift of a block transposition is an involution. It has three
transpositions on the exchanged pair of blocks. The lifts permuting
the other blocks commute with it and make its fixed-block restrictions
conjugate. Their number is even, so they contribute even sign. This
contradicts membership in the alternating group. Every hypothesis of
this argument is present in the (3,6) application.

For the faithful S4 action with four blocks, the block stabilizer is S3.
An orbit meets a block in1,2 or3 points, with point stabilizer S3,A3 or
C2 respectively. A twelve-point orbit leaves a four-point orbit; their
block-fixing centralizers are both trivial and their different sizes
prevent exchange. The assumed nontrivial D excludes this case. All
remaining orbit stabilizers have normalizer exactly the same S3 inside
S4. This proves block fixation both for maps within an orbit and for
equivariant maps between equal orbits. The latter point is essential:
the argument does not merely compute separate orbit centralizers.

For the two-mark group D on four letters, the possible groups are the
two Klein actions, S3 and a Sylow2 subgroup D8. Their centralizers are,
respectively, the Klein group itself, the trivial group and the centre
of D8. Each is an elementary abelian group contained in D. Transport
by B makes D diagonal, and the kernel of B lies in Z^4. Since Z is
abelian, the induced action on this kernel is the full coordinate S4.
The binary-module lemma would put a nontrivial diagonal element in
B intersect D. IP excludes it. B is therefore faithful and the preceding
coset argument applies. No projected intersection property is assumed.

Small actions and the final list
-------------------------------
Three independent involutions in A5 cannot generate a proper subgroup.
The intransitive overgroups are A4 and the even S3 times S2 action;
their involutions supply at most two independent elements. The proper
transitive orders listed in the proof are exhaustive by divisibility.
The coset arguments at orders20 and30 use simplicity; the order15
case is cyclic, and order10 is dihedral. These justify every rejection.

For an intransitive subgroup of A6, an invariant set of size at most3
gives A5, S4, or a group of order18. Their independent lengths are at
most3 by Whiston's equality clause or the subgroup-chain bound.
The transitive case uses the complete acquired L047 conclusion,
including all proper actions and the separate A6 rank-four exclusion.
The retained A6 output is empty; the exceptional proper degree-six
rank-four markings all contain an odd permutation. No whole A6 rank
spectrum is inferred from a partial search. The A12 bound uses the
accepted complete all-rank L071 census, not an assumed source value.

For km>12, failure of the integer bound and r<=k+m leaves precisely
(3,5),(3,6),(4,4),(5,3),(6,3); each forces equality. The five arguments
then use, respectively, odd sign on five blocks; the three-point kernel
lemma; the four-point centralizer lemma; diagonal A5 and odd block
transpositions; and the faithful rank-four restriction into A6.
Their domains and parity assumptions match exactly. The case n=12
is already covered independently by the acquired census.
