The row lemma and the small degree
---------------------------------

For odd N>=5, the rank N-2 is above N/2, and all proper exceptions of
the complete accepted L047 theorem have even degree. For even N>=8,
N-2>N/2+1 excludes its entire proper exception list. These cases leave
only natural A_N or S_N, whose natural centralizers are trivial. At N=6,
the exact rank-four imprimitive types (3,2,3), (2,3,3), (2,3,4) and
(2,4,3) all have a commuting break, including their reversals. Thus none
has the required connected diagram. The remaining proper case is the
primitive degree-six S5. Its nontrivial point stabilizer is maximal and
self-normalizing: if the normalizer were the whole group, faithfulness
would force the stabilizer to be trivial, contrary to this nonregular
action. The usual centralizer/normalizer quotient therefore proves its
centralizer trivial. This is the exact degree-six case, not a bound
applied beyond its hypotheses.

In the intransitive case, the N-2 eligible selected edges form a forest
with exactly two components. Intransitivity makes these the two actual
orbits. All cuts except edge i force mark h_i to fix every point except
the endpoints of its selected edge, which it swaps. Connectedness forces
one component to be an isolated point and the other to generate natural
S_(N-1). A branching vertex of degree three would create three pairwise
noncommuting edge transpositions, impossible in a string. Hence the tree
is a path. Its line graph is also a path, and a connected string ordering
of all its edges is the path order or its reversal. In particular its
first edge is an endpoint edge, a fact required later in the proof.

The block-action cases
----------------------

A distinguished omission in B cannot be transitive on E: together with
the head and rho_3 linking the head to b, it would make the corresponding
full ambient omission transitive. Let M be a minimal distinguished family
for the complete pair action. The acquired actual-orbit forest uses actual
M-orbits, not merely quotient orbits, and gives d-|M|<=c-1. Since each
M-orbit meets every pair, c<=2. Minimality makes the block images independent,
so Whiston bounds |M|<=m-1. With d=m-1 only |M|=m-1 or m-2 remains.
Sigma must belong to M and move the anchor pair: all other block images
fix it, and the full block action is transitive. Consequently x is outside
the pair {b,b'}.

If |M|=m-1, the K block images form an independent family of size m-2
on the remaining m-1 pairs. Whiston's equality clause gives natural
S_(m-1). K fixes b,b' pointwise. Its remaining point orbits either form
one orbit or two rows meeting every pair once. In the first case delta's
fixation of x and centralization of K force pointwise fixation of that
orbit. In the second, each row action is faithful: an element fixing one
row fixes every pair and preserves both actual rows, hence fixes both.
The image on each row is natural S_(m-1). Delta fixes the x row pointwise,
then the other row by its trivial centralizer. Its remaining fixed-point
set is {b,b'}, and fixing b forces b' fixed. Thus delta is the identity,
contrary to oddness.

If |M|=m-2, equality forces exactly two actual M-orbits, U and V, each
meeting every pair once. The same argument makes both row actions faithful,
so the inherited full intersection property is valid there. A diagram
component of M not containing sigma fixes b and commutes with the other
components. By transitivity of M on U it fixes the entire row; faithfulness
would make that component trivial. Thus M is connected. It contains the
first B mark and omits only one mark; deleting an interior index would
separate two nonempty commuting intervals. The omitted mark must therefore
be the final one, exactly as stated.

The actual subgroup H on the remaining interval is faithful and diagonal
on U minus b and V minus b', of degree N=m-1 and rank N-2. Every row
omission changes its orbit partition: otherwise the unchanged partitions
on both rows, together with fixed b,b',D, would give the same orbit relation
for H; adjoining the other ambient marks would make the full omission
transitive. This uses the join of actual orbit equivalence relations and
does not infer IP for an arbitrary quotient.

If H is transitive on its row, delta centralizes it and fixes x in U minus
b, since sigma itself belongs to M. It fixes that row pointwise, and then
the other row through the trivial centralizer supplied by the row lemma.
The remaining two fixed points are again fixed, contradicting oddness.

If H is intransitive, it is a natural path on W together with singleton p.
Removing its first mark detaches exactly one endpoint a. The fixed set in
U of G_(6,...,r-2) is precisely {b,p,a}; the remaining path has at least
three vertices because m>=6. Sigma centralizes this group and preserves
that fixed set. Yet transitivity of <sigma,H> on U forces sigma to send
both H-fixed points b,p into W. Fixing either leaves a singleton invariant,
and swapping them leaves their pair invariant. Both images would have to
be a, the only point of W in that three-point fixed set. This is impossible
for an injective permutation. The two block-action cases are exhausted.
