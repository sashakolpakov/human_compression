Distinct review of the one-step witness separator
================================================

Disposition and acquired premises
---------------------------------
I accept the three stated bounded results. I read the complete three-page
proof, scope and dependency records, and the actual acquired anchored-path,
commuting-factor, simultaneous-witness, minimal-orbit, local-pair and head-anchor
interfaces. The two local lemmas assume involutions and string commutations,
not a restricted intersection property. The final theorem retains the complete
actual head and paired-tail hypotheses. The earlier unequal-end packet is not
a premise of this proof. I did not author these new arguments; my earlier
independent reviews of acquired lemmas are disclosed.

One right mark
--------------
For h>=3, the strengthened commuting-factor bound prevents both active maxima
from lying on the same side. Saturation then gives the natural symmetric action
on h+1 points and the two-point right side. Centralization fixes the first side
under the right involution. The nearest left mark can transpose the two-point
side, but that action is already supplied by the right mark; every farther
left mark fixes both points because it fixes their anchor and commutes with
the right factor.

For h=2, the possible four-point side and singleton require care. In that
case the right involution c is nontrivial, centralizes a transitive left action,
and is fixed-point-free. The far left mark t_1 fixes a and a^c, so is exactly
the transposition on the other c-pair. Transitivity forces t_0 to exchange the
two c-pairs; commuting with c makes it a product of two cross-pair transpositions.
Thus <t_0,c> is the transitive Klein four group on the side. Both survive the
first omission, together with the crossing to the singleton. This contradicts
that omission's intransitivity and removes this exception.

The reversed exhausted path has only the extra transposition allowed by the
actual acquired proof. It would connect the omitted last point to the surviving
path, so is absent. The remaining left action connects A minus its last point.
For h>=3 the far marks supply a pure symmetric group of degree h inside W,
fixing the two-point side; rho_j therefore fixes that support. In degree three
on A (h=2), its only extra transposition would itself connect the first omission
and is likewise absent. The last point is fixed, with a transitive complement.
This is a statement about local orbits, not unsupported global purity.

For h=1, the omission sides are transitive orbits of a group generated by two
commuting involutions. Their sizes are powers of two, and they partition four
points into two nonempty sets, forcing sizes two and two. The unique crossing
has no possible additional internal transposition. If R swaps both sides the
first omission is transitive; since R is active it swaps exactly one otherwise.
The crossing then yields a three-point orbit and a singleton. For h=0, the
active right involution on three points is itself the first omission and has
precisely those two orbits. All short cases are covered.

A fixed anchor in the left endpoint orbits
-----------------------------------------
For h>=1 the anchored lower bound, active maximum h and total h+2 force one
left endpoint orbit of size h+1 and the other a singleton. The first is the
acquired natural symmetric action. A point z in the union fixed by the first
omission cannot be a crossing endpoint, because rho_j survives when h>=1.
It lies in the large left orbit. The right factor fixes z and commutes with L,
hence fixes the whole orbit. Consequently it is a full omission side. The
opposite side is the right orbit of the other endpoint, and the left factor
fixes that entire side. The surviving left path connects all but its final
point, so its stipulated fixed point must be that final point; the possible
extra edge is excluded directly by fixation. The crossing and the transitive
right factor join the complement. For h=0 the first omission is R, so its fixed
endpoint gives one singleton actual side and its other actual orbit is the
complement. There is no assumption that the full right action is symmetric.

Actual fixed-set argument
-------------------------
At x=j+1 the acquired bound gives x<r-1 and |F|>=2x. Equality forces the
specific acquired sets to exhaust F: seven head points, O_1 of size x-3, and
the two left endpoint orbits C in O_2 of size x-4. With h=j-5 these sizes are
h+3 and h+2, respectively. The first local lemma gives K-orbits {z_1},T_1 on
O_1, with |T_1|=|C|. The actual head anchor b^rho_4 is outside the head,
K-fixed and in F, hence equals z_1 or belongs to C. Only the elementary anchor
property is used; no stronger former assignment result is assumed.

The proof checks every actual generator when asserting that preserving the
corresponding complement T_i under rho_4 would preserve the proper union of
the head and O_i. Pointwise delta-fixation is used for rho_3. Thus rho_4 must
move that complement to a different actual K-orbit.

If the anchor is z_1, the moved orbit T_1^rho_4 lies in F, cannot meet any
K-fixed point or T_1, and therefore lies in C. Equal sizes make it exactly C,
so C is K-invariant. The remaining H-generator rho_5 preserves C: for j>5
it belongs to the left factor whose orbits define C; for j=5 it swaps the
two crossing endpoints which comprise C. Hence C is H-invariant and must be
the whole actual H-orbit O_2. This contradicts the activity of rho_y on O_2,
since y>x and C is contained in F. This H-invariance step is essential and
is explicitly justified in both cases.

If the anchor is in C, the second lemma gives K-orbits {z_0},T_2 on O_2.
The actual invariant-set argument forces T_2 to move. A point of C minus z_0
exists (|C|>=2), and its image lies in F and in the moved nontrivial K-orbit.
The exact partition forces that orbit to be T_1. But rho_y is identity on
T_1 and nonidentity on T_2, whereas rho_4 centralizes rho_y. This is impossible.
Thus equality is excluded and the extra fixed point follows.
