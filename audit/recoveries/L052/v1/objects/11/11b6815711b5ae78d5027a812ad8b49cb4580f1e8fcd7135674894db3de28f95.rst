The new A6 correction
---------------------

The accepted finite data give the literal involutions from source page 10,
A6 as their generated group, and the full C-property. For supports {0,2,3}
and {0,1,3}, both generated groups have order 60, while their intersection
is the order-10 group on {0,3}. Each order-60 subgroup is A5: its index-six
coset action has nontrivial kernel only if the simple A6 lies in that
kernel, which would make the action trivial. The faithful image is the
unique index-two subgroup A6 of S6; its point stabilizer is A5.

For an involutory marking, the subgroup generated by all pairwise products
is normal, and the quotient identifies all involutions and has order at
most two. In a simple A5 this subgroup is the whole A5. Since the pairwise
products rho_i rho_j equal alpha_i^{-1} alpha_j, both C-plus subgroups for
the displayed supports are therefore the complete order-60 parabolics.
Their intersection has order 10. The subgroup for their common support is
cyclic, generated by rho_0 rho_3, of order 5. The involution rho_0 is an
explicit element of the larger intersection outside that cyclic subgroup.
This proves the claimed failure of IC-plus. It does not decide a general
converse for arbitrary C-plus groups. I directly viewed source page 17:
its sentence does present precisely this rotation marking as a C-plus
example, so the qualification is warranted for this fixed edition.
