Distinct review: N012 integral chapter
=====================================

I accept the complete Chapter 2 classification in the submitted five-page
manuscript, with its two finite nonadmissibility results retained as explicitly
attributed external inputs. This is not whole-N012 acceptance. The new proof,
actual arithmetic program, scope/dependency/provenance files and complete fixed

Elementary generation and boundaries
------------------------------------
The initial lemma uses unit elementary matrices and the Euclidean algorithm over
Z, not generation inferred from reductions modulo finitely many primes. Signed
coordinate swaps and their squares handle the remaining pivot positions and signs.
The commutator convention is explicit and gives [t_ij(a),t_jk(b)]=t_ik(ab) for three
distinct indices. A directed cycle has a simple directed path between every ordered
pair of vertices; successive commutators therefore supply every root subgroup.
For dimension two the two opposite directed edges are already the full list.
The determinant-minus-one element then supplies the other coset of SL_n(Z).

Admissibility passes to the specified projective and modular quotients. The source
explicitly retains PSL3(2) and PSL4(2) nonadmissibility; the packet does likewise
and does not claim to acquire their full external proofs. Only this finite
classification input is retained from the N011 provenance; N011's integral
existence input is not imported back, so there is no circular integral premise.

In GL2(Z), commuting nonscalar involutions diagonalize simultaneously over Q and
are equal up to sign. Including scalar involutions only reduces their projective
image. Thus a commuting-pair triple would make PGL2(Z) generated by two involutions.
Reduction modulo three really is onto PGL2(3): integral elementary matrices supply
SL2(3), and determinant -1 supplies its other determinant coset because F3 has only
two units. The action on four projective points identifies that quotient with S4.
Two involutions generate a dihedral group with product order at most four in S4,
so cannot generate S4. Conversely, the three displayed integral matrices square
to one, the first two anticommute, and BC and its A-conjugate are the two unit
transvections. Their projective images commute as required, and the matrices
supply GL2(Z) using the determinant. This proves both boundary statements without
retaining the older two-dimensional result as a proof premise.

Dimensions five and six
-----------------------
The n5 and n6 matrices agree literally with source pages 23 and 25. I matched the
new commutator words, convention and complete matrix values against the exact
arithmetic records. Sign differences from the source's opposite commutator
convention are not mathematical source errors. The source p27 undefined symbols
and the reversed signed-transposition subscript really are present; the new named
words avoid them rather than silently relying on them.

The n5 chain ends in t45(1), adjacent on its signed cycle E=-mu. In n6 the chain
ends in R=t61(1); conjugation by C gives t16(1), the next commutator gives t26(1),
and conjugation by BC gives t62(1). Thus both w16 and w26 are available. Conjugating
w16 by w26 supplies the needed signed transposition on coordinates 1,2; it need
not be assumed before t21 is extracted. The subsequent conjugation and inversion
give t21(1), and its inverse times V gives the pure p36. The five monomial elements
in (6) have exactly the listed transposition images. Their edges connect all six
coordinates, so their permutation images generate S6. Conjugation consequently
reaches every ordered root position, with sign removed by inversion. This is a
proof over Z; the optional GL6(3) consequence follows from the already proved
integral statement. The 30-position orbit check supports this argument without
replacing its group-theoretic reason.

Uniform dimensions and the seven-dimensional collision
------------------------------------------------------
I checked all four residue classes in the B,C table. A is reversal invariant;
tau mu fixes the final coordinate and reverses the others. Each modified C still
squares to one. Counting the reversal and diagonal signs yields the stated
(det B,det C), hence det E=-1. Up to a scalar sign, E is the ordinary cyclic shift
with wrap factor sigma=(-1)^n. This verifies both conjugation rules used in the
uniform computation, not just a numerical parity sample.

For n>=8 the labels 1,2,3,4,5,n-2,n-1,n are genuinely distinct. The matrices A,
A^E and A^(E^2) have only the displayed nonidentity entries on those labels. The
wrapped off-diagonal coefficient in A^(E^2) is +1 because the two wrap signs
cancel. All other coordinates are fixed. The pre-conjugation T0 calculation uses
only indices for which the explicit partial successor is defined; these have no
additional wrap factor. Likewise R has only the two indices on which the explicit
reversal map is defined. Thus the eight-label calculation is a uniform algebraic
calculation, not checking a representative dimension and assuming stability.
The six output rows agree with the TeX formula for both signs sigma, exhausting
its possible values. The result S=t21(1) is a unit, so the root-cycle lemma applies.

At n7 precisely the identified label collision invalidates that eight-label model.
The separate seven-coordinate calculation gives R,V,S and Q exactly as printed.
The last word multiplies S by [A,Q] inverse and cancels its t31(-2) factor, leaving
t21(1). No inversion of the integer two or unsupported modular division occurs.
The initial involution and commuting-pair conditions remain valid in this case.
This closes every n>=5 construction, and the quotient argument closes every
projective positive case. No Schlaefli type or polytope intersection property is
claimed from admissibility alone.
