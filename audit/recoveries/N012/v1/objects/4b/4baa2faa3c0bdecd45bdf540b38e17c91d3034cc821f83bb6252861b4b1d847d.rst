Uniform intersection property and types
---------------------------------------
The five distinguished coordinates are distinct for every n>=5. I checked their
literal A,C action (5), its square, and the complement: on remaining coordinates

fourth power one. Squaring the displayed five-space action twice gives

This proves the formula on the entire natural space, not just a projection.

For exponent divisible by four, I+lN has three nonzero entries in its final
column unless l=0 mod p. For residues one,two and three, the displayed two
nonzero entries are in columns other than n. Multiplication by I+lN changes only
column n, so those two entries cannot be canceled. The inverse-power expression
covers residue three without a separate unsupported pattern assertion. Therefore
only multiples of 4p give monomial powers, and they are identity. In particular
the order of the PRODUCT AC is 4p; the dihedral group <A,C> has order 8p.

Every element of that dihedral group is a power of AC or such a power times C.
Since C is monomial, the only monomial elements are I and C. The other dihedral
group <B,C> is monomial, giving the exact intersection <C>. A is nonmonomial and
B,C have distinct permutation parts, so all three involutions are distinct. The
order (A,C,B) has commuting end marks and satisfies the retained rank-three
intersection criterion, giving an actual polytope rather than admissibility alone.

For BC the permutation part is an n-cycle, so scalarity cannot occur at a smaller
positive exponent. In n5 its order is 2n. In n>=7 its nth power is epsilon I.
For normalized n6, det BC=-1 and the six-cycle permutation also has sign -1,
forcing the product of weights to be one; the order is six. For the q3 exception
the sixth power is -I and the order twelve. These are precisely the claimed types.

In a scalar quotient, equal images x,y from the two dihedral subgroups imply
x=ys with s scalar. Thus x is monomial and is I or C. This proves quotient IP
by actual lifts; it is not inferred from a general quotient-preservation claim.
The first dihedral group meets the scalar kernel trivially, since its only

Nonidentity and distinctness of the marked images follow from their nonmonomial
