This note applies the accepted orbit and residue theorem to the complete
rank-three and rank-four Hall--Janko catalogue. Its primary comparison is
Jacobs and Leemans, \emph{An algorithmic analysis of the intersection
property}, LMS J. Comput. Math. 7 (2004), 284--299, Tables 1--4 and 7--8.
The full common proof in \emph{Shadows and residues in finite coset
geometries}, and the accepted complete $J_2$ census, remain explicit
premises. We do not repeat the subgroup census or identify historical
geometry names merely by matching counts.

\section{Complete inputs and archive reconciliation}
The accepted census contains eight rank-two, nineteen rank-three and
three rank-four geometries, with no higher-rank examples, under the
stated flag-transitivity, firmness, residual connectivity, residual weak
primitivity, local two-transitivity and rank-two intersection hypotheses.
Write $R_9,\ldots,R_{27}$ and $R_{28},R_{29},R_{30}$ for its rank-three and
rank-four representatives. These are identifiers in the accepted complete
witness file, not the original printed geometry numbers.

The supplied \texttt{j2.rk3} archive has twenty-four active entries, after
its literal final reordering; \texttt{j2.rk4} has only one. An actual ambient
group isomorphism transports all these subgroup families to the accepted
model of the simple group of order $604800$. Matching entire families
under $\operatorname{Aut}(J_2)$ and type permutations gives the following
sequence for the twenty-four active rank-three entries:
\[
27,26,25,24,21,22,23,19,20,18,17,16,15,14,14,10,10,12,12,11,9,9,13,13.
\]
Thus all nineteen classes occur; the five repetitions are $R_9,R_{10},
R_{12},R_{13},R_{14}$. They are not rejected geometries. The sole active
rank-four entry is $R_{30}$; the accepted complete census supplies
$R_{28}$ and $R_{29}$.

For completeness, the matching tries every type permutation. For a fixed
ordering it successively transports the first subgroup, restricts to its
stabilizer in the automorphism group, transports the next, and continues.
If one transporter exists, all such transporters form a coset of that
stabilizer. Induction therefore preserves exactly all possible completions.
Matching subgroup orders is only a preliminary rejection test. The final
test is equality of every transported subgroup. An ambient isomorphism
mapping the whole family induces a type-permuting incidence isomorphism
on its cosets and all residues. Consequently every stated geometry
hypothesis transfers from the accepted census. In particular, weak
primitivity is retained through this exact equivalence; it is not inferred
from the numerical agreement below.

\section{A sparse realization of the common shadow theorem}
For a finite permutation group $K$ and subgroups $P_1,\ldots,P_r$, write
$P_T=\bigcap_{t\in T}P_t$ and $P_\varnothing=K$. The $i$-shadow of the
base flag of type $T$ is $\{P_i x:x\in P_T\}$. The original implementation
realizes the whole right-coset action of degree $[K:P_i]$. The following
representation avoids constructing a large universe when only small
shadows are used.
\begin{proposition}
For nonempty $T$, take a right transversal of $P_i\cap P_T$ in $P_T$ and
replace each representative $x$ by a canonical representative of $P_i x$.
This set represents the entire $i$-shadow without duplicates. Its right
translate by $g$ is obtained by replacing each representative $x$ by the
canonical representative of $P_i xg$. The empty flag may instead be
represented by the cardinality $[K:P_i]$, provided its universal-set role
is handled explicitly as below.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $x,y\in P_T$, equality $P_i x=P_i y$ holds exactly when
$xy^{-1}\in P_i\cap P_T$. Thus the transversal gives exactly one
representative per shadow object. Canonicalization changes only its name.
If $x'=hx$ with $h\in P_i$, then $P_i x'g=P_i xg$, so the translated
representative is well defined and realizes the same right action.
Intersections, containment, and membership are consequently unchanged.

The empty flag has the full object set as shadow. Its flag-extension
identity is tautological and is counted as such. In a witness test, the
computed intersection is already a subset of the $i$-object set. It is
therefore the empty flag's shadow exactly when its cardinality is
$[K:P_i]$; the empty flag is automatically incident with both tested flags.
When this equality fails, the empty flag cannot witness the intersection,
and every nonempty witness is tested by the displayed representation.
No intersection with a fictitious empty set is performed. The same degree
is used in the rank-two complete-bipartite exception.
\end{proof}
The normalization, double-coset enumeration and flag-constrained witness
search are exactly those of the accepted common theorem. Only the
representation of each finite shadow changes. Trivial panel stabilizers
are allowed: the subgroup generated by an empty generator list is formed
with the identity included, so a two-object panel is still recognized.
The original accepted implementation remains unchanged.

A bounded comparison reconstructs both representations in 161 actual
residue families whose relevant degrees are at most 2000. All 915
nonempty shadows, 6433 generator translates and 271 full-universe
cardinalities agree. Six small models also agree in flag transitivity and
every shadow direction, including a non-flag-transitive example; a
separate trivial-stabilizer panel is checked directly. These are
validation of the adapter, not a substitute for the proposition or the
complete calculation on the twenty-two inputs.

\section{Every rank of every residue}
\begin{theorem}
All nineteen rank-three inputs satisfy $(\mathrm{IP})_2$. Their full
intersection and weak-intersection properties have the following partition:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{IP}:&\quad9,11,12,20,24,25,26,27,\\
\mathrm{WIP}\setminus\mathrm{IP}:&\quad18,19,23,\\
\text{not }\mathrm{WIP}:&\quad10,13,14,15,16,17,21,22.
\end{align*}
For rank four, all three inputs satisfy $(\mathrm{IP})_2$; $R_{28}$ has
$(\mathrm{IP})_3$ and $(\mathrm{IP})_4$, whereas $R_{29},R_{30}$ fail both
$(\mathrm{WIP})_3$ and $(\mathrm{WIP})_4$. Hence the full IP/WIP partition
is one IP example and two non-weak examples.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For every proper fixed-type set $J$, construct the actual residue group
$H_J$ and its remaining parabolics $H_J\cap H_i$. The accepted census and
complete-family correspondence provide the required geometry hypotheses.
The new run additionally checks every flag-extension equality, residual
parabolic generation, panel size and two-transitivity, and both rank-two
shadow directions; it does not repeat the weak-primitivity computation.
For each remaining target type it exhausts the normalized object--flag
orbits and all admissible witness flags. Every negative decision stores
an actual normalizing permutation and the failed shadow intersection.

There are 178 base residues including panels, of which 109 have rank at
least two. Their 255 directional decisions are retained in full. For each
rank $k$, $(\mathrm{IP})_k$ requires all directions in all base residues;
$(\mathrm{WIP})_k$ allows a different successful direction in each residue.
Actual residue multiplicities are $[G:H_J]$. The saved complete decisions
and these quantifiers give the asserted partition. Full IP and WIP take
the conjunction over ranks $2\leq k\leq\operatorname{rank}$; rank one is
automatic and rank zero is not an existential weak test.
\end{proof}
The aggregate columns are therefore $(8,3,8)$ in rank three and $(1,0,2)$
in rank four, agreeing with the corresponding rows of Tables 1, 3 and 7.
Tables 2, 4 and 8 use identifiers from the earlier classification. The
present correspondence does not establish those historical names; in
particular it does not rename $R_{28}$ as the printed $4.2$ without a
separate source identification.

The source orbit algorithm and published shadow results are attributed
to Jacobs and Leemans. Catalogue completeness, its subgroup-data trust
boundary, and the full accepted residue proof are retained. The sparse
representation and its checks do not establish a new catalogue or a
complete Lean formalization. Other groups and the full original remain
outside this bounded packet. There is no new source, question or numerical
credit. Source Section 6 contains executable Magma; any later measurement
must treat concrete source and replacement programs symmetrically.
