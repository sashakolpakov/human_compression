\section{The roster and the retained classification}
This note processes the exact supplied Higman--Sims permutation families
in the supplementary archive to L096. The four files contain respectively
$44,67,12,1$ active entries of ranks $3,4,5,6$. The parser preserves each
literal final reordering and records its construction index separately.
Every one of these $124$ entries is checked for flag transitivity,
firmness, residual connectivity, residual weak primitivity, local
$2$-transitivity and the rank-two intersection property. No prior
Higman--Sims census is used to assume any of these predicates.

Entire-family equivalence under group automorphisms and type permutations
reduces this supplied roster to $30,41,8,1$ distinct families. There are
$44$ classes represented twice and $36$ represented once. These are
exhaustive conclusions about the supplied roster. The assertion that it
exhausts the intended classified geometries is retained from the earlier
classification cited by L096, reference~[21]: D.~Leemans, \emph{Supplement
to ``The residually weakly primitive geometries of HS''} (2003).
We do not replace that classification by a count match. In particular,
no published historical suffix such as $4.1$ is identified with an active
archive position: the archive itself gives examples where they differ.

\section{The common finite criteria}
The accepted orbit--double-coset shadow theorem and the accepted sparse
right-coset adapter are used with their full hypotheses. For a family
$(G_i)_{i\in I}$ in $G$, put $G_J=\bigcap_{j\in J}G_j$ and
$G_\varnothing=G$. The base residue at a flag of types $J$ has ambient
$G_J$ and parabolics $G_{J\cup\{i\}}$, $i\notin J$. The separately
checked flag transitivity transports this residue to every flag of those
types. All proper subsets $J$ are processed; there are
$[G:G_J]$ translates. Rank-one residues supply the panel tests; only ranks
$2$ through $|I|$ enter the intersection and weak-intersection quantifiers.
Rank zero is not subjected to an existential weak-intersection test.

For each residue and each type, the accepted algorithm tests every
required orbit representative, retaining actual nonsingleton shadow
intersection witnesses when the test fails. Sparse shadows are sets of
canonical right-coset representatives. The empty flag's whole universe
is used by its exact cardinality, avoiding unnecessary materialization;
this is the previously proved adapter, unchanged byte for byte here.
The two new implementation interfaces are given with proofs below.

\begin{proposition}
On the fixed supplied roster, with the retained finite group algorithms,
the preceding table and every recorded directional result are complete.
The full run covers $1\,136$ base residues including panels, of which
$836$ have rank at least two, and $2\,204$ directional decisions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The parser supplies every literal active tuple. The actual eligibility
run covers all $124$ tuples and all $1\,748$ of their proper fixed-type
subsets. It tests flag extensions and panel actions, connectivity,
rank-two intersections and the genuine primitive predicates established
above. Thus all hypotheses required by the residue theorem are verified.
The exact family transporter partitions the roster, preserving all
incidence and residue predicates under its isomorphisms. Applying the
unchanged shadow algorithm to each of its $80$ representatives therefore
suffices. For every representative the checker reconstructs the complete
set of proper subsets, their residue ranks, flag-orbit multiplicities,
and every all/exists quantifier from the recorded directional values.
Summing the resulting three-way classifications gives the displayed
table. No conclusion is drawn from agreement with the printed counts;
that agreement is checked afterwards.
\end{proof}

\section{Reproducibility and scope}
The first six eligibility entries use explicit coset actions. Their
completed outputs are preserved from a bounded run that timed out on
the next entry. The remaining $118$ use the exact maximality criterion;
$31$ small direct-action comparisons include degree-one exclusions and
nonfaithful primitive actions. Likewise the first $31$ family decisions
are preserved from the initial bounded matcher. Failed continuations and
unsuccessful accelerations are retained as execution history, not as
mathematical evidence. The successful continuation takes approximately
$54$ seconds, and the complete shadow run approximately $21$ seconds in
the recorded environment; these are replay observations, not asymptotic
claims or a comparison to historical timings.

The required whole programs, generated input data, complete successful
outputs and validation roles are inventoried separately. The default
checker verifies their bindings and reconstructs all quantifiers; an
optional bounded replay recomputes eligibility, the complete optimized
partition and every shadow decision. The scientific code has no Lean
formalization. This bounded note does not award a whole-original L096
recovery, a historical-label identification, a new classification or a
compression factor. Standard block and transporter arguments are
attributed as such; the separately accepted residue and sparse-shadow
proofs remain explicit dependencies.
