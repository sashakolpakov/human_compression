## 1. Scope and the shared input

An incidence system has a finite set of types; incidence is symmetric and distinct objects of the same type are never incident. Write $C(X)$ for the group of correlations, which may permute types, and $K(X)$ for its type-preserving subgroup. A representation of a normal pair $H\lhd G$ is an isomorphism of pairs
\[
(G,H)\cong(C(X),K(X)).
\]
In particular, a representation of a group $L$ means a representation of $(\operatorname{Aut}L,\operatorname{Inn}L)$. A *weak* representation uses compatible injections instead. A geometry is an incidence system in which every flag extends to a chamber. The distinction is material in two examples below.

Our source is Leemans–Stokes–Tranchida, *Groups represented by incidence geometries*, arXiv:2506.10464v1 [L126]. The constructions in §§2–5 below originate there. We recover their finite group pairs through one recognition argument, supplying missing hypotheses and treating small parameters explicitly. The cross-ratio construction is handled separately by the shadow-fiber paper S1, and the infinite constructions by application paper R2. This division is a dependency map, not a claim that every printed assertion of [L126] is true.

**Shared input R.Reconstruction [OLD].** Suppose a construction $X\mapsto Y$ has a distinguished old substructure such that: every correlation of $Y$ restricts to one of $X$; restriction has trivial kernel; every correlation of $X$ extends; and an extension preserves types precisely when its restriction does. Then restriction identifies both group pairs.

This elementary principle is already proved in the retained F010 reconstruction manuscript. Its use here is not a new existence theorem. In practice we verify injectivity by showing that each new object is determined by its old neighbors. If a correlation fixing the old objects could still permute the new types, the old shadow alone must distinguish new objects globally; a signature that includes an unfixed new type would not suffice. Conversely, repeated signatures can create a kernel. The separate theorem S.1 computes that kernel when all new–new incidences depend only on types.

We will use the following version without repeatedly invoking the four conditions.

**Recognition lemma [SHARED].** Let the old objects of $Y$ be recognizable under every correlation. Suppose their induced structure is $X$, every new object has a distinct old-object shadow, and a group $G\le C(X)$ consists exactly of the restrictions that preserve the new-object family and all prescribed new incidences. Then restriction gives $C(Y)\cong G$. Its type-preserving subgroup is the kernel of the resulting $G$-action on the full palette.

**Proof.** A correlation fixing the old objects fixes every new shadow and therefore every new object. Thus restriction is injective. An element of $G$ has a forced permutation of the new objects; by hypothesis that permutation is an incidence-preserving bijection inducing a palette permutation. It is consequently a correlation. Finally a correlation is type-preserving exactly when its permutation of the full palette is trivial. $\square$

Recognition, faithfulness, and existence of extensions are separate obligations. The last cannot be inferred from the first two.

## 2. The dihedral family

Let $n\ge3$, let $V=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, and put $U_n=(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times$. For $k\in U_n/\{\pm1\}$ let
\[
E_k=\{\{x,x+k\}:x\in V\}.
\]
Each $E_k$ is a Hamiltonian cycle. If $n$ is odd, all vertices have one type. If $n$ is even, even and odd vertices have separate types. Each $E_k$ is a further type. Vertex–edge incidence is containment, edges of different types are mutually incident, and in the even case every even vertex is incident to every odd vertex. These are exactly the incidence systems of [L126, §§3.1–3.2].

**Theorem R1.Dihedral [APPLICATION].** For $n>3$, the full correlation group is the affine group
\[
C(\Gamma_n)=V\rtimes U_n,
\qquad x\longmapsto ax+b.
\tag{D1}
\]
Its type-preserving subgroup consists of the affine maps with $a=\pm1$, with arbitrary $b$ for odd $n$ and even $b$ for even $n$. This gives the exact pair $(\operatorname{Aut}D_{2n},\operatorname{Inn}D_{2n})$, where the subscript is the group order. For $n=3$ it gives the weak representation stated in the source.

**Proof.** First the vertices are recognizable. For even $n$, their two types have size $n/2$, while every edge type has size $n$. For odd $n>3$, put $d=\varphi(n)/2\ge2$. A vertex has degree $2d$, and an edge object has degree $2+n(d-1)$. Their difference is $(n-2)(d-1)>0$. Thus every correlation preserves $V$ in both cases. Each edge object is determined by its two endpoints.

The affine maps in (D1) preserve incidence and send $E_k$ to $E_{ak}$. They act transitively on incident vertex–edge pairs: the image of the pair $(0,\{0,1\})$ may be prescribed by choosing its vertex image $b$ and one of the two endpoint differences $a\in U_n$. There are $n\varphi(n)$ such pairs. A correlation fixing one pair fixes its edge type, hence acts on its Hamiltonian cycle fixing an edge and both endpoints. An automorphism of a cycle fixing two adjacent vertices is the identity, since successive neighbors are forced. It fixes all vertices and therefore all edge objects. The action on these pairs is thus free as well as transitive, proving (D1).

An affine map fixes every edge type only if it fixes $E_1$, equivalently $a=\pm1$; these two multipliers indeed fix every $E_k$. For even $n$ it fixes both vertex types exactly when $b$ is even. For odd $n$ there is no further constraint.

Write $D_{2n}=\langle r,s:r^n=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle$. Its automorphisms are exactly
\[
r\mapsto r^a,\qquad s\mapsto r^b s,
\quad a\in U_n, b\in V.
\]
Their composition is the affine composition law. Conjugation by $r$ has parameters $(1,2)$ and conjugation by $s$ has parameters $(-1,0)$, so the inner subgroup is precisely the subgroup just computed. These formulas also verify compatibility of the two pair isomorphisms.

For $n=3$ the incidence graph is a hexagon. Its type-preserving group is $S_3$ and its full group has order twelve. Since $\operatorname{Aut}S_3=\operatorname{Inn}S_3=S_3$, the same inclusion into the type-preserving subgroup gives compatible injections. $\square$

**Source qualifications [CORRECTION].** The unit group $U_n$ need not be cyclic. The source's factor $C_{\varphi(n)}$ must be replaced by $U_n$; for instance $U_8\cong C_2^2$. The printed equality involving $\operatorname{Out}D_6$ in the small-case proof is also unnecessary: $\operatorname{Out}S_3$ is trivial. Neither correction changes the asserted pair in Theorem 3.1.

For some parameters $\Gamma_n$ is only an incidence system. At $n=5$, two differently colored disjoint edges form a flag with no vertex completing it. At $n=6$, opposite-parity vertices at difference three are incident but have no incident edge object in common. This does not invalidate Theorem 3.1, whose defined notion of representation uses incidence systems. If a genuine finite geometry is required, the pair-preserving cleaning and chamber-completion constructions of Díaz Ramos–Molinier–Viruel [F010] apply; their theorem and its additional construction cost are retained inputs.

## 3. Symmetric groups and the exceptional degree

For $n\ge2$ let $X_n$ be the rank-two incidence geometry of vertices and edges of the complete graph on $n$ vertices. The point and edge classes have sizes $n$ and $\binom n2$.

**Theorem R1.Symmetric [APPLICATION].** If $n\ge4$, then
\[
C(X_n)=K(X_n)=S_n.
\]
Consequently $X_n$ is an exact representation of $S_n$ for $n\ge4$, $n\ne6$. For $n=3$ it is the preceding weak representation. The literal $n=2$ instance is not a representation of $S_2$ in the source's sense.

**Proof.** For $n\ge4$ the two type sizes differ. Correlations therefore preserve the vertices. Edges are uniquely recovered from their endpoint sets, and all vertex permutations extend. This proves the displayed pair. We retain the classical theorem $\operatorname{Aut}S_n=\operatorname{Inn}S_n$ for $n\ge4$, $n\ne6$, also used in [L126, Theorem 4.1]. For $n=2$, both groups of $X_2$ have order two, whereas both $\operatorname{Inn}S_2$ and $\operatorname{Aut}S_2$ are trivial. Thus the source's parameter exclusion $n\ne3,6$ needs a small-parameter qualification. A one-object rank-one geometry supplies the trivial pair when desired. $\square$

We can make the exceptional construction exact, without assuming an order calculation for $\operatorname{Aut}S_6$. A *duad* is a two-element subset of a six-element set, and a *syntheme* is a partition into three duads. Form $Q$ with the fifteen duads as points, fifteen synthemes as lines, and containment as incidence, as in [L126, pp.5–6]. Every point lies on a line and every line has three points, so $Q$ is a geometry.

**Theorem R1.Six [APPLICATION].** The duad–syntheme geometry is an exact representation of $S_6$.

**Proof.** Distinct duads are collinear exactly when they are disjoint. Hence a type-preserving automorphism preserves disjointness of duads. The largest families of pairwise intersecting duads are precisely the six stars consisting of all five duads through a fixed symbol. Indeed, if an intersecting family has no common symbol, choose $\{a,b\}$ and $\{a,c\}$ in it and then a member avoiding $a$. That member must be $\{b,c\}$, and any further member must meet all three edges of this triangle, so lies in the triangle. Such a family has at most three members. The six five-member stars therefore recover the six original symbols. Every type-preserving automorphism is the corresponding permutation of symbols; synthemes are then determined by their incident duads. Thus $K(Q)=S_6$.

In the abstract group $S_6$, the involution classes of transpositions, products of two disjoint transpositions, and products of three have sizes fifteen, forty-five, and fifteen respectively. This follows by choosing the moved symbols and pairing them. Therefore every automorphism of $S_6$ permutes the first and third classes as a pair. A transposition commutes with a triple transposition exactly when it is one of its three factors: a commuting transposition must exchange two symbols paired with each other by the triple transposition. Consequently the duad–syntheme incidence relation is intrinsically the commuting relation between these two classes. Every automorphism of $S_6$ acts on $Q$ as a correlation, and this action is faithful because transpositions generate $S_6$.

Conversely every correlation of $Q$ normalizes $K(Q)$ and hence acts by conjugation on its identified $S_6$. This conjugation action is faithful. A type-preserving element in its kernel belongs to the trivial center of $S_6$. A type-swapping element in its kernel would induce an $S_6$-equivariant bijection between duads and synthemes. Their stabilizers cannot be conjugate: a duad stabilizer contains seven transpositions, whereas a syntheme stabilizer contains only its three constituent transpositions. Thus no such bijection exists.

The two embeddings are inverse because the first action sends each inner automorphism to the natural symbol permutation, and conjugation by its image acts on that subgroup as the original group automorphism. Hence the pair is exactly $(\operatorname{Aut}S_6,\operatorname{Inn}S_6)$. $\square$

The native finite replay also gives $|C(Q)|=1440$ and $|K(Q)|=720$. These counts corroborate the proof; they are not needed to deduce the pair isomorphism. We do not rederive the atlas examples for $A_6$ or $A_7$ cited but not constructed in [L126]. General finite existence is already supplied by F010, with its original attribution. The $A_5$ case appears next with a small explicit construction.

## 4. Cube and octahedron

Regard the cube graph as the Hamming graph on $\{0,1\}^3$. Give even and odd vertices distinct types, and give edges and square faces two further types. First take the source's literal incidence induced from the cube: vertex–edge, vertex–face and edge–face incidence are containment, and different vertex objects are not incident.

**Theorem R1.Cube [APPLICATION].** The literal rank-four incidence system has pair
\[
(C,K)\cong(S_4\times C_2,S_4),
\tag{C1}
\]
with the second factor in $C$ generated by the antipodal map. It represents $S_4\times C_2$ exactly. Adding incidence between opposite-color vertices precisely when they are adjacent cube vertices gives a geometry with the same pair and twenty-four chambers.

**Proof.** The type sizes are four, four, twelve and six, so every correlation preserves the vertex union, the edges, and the faces. The vertex–edge truncation recovers the cube graph. Edges and faces have distinct vertex shadows, and every graph automorphism preserves their families and the parity bipartition. Recognition therefore identifies the full correlation group with the cube graph group.

Translations of $\{0,1\}^3$ and coordinate permutations give a group of order $8\cdot6=48$. An automorphism fixing zero permutes its three neighbors. Once these are fixed, each weight-two vertex is the common neighbor of the appropriate pair other than zero, and the weight-three vertex is forced. Thus the displayed group is the full graph group. The parity-preserving subgroup has order twenty-four and acts faithfully on the four even vertices, hence is $S_4$. Translation by $(1,1,1)$ is central, interchanges parity, and supplies the direct factor in (C1).

For $L=S_4\times C_2$, its center is the second factor and is characteristic. Every automorphism consequently has the form
\[
(s,\epsilon)\longmapsto
(a s a^{-1},\epsilon\,\chi(s)),
\]
where $a\in S_4$ and $\chi:S_4\to C_2$ is either trivial or the sign character. The description follows by passing to $L/Z(L)\cong S_4$, using completeness of $S_4$, and then inspecting the central coordinate. The sign shear is a central involution in $\operatorname{Aut}L$, and the other factor is its inner group. Identifying it with the antipodal map proves compatibility of (C1) with the required pair.

The literal system has no chamber, since its two vertex types are never incident. In the repaired system, each chamber consists of an edge, its two endpoints and one of its two incident faces. There are $12\cdot2=24$. Every flag extends: within a square, two opposite-parity vertices are adjacent; every vertex–edge or edge–face flag extends through an endpoint or a face; and a vertex–face flag extends through either boundary edge at that vertex. These observations also handle singleton and empty flags. The added adjacency is already recovered by the edge objects, so it changes neither group. $\square$

The repair establishes geometry but does not assert firmness: some panels have a unique completion. The source's group-pair theorem survives for its literal incidence system even though its use of the word geometry does not.

## 5. Dodecahedron, icosahedron and $A_5$

Use the Petersen graph with vertices the two-element subsets of a five-element set and adjacency given by disjointness. It has ten vertices and fifteen edges. Its twelve pentagons correspond to Hamiltonian cycles of the complete graph on the five symbols: the five edges of such a Hamiltonian cycle are Petersen vertices, and disjointness puts them in pentagon order. Conversely the five pairs in a Petersen pentagon use each symbol twice, since pairs through one symbol form an independent set of size at most two in a pentagon. They consequently form a Hamiltonian cycle on the five symbols.

The natural $S_5$ acts transitively on these twelve cycles. A cycle stabilizer is a dihedral group of order ten, all of whose permutations on the five symbols are even. Thus $A_5$ has two orbits $\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal P$, each of size six, and every odd permutation interchanges them. These are the face and Petrie families in the source's hemidodecahedral construction.

Take vertex, edge, $\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal P$ as four types. Use containment for their vertex and edge incidences. For a face $F\in\mathcal F$ and a Petrie cycle $P\in\mathcal P$, set
\[
F*P\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F\text{ and }P\text{ have a common Petersen edge}.
\tag{P1}
\]
The rule (P1) is an explicit completion of an incidence left unspecified in [L126, p.7]; it is not quoted as a printed rule.

**Theorem R1.Petersen [APPLICATION].** The completed system is a geometry with 120 chambers and group pair $(S_5,A_5)$. It represents both $A_5$ and $A_5\times C_2$ exactly.

**Proof.** For completeness, the required small cycle calculation has two outcomes:

| Number of pairs in $\mathcal F\times\mathcal P$ | Common vertices | Common edges |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 30 | 3 | 2 |
| 6 | 0 | 0 |

In the first case the common edges form a path on the three common vertices. This table can be checked on a fixed Hamiltonian cycle $(1,2,3,4,5)$ and the six cycles obtained by odd permutations, with rotations and reversal identified. Its complementary Hamiltonian cycle is disjoint as a set of Petersen vertices; each of the other five has the three-vertex path just described. The accompanying replay enumerates these twelve cycles from permutations of five symbols and verifies every one of the thirty-six cross-pairs.

Every Petersen edge belongs to two cycles in each family, by transitivity and the count $6\cdot5/15=2$. Each incident face–Petrie pair has a common edge, and each of their common vertices lies on a common edge by the table. Hence every flag extends to a vertex–edge–face–Petrie chamber. The number of chambers is
\[
15\cdot2\cdot2\cdot2=120.
\]

The type sizes ten, fifteen, six and six recognize vertices and edges. The graph group is $S_5$: its largest independent sets are the five four-member stars of pairs through a symbol. As in the duad proof, a pairwise intersecting family with no common symbol has size at most three. Recovering these stars recovers the five symbols. Faces and Petrie objects have distinct vertex supports, so restriction is injective. All of $S_5$ preserves (P1), while exactly $A_5$ fixes both cycle families. The recognition lemma gives the asserted group pair.

Finally $\operatorname{Aut}A_5=S_5$ and $\operatorname{Inn}A_5=A_5$, a classical retained group fact. For $L=A_5\times C_2$, the center is characteristic and the quotient is $A_5$. There is no nontrivial homomorphism $A_5\to C_2$, since $A_5$ is perfect. Thus $\operatorname{Aut}L=\operatorname{Aut}A_5=S_5$, with inner subgroup $A_5$. The same pair proves both representations. $\square$

Among $S_5$-invariant completions of the source's two specified truncations, (P1) is the only one that gives a geometry. The thirty and six cross-pairs are the two orbits. To see transitivity on the thirty, fix one Hamiltonian cycle. Its order-five rotation is transitive on the five noncomplementary opposite-family cycles: the only Hamiltonian edge sets fixed by this rotation are that cycle and its complement. Including the six disjoint pairs produces flags having no common point, while excluding the thirty intersecting pairs leaves no chambers. This uniqueness concerns that precise completion problem, not all possible representations of these groups.

## 6. Provenance, verification and local cost

The source supplies the dihedral systems, complete-graph systems, duad–syntheme geometry, parity-colored cube, and two Petersen cycle families. Classical retained inputs are the automorphism groups of $S_n$ away from degree six and of $A_5$. F010 supplies the general normal-pair existence and pair-preserving geometry conversion; its theorem is neither a conjecture solved here nor a substitute for checking a particular construction.

The common work in R1 is recognition of an old structure, injectivity from old shadows, extension, and the kernel of the palette action. Local work is the dihedral flag stabilizer, the six-symbol reconstruction, the sign shear, and the two Petersen cycle orbits. Corrections are recorded where they enter. The companion S1 packet shows why ordered projective quadruples fail injectivity; it cannot be counted as a proof of the source's false projective conclusions.

The finite certificate reconstructs twenty-one models, checks all flags for chamber extension, and obtains the full groups with native GAP/GRAPE. A palette vertex attached to every object of its type makes the calculated automorphism group preserve the type partition while allowing a permutation of the palette. Fixing these palette vertices computes $K$; preserving the palette as a set computes $C$. This avoids confusing arbitrary uncolored graph automorphisms with correlations. The bounded checks support the written uniform proofs; they do not replace them.
