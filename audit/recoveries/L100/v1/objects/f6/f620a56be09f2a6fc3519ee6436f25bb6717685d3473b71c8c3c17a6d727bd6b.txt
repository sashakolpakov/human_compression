              Rank-two geometries from primitive residues
                     Human Compression project
                    Working proof, 14 October 2026
         Let G be a finite group. For proper subgroups A,B < G, put H = A ∩ B. The coset
       incidence structure Γ(G;A,B) has the right cosets of A and of B as its two types. Two cosets
       are incident when they intersect. We allow an isomorphism induced by an automorphism of G
       to interchange the two types. This is the equivalence used throughout this note.
         We consider connected, firm, flag-transitive geometries for which at least one of the two
       actions on a type is primitive and both actions on the neighbours of a vertex are doubly tran-
       sitive. In rank two these are the weak primitivity and local double transitivity conditions. The
       intersection property requires a partial linear space or a complete bipartite geometry. All group
       actions below are the specified actions of G; we do not replace them by the full automorphism
       group of the incidence graph.
       Theorem 1. Every geometry with these properties is represented by the following finite con-
       struction.
        1. Choose A from a complete set of conjugacy classes of maximal proper subgroups of G.
        2. Choose H from a complete set of A-conjugacy classes of maximal proper subgroups of A,
          retaining those for which A acts doubly transitively on A/H.
        3. Choose each actual subgroup B with H < B < G. Retain it when A ∩ B = H and the
          action of B on B/H is doubly transitive.
        4. Apply the incidence test in Lemma 2, and then identify pairs under Aut(G) and interchange
          of the two entries.
       Conversely, every retained pair defines such a geometry.
       Proof. Choose a flag of an arbitrary geometry and let A,B be its vertex stabilizers. Flag transi-
       tivity identifies its incidence relation with intersection of the corresponding cosets. Primitivity
       on one type says that its vertex stabilizer is maximal; interchange the types if necessary. Conju-
       gating the flag puts A in the chosen list. Its flag stabilizer is H = A∩B. Double transitivity on
       the neighbours implies primitivity on them, so H is maximal in A. An additional conjugation
       within A puts H in the second list without changing A. The conjugated B occurs among the
       actual overgroups in the third step. Thus none of the required geometries is omitted.
         Conversely, both indices [A : H] and [B : H] are at least two, giving firmness. The strict
       inclusion H < B and the equality A∩B = H imply B ⊈ A. Maximality of A gives ⟨A,B⟩ = G,
       which is exactly connectivity of the coset incidence graph. Right multiplication is transitive
       on flags: an incident pair has a common element g and is the translate (Ag,Bg) of the base
       flag. The two local actions are precisely those on A/H and B/H, and the action on the cosets
       of A is primitive. The incidence test supplies the remaining intersection condition. Finally,
       an automorphism of G taking one subgroup pair to another induces the stated equivalence
       of coset geometries. This last quotient neither discards an orbit nor changes the equivalence
       convention.

                Application to the Higman–Sims group
                The current computation uses the degree 100 permutation copy of HS already fixed in the
                project’s reviewed higher-rank roster. It does not assume that roster is complete. The subgroup
                classifications supplied by GAP’s table of marks library are retained inputs. In particular, the file
                tmhsmcl.tom, credited to Thomas Merkwitz and present in its recorded history in 1997, predates
                the 2005 article considered here. A library lookup and an isomorphism to its underlying group
                are not a fresh proof of that subgroup classification.
                    There are twelve classes of maximal subgroups. The local test leaves 38 choices of (A,H);
                their actual overgroups give 13 pairs satisfying both double transitivity conditions. Three fail
                the incidence test: one has c = 6 and two have c = 90. The ten remaining pairs form six orbits
                under Aut(HS) with type interchange. Their orders and local degrees are:

                Different rows are inequivalent already by the unordered pair of parabolic orders. The four
                additional pairs are identified with their row representatives by explicit automorphisms, recorded
                as images of generators of G. Each map is checked to be a bijective homomorphism and to carry
                both parabolics to their targets, with the stated interchange of types. Thus this quotient does
                not need a separate completeness assertion for a computed automorphism group. The search
                uses the previously reviewed simultaneous subgroup matcher. The full outputs, including the
                three rejected pairs, are retained beside this working manuscript.
                    This computation reproduces the rank-two count in D. Leemans, The residually weakly prim-
                itive geometries of HS, Australasian Journal of Combinatorics 33 (2005), 231–236, Section 2.
                The general construction and its proof above separate the finite local actions, actual overgroups
                and incidence test; the classical coset correspondence and subgroup classifications are borrowed.
                No claim of historical novelty for the enumeration principle is made.
