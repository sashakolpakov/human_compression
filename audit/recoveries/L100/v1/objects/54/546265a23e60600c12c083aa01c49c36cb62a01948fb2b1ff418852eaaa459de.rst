Concrete sufficient hypothesis and proof
---------------------------------------
Let X be a point type and E another type whose X-shadows are distinct
two-element subsets. Retain the other types. Suppose that for every other
object z and e in E, e is incident with z exactly when both points of its
X-shadow are incident with z. Replace E by a second copy X-minus of X,
while the original point type is X-plus. A point x-plus is incident with
y-minus exactly when{x,y} is the shadow of some e. Incidence from either
copy to an unchanged object z is the old incidence of that point with z;
all remaining relations are unchanged.

Every full flag in the new system corresponds exactly to an old full flag
(x,e,Z), the other point y of e supplying the minus copy. Conversely, new
flags using both copies correspond to old flags by the sufficient hypothesis.
A new flag with one copy can extend an old flag through that point, and a
flag with neither copy can extend an old flag on the retained types.
Thus all new flags extend. The correspondence is equivariant for the exact
old G-action, so old chamber transitivity yields new chamber transitivity.
Other target geometry properties will be transported only after complete
incidence/action maps to the separately acquired target models are supplied.

The same sufficient hypothesis pays the source's I12 literally: if the
intersection of the X-shadows of e and z is empty there is nothing to prove;
if it is{x}, use flag{x}; if it is both points, e is incident to z and
flag{e} has exactly that shadow. In the already accepted5.1/5.2 projective
models this is immediate from containment/disjointness of the actual point
pair with the relevant hyperoval/line. The4.1 truncation inherits it.
For rank two the hypothesis on other types is vacuous; this is the ordinary
bipartite double of the graph with edge set E. This supplies a direct proof
for2.7 ->2.6 without misapplying quotedCor2.1, which requires rank>=3.
