The replacement proof
---------------------

For a simple group, a commuting adjacent pair splits a string into
commuting normal subgroups, contrary to simplicity and independence.
The end parabolics therefore have noncommuting adjacent generators.

The dihedral-product lemma is correct for any elementary abelian
two-factor and odd dihedral rotation order. A reflection with trivial
dihedral projection would be central. Commuting endpoint reflections
therefore have the same projection. Squaring the adjacent products
removes their central two-coordinates and gives the same nontrivial
odd rotation subgroup in the two adjacent parabolics. Their marked
intersection should have order two, a contradiction. This supplies
the previously needed exclusion without a subgroup census.

The end-centralizer lemma uses the abelian centralizer of a noncommuting
adjacent pair. Any involution centralizing the whole end parabolic then
commutes with the remaining endpoint too, hence is central in the
generated group. This eliminates every involution-centralizer maximal
subgroup, even when encountered inside a smaller subfield subgroup.

The end-parabolic descent is complete relative to the explicitly retained
maximal-subgroup list. Point stabilizers and cyclic Hall normalizers have
two-part two and cannot contain the commuting distinct end generators.
The four-group normalizer has odd index over the elementary-two times
odd-dihedral subgroup, so all three involutions lie in the latter and
the preceding lemma applies. Descending subfields either terminates
at a full simple Ree group or reaches R(3). In the latter case the
involutions lie in PSL2(8). Its proper maximal subgroups are an elementary
two-group by C7 and the two odd dihedral normalizers; none contains the
required nondegenerate rank-three marking. Thus the end is PSL2(8).

The field-degree reduction is particularly useful. An odd-order field
automorphism fixing an end parabolic stabilizes the centralizer of its
last adjacent pair. That centralizer contains the opposite endpoint,
an involution, so Pierro's characteristic-three case is impossible.
If the automorphism fixed this endpoint as well, it would fix every
generator and be the identity. Its induced nontrivial odd action on a
subgroup of V4 therefore has order three. Its cube fixes all four
generators, proving that the actual field degree is three. For an end
PSL2(8), use the automorphism fixing its containing R(3); this forces
q=27. Otherwise both ends are conjugate R(s), with q=s^3.

The main fixed-subgroup theorem avoids the source's restriction on the
middle dihedral order. Write sigma=tau inn(u) for two automorphisms
fixing the conjugate ends and having the same outer class. Since both
fix b,c, the element u belongs to C_G(b,c). If u were trivial the two
fixed subgroups would coincide, violating independence.

In the elementary-three case, the group generated by the three tau
conjugates of u is a nontrivial tau-invariant elementary abelian group.
An order-three action on it has a nonidentity fixed point v by the
orbit count. Abelianity makes v fixed by sigma too. The intersection
property puts v in <b,c>, while its construction centralizes that
dihedral group. Its center has no element of order three. This is the
required contradiction, including middle products of order three.

In the two-group case u is an involution. The equation fixing a under
sigma gives a^tau=a^u, distinct from a because a is outside Fix(tau).
Both a and a^tau belong to the abelian centralizer of c,d, so form a
four-group. Conjugation by u exchanges its displayed generators. Since
u cannot belong to that four-group, they generate a nonabelian group
of order eight. This contradicts the retained elementary abelian Sylow
two-subgroups. The multiplication convention and direction of the inner
automorphism in this calculation agree with the written convention.

For q=27 both ends are PSL2(8). A product cd of order three or nine
would have a unique natural fixed point. Both its inverting involution
c and its centralizing endpoint a preserve this point; their four-group
cannot lie in a point stabilizer of two-part two. Thus cd and ab have
order seven. In the PSL2(8) split torus model, translations, inversion
and the field multiplier two generate all affine permutations of the
seven reflections. These actions give all ordered generating pairs
of D14. A different, outer, field automorphism of R(27), fixing R(3)
pointwise, cycles the three nonidentity elements of C_G(D14)=V4: a
fixed involution would belong to PSL2(8), whose centralizer of that
dihedral group is trivial. These two field automorphisms are correctly
distinguished in the text.

Consequently the full automorphism group is transitive on the relevant
ordered triples. Mapping (a,c,d) to (d,b,a) exchanges a,d and induces
a transposition on <a,d>. Its inner normalizer induces only C3, and
the quotient of the full normalizer by the inner normalizer embeds
in the odd outer automorphism group. Its induced action therefore
has odd order and cannot contain that transposition. This completes
the exceptional exclusion without assuming the stronger inner-triple
transitivity used in the source.
