Mathematical review
-------------------

The support-merging lemma handles all possible intersections. For two old
points, A_m is transitive on ordered pairs when m>=4. At m=3 its action on
unordered pairs, followed by inversion of a three-cycle, supplies both
orientations. Thus all three-cycles involving the new point are present.
When only one point is shared, conjugating a three-cycle on the old support
introduces one new point while retaining two old points. This reduces
twice to the first case. The transposition-pair identity and the conclusion
that all three-cycles generate the alternating group are correct.

For a primitive group, the connected components of the hypergraph of all
conjugate three-cycle supports form an invariant partition. Transitivity
rules out a mixture of isolated vertices and larger components, and
primitivity forces one component. A connected sequence of triples then
meets the exact hypotheses of the merging lemma. This proves the needed
three-cycle criterion without borrowing a stronger classification result.

For even n, the point fixed by x lies in a block fixed by x. The long cycle
forces that block to be either a singleton or the whole domain. Since the
generated group is transitive, only the two trivial partitions remain.
The stated x^3-conjugate of t is valid already at n=8; its product with t
has one three-cycle and two disjoint transpositions. Squaring gives the
required single three-cycle.

For odd n, every block in a nontrivial partition has odd size at least
three. An involution moving a block must move a second block, so its
support would have size at least six. Since t moves exactly four points,
it fixes every block. Its transposition (1,2), together with the full cycle
x, then forces all points into one block. The x^2-conjugate and the squared
product are correct at n=9 and uniformly thereafter.

In the even case every inverter of x fixes its unique fixed point. Fixing
t then fixes its partner 2, determining the reflection on the long cycle.
That reflection cannot preserve the remaining pair {3,4}. In the odd
case every inverter is a reflection z -> c-z. The differences 1 and 3 are
inequivalent up to sign for all n>=9, so the two support pairs cannot be
exchanged. Preserving both would require c=1=7 modulo n, impossible in
the stated range. These arguments use the actual full automorphism
premise and do not substitute an inner-automorphism test.
