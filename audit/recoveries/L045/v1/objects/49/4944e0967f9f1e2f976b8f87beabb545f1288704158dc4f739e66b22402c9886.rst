Tori and automorphisms
---------------------

centralizer normalizes the field generated by the irreducible lift. Its field
exponent j would imply M divides q^j-1; the proper-divisor bound excludes it.
An inverter would imply M divides q^j+1, and the only possible squaring
exception j=n/2 is excluded by the same strict bound. A nonzero field component
of an involutory outer image has exponent f/2. Squaring the corresponding

2j+1=n. This pays all field and graph-field cases, not just inner reversers.

For odd unitary degree, the stated gcd(q^n+1,q^(2j)-1)=q^g+1 is valid because
n/g is odd. The two inequalities for g=1 and g>=3 pay the determinant/centre
loss d(q+1). For even degree, the one-dimensional primary summand forces any

and the normalizer quotient is odd in both unitary cases. Thus it cannot
invert an element of order greater than two. The full unitary outer quotient

q-Frobenius coset. These arguments establish the actual centralizer and the
single-coset conclusion. The final |T| bound follows by taking the difference
