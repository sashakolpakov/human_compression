Uniform proof audit
-------------------
The ambient-conjugacy counting convention is valid: conjugates remain in the
special matrix subgroup, even if its own conjugacy classes split. If X squared
is scalar, scalar multiples in the same ambient orbit must satisfy z squared
= 1. Unequal eigenspace multiplicities exclude the negative scalar as well.
This pays every projective factor without an assumption about class splitting.

For GL and GU in odd characteristic the selected even negative eigenspace has
exactly the claimed balanced or nearly balanced dimension. The exponent is
floor(n squared/2). The remaining even degree with odd half-degree uses a genuine
determinant-one lift with square minus I; the congruences for the split linear
and split unitary centralizers are opposite, as written. In the nonsplit
unitary case the two eigenspaces are totally isotropic and paired, so GL_m(q^2)
is the actual centralizer. The product cancellations and strict constants
13/18 and 5/8 are correct. Multiplicities are at least two where the interval
bound is invoked.

In characteristic two, the linear centralizer has the repeated image block,
the residual block and exactly k(2n-3k) radical parameters. For the unitary
construction X inverse = X gives N adjoint = N; hence image N is isotropic and
the rank-k maps are nonsingular hermitian matrices on the induced pairing.
The stabilizer radical has k squared plus 2kl parameters. Both displayed
counts and their exponents follow, and unipotent matrices have determinant
one and no scalar fusion. The strict linear 3/4 and unitary 7/12 bounds are
valid from the stated dimension ranges, including q=2.

The odd-characteristic symplectic complex structures have the actual GL_m
or GU_m centralizers indicated. The product ratio for the second case is
product(1+(-q)^(-i)); discarding its even factors bounds it below by the
product over odd i. Scalar fusion is again at most two.

For characteristic-two symplectic groups, the nonalternating symmetric form
is equivalent to I_k. Its distinguished vector represents the square root of
self-pairing; the odd-dimensional complement is symplectic, while the even
case has the stated q^(k-1) kernel over a symplectic quotient. Thus the
isometry orders J_k and the isotropic-stabilizer radical exponent give the
exact displayed class count. Setting k=m yields the written product starting
at ceiling(m/2), with strict lower constant 2/3.

For orthogonal groups in odd characteristic the negative eigenspace can be
chosen with square determinant discriminant, which makes its spinor norm
trivial. In the formula's sign convention its sign is chi(-1)^a, agreeing
with the actual code. The complementary sign is available in every stated
range. Odd dimension has no projective scalar loss; in even dimension the
unequal multiplicities for odd m remove it, and the extra q factor for even
m pays the possible loss of two. I checked the leading factors 1/2, product
ratios, exponents m squared plus m and 4ab, and all numerical margins.

In characteristic two the quadratic forms with fixed polar form are counted
by their 2m coefficients. Invariance under f_i -> f_i+e_i imposes exactly
Q(e_i)=1, and a free Q(f_i) makes the two Arf traces equally frequent. The
symplectic orbit acts transitively on forms of each sign. Double counting
therefore gives the stated fraction of each involution class inside either
orthogonal group. Even k puts the entire class in the Dickson kernel. The
choices k=m or m-1 have the claimed exponents and product cancellations;
the uniform margin 44/51 is valid for m at least four. The scalar centre is
trivial in this characteristic.

The infinite-product estimates follow from paired factors and the elementary
product-versus-sum inequality, not from the finite arithmetic controls. Their
strictness is justified by at least two positive factors in the products used.
No asymptotic estimate, unexpanded source lower bound, or maximal-subgroup
classification entered these deductions.

Exact unitary correction
------------------------
A projective involution lifts to SU4 with square in its scalar centre mu_4.
For squares 1 and -1, the only noncentral determinant-one multiplicities are
2+2. Their images coincide; the negative scalar gives exactly the factor two.
For a square of order four, the eigenvalues have order eight. At q=3 modulo 8
they have norm minus one, forcing paired two-dimensional isotropic eigenspaces,
whose determinant is minus one; this case is impossible in SU4. At q=7 modulo 8
they have norm one, and determinant one forces multiplicities 1+3 or 3+1.
The scalar centre identifies all root choices and orientations, leaving one
additional projective family, with no fusion inside its chosen ambient orbit.
These cases exhaust the scalar centre and all semisimple eigenvalue patterns.
The two polynomial differences from q^8/2 are strictly negative on their
stated ranges. The source's unqualified stronger estimate is therefore
refuted by the uniform formula, not merely by the q=3 control.
