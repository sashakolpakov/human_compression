Triality argument
-----------------

The half-spin parity lists follow from the Clifford exterior model.  For odd
q, comparing the doubled exponents modulo q^3+1 gives precisely comparison
modulo (q^3+1)/2; it does not silently divide by two in a non-field.  For even
q, two is invertible in the exponent modulus.  The factor b=q^2-q+1 is odd,
the six large exponents reduce to a full q-orbit modulo b, and the two small
exponents are multiples of b.  Hence a similarity with the natural module
must match the six-dimensional pieces.  It forces u=+1 or -1 modulo q+1,
contrary to the chosen primitive small multiplier.  The proposed u exists
in exactly the stated generic range.

For a nontrivial involutory field projection the squared congruence is
q^3=q^(2j) modulo b.  Since q has order six there, it is impossible.
The remaining natural diagram possibility is excluded by the explicit
proper inverter: the two Frobenius determinants, or Dickson invariants,
add to an even value.  Quotients of inverters centralize x in the acquired
connected torus, so the count is AB, not the smaller cyclic-group order.
A fixed nonsingular line needs scalar-sign eigenvalue on an isometric lift;
none exists in the three generic spectra.

At q=5,u=1, exactly one of the half-spin modules has a scalar two-space.
The other two spectra agree modulo signs.  This intrinsically distinguished
module limits the diagram stabilizer to two possibilities.  In that module
the large eigenvalue has order63 and degree6.  The acquired inversion
argument gives square equal to conformal multiplier.  Its possible fixed
space dimensions are 5+3, 4+4, 3+5, or the nonsplit balanced case.  The
acquired fixed-group bounds therefore give the stated24q^8.  The scalar
plane is negative, so its six projective lines are all nonsingular; counting
one stabilizer per actual line gives six, not a count of conjugacy types.
