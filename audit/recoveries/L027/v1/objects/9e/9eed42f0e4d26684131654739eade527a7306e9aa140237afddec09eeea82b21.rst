Rank-two proof and the requested correction
------------------------------------------

The scalar kernel of SL3 to PSL3 has odd order dividing three. Squaring
is a bijection on it, so each projective involution has a unique
involutory determinant-one lift. The product of two such lifts is
conjugate to its inverse. Reciprocal eigenvalues in odd dimension, with
determinant one, give the spectrum 1,lambda,lambda^{-1}. Distinct
eigenvalues give exactly the split or norm-one semisimple cases. The
all-one and 1,-1,-1 Jordan cases give the stated p, four or 2p bounds.
The eigenvalue one prevents a nontrivial scalar shortening in the
projective quotient. Consequently the dihedral order divides one of
2(q-1), 2(q+1), 4p, with the stated characteristic-two rotation bound.

The odd-characteristic orthogonal embedding supplies the split and
nonsplit normalizers, and the displayed order-2p matrix and its diagonal
inverter give the unipotent family. The characteristic-two block
embedding and two elementary transvections supply the corresponding
semisimple and D8 cases. At q=2, however, the split torus has order one:
its full normalizer in SL2(2) is S3, not D2. The correction restricts this
normalizer sentence to q>2 and retains the valid q=2 nonsplit and
unipotent constructions. A cyclic rotation of order one also cannot
come from two distinct involutions, so smaller realized rotations are
restricted to order at least two. The theorem's numerical bound was
already correct. The source's assigned regular-unipotent order on page
11 is corrected to p in odd characteristic and four in characteristic
two. No conjugacy classification of all dihedral embeddings is claimed.

Actual block action and generic affine groups
--------------------------------------------

Commuting projective involutions have commuting unique involutory lifts:
conjugating one lift can only multiply it by a scalar, whose square must
be one. Their Klein group can therefore be diagonalized as stated. A
common invariant point or line is coordinate for this diagonal group;
duality, if necessary, puts a coordinate point on the middle axis. This
gives the complete block normalization (1). All generated matrices have
bottom eigenvalue plus or minus one, so the scalar centre is met

nonfaithful image or an assumed quotient intersection property.

left positive eigenspace gives the two bottom-row cases. In the nonzero
