Chiral constructions and regular subgroups in dimension three
Principal synthesis for Leemans–Vandenschrick
We recover the three principal theorems ofOn chiral polytopes having a groupPSL(3, q)as
automorphism group, in its fixed 25-page author edition. The constructions and their original
matrices are due to Leemans and Vandenschrick. The accompanying papers give complete new
proofs, distinguish their classical inputs, and record several corrections to intermediate formulas
and tables. The final unitary examples in Section 7 are supplied with explicit generators. Ear-

boundary stated below.
The common ideas are the actual translation kernels of small projective subgroups, elemen-
tary matrix generation, and the action of an involution’s centre and axis. A subgroup order
does not by itself prove an intersection property: the constructions use the actual common sub-
groups, and the finite unitary examples check every indexed intersection. A separate centralizer
calculation excludes reversing automorphisms of the whole ambient group.

Section 7: positive examples and retained prior results
Three explicit unitary examples in rank fourrecovers the three positive PSU 3(17) examples
reported in the source, with types [3,7,3], [3,8,3] and [3,9,3]. Its matrices are defined by
three Hermitian centres overF 17[β],β 2 = 3. Fresh exact permutation calculations prove full
ambient generation and all 768 indexed intersections. A written calculation of the full semilinear
centralizer excludes every reversing automorphism. This is a positive existence statement; no
absence of other types is inferred from the source’s sentence or from the exploratory search.
The negative lists for PSL 3(q) withq≤4 and for PSU 3(q) withq∈ {2,3,4,5,7,9,13,16}
are explicitly attributed in the source to earlier computational work. The three PSU 3(11)
pairs, of types [5,4,5] twice and [4,5,4] once, are likewise attributed there to data collected for
Buekenhout, Leemans and Tranchida. We retain these as prior source-attributed results, not
as our independently repeated censuses. The available edition of that earlier paper provides
timings and group data but not those output tuples; the project’s accepted algorithmic recovery
does not manufacture the missing execution receipts. This evidence boundary is explicit in the
retained-input register.
