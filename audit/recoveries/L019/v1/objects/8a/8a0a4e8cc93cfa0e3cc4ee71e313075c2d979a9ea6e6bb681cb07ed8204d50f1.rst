Perfect cuts under a subdivision
================================

Working proof, 13 October 2026. Not yet independently reviewed. This
records an exact geometric interface for L019; it proves neither a valid
C-extension nor a stability bijection. No source credit is claimed.

Hypotheses and notation
----------------------

Let rho_0,...,rho_(r-1) be an actual transitive string marking. At an
internal perfect cut i write q=rho_i=(a b) beta, with omission orbits
A,B, a in A, b in B, and beta supported in B minus {b}. Thus L=<rho_j:j<i>
is supported and transitive on A, while R=<rho_j:j>i> is supported and
transitive on B. This is a usable cut with its A-internal factor trivial.

Introduce a point c fixed by the old action, and replace q by

  u=(a c),   v=(c b) beta = u q u.

The two new positions are i,i+1. The other positions j become j'=j for
j<i and j'=j+1 for j>i. All statements below concern actual subgroups
and orbits; the intersection property is not used.

Orbit fact
----------

If H fixes c, the orbits of <H,(a c)> are the H-orbit of a enlarged by c,
and the other H-orbits unchanged. Indeed H and (a c) preserve those
sets, and the enlarged set is transitive because (a c) joins c to a.

For j different from i, let H_j be the old maximal omission. The new
maximal omission at j' is literally <H_j,u>. Both inclusions follow from
v=u q u and q=u v u. Consequently the new omission orbits are obtained
from the old ones by adjoining c to the orbit containing a.

Preservation away from the subdivided cut
----------------------------------------

Suppose first that j<i is an old perfect cut, with left and right
omission orbits C,D. The old suffix contains q, which moves a, while
it is supported on D. Hence a belongs to D. The new prefix at j' is
the old prefix. The new suffix is the group generated by the old
suffix and u, so it is supported and transitive on D union {c} by the
orbit fact. The crossing mark is still rho_j; it fixes c and has its
old unique crossing and old internal factors. Thus the new cut is
perfect with the same internal factors. For j>i reverse left and right:
the new prefix is the old prefix enlarged by u, and c joins its orbit.

Conversely, suppose j'<i is a new perfect cut. The new suffix contains
u, so both a and c belong to its right omission orbit D'. The old prefix
is unchanged. The identity new suffix=<old suffix,u> and the orbit fact
show that the old suffix is supported and transitive on D' minus {c}.
The old and new crossing marks coincide and fix c. Deleting c therefore
gives exactly an old perfect cut, with the same internal factors. For
j'>i+1 use the reversed argument. This proves the equivalence, including
purity and the condition that at least one internal factor is trivial.

The two new cuts
----------------

Omitting u gives the groups L and <v,R>, supported and transitive on
A and B union {c}, respectively. The sole crossing of u is (a c), so
position i is a pure perfect cut. Omitting v gives <L,u> and R, supported
and transitive on A union {c} and B. The sole crossing of v is (c b),
and its internal factors are the identity and beta. Thus position i+1
is a usable perfect cut. Both positions are internal since i was internal.

Hence the set of internal usable indices is transformed exactly by
replacing i with i,i+1 and shifting the later indices by one. In particular,
if i was the least internal usable index, it is still the least such
index after subdivision. This conclusion is valid even before one proves
that the new marking is C.

Boundaries of this deduction
---------------------------

The sufficient condition used in our existing induction is stronger
than usability: it requires a pure mark, or a trivial internal factor
and odd permutations in both attaching groups. Inserting u can turn a
previously even attaching group into an odd one. Preservation of the
least usable index therefore does not establish preservation of the
least sufficient index. The existence of one sufficient split does not
justify subdividing a different, least usable split.

The geometric inverse also needs a separate group argument. At two
consecutive perfect cuts with singleton middle point c, if the first
mark is u=(a c), and the next is v, then q=u v u fixes c and gives the
candidate contracted marking. The intersection property of the new
marking does not automatically imply the intersection property of this
candidate: an intersection of enlarged subgroups can have a stabilizer
of c larger than the subgroup generated by the old marks. The acquired
restriction-kernel issue must be resolved, not omitted.

Finally, the source counts markings up to both isomorphism and global
reversal. Choosing the leftmost usable index is not by itself a rule on
those equivalence classes. A compatible inverse and a confluence or
equivariance argument for different cut choices are still required.
