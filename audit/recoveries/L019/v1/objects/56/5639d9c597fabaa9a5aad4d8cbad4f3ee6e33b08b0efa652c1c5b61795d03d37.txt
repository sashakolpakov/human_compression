Two endpoint anchors on seven points
Human Compression project
Lemma 1.Leta, b, cbe involutions on a setXof seven points, withac=ca. Suppose that
distinct pointsu, v∈Xsatisfy
a(u)̸=u, b(u) =c(u) =u, c(v)̸=v, a(v) =b(v) =v.
Then⟨a, b, c⟩is intransitive onX.
Proof.PutK=⟨a, c⟩. Sinceaandccommute and have order two, everyK-orbit has size
1, 2 or 4. The orbit ofuis exactly{u, a(u)}: indeed,cfixes both points becausec(a(u)) =
a(c(u)) =a(u). Similarly, the orbit ofvis exactly{v, c(v)}. These two orbits are distinct.
Otherwisevwould belong to the first orbit, on whichainterchanges its two points, contrary
toa(v) =v.
These orbits account for four points. The other three cannot form a singleK-orbit, since
three is not an allowed orbit size. HenceKhas at least four orbits onX.
The involutionbfixesuandv. It is therefore a product of at most two disjoint transpo-
sitions on the other five points. Start with the graph whose connected components are the
K-orbits, and add an edge for each transposition ofb. Each added edge reduces the number
of components by at most one. There remain at least 4−2 = 2 components. They are
precisely the orbits of⟨a, b, c⟩, since paths using the edges of the three involutions describe
their generated orbits. Thus the generated group is intransitive.
Application and scope.A rank-three string representation on seven points with anchors
at its first and last marks satisfies the hypotheses. It cannot generate the natural symmetric
group. The conclusion requires neither the intersection property nor two eligible edges for
each mark. It resolves only this seven-point boundary; it makes no assertion about higher
degrees.
V erification and attribution.The written proof is self-contained. A separate enumera-
tion normalizesu= 1, v= 7 by relabelling. It examines all 231 nonidentity involutions, then
all 200 commuting endpoint pairs and 5000 anchored triples. None is transitive. An indepen-
dent GAP enumeration using actual generated group orbits gives the same counts. Positive
controls in degrees four and eight, and a seven-point control missing one anchor condition,
show that these tests do not merely reject every input. These computations supplement the
proof; they are not its completeness premise. The requested boundary was specified by the
investigator of the critical endpoint argument. The orbit proof and the two enumerations
were developed here. No priority, whole-manuscript, conjecture, Lean or compression claim
is made.
