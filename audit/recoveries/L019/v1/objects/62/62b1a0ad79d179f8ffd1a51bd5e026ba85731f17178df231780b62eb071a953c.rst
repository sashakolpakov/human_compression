Contraction of two consecutive pure perfect cuts
================================================

Working proof, 13 October 2026. No independent acceptance yet.
This is a bounded inverse theorem, not a proof that every target in the
stability range has such a pair.

1. Literal adjacent geometry
----------------------------
Let a transitive string C-marking have consecutive perfect cuts at i and
i+1, with the i-mark u pure. Write L for the marks before i and R for
those after i+1. At the first cut L is supported and transitive on A,
while the suffix is supported and transitive on its complement. Write
u=(a c), with a in A. The prefix at the second cut is <L,u>, so its
orbit is exactly A union {c}. The opposite orbit is B. Thus the whole
set is A disjoint {c} disjoint B, R is supported and transitive on B,
and the next mark is v=(c b) beta, with b in B and beta supported in
B minus {b}. It fixes A because it belongs to the suffix at the first
perfect cut. Every other mark fixes c. This proves the singleton middle
and the exact literal form; they are not inferred from adjacency alone.

Assume now v is pure too, so beta=1. Put q=u v u=(a b) and delete c.
The contracted marking consists of L, q, R.

2. One bridge rectangle
-----------------------
For arbitrary subgroups P of Sym(A) and T of Sym(B), put
H(P,T)=<P,T,(a b)>. Let X=a^P and Y=b^T. Then literally

 H(P,T)=Sym(X union Y) x P^(A minus X) x T^(B minus Y).

Indeed conjugating (a b) by P and T gives every (x y), x in X, y in Y.
Their connected complete bipartite graph generates Sym(X union Y).
Cancel the restrictions of P and T on X and Y to obtain their actual
complementary images, independently on disjoint supports. Both reverse
containment and directness are immediate from supports. This also covers
one-point X or Y, including trivial P or T.

Consequently, for P0<=P1 and T0<=T1,

 H(P0,T1) intersect H(P1,T0) = H(P0,T0).

To justify the restriction statement explicitly, their large symmetric
supports intersect in X0 union Y0. Each intersection element preserves
that intersection. Its action on A minus X0 belongs to the actual image
of P0, by the first group; its action on B minus Y0 belongs to the image
of T0, by the second. The three disjoint sets partition the domain.
The displayed direct-product formula proves the required membership.

3. Complete C-property of the contraction
-----------------------------------------
All old marks retain their orders and distant commutations. The new q
crosses A to B and is therefore distinct from all old marks, which are
supported separately. An old left mark except the one next to q fixes
a because it commuted with u; an old right mark except the next one fixes
b because it commuted with v. Hence all new string commutations hold.

Every interval ending at q is a literal point renaming of the old
interval ending at u: rename its point c to b, which all old left marks
fix. Thus it is C. Every interval beginning at q is similarly obtained
from the old interval beginning at v by renaming c to a. Intervals not
containing q are old submarkings and are C.

Induct on the length of an interval that contains q and at least one old
mark on each side. Its two end deletions are C by induction. Their groups
are H(P0,T1) and H(P1,T0), and their middle is H(P0,T0), where deleting
the first or last label gives the indicated inclusions. The bridge
rectangle pays the exact end-intersection. The standard end criterion
therefore proves the interval C, and finally the entire contracted list.
This is an actual subgroup proof, not a stabilizer or quotient shortcut.

Since L and R are transitive on A and B, the bridge formula for their
full groups is Sym(A union B), with no complementary factor. Thus the
contracted marking generates the full symmetric group of degree one
less. Its rank is one less as well, and its new q remains a pure perfect
cut. Subdividing q recovers the original ordered marking literally,
after giving the new point its old name c. Conversely contracting the
two pure marks introduced by subdivision at a pure perfect cut recovers
the original marking. These are inverse equations at a specified cut.

4. What remains outside this theorem
------------------------------------
No conclusion is made for a nontrivial beta, whose one-bridge group can
retain a parity correlation. No claim is made about the existence or
canonical location of a pure-pure pair, or compatibility between different
cut choices and reversal. These are still part of the Section 9 contract.
The bridge calculation reuses the supported-transposition factorization
in the acquired restriction-kernel manuscript; the source supplies the
rank and degree subdivision operation. No priority claim is made.
