Two endpoint anchors on nine points
Human Compression project
We give a finite boundary calculation for transitive permutation representations with four
marked involutions. Its purpose is to supply the small case in a separate induction concern-
ing the high-rank representations studied in [1]. The calculation below does not assume that
the generated group is symmetric or alternating, and does not use a pre-existing list of string
C-groups.
Let Ω be a set of nine points and leth 0, h1, h2, h3 be nonidentity involutions of Ω. Assume
the string relations [h i, hj] = 1 whenever|i−j|>1. WriteH i =⟨h j :j̸=i⟩. A transposition
ofh i is calledeligibleif its two points belong to differentH i-orbits. An initial anchor is a point
moved byh 0 and fixed by the other three marks; a final anchor is moved byh 3 and fixed by the
other marks.

(i)enumerate boundary.pygenerates all permutations of the seven unanchored points and
keeps precisely the nonidentity involutions. For each of the eleven pairs it tests the two
commutation conditions entry by entry. The connected components of the graph of all
marked swaps are exactly the generated group orbits: each generator preserves these com-
ponents and each edge is itself a generator step. The same procedure after omitting each
colour gives exactly its omission orbits. It therefore computes transitivity and eligibility
without a group library or a presupposed generated group.
(ii) The output gives all four literal surviving tuples and a bijection from each to (1). Each
bijection is constructed from 17→1 by propagating all four labelled edges. The certificate
checks that every point gets one value, that the values are distinct, and that every marked
edge is preserved. Both anchors have their specified images. Thus it proves ordered
conjugacy, not merely equality of unlabelled graphs or of group orders.
(iii)endpoint control.pyseparately enumerates matchings on six points. After fixing the

Completion of the proof.The orbit reduction and the exhaustive middle-pair calculation prove
the classification in Theorem 1. The checks in (iv) prove the additional generation and intersec-
tion assertions. The omission graph forh 1, h2, h3 in (1) immediately gives (2). Any permutation
centralizingHmapsH-orbits toH-orbits of the same size, so it preserves each of these three or-
bits. On a transitive orbit an equivariant permutation fixing a point is the identity: ifxis fixed,
then (xh)z = (xz)h =x h for everyh∈H. Consequently a centralizing permutation fixing 9 fixes
the whole six-point orbit. It also fixes 1, and can only swap 2,3. That swap commutes with each
of the three tail marks, so both possibilities occur. This proves the centralizer assertion.
In the notation used for an outer extension, takez= 1, p= 2, q= 3, w= 4. The first mark
is literally (z p)(q w) and the only nonidentity anchored tail centralizer element is (p q). No
assertion about the outer extension, about higher degrees, or about the whole source manuscript
is included here. The finite arithmetic is part of this boundary proof; no earlier small-degree
classification is retained as a premise.
