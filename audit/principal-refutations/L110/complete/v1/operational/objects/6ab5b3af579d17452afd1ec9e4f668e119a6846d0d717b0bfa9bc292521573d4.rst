Proof review
------------

The central-cover argument is exhaustive under the stated classical
subgroup classification. The root-group commutator gives a in K by
subtracting c times the c=1 relation. The diagonal scalar is also in K.
For an overgroup L, if L intersect P equals U_K, its normality gives
L=B_s. Otherwise the finite-p-group normalizer condition supplies a
strict intermediate normalizer; maximality makes U_K normal in L and
forces L into Z_F B_s. The acquired scalar-subspace correspondence then
gives exactly dimension-two spaces, rather than the full field centre.
The final exhaustion step correctly excludes torus normalizers by their
two-parts, identifies the unique point stabilizer using an involution,
and excludes a different simple overgroup through the previously proved
uniqueness theorem for B_s.

For the other intersections, the characteristic odd subgroup identifies
the ambient torus. An inverting involution cannot lie with its odd
subgroup in a point stabilizer's abelian odd quotient. Prime cyclic
extensions are exactly covers, including a prime already dividing the
smaller torus order. The nonsplit-order intervals do not overlap between
successive odd exponents. The uniqueness incidence argument for simple
subfields is valid for each prescribed field order. For distinct relative
primes r and p the second field exponent is er, not ep; equal primes
give the same subfield and no configuration. The solvable Sz(2) exception
is explicitly assigned to the nonsplit case.

The fixed-line formula has been checked in both gcd cases, including
r dividing d. Writing m=d/h gives gcd(d/h,j/h)=1 and hence
g=h*gcd(r,j/h), with no missing coprimality assumption. When g=rh,
averaging gives the fixed quotient and norm-one scalar rescaling gives
exactly 2^h-1 fixed nonzero vectors per stable line. When g=h,
rho=sigma^m has order r. The trace-zero complement removes its sole
one-eigenspace K. Each nontrivial rth root mu in the fixed scalar field
has one K-eigenline, by the elementary finite-field norm argument; it
is sigma-stable precisely when sigma fixes mu. Thus the second formula
counts exactly the nontrivial roots in F_(2^h), including the nonzero
case tested by n=21,d=3. Burnside then gives the orbit count.

The total for composite n counts the universal family once, the central
and torus families once for each maximal subfield, and unordered distinct
prime pairs once. The full ambient torus normalizers already included
in those rows are not added a second time. For prime n, summing the
accepted split and nonsplit family tables gives the displayed formula;
the mixed pair of prime nonsplit orders is the sole duplicate requiring
subtraction. The subgroup orders and the specified-group equivalence
convention prevent other claimed identifications.

The local double-transitivity conclusions follow from the acquired
affine and ovoid actions for the universal and central families. In the
prime split case the local stabilizer of order two is too small. In the
prime nonsplit case the complement has order four, forcing torus order
five; only q=8 supplies that case. The other subfield rows fail the
explicit stabilizer-size inequality. The estimates hold already at
s=8 and become stronger as s increases. Both q=8 exceptions are retained.
