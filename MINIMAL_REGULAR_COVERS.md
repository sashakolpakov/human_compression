# Minimal regular covers and monodromy

**An abstract polytope of finite rank has a unique minimal regular
polytopal cover if and only if its monodromy group, with the distinguished
generators, is a string C-group. Every such polytope has at least one
minimal regular polytopal cover.**

The flag set and the covers may be infinite. Thus a polytope with
nonpolytopal monodromy has at least two minimal regular polytopal covers.
This answers the unrestricted question in Cunningham–Pellicer's
*Open problems on k-orbit polytopes*, Problem 5, and Cunningham's later
discussion in *Chiral polytopes whose smallest regular cover is a polytope*, §4.
The proof does not require membership in one of the previously treated families.

The complete argument is divided into the
[covering and kernel proof](research/case_study/phase216_general_covers.md)
and the [geometric filling proof](research/case_study/phase216_geometric_filling.md).
The [independent review](research/case_study/phase222_general_cover_review.md)
checks both arguments against their primary sources. The
[publication record](provenance/phase117_review.md) records the exact
admitted versions and formal execution.

## The argument

Let W be the group generated by the flag adjacencies, imposing only
involutivity and commutation of nonconsecutive generators. Let K be the
kernel of its action on the source polytope's flags. Regular polytopal
covers correspond to normal subgroups N contained in K for which W/N
has the intersection property. Call these kernels admissible.

1. **Minimal covers exist.** A union of a chain of admissible kernels is
   admissible: an equality modulo the union already occurs in one member
   of the chain, which supplies the required intersection witness.
   Zorn's lemma gives a maximal admissible kernel above every admissible
   kernel. A unique maximal kernel would therefore contain every
   admissible kernel.

2. **The flag action reduces the relations to intervals of ranks.**
   If a product of words supported on disjoint sets of ranks fixes every
   flag, each factor fixes every flag. Graph-product normal forms then
   show that K is normally generated by cyclically reduced relations
   supported on consecutive intervals.

3. **Large powers preserve all proper parabolics.** For a relation with
   full support in an interval, cone off every proper standard parabolic
   coset in its right-angled Coxeter group. The resulting graph is
   hyperbolic, its action is acylindrical, and the relation acts
   loxodromically. The proof supplies the comparison of graphs and a
   uniform projection bound. Theorems of Hull and Dahmani–Guirardel–Osin
   then imply that imposing any sufficiently large power of the relation
   preserves the entire union of proper parabolics. This proves the
   intersection property of the quotient. An amalgamated-product
   argument restores the generators outside the interval.

4. **Consecutive powers recover K.** For each normal generator w of K,
   the normal closures of wᵐ and wᵐ⁺¹ are admissible for sufficiently
   large m. Their join contains w. Therefore the join of all admissible
   kernels is K. If the maximal admissible kernel were unique, it would
   equal K, making the monodromy group itself a string C-group.

The marked-kernel correspondence also respects unbased, rank-preserving
isomorphism classes of regular covers. The proof includes this step;
uniqueness is not restricted to a chosen base flag.

## Borrowed results and contribution

The source question remains attributed to Cunningham and Pellicer.
The group–polytope correspondence, graph-product normal forms and amalgam
normal forms are retained standard results. The geometric proof uses
substantial theorems of Chen–Heuer, Caprace–Fujiwara, Bestvina–Fujiwara,
Behrstock–Hagen–Sisto, Hull, and Dahmani–Guirardel–Osin. Their exact
versions and roles are recorded in the source ledger.

The contribution here is the application to actual polytope flag kernels,
the comparison with the graph obtained by collapsing all proper parabolic
cosets, the uniform projection argument, and the resulting equality of
the join of admissible kernels with K. These steps and the source
interfaces receive a separate independent review.

Five new Lean declarations in
[CoverKernelGeneration.lean](research/case_study/formal/CoverKernelGeneration.lean)
verify the disjoint-coordinate argument, preservation of intersections
under injection on the proper parabolics, and the final normal-generation
and consecutive-power implications. They use the earlier six
[kernel-chain lemmas](research/case_study/formal/CoverKernelChains.lean).
The supply of powers and normal-generation hypotheses remain explicit;
the written geometric proof establishes them. The full theorem is not
formalized in Lean.

## What this settles, and what remains

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| Can nonpolytopal monodromy coexist with a unique minimal regular polytopal cover? | No, in every finite rank, with arbitrary flag sets and arbitrary covers. |
| Does every abstract polytope of finite rank have a minimal regular polytopal cover? | Yes. |
| Must a finite polytope with nonpolytopal monodromy have two finite minimal regular covers? | [Yes in rank four, with infinitely many finite covers](FINITE_RANK_FOUR_COVERS.md). Higher ranks remain unresolved here. |
| Must nonpolytopal monodromy always give infinitely many minimal covers? | [Proved for every finite rank-four polytope](FINITE_RANK_FOUR_COVERS.md). Not established in general. |
| Is this the first solution in the literature? | Historical priority remains unverified. |

The [two-cover argument](FINITE_RANK_FOUR_COVERS.md) now gives infinitely
many finite minimal covers throughout rank four. The earlier
[surface construction](INFINITE_MINIMAL_COVERS.md) retains its exact family
results and attribution.
The general theorem answers the existing minimal-cover question; it does
not replace the source papers' classifications or increase the
principal-source compression count. No numerical compression factor is
assigned to a question for which the source supplied no proof.

```sh
python3 -B -O research/case_study/phase222_check.py --out /tmp/NEW-general-covers --lean LEAN --packages PACKAGES
```

This checks the frozen source bindings, eleven scoped Lean declarations,
three altered-proof rejections and four finite countermodels to false
weakenings. The geometric theorem receives the separate written review.
