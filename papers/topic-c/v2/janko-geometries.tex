\begin{abstract}
We recover the six rank-four geometries of Hartley and Leemans by
endpoint conjugation and a central twist. Two subgroup intersections
control the four conjugation steps, while explicit coset tests verify
flag completion. A complete extension search over proper residues
then proves that these are the only such geometries and excludes all
higher ranks. The proof retains the full subgroup catalogue of the
first Janko group and exact group algorithms, but not a previous
catalogue of its geometries. Both required finite programs are given
in full in the accompanying appendix.
\end{abstract}
\section{The common statements and finite inputs}
The specified source is the ten-page journal article [HL05]. The
common paper [U] supplies C.Endpoint, its odd-order consequence
C.Odd, the central-twist lemma C.Twist and the exact finite coset
criterion C.HellyCount. These provide the group and geometric steps
used here. In particular, the intersection property alone is not
treated as proof of flag completion for a nonstring diagram.

The mathematical finite inputs are the complete TomLib subgroup
catalogue and its permutation model of $J_1$, exact GAP subgroup and
conjugacy algorithms, and a completed Todd--Coxeter calculation.
The classical spherical group $[3,5]$ has order $120$.
None of these inputs supplies the desired list of geometries.

\section{Complete finite programs and formal scope}
The companion appendix contains two unchanged full programs:
\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{phase40\_compress\_operations\_check.g}\\
\texttt{phase40\_compress\_operations\_exhaustive.g}.
\end{quote}
The first finds the source marking, completes its presentation index,
checks all six constructions and both failed operations, and verifies
every required subgroup and coset equality. The second derives the
seven maximal subgroup classes from all forty catalogue classes,
enumerates the local involution sets and every admitted extension,
and produces the exact conjugacy keys used above. The geometric
census is an output, not a lookup table read by either program.

The retained exact execution used GAP 4.15.1 and TomLib 1.2.11, with
the actual Janko data file bound in its record. It checked 1,536
ordered marked-subgroup intersections and 96 coset equalities for the
six constructions, all 100,016 rank-four extensions and all 21,352
rank-five extensions. The unchanged runner
\texttt{phase150\_module\_C\_run.py} permits a separate fresh replay
in an empty output directory. Merely checking its saved record is not
described as rerunning the census.

The source's six existence theorems, generation lemmas, full rank
classification, two failed changes, incidental normalizer and initial
spherical residues are all included. The transposition of two figure
labels is explicitly separated from its six constructions. No seventh
geometry or new historical classification is claimed.

The existing Lean component for endpoint intersections proves a
generic mixed-subgroup identity under explicit hypotheses. It does
not formalize the complete endpoint criterion, typed coset tests,
TomLib catalogue, coset enumeration or this source classification.
Those dependencies remain written or computational at the scopes
stated here.
