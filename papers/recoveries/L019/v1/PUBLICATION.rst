Stability and finite starting cases for symmetric string C-groups
================================================================

This recovery proves the fixed source's principal stability theorem and
constructive correspondence. For every kappa >= 1, the number of string
C-markings of S_n of rank n-kappa is constant for n >= 2kappa+3, where both
marked-group isomorphism and reversal of the marking are imposed. Pure
subdivision and actual contraction give mutually inverse maps on these
classes. The six stable constants are 1, 1, 7, 9, 35, 48.

The source is Peter J. Cameron, Maria Elisa Fernandes and Dimitri Leemans,
*The number of string C-groups of high rank*, arXiv:2212.12723v2,
11 January 2023. Its Theorems 1.1 and 9.10, the constructive reduction
stated after Theorem 1.1, and its six finite constants form the principal
contract recovered here. These were results stated by the original
authors; this publication makes no claim to solving a new open problem.

The correspondence checks actual subgroup intersections under contraction;
it does not infer the intersection property from a quotient. All pure
perfect cuts lie in a consecutive interval. The chosen inverse contracts
the least internal pure pair, with both inverse equations and compatibility
with reversal explicitly proved. At the finite boundary, a unique balanced
pair splits the marking into two rooted wings. Complete searches on degrees
2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 give respectively 1, 1, 6, 3, 11 and 19 rooted classes.
The unordered-pair formula gives the six constants above. All 41 wings and
101 glued representatives are retained and checked. Source table counts and
atlas completeness are not inputs to this finite enumeration.

The degree-six distinction matters. The global abstract marked simplex
class may use its ordinary natural representative. The rooted wing census
instead uses actual point conjugacy fixing the root, and never identifies
its degree-six actions using outer automorphisms.
