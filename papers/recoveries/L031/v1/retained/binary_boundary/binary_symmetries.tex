\title{Three binary exceptions to a symmetry exclusion}

The supplied edition of Brooksbank--Ferrara--Leemans~\cite{BFL},
Theorem 1.4, excludes the field of two elements from its existence
statement in every dimension $d\geq3$. There are exceptions in
dimensions three, four and five. We prove them directly, including
generation of the full isometry group and the intersection property.
This corrects a boundary of the supplied theorem. It does not replace
its positive constructions over larger fields, decide other binary
dimensions, or supply a new answer to an open problem.

For $n\geq4$ put
\[
 E_n=\{x\in\mathbf F_2^n:\textstyle\sum_i x_i=0\},\qquad
 Q(x)=\frac{|\operatorname{supp}x|}{2}\pmod2.
\]
The numerator is even. The identity
$|A\mathbin\triangle B|=|A|+|B|-2|A\cap B|$ shows that the
polar form of $Q$ is $B(x,y)=\sum_i x_i y_i$ on $E_n$.
Thus $Q$ is a quadratic form. Put $v_i=e_i+e_{i+1}$,
$1\leq i<n$. These vectors form a basis, $Q(v_i)=1$, and
$B(v_i,v_j)=1$ exactly when $|i-j|=1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{path}
The symmetries $s_i:x\mapsto x+B(x,v_i)v_i$ generate a
faithful copy of $S_n$ on $E_n$. On this ordered generating set
they form a string C-group of rank $n-1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The displayed formula interchanges coordinates $i$ and $i+1$,
so the generators are the usual adjacent transpositions. Their
action on $E_n$ is faithful: an element acting trivially fixes
every vector $e_a+e_b$, hence every two-element subset. For
distinct $a,b,c$, the intersection of $\{a,b\}$ and $\{a,c\}$
is $\{a\}$, so it fixes every coordinate. The adjacent
transpositions generate all permutations, as repeated adjacent
interchanges move any entry to any prescribed position.

For a subset $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$, join $i$ to $i+1$
when $i\in I$. Its components are intervals. The subgroup
$\gen{s_i:i\in I}$ is exactly the group of all permutations
preserving each component: each interval has its adjacent
transpositions, and generators on distinct components commute.
A permutation lies in two such subgroups exactly when it
preserves every intersection of a component of the first path
with one of the second. These nonempty intersections are the
components of the path with edge set $I\cap J$. Consequently
\[
 \gen{s_i:i\in I}\cap\gen{s_j:j\in J}
       =\gen{s_k:k\in I\cap J}.
\]
Nonadjacent generators commute and adjacent ones have product
of order three. This proves all string C-group requirements.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
For $n=4,5,6$, the full isometry group of $(E_n,Q)$ is the
faithful $S_n$ of Lemma~\ref{path}. It is therefore a string
C-group generated by symmetries of rank $d=3,4,5$, respectively.
Its polar radical has dimension one for odd $d$ and zero for
even $d$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The orthogonal complement of $E_n$ in $\mathbf F_2^n$ is
spanned by the all-one vector $z$. Hence the polar radical is
$\gen{z}$ when $n$ is even, and zero when $n$ is odd.

For $n=4$, $Q(z)=0$. The quotient $E_4/\gen z$ has dimension
two. Every isometry fixes $z$ and induces an invertible map on
that quotient, giving at most $|\mathrm{GL}_2(2)|=6$ choices.
The kernel consists of maps $x\mapsto x+f(x)z$, where $f$ is
linear and $f(z)=0$. There are at most four such maps. Thus
the isometry group has order at most 24. It already contains
the faithful $S_4$ of order 24, proving equality.

For $n=5$, the nonsingular vectors are precisely the ten
vectors of weight two; the vectors of weight four are singular.
Two distinct nonsingular vectors have polar product one
exactly when the corresponding pairs share a coordinate.
Every isometry therefore acts on the graph of the two-element
subsets of a five-element set, adjacent when they intersect.
The cliques of size four in this graph are exactly the five
stars of all pairs containing one fixed coordinate. Indeed,
three pairwise intersecting pairs without a common coordinate
are the three edges of a triangle, and no fourth distinct pair
can meet all three. Graph automorphisms consequently permute
these five stars. Their action on the stars is faithful because
each vertex is the unique common member of two stars.
The isometry action on this graph is faithful too, since its
weight-two vectors span $E_5$. Thus the isometry group has
order at most $5!$, attained by the coordinate permutations.

For $n=6$, $Q(z)=1$. The quotient $W=E_6/\gen z$ is a
nondegenerate symplectic space of dimension four. The induced
homomorphism from the isometry group into $\mathrm{Sp}(W)$
is injective. An element in its kernel is
$x\mapsto x+f(x)z$ with $f(z)=0$, and preservation of $Q$
gives $Q(x+f(x)z)=Q(x)+f(x)=Q(x)$, so $f=0$.
To count the symplectic group, choose the first vector of a
symplectic basis in 15 ways, its partner in 8 ways, and a
symplectic basis of their two-dimensional perpendicular
complement in $3\cdot2$ ways. A symplectic map is uniquely
determined by the image of a fixed symplectic basis, so its
order is $15\cdot8\cdot3\cdot2=720$. The faithful coordinate
$S_6$ already has this order. Equality follows once more.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Relation to the printed matrices.}
In the basis $v_1,\ldots,v_{n-1}$ the quadratic form has
diagonal entries one and consecutive upper entries one,
with all other entries zero. This is precisely the source's
$\Phi(1,\ldots,1)$. The group it generates is $S_{d+1}$;
the supplied proof on physical page 8 instead writes $S_d$.
The three full-isometry examples above disprove the stated
binary exclusion, independently of how that subscript arose.
The first example has singular radical, the third nonsingular
radical; both are allowed by the wording of Theorem 1.4.

The proof uses only elementary linear and permutation group
arguments written here. No finite program, external group-order
table or formal verification is a premise. The standard
permutation model and the known small-group identifications
are not claimed as new ideas. The earlier authors retain
attribution for the larger-field construction; its complete
recovery remains a separate obligation.
