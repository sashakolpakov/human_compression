\paragraph{Finite transport and acquired names.}
The finite certificate gives images of the ordered generators of a named
model $M$ in an actual subgroup $U$. The verifier closes both permutation
groups and constructs a bijection $f:M\to U$ fixing identity. For every
element $x$ and every model generator $s$ it checks
$f(xs)=f(x)f(s)$. Induction on positive words proves that $f$ is a group
isomorphism; inverses are positive powers because the groups are finite.
This is the previously acquired generator-edge transport argument.
The names of the reused subgroup-atlas models are acquired with their
exact trees, degrees and generators. An order match alone is never used
as a named isomorphism. New elementary factors are the literal cyclic,
dihedral, quaternion, symmetric, alternating and affine models already
proved in the first local-atom component. Direct products act on disjoint
supports. The generalized dihedral model of order 50 acts on
$\mathbb F_5^2$ by its two translations and $v\mapsto-v$.

Eight additional models have a recursive certificate $U=N:C_p$, with
$p=2$ or $3$. A full literal map identifies $N$ with an earlier model.
The supplied element of order $p$ lies outside $N$, normalizes it, and
the verifier checks that the products $nt^i$ are exactly $U$. Consequently
the claimed split extension follows with its actual conjugation action.
The recursive dependency order is checked; no model is justified by a
circular reference. All maps and recursive factors are verified by
\texttt{compound.py}, which makes no group-software or isomorphism call.

\begin{lemma}[The common central kernel of order 64]
The kernels in 2.2, type 2; 3.4, type 3; and 3.5, type 1 are central
nonsplit extensions $C_4\mathbin{\cdot}E_{16}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the first actual kernel the certificate gives a central element
$z$ of order 4 and four quotient lifts. Their squares and commutators
lie in $\langle z\rangle$. The sixteen binary words in these lifts give
sixteen distinct cosets covering the group. The quotient is therefore
$E_{16}$. Two supplied lifts do not commute. Multiplying either lift by
a central element leaves their commutator unchanged; the verifier also
checks all sixteen such pairs. An elementary abelian complement would
provide commuting lifts of every two quotient elements, a contradiction.
The other two kernels are explicitly isomorphic to this one by their
common exact model. This proves the three printed $4\cdot2^4$ labels
with the stronger central and nonsplit qualification.
\end{proof}

For 2.3, four explicit commutators generate the actual group of order

For each of the three order-32 kernels, transport identifies it with
$X$. Direct checking gives $Z(X)=X'=C_2$, and every square lies in the
centre. The commutator pairing on $X/Z(X)\cong\mathbb F_2^4$ is

centre. If $A\le X$ is abelian, $AZ(X)/Z(X)$ is isotropic, so has
dimension at most 2. Hence $|A|\le 8$. In particular $X$ has no
elementary abelian subgroup of order 16 and cannot have the printed
