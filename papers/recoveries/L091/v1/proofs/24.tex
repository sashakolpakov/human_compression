\section{Subgroup counts from complete retained inputs}
For a representative $H_i$ of a $G$-conjugacy class, its class length is
$\ell_i=[G:N_G(H_i)]$. Thus the total number of subgroups is
$\sum_i\ell_i$, provided that the representative list is complete.
The six atlas inputs for $M_{11},M_{12},J_1,M_{22},J_2,M_{23}$ have
separately accepted complete numerical tables, with literal class-length
and source-row correspondences. Their exact review records, all rows and

\begin{proposition}
In a complete table of marks, let $m_{i,1}$ be the mark at the trivial
subgroup and $m_{i,i}$ the diagonal mark. Then
\[
 \ell_i=\frac{m_{i,1}}{m_{i,i}}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The transitive coset set associated with $H_i$ has $[G:H_i]$ elements,
all fixed by the identity. Its $H_i$-fixed cosets are precisely those
represented by elements of $N_G(H_i)$: a containment between conjugates
of the same finite order is equality. Hence the two marks are
$[G:H_i]$ and $[N_G(H_i):H_i]$. Their quotient is $[G:N_G(H_i)]$.
Distinct conjugacy classes partition the subgroup set.
\end{proof}

For HS the retained complete 589-class input is already used by its
accepted geometry census. A bounded extraction checked equality of all

against that exact table model. It returned every pair of marks, and
compared their ratios with the library class lengths. The clean terminal
execution is retained; the present Python check independently recomputes
every ratio and the sum. Thus this is a new arithmetic join to an acquired
complete input, not a new subgroup enumeration.
