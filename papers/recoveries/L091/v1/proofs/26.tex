\begin{proposition}
The seven acquired rank-two prescriptions are eligible and mutually
nonisomorphic, even allowing a permutation of the types. Exactly two
are truncations of a supplied rank-three geometry: $2.3$ is the
truncation of active row $2$ on types $1,3$, and $2.6$ is the truncation
of active row $1$ on those types. The other five are not truncations of
any of the twenty-one supplied rank-three geometries.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Eligibility of $2.1,2.2,2.4,2.5,2.7$ is already established by the accepted
literal realizations. For $2.3$ and $2.6$ we use the actual subgroups
$P_1,P_3$ of their accepted parent. The incidence maps are the identity
on the retained right-coset sets, so preserve both incidence and
nonincidence. Their intersections and indices are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrr|rr}
 & |P_1|&|P_3|&|P_1\cap P_3|&[P_1:P_1\cap P_3]&[P_3:P_1\cap P_3]\\\hline
2.3&8160&2880&480&17&6\\
2.6&1080&2880&180&6&16
\end{array}
\]
The complete actual local coset actions are doubly transitive, hence
primitive, and the two subgroups generate the accepted ambient group.
Thus these geometries are connected and firm; all rank-one residues are
nonempty. Rank-two coset incidence is flag-transitive because an incident
pair has a common representative. For weak primitivity at the empty
flag, retain the parent's already checked primitive type: type $1$ in
active row $2$, and type $3$ in active row $1$. These are the same actual
subgroups and ambient actions, not merely subgroups of the same order.
Together with the primitive local actions this proves residual weak
primitivity.

For completeness, the rank-two intersection test uses all double cosets.
Put $B=P_1\cap P_3$. The neighbours of $P_1$ are the cosets $P_3h$,
where $h$ runs over a right transversal of $B$ in $P_1$. Two distinct
objects of the first type with a common object of the second type can
be transported to $P_1,P_1x$ with $x\in P_3\setminus B$. The orbits
of such pairs are indexed by the nonidentity $(B,B)$ double cosets in
$P_3$. For every representative the two complete neighbour sets intersect
in exactly one object, as checked in the actual finite records. Hence
both geometries satisfy $\mathrm{IP}_2$. No implication from local
double transitivity to this intersection assertion is assumed.

The seven unordered pairs of object cardinalities are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rr@{\qquad}c|rr}
2.1&17442&43605&2.2&17442&17442\\
2.3&6156&17442&2.4&26163&43605\\
2.5&23256&25840&2.6&17442&46512\\
2.7&14688&14688&&&
\end{array}
\]
They are distinct, proving bare nonisomorphism. For a flag-transitive
rank-two coset geometry, its two object counts and its number of incident
pairs are $N/|P|$, $N/|Q|$, and $N/|P\cap Q|$. These are intrinsic even
if an isomorphism does not preserve the specified action. We compare
the two parabolic orders and their full intersection order for each of
the three pairs in each of the twenty-one supplied rank-three families.
Among all sixty-three comparisons, only active row $1$, types $1,3$,
matches $2.6$, and only active row $2$, types $1,3$, matches $2.3$.
All other candidate truncations are excluded by the intrinsic counts.
The two matches are actual isomorphisms by the earlier literal coset
maps, completing the assertion.
\end{proof}
