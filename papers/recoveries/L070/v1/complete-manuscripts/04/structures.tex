\begin{lemma}[Finite transport and decomposition]
Let $X=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_s\rangle$ and
$Y=\langle y_1,\ldots,y_s\rangle$ be finite permutation groups.
Suppose a map $f:X\to Y$ is defined on every element, sends identity
to identity, and satisfies $f(xx_i)=f(x)y_i$ for every $x,i$.
Then $f$ is a surjective homomorphism. If $|X|=|Y|$, it is an
isomorphism. If instead its identity fibre is $N$, it induces
$X/N\cong Y$.

If $N,K\leq H$, $N\trianglelefteq H$, $N\cap K=1$ and
$|N||K|=|H|$, then $H\cong N\rtimes K$. If also $[N,K]=1$,
then $H\cong N\times K$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Following any word in the generators through the edge identities gives
the corresponding word in their images. Every group element has such
a word, because inverses are positive powers in a finite group.
Appending the word for $z$ to the word for $x$ proves
$f(xz)=f(x)f(z)$. The images generate $Y$. The two conclusions follow
from the kernel and order formulas. For the second statement, the
normal product $NK$ has order $|N||K|$, hence equals $H$.
Multiplication identifies the corresponding semidirect product with
$H$; commuting factors give the direct product.
\end{proof}

The reader constructs complete generated sets by breadth-first closure.
It propagates each proposed map from identity and tests every generator
edge, rejecting inconsistent assignments. For a proposed factorization,
it checks actual membership, conjugation of each normal-factor generator
by every ambient generator, trivial intersection and the exact product
order. In the direct-product case it checks commutation of the two
generator sets. Thus the lemma applies to the literal subgroups,
recursively, rather than merely to their names.

The elementary atoms require no naming database. A cyclic generator is
checked by closure. Commuting generators of exponent three and order
$27$ give $E_{27}$. The dihedral atom is generated by $x,y$ with
$|x|=9$, $y^2=1$, $y^{-1}xy=x^{-1}$ and group order $18$.
For $Q_8$, the corresponding tests are
$|x|=4$, $y^2=x^2$, $y^{-1}xy=x^{-1}$ and group order eight.
These relations reduce words to the usual distinct normal forms.
Finally an explicit generator-edge isomorphism from the full group of
24 permutations on four letters certifies each $S_4$ atom.

\begin{theorem}[The structural assertions of Table 1]
For the retained rank-four entries, the cover and rotation groups have
the following structures. The same conclusions hold for their duals.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{ccc}\toprule
Type&Cover group&Rotation group\\\midrule
$\{3,3,8\}$&$((Q_8\times C_2)\rtimes C_2)\rtimes S_4$&$Q_8\rtimes S_4$\\
$\{3,6,9\}$&$E_{27}\rtimes(D_9\times C_2)$&$(E_{27}\rtimes C_3)\rtimes C_2$\\
$\{3,6,18\}$&$(E_{27}\rtimes(D_9\times C_2))\times C_2$&$((E_{27}\rtimes C_3)\rtimes C_2)\times C_2$\\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each of the eight accepted entries let $W=\langle r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3\rangle$
be its literal cover group, $H=\langle r_0r_1,r_0r_2,r_0r_3\rangle$,
and let $N$ be generated by the source words. The checker reconstructs
these actual permutations. It verifies a full injective generator-edge
map from $W$ to its compact certificate model. It separately propagates
the images of the three displayed generators of $H$ and checks that the
identity fibre is exactly the literal subgroup $N$. This identifies the
rotation quotient with its compact model, including the actual kernel.
It also verifies $[W:H]=2$ and the stated cover and quotient orders.

The 16 atomic and compound registry models have explicit normal factors,
complements and generator-edge transports. Applying the lemma at every
registry node proves the six displayed names. All eight actual cover
and quotient pairs are transported to these same named models. In
particular the last two source rows and their duals use identical
registry models; their common names are proved by maps, not by repeated
order comparisons. The finite verification has 53 maps and 133,673
generator-edge checks. The actual kernel orders are respectively
$2,3,3,3$ for the four source rows.
\end{proof}
