An independent generating family means an irredundant one: omitting any
member gives a proper subgroup. A string of involutions satisfies
$[t_i,t_j]=1$ whenever $|i-j|>1$. No intersection property is assumed
in this note.

\section{The literal independent-generation claim}
Proposition~3.3 on physical page8 of the supplied 2016 arXiv edition of
\emph{Highest rank of a polytope for $A_n$}, by Cameron, Fernandes,
Leemans and Mixer, asserts an $n-4$ bound for arbitrary independent
generating families of primitive groups of degree $n\ge8$ other than
$A_n,S_n$. The following literal permutations contradict that statement:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 t_0&=(2\,3)(6\,7),&t_1&=(1\,4)(6\,7),\\
 t_2&=(2\,4)(5\,7),&t_3&=(2\,7)(4\,5),\\
 t_4&=(1\,2)(3\,4)(5\,6)(7\,8).
\end{aligned}
\]
The required exact certificate generates this permutation group directly,
verifies that it is primitive of order1344, and obtains the five
omission orders
\[
 192,\quad192,\quad192,\quad96,\quad168.
\]
Thus these five even involutions are independent, whereas $n-4=4$.
The group is also checked to be the actual degree-eight catalogue
copy of $\operatorname{AGL}_3(2)$; that identification is not needed
for the counterexample. The input is the five literal permutations,
not a choice-dependent isomorphism from a different action.

This family has no string ordering: $t_0$ fails to commute with
$t_2,t_3,t_4$, so its noncommuting graph has a vertex of degree three.
Every string ordering would make that graph a subgraph of a path.
The certificate also checks all120 orders as a confirmation.
Consequently this is not a counterexample to the source's principal
theorem about string C-groups.

\section{The replacement needed for a projected string}
\begin{theorem}\label{replacement}
Let $P\le S_n$ be primitive, $n\ge8$, with $P\ne A_n,S_n$.
An independent generating string of involutions for $P$ has length
at most $n-4$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First suppose its noncommuting diagram is disconnected. The accepted
primitive commuting-factor argument applies without an intersection
property: commuting nontrivial normal factors are transitive and
regular, and each is simple, since any of its proper normal subgroups
would be normal in the whole primitive group. Their intersection is
trivial unless the group is regular abelian of prime degree, which
cannot occur here with involutory generators. There are exactly two
factors $T$, and $P=T\times T$ acts on $n=|T|\ge60$ letters.
Independence supplies a strict subgroup chain, so the length is at most
$2\log_2n<n-4$.

For connected diagrams, degrees8 through25 are settled by the complete
finite argument below. For $n\ge26$, retain Mar\'oti's original
primitive-order alternative, as precisely stated in the accepted L047
common proof. Its product-action case has
$A_m^\ell\le P\le S_m\wr S_\ell$, $m\ge5$,
$1\le k\le m/2$ after replacing subsets by complements, and
$n=\binom mk^\ell$. If $\ell=1$, then $k\ge2$, and Whiston's
bound in the faithful $m$-point action gives length at most
$m-1\le\binom m2-4\le n-4$. If $\ell\ge2$, the faithful
$m\ell$-point action and Whiston give length at most $m\ell-1$.
The inequality $m^\ell\ge m\ell+3$ for $m\ge5,\ell\ge2$
implies $m\ell-1\le n-4$. No quotient intersection property is used.

The Mathieu actions have degree at most24. In the remaining alternative,
put $q=\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor$ and
$M(n)=n\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}(n-2^j)$. The certificate checks the38 exact
inequalities $M(n)<2^{n-3}$ for $26\le n\le63$.
For $n\ge64$, one has
\[
 M(n)<n^{q+1}<2^{(q+1)^2}\le2^{2^q-3}\le2^{n-3},
\]
where $(q+1)^2\le2^q-3$ holds at $q=6$ and persists by induction.
A family of length at least $n-3$ gives a strict subgroup chain of
that length, hence $|P|\ge2^{n-3}$, contradicting the retained order
bound. This argument uses neither Conder's bound nor the intersection
property.
\end{proof}

\section{The complete finite certificate}
The program visits every primitive catalogue action of degree8 through25
other than $A_n,S_n$. Catalogue completeness and exact GAP group
operations are retained computational premises. Let $d$ be the length
of a hypothetical independent generating string. Necessarily
\[
 d\le\Omega(|P|),\qquad
 d\le1+\Omega(|C_P(t_0)|).
\]
The first is the strict-chain bound, with prime factors counted with
multiplicity. For the second, the independent subfamily
$t_0,t_2,\ldots,t_{d-1}$ lies in $C_P(t_0)$ and has $d-1$ members.
All involutions together must generate $P$. Testing these necessary
conditions at $d\ge n-3$ leaves exactly
\[
 (8,3),\quad(8,5),\quad(9,7),\quad(16,11)
\]
in the pinned current PrimGrp catalogue. Their orders are respectively
$1344,336,432,322560$. These current indices are not source table labels.

For each of these four actual actions, enumerate every independent
connected involutory string of length from $n-3$ to $\Omega(|P|)$.
At a prefix $s_1,\ldots,s_j$ generating $H$, extend by each involution
outside $H$ which commutes with $s_1,\ldots,s_{j-1}$ and does not
commute with $s_j$. Reject an extended family if any member becomes
redundant. Every subfamily of an independent family is independent,
so this loses no admissible completion. If
$[P:H]<2^{\max(0,n-3-j)}$, the remaining strict chain is impossible.
Use the full normalizer of $P$ in $S_n$ at the empty prefix, then
the pointwise conjugation stabilizer of each fixed prefix. These are
genuine symmetries of all the completion conditions; taking one
representative of each candidate orbit is exhaustive up to simultaneous
conjugation. A prefix already generating $P$ cannot extend independently.
\emph{There is no intersection-property test and no
centralizer-quotient pruning test.}

The complete lists at or above the forbidden ranks are empty.
The numbers of visited prefixes at successive depths are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
(8,3)&1,3,18,180,26,0,0,0,0\\
(8,5)&1,2,11,31,0,0,0\\
(9,7)&1,2,10,18,2,0,0,0\\
(16,11)&1,4,82,3987,6385,6905,246,0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0.
\end{array}
\]
A positive smaller-degree control recovers independent rank-three
strings for the actual degree-six $A_5$ action. A second control checks
the Coxeter string for $S_4$ directly against the same conditions.
