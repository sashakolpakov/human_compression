We recover the finite assertions of Araujo-Pardo, Conder, Garc\'ia-Col\'in,
Kiss and Leemans~\cite{source}, using their middle-graph idea. The source
is the complete eight-page arXiv v1, not an implicitly updated edition.
For degrees three, four and five our calculation is exhaustive. For degree
six we verify two examples and make no completeness assertion. All graph
adjacencies, Boolean assignments, automorphism generators and identifying
permutations are supplied as mathematical data. There is no new formalization
or numerical length comparison.

\section{The exact finite search space}
A graph here is finite, simple and undirected. Fix $k\geq3$, and write
\[
 V_k=\{(i,j):0\leq i,j<k,\ i\ne j\}.
\]
A possible middle edge joins two different rows and two different columns.
For each such unordered pair $e$ introduce a Boolean variable $x_e$.
Impose the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For each $v\in V_k$, $\sum_{e\ni v}x_e=k-2$.
\item If $a,b$ share a row or column and both $va,vb$ are possible edges,
then $x_{va}+x_{vb}\leq1$.
\item For every possible triangle, its three variables have sum at most two.
\item For each possible four-cycle, its four variables have sum at most three.
\end{enumerate}
The whole file \texttt{search.py} builds precisely these conditions. Duplicate
forbidden edge sets are removed as sets, without removing any condition. It
imposes no cyclic symmetry, lexicographic restriction, isomorphism pruning or
connectivity condition on the middle graph.

\begin{lemma}
The satisfying assignments are exactly the $(k-2)$-regular graphs on $V_k$
with no triangles or four-cycles and with no path of length one or two between
different vertices in a common row or column.
Every $k$-regular graph of girth five, diameter four and order $k^2+k+2$
is obtained by extending at least one such assignment.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows directly: possible edges already exclude equal
rows or columns; the second condition excludes exactly the prohibited
length-two paths; the last two conditions exclude exactly the short cycles.

For the second assertion, choose vertices $r,c$ at distance four in the given
graph. Their neighborhoods are disjoint, have no internal edges and have no
edges between them. The $k(k-1)$ neighbors beyond $N(r)$ are distinct: a
repeated neighbor gives a four-cycle through $r$. They avoid $r,c,N(r),N(c)$.
The order hypothesis therefore makes them exactly the remaining set $M$.
The same argument applies from $c$. Thus every vertex of $M$ has exactly one
neighbor in $N(r)$ and exactly one in $N(c)$, and has $k-2$ neighbors in $M$.
Write the resulting rows and columns as $R_i,C_j$, each of size $k-1$.
An intersection $R_i\cap C_j$ has size at most one, since two elements of it
would give a four-cycle. Every row misses exactly one column, and every
column misses exactly one row. Relabel the columns along this perfect
matching of missing intersections. Then $M$ is exactly $V_k$ with the stated
labels. An edge or a length-two path within a row or column gives a triangle
or four-cycle in the full graph. The induced middle graph thus satisfies
all four conditions.
\end{proof}
The extension adds row vertices $r_i$, column vertices $c_j$ and two end
vertices $r,c$, and edges $rr_i$, $cc_j$, $r_i(i,j)$ and $c_j(i,j)$.
For every assignment in the finite lists, our separate literal adjacency
checker verifies regularity, connectedness, exact girth five and exact
diameter four in this extension. Consequently no sufficiency inference is
hidden in the finite calculation. The lower bound $k^2+k+2$ follows from the
same distinct-neighbor argument without assuming equality. Thus all reported
examples attain the lower bound and are cages.

\section{Complete automorphism and identification certificates}
Here is the exact algorithm in \texttt{direct\_graphs.py}. To test an
isomorphism $A\to B$, maintain paired ordered partitions of the two vertex
sets. Repeatedly split each cell by the vector counting neighbors in every
current cell. If corresponding cells have different signature multiplicities,
reject the branch. If all cells are singletons, test the resulting permutation
against every adjacency. Otherwise select the first smallest nonsingleton
source cell, fix its first vertex $a$, and branch over every vertex $b$ in
the corresponding target cell, making $a,b$ paired singleton cells. Refine
and continue. For an isomorphism test one successful branch suffices; for an
automorphism count every branch is explored.

\begin{lemma}
The algorithm finds every graph isomorphism exactly once. In particular its
full run with $A=B$ computes $|\Aut(A)|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every isomorphism respects the initial single-cell partitions. If it respects
paired cells, it preserves every vector of neighbor counts, so it respects
each refinement. A signature mismatch makes any such isomorphism impossible.
At a branch, the image of $a$ is exactly one of the enumerated vertices $b$;
therefore exactly one child can contain a given isomorphism. Each branch
strictly increases the number of singleton constraints, so the process
terminates. At a leaf the unique candidate is explicitly checked. Induction
over the search tree proves both exhaustiveness and absence of duplication.
\end{proof}
We also check each supplied generator as an actual adjacency-preserving
permutation and close the generators under composition by finite breadth-first
search. The resulting subgroup order equals the complete automorphism count
in all fifteen graphs. These two sides prove full automorphism equality;
there is no inference from a convenient subgroup alone.

\section{The source's chosen middle graphs}
A middle graph depends on the chosen diametral pair. We test all unordered
pairs at distance four in each representative and supply a specified pair
realizing the following source choices. The degree-three middle is $3K_2$;
its girth and diameter are recorded as absent rather than assigned a spurious
finite value. For each degree-four class the chosen middle is $C_{12}$,
verified by connectedness and degree two on twelve vertices.
For degree five the chosen profiles are
\[
 2\times(5,4),\qquad3\times(5,5),\qquad2\times(6,4),
\]
where a pair denotes (girth, diameter). The two order-four automorphism classes
use $(6,4)$, the two order-ten classes use $(5,4)$, and the order-48, 64 and
1920 classes use $(5,5)$.
This selection matters: the order-48 class has six diametral pairs with
middle profile $(5,5)$ and two with $(6,4)$. Thus the chosen profile is an
existence statement for a specified pair, not an invariant of the full graph.
All chosen pairs, all alternative profiles, and their original vertex sets
are explicit in \texttt{finite\_report.json}.

The finite component does not recover the infinite-family arguments, assert
completeness in degree six, settle the source's future-work questions, or
make a current record-holder claim. Those boundaries are separate from the
exact finite conclusions proved here.
