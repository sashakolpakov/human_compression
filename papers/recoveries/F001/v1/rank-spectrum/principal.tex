This paper assembles the complete principal assertion of Brooksbank's
fixed preprint \cite[Theorem~1.1]{B}. The even-characteristic argument
is acquired from the reviewed L031 and L037 recoveries. The odd
existence theorem, exceptional-field certificate and upper bound

The argument below identifies the precise components required for

For a finite group $G$, let $\rk(G)$ be the set of lengths of its
string C-group markings. A marking consists of generating involutions
with nonconsecutive generators commuting and with the intersection
property for every two subsets of the generating set.

\begin{theorem}\label{main}
For every prime power $q$,
\[
 \rk(\PSp_4(q))=
 \begin{cases}
 \varnothing,&q=3,\\
 \{3,4,5\},&q\ne3.
 \end{cases}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We retain the classical identification
$\PSp_4(q)\cong\Om_5(q)$ in odd characteristic and
$\PSp_4(q)=\Sp_4(q)$ in characteristic two. These are also the
identifications used in \cite[\S1]{B}. The group is nonabelian and
simple for $q\ge3$; the case $q=2$ is treated below without a
simplicity assumption.

Suppose first that $q\ge4$ is even. Theorem~5 of the acquired
\emph{Endpoint variation in orthogonal symmetry markings} gives a
rank-five string C-marking of $\Sp_4(q)$ by taking its odd-dimensional
construction with $2m+1=5$. Every adjacent product has odd order.
The acquired rank-contraction theorem and its iterated odd-tail
corollary from L037 therefore give ranks four and three for the
same group. For the upper bound, a marking of this simple group
must be connected. The acquired \emph{A centralizer bound for
symplectic strings in dimension four}, Theorem~3, bounds every
connected involutory string in characteristic two by five. Thus no
rank greater than five occurs.

For $q=2$, the acquired \emph{Three binary exceptions to a symmetry
exclusion}, Lemma~1 and Theorem~2, identifies $\Sp_4(2)$ with the
faithful coordinate $S_6$ acting on the quotient of the even-weight
six-coordinate space by its constant line. Its adjacent transpositions
form a rank-five string C-marking and all adjacent products have
order three. The same two acquired contractions give ranks four and
three. Every C-marking is an independent generating family; the
retained Whiston bound for $S_6$ limits its length to five. This
argument applies also to disconnected markings, so no simplicity
assumption has entered at this boundary.

For every odd $q\ge5$, the acquired \emph{A free endpoint for odd orthogonal strings in dimension five} proves the existence of ranks three, four
and five. It combines a uniform construction for $q\ge33$, exact
parameter rows for $13\le q\le31$, and exact marked groups for
$q=5,7,9,11$. The finite rows cover every odd prime power below
33, with the case $q=3$ kept separate. The upper bound is Theorem~4
of \emph{Mixed involutions in orthogonal strings of dimension five}:
it treats both nontrivial involution types in the full $\Om_5(q)$.
The previously acquired negative-reflection bound is one of its
complete dependencies, not a substitute for the mixed case.

For $q=3$, the complete finite extension certificate in the odd
component proves that $\PSp_4(3)$ has no marking of any rank.
Its explicit forty-point projective matrix group has order 25920;
the exhaustive prefix search has counts $2,13,121,20,2$ in lengths
one through five and no longer prefix. None generates the group.
The accompanying proof establishes complete orbit coverage, valid
prefix rejection and termination independently of those observed
counts. Simplicity excludes disconnected markings. We acquire that
entire proof, the exact programs and their reviewed execution records;
we do not infer the result merely from the absence of rank three.

Finally, rank one would give a cyclic group of order two, and a group
generated by two involutions is dihedral. None of the groups above
is cyclic of order two or dihedral: for $q\ge3$ this follows from
nonabelian simplicity, and for $q=2$ from $S_6$. This excludes the
remaining ranks and proves the stated spectrum.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Principal result and auxiliary correction.}
Theorem~\ref{main} recovers the fixed source's principal rank spectrum.
The source's Lemma~4.1 asserts a stronger bound for arbitrary subgroups
of a five-dimensional orthogonal group; that literal assertion is
false. The separate reviewed packet gives the deleted-permutation
$S_7$ marking of rank six over $\mathbf F_7$, in both $O_5(7)$ and $SO_5(7)$. Our upper bounds
concern the full symplectic or $\Om_5$ group and do not use that
auxiliary lemma. The correction and the recovery of the principal
result are therefore distinct claims.

The present construction also replaces the source's rough scalar-density
argument by a uniform parameter bound and exact finite boundary proofs.
It does not assert that every scalar in the source's displayed ansatz
works, or reproduce every marked representation discussed there. In
particular the separate four-matrix illustration in \cite[\S5.3]{B}
is not needed for the rank-spectrum contract. The higher-dimensional
suggestion in \cite[\S5.2]{B} remains outside this recovery; no open
question is declared resolved by the present assembly.
