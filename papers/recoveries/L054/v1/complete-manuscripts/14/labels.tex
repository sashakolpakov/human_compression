\begin{lemma}[Two additional atom criteria]
\begin{enumerate}
\item Two commuting elements of order four which generate a group of
order sixteen identify that group with $C_4\times C_4$.
\item Suppose $|H|=32$ and $z$ has order two. If $Z(H)=\langle z\rangle$,
every square lies in $\langle z\rangle$, and generator commutators lie in
$\langle z\rangle$ with at least one equal to $z$, then $H$ is extraspecial.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In the first case the defining commuting generators give a surjection
from $C_4\times C_4$ onto $H$, and equal orders make it an isomorphism.
In the second case the normal closure of the generator commutators is
$H'$, so $H'=Z(H)=\langle z\rangle$ and $H/Z(H)$ is elementary abelian.
For completeness, $\Phi(H)=H'H^2$: every maximal subgroup of a finite
2-group is normal of index two, hence contains $H'H^2$; and hyperplanes
in the elementary abelian quotient $H/H'H^2$ have trivial intersection.
The asserted maximal-subgroup fact follows from the normalizer condition:
a proper subgroup is strictly contained in its normalizer, by induction
modulo the center (if the center is not contained in the subgroup it
already enlarges the normalizer). A maximal subgroup is therefore normal,
and its quotient has order two. Thus $\Phi(H)=\langle z\rangle$ as well,
which is precisely the extraspecial condition.
\end{proof}
The checker computes the center by testing every group element against
every generator, checks every square, and checks all generator
commutators. It does not infer an extraspecial type from order alone.
