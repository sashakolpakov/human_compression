\paragraph{Source and scope.}
Connor and Leemans, \emph{An atlas of subgroup lattices of finite almost
simple groups}, Ars Math. Contemp.\ 8 (2015), 259--266, Section~3,
distinguish an unspecified extension $N.Q$, a nonsplit extension $N\cdot Q$,
a semidirect product $N:Q$ and a direct product $N\times Q$. Unparenthesized
products associate to the left. The authors explicitly permit nonunique
descriptions; their Figure~1 also uses an identification database and choices
of desirable normal subgroups. We verify the displayed existence assertions,
without claiming to reproduce those historical choices or to give canonical
names. This is an elementary certificate method, not a novelty claim.

The inputs are the retained \emph{current downloaded} companion PDFs for
$A_5$ and $M_{11}$, both internally dated 25 November 2012, and their exact
row maps to previously saved literal subgroup representatives. Their
identity with every file available at the fixed 2013 edition is not assumed.
All three table pages and both pages of \emph{How to read the atlas} were
directly inspected. In particular, $2^2,3^2$ are native superscripts;
they were not inferred from flattened strings ``22'' or ``32''.
The finite roster below is the entire claim of this component.

\begin{proposition}[Elementary verification rules]
Let $H$ be a finite group whose complete elements and multiplication are
given literally. The following data certify the indicated assertions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A generating element of order $m$ certifies $\C_m$. For $p=2,3$,
commuting elements of order $p$ whose exponent vectors give a bijection
$\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^a\to H$ certify $\C_p^a$.
\item A bijective multiplication-preserving map from the standard finite
permutation model certifies $A_4,A_5$ or $S_3$. Generators $r,s$ of a group
of order $2m$, with $|r|=m$, $|s|=2$ and $srs=r^{-1}$, certify $D_{2m}$.
Generators $i,j$ of a group of order eight, both of order four, satisfying
$i^2=j^2$ and $j^{-1}ij=i^{-1}$ certify $Q_8$.
\item Actual subgroups $N,K\le H$ with $N\lhd H$, $N\cap K=1$ and
$\langle N,K\rangle=H$ certify $H=N:K$. If they commute, they certify
$H=N\times K$. Their two factor labels can be certified recursively.
\item If $N\lhd H$ has index two, its quotient is $\C_2$. The extension
is nonsplit if and only if every element of $H\setminus N$ has order
different from two.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first assertions give explicit cyclic or elementary abelian coordinate
isomorphisms. In the second, multiplication preservation and bijectivity
give an isomorphism. The dihedral relations reduce every word to $r^a$ or
$r^as$, so there are at most $2m$ elements; generation and the stated order
give the dihedral presentation exactly. The quaternion relations similarly
reduce words to $i^a$ or $i^aj$ for $0\le a<4$, and give the usual order-eight
quaternion presentation. Normality in the third assertion gives $NK=H$,
and trivial intersection makes $(n,k)\mapsto nk$ bijective. Multiplication
is the semidirect multiplication for the actual conjugation action; when
the factors commute it is direct multiplication. Finally, a section of
$H\to H/N\cong\C_2$ exists exactly when the nonidentity quotient element
has an outside lift of order two. This requires checking all outside
elements: a single badly chosen section proves nothing.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Reuse by explicit transport]
A literal bijective homomorphism from one certified concrete finite model
to an actual subgroup transports its certified structure. A shared registry
may therefore store one such model and its certificate, with a separately
checked isomorphism for each occurrence. A registry key need not, and must
not, assert that the displayed label determines a unique isomorphism type.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An isomorphism preserves products, orders, normality, intersections and
generation. It transports complements and the entire outside-element list.
Thus every hypothesis of the preceding proposition is preserved, inductively
through the label tree. In the implementation each registry record has a
concrete literal origin and generators. Every transport is a full bijection
checked on every multiplication pair. Distinct concrete models with the same
tree may have separate registry records.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The bounded atlas roster]
Under the acquired literal row maps, all nine $A_5$ labels and the following
fifteen $M_{11}$ labels are true of their actual saved subgroup representatives:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l@{\qquad}c|l}
\text{row}&\text{structure}&\text{row}&\text{structure}\\\hline
12&11:5&13&Q_8:S_3\\
14&(3:S_3)\cdot2&16&S_3\times S_3\\
17&2^2:S_3&18&Q_8:3\\
19&D_{10}\cdot2&20&3:S_3\\
21&S_3\times3&22&Q_8:2\\
27&3^2&28&Q_8\\
29&8&30&D_8\\
36&2^2&&
\end{array}
\]
The $A_5$ rows, in order, are $A_5,A_4,D_{10},S_3,5,2^2,3,2,1$.
Here a bare integer denotes a cyclic group, and the dihedral subscript
denotes its order.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The certificate files give complete actual permutation elements and original
subgroup generators for each of the twenty-four rows. An independent token
parser reads all thirty-nine retained $M_{11}$ subgroup constructors and
joins the chosen fifteen exactly to those generators. For $A_5$, the
lexicographically sorted list of all sixty even permutations gives the
indexing of the acquired literal subgroup array; all nine element lists
are joined exactly. The accepted numerical row maps, the native label
transcriptions and these actual-generator joins are checked separately.
No source label is used to change a row map or to filter an ambient census.

The verifier closes each displayed generating list and compares it to the
complete listed elements. It computes products by literal permutation
composition. Each certificate is then checked by the preceding rules,
with its exact native label tree. This pays forty-four factor nodes,
4,032 standard-model multiplication pairs and all twenty-eight outside
elements of the two index-two nonsplit assertions. The outside lists are
exhaustive, and the actual two quotient cosets are retained. Each colon
node also records its actual nontrivial-action Boolean; each direct node
checks commutation. The shared registry has twenty-four concrete models
for forty-four factor occurrences, giving twenty reuses; all 15,671
transport multiplication pairs are explicitly checked. These finite
certificates supply every hypothesis of the two propositions.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite evidence and limits.}
The bounded witness discovery used only these already retained small
subgroups (order at most sixty), finished in less than one second, and
performed no ambient subgroup census. Its exact inputs, code and terminal
receipt are preserved. Discovery is not a verification premise: the final
checker uses only the literal certificates, original subgroup arrays,
source joins and multiplication rules. Eighteen damaged examples are
rejected, including the nonnormal diagonal $S_3$ in $S_3\times S_3$,
noncommuting proposed direct factors, a nonhomomorphic bijection, and an
index-two subgroup in $\C_4\times\C_2$ with a bad chosen section but an
outside involution. The same checks pass with Python optimization enabled.

The numerical class catalogue and its completeness retain their separately
accepted interfaces; this proof adds the stated labels only. It does not
certify any other row, the atoms $M_9,M_{10}$, larger classical aliases,
additive-exponent conventions, or the historical naming software.
There is no whole-atlas, whole-original, conjecture, Lean or measurement
credit. The current author supplied this proof and implementation; root
proposed the shared concrete-model registry and authorized the narrow pilot.
Distinct review is required before this component is acquired.
