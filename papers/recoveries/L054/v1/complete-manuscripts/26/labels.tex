\begin{lemma}[Complete quotient-generator lifts]
Let $N\lhd X$ be finite. Let $Q$ be a finite group with ordered generators
$q_1,\ldots,q_k$, and let $\alpha:Q\longrightarrow X/N$ be a verified
isomorphism. Choose $x_j\in X$ lifting $\alpha(q_j)$. Then $N$ has a complement
in $X$ if and only if at least one of the $|N|^k$ ordered choices
\[
                     y_j\in Nx_j\quad(1\leq j\leq k)
\]
extends to a homomorphism $Q\to X$. Such an extension is decided by testing
all edges of the complete ordered Cayley graph of $Q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A complement gives a section of the quotient map and hence one of these
choices. Conversely, if $s:Q\to X$ extends a choice, its composition with
the quotient map agrees with $\alpha$ on the generators, and therefore on
all of $Q$. Consequently $s$ is injective, its image meets $N$ trivially,
and the image has index $|N|$ in $X$; it is a complement.

For the decision procedure, list every element of $Q$ by breadth-first
multiplication in the ordered generators, with the identity first. Assign
identity image, and propagate the proposed images along every generator
edge. A conflicting image proves that the assignment is not a homomorphism.
If there is no conflict, every group element has an image and every generator
edge is preserved. The same induction on words proves a homomorphism.
The list is complete, so all relations needed for the conclusion have been
checked, without choosing or assuming a presentation. Every generator lift
has the form $nx_j$, so the finite product of these lists omits no section.
\end{proof}
