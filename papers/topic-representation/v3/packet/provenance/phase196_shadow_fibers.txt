# Shadow fibers and faithful incidence extensions

Root working draft, 10 October 2026. This is not a reviewed or published
source-contract admission. It gives an elementary reusable theorem and
applies it to the literal construction in L126, Theorem 6.1 and Corollary
6.2. No whole-source, priority or open-conjecture claim is made.

## 1. A finite extension theorem

Let P be a finite typed incidence system. Let J be a finite set of new
types, disjoint from the types of P. For each j in J take a finite set
Q_j, disjoint from P and from the other Q's, and a map

    s_j : Q_j → subsets of P.

Construct Γ by retaining the incidences of P, making x in P incident
with q in Q_j precisely when x belongs to s_j(q), and making every
object of Q_j incident with every object of Q_k for j≠k. There are no
incidences within Q_j. Thus s_j(q) is the complete old-object shadow,
not just its point shadow. Write m_j(S)=|s_j⁻¹(S)|, including zero for
an absent shadow. Define H to be the subgroup of the type-preserving
automorphism group of P consisting of those h for which

    m_j(hS)=m_j(S) for every j and every subset S of P.

**Theorem S.1.** Restriction gives a split exact sequence

    1 → ∏_(j,S) Sym(s_j⁻¹(S)) → Aut_I(Γ) → H → 1.

Only nonempty fibers need occur in the product. In particular, restriction
is faithful if and only if every nonempty fiber has size one. The splitting
depends on choices of fiber enumerations.

**Proof.** A type-preserving automorphism preserves P because its old
types are disjoint from J. Its restriction h preserves all incidences
on P. If q has shadow S then its image has shadow hS, by incidence
preservation in both directions. The automorphism therefore maps the
fiber s_j⁻¹(S) bijectively onto s_j⁻¹(hS), proving h∈H.

For an automorphism restricting to the identity on P, this argument says
that every fiber is preserved setwise. Conversely every independent
permutation of every fiber preserves all incidences: old incidences
are fixed, old–new incidence depends only on S, and new–new incidence
depends only on whether the new types differ. This proves exactly the
stated kernel, including surjectivity of its natural embedding.

For each nonempty fiber choose a bijection e_(j,S) from {1,…,m_j(S)}
onto that fiber. Given h∈H, define a permutation on Γ by h on P and by

    e_(j,S)(r) ↦ e_(j,hS)(r)

on the new objects. The defining condition for H ensures that the target
index set has the same size. Old–new incidences are preserved because
x∈S if and only if h(x)∈hS; the other incidences are preserved by
construction. Replacing h by h⁻¹ gives its inverse. Keeping the same
index r through successive maps proves that these lifts form a group
homomorphism H→Aut_I(Γ). Their restrictions equal h. This proves the
split exact sequence. A finite symmetric group is trivial exactly when
its underlying set has at most one element, giving the final criterion. ∎

This is the usual permutation symmetry of indistinguishable objects,
stated with the complete kernel and extension conditions. No claim of
new historical mathematics is intended. It refines the existing retained-
object reconstruction test: recognizable old objects do not by themselves
give faithfulness; the new objects must also be uniquely determined.

## 2. Binding to the projective cross-ratio construction

The primary source is D. Leemans, K. Stokes and P. Tranchida, *Groups
represented by incidence geometries*, arXiv:2506.10464v1 (12 June 2025),
pp.7–8, Theorem6.1 and Corollary6.2. The local primary PDF is
`sources/incidence_representation2025.pdf`, SHA256
`bd0ffe65fc8da4643f1a60bc4384658da3344ac10e0891787420f2331d8e2129`.
Root visually checked the definitions and both statements on those two
pages; a full extracted-text reading was completed earlier. The source
defines new objects as ordered quadruples, without quotienting by their
permutations. Incidence with old objects uses the underlying four-point
set. Incidence with other new objects uses only distinctness of their
cross-ratio types. Hence Theorem S.1 applies literally.

Choose K=F₄=F₂[a]/(a²+a+1), F=F₂ and n=3. The old system P is the
point–line geometry PG(2,4). It has 21 points and 21 lines, with five
points on every line. For a line ℓ and a four-element subset A of its
points, all ordered quadruples with underlying set A have the same old
shadow: the four points in A and the unique line ℓ. Their cross-ratio
is either a or a+1. Distinct points ensure that it is neither zero nor
one. Swapping the last two entries inverts the cross-ratio. Since
a⁻¹=a+1, this is a bijection between the two sets of orderings. There
are 4!=24 orderings, so each cross-ratio occurs exactly twelve times.

Each of 21 lines contains five four-element subsets. For each of the
two new types there are therefore 105 fibers of size twelve and 1260
new objects. Shadows from different lines or different four-element
subsets differ. Theorem S.1 consequently gives the exact pointwise kernel

    ker(Aut_I(Γ) → Aut_I(P)) ≅ Sym(12)^210.

In particular Aut_I(Γ) contains Sym(12), of order 479001600. By elementary
linear algebra,

    |PGL(3,4)| = (4³−1)(4³−4)(4³−4²)/(4−1) = 60480.

Thus the type-preserving group in the source construction cannot be
isomorphic to PGL(3,4). This disproves the stated finite instance of
Theorem6.1 without needing a full classification of its correlations.
The model has 2562 objects in all.

For Corollary6.2 take α=a. Its minimal polynomial over F₂ is X²+X+1,
whose roots in F₄ are exactly a and a+1. With n=3 the specified truncation
therefore removes no type at all. The very same finite instance refutes
that corollary's stated conclusion.

The precise failure in the source proof is uniqueness of the ordered
quadruple from its four incident point objects. Those incidences determine
its unordered support. At this parameter, twelve objects of each given
cross-ratio have that support. Even the always-valid double interchange
(p₁,p₂,p₃,p₄)↦(p₂,p₁,p₄,p₃) preserves cross-ratio and demonstrates
the failure of uniqueness over every field where such quadruples exist.

## 3. The quotient also forgets the field label

In this F₄ instance every four-element subset on every line occurs at
both new types with the same multiplicity. Hence every type-preserving
automorphism of P preserves the shadow multiplicities and belongs to H.
The source intends the new types to distinguish field automorphisms,
but the multiplicity data do not do so here.

For example the coordinate Frobenius x↦x² permutes points and lines and
preserves their incidence. On ordered quadruples apply Frobenius to all
entries and then exchange the last two entries. The first operation
changes the cross-ratio λ to λ², and the second changes it to (λ²)⁻¹=λ
for λ∈F₄\F₂. This is an involutory, type-preserving automorphism of Γ.
It has the same point action as the nontrivial field automorphism.
The two operations commute, so their composite squares to the identity.

Consequently quotienting out the twin fibers alone would not recover
the intended distinction between inner and field automorphisms. A repair
must preserve ordered positions, or some equally strong information,
and separately prove recognizable old objects, unique extension and the
intended type action. The existing finite normal-pair construction supplies
a representation existence theorem for finite groups, but does not by
itself repair this particular natural projective model.

## 4. Computational and formal scope

`phase196_cross_ratio_check.py` constructs the literal PG(2,4) point and
line sets from arithmetic in F₄, enumerates every ordered collinear
quadruple, computes its cross-ratio by two-by-two determinants, and groups
the objects by their complete old shadows and types. It checks all fiber
sizes, the double-interchange identity and the Frobenius-plus-interchange
automorphism. Its report records a specific pair of indistinguishable
objects and rejects an intentionally altered old incidence.

This is a root-authored finite corroboration. The written general theorem
and source binding require independent review. No Lean certificate has
yet been produced for this packet, and no public counters or compression
rates change. An independent reviewer should reconstruct the field and
incidence model rather than relying on this program's reported counts.
