Let $G=\ON$ and $A=\Aut(G)=G:2$, in the already acquired faithful
permutation model. We use the accepted complete centralizer, pair and
third-involution certificates listed below. No new group enumeration or
generation test enters this application.

\begin{theorem}
There are no three outer involutions $a,b,c\in A\setminus G$ with
$a\ne c$, $ac=ca$, $|ab|=6$ and $|bc|=12$.
Consequently no generating pair $(R,S)$ of $G$ with $|RS|=2$ and
$\{|R|,|S|\}=\{6,12\}$ is simultaneously inverted by an outer
automorphism.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $z=(ab)^3$. This is an inner involution, central in
$\langle a,b\rangle$. The acquired unique inner involution class allows
us to conjugate $z$ to the fixed representative. Then $a,b$ lie in
$K=C_A(z)$, of order $322560$. Moreover a conjugator between two such
normalized pairs fixes $z$, so pair equivalence and pair stabilizers are
computed inside $K$ without losing any $A$-orbits.

The accepted centralizer certificate exhausts the involutions of $K$:
the inner class sizes are $1,1120,630$, and the outer involutions form
one class of size $1344$. Choose its representative $x_4$.
Its full centralizer in $K$ has order $240$. The accepted pair reader
examines every outer involution $y$ of $K$ and tests both $|x_4y|=6$
and $(x_4y)^3=z$. Exactly $20$ elements qualify. They constitute one
complete $C_K(x_4)$-orbit; its full pair stabilizer has order $12$.
Thus the single recorded pair \texttt{class4\_pair1} represents all
outer pairs with first rotation order six.

The accepted transport sends the first member of this pair to the
fixed outer involution $t$. Its full centralizer is the acquired direct
product $C_A(t)=\langle t\rangle\times J$, where $J\cong J_1$ lies
in $G$. Hence every outer involution $c\ne t$ commuting with $t$ is
uniquely $tj$ for a nonidentity involution $j$ of $J$. The complete
acquired involution domain has size $1463$. The full pair stabilizer
$H$ of order $12$ acts on this domain. Its projection to $J$ is
injective: a kernel element would be $t$, but $t$ cannot commute with
the second pair member when their product has order six.

The accepted literal reader partitions this complete domain into
$141$ disjoint $H$-orbits, of total size $1463$. It lifts a representative
of \emph{every} orbit to the full faithful permutation action and computes
the exact order of $bc$. This order is constant on the orbit, since
$H$ centralizes $b$. Table~\ref{orders} gives the complete result.
Order twelve does not occur. This proves the first assertion.

For the second, a simultaneous inverter $b$ of a generating pair has
$b^2=1$: its square centralizes both generators, hence induces the
identity on $G$. With $a=Rb$ and $c=bS$, the three elements are outer
involutions, $ab=R$, $bc=S$ and $ac=RS$. Thus $a\ne c$ and $ac=ca$.
If necessary reverse the triple, which sends the rotation pair to
$(S^{-1},R^{-1})$, to put order six first. The first assertion rules out
the resulting triple. No intersection-property or generation algorithm
is required for this implication.
\end{proof}
