\section{Rotation presentations and the three shared marking adapters}
Let $W$ have involutory Coxeter generators $r_0,\ldots,r_3$ and
pair orders $m_{ij}$. Its sign map sends every generator to the
nonidentity element of $C_2$. Reidemeister--Schreier rewriting with
transversal $1,r_0$ gives the exact presentation of its even subgroup
on $a_i=r_0r_i$:
\[
 a_i^{m_{0i}}=1,\qquad (a_i^{-1}a_j)^{m_{ij}}=1.
\]
The other Schreier generators are their inverses; rewriting both
cosets of each Coxeter relator gives conjugates or inverses of these
same relators. This proves a presentation identity, not a finite-image
argument.

Suppose additional local rotation relators impose regular torus periods
$(q,0)$ or $(q,q)$. Their normal closure inside each local triangle
rotation group is invariant under its reflections: in the retained
Euclidean lattice realization these are exactly the reflection-invariant
period lattices. Their normal closure $N$ in $W^+$ is then invariant
under every global odd generator as well. For a local period and an
odd local reflection $r_j$, write $r_0=(r_0r_j)r_j$; the first factor
is even and the second preserves the local period subgroup. Thus
$N\lhd W$, and
\[
 (W/N)^+=W^+/N.
\]
Consequently the displayed source rotation presentation, with these
local period words, is exactly the even subgroup of the corresponding
full reflection presentation. No additional, unverified reflected
relation is being discarded.

This supplies the following explicit marking adapters for the shared
families. All letters on the right are the involutions in the complete
proof sections below; their specified order is preserved.
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{llll}\toprule
Family&$x$&$y$&$z$\\\midrule
Cubic $(a,b,d,c)$&$ba$&$bc$&$bd$\\
Square $(a,b,c,d)$&$ad$&$ab$&$ac$\\
Finite $(a,b,c,d)$&$ca$&$cd$&$cb$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
For the cubic family the two period words become
$c(bd)^2b$ and $a(bd)^2b$, with exponents two and $s$.
For the square family, its four toroidal relations become, respectively,
\[
 (b(ad)^2a)^2,\quad (c(da)^2)^2,\quad
 (d(cb)^2)^{2s},\quad (a(bc)^2b)^2.
\]
For the finite family they become
\[
 (d(ca)^2c)^2,\quad (b(ac)^2a)^2,\quad
 (a(bd)^2)^2,\quad (c(db)^2d)^{3t}\quad(t=1,2).
\]
These identities follow by cancellation of adjacent involutions, using
the indicated commuting pair relations. In the square $(s,s)$ relation,
the two translation words commute, so their product raised to $s$
equals $(d(cb)^2)^{2s}$. The parameter $t$ here is exactly the shared
finite theorem's parameter. Thus both universal presentations and the
ordered local markings, not just their orders, transfer from the common
proofs. Their subgroup intersections and flag geometry are unchanged
under these explicit marker permutations.
