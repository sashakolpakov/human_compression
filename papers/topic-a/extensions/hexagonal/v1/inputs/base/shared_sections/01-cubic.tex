\section{The cubic family and its period lattice}
Write $a=\rho_0,b=\rho_1,d=\rho_2,c=\rho_3$. Let $E$ be generated by
\begin{align*}
a(x)&=(x_2,x_1,x_3),& b(x)&=(x_1,x_3,x_2),\\
c(x)&=(-x_2,-x_1,x_3),&d(x)&=(-x_2,-x_1,1-x_3).
\end{align*}
Products act from right to left. Let $Q$ be the signed permutation group
in three coordinates, $\epsilon(q)\in\{0,1\}$ the parity of its number of
sign changes, and put
$D=\{v\in\Z^3:\sum v_i\equiv0\pmod2\}$,
$A=\{v\in\Z^3:\sum v_i=0\}$.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:group}
The group $E$ is precisely
$\{(q,v)\in Q\ltimes\Z^3:\sum v_i\equiv\epsilon(q)\pmod2\}$,
with translation kernel $D$. Its marked presentation is
\begin{align*}
\langle a,b,d,c\mid& a^2,b^2,d^2,c^2,(ab)^3,(ad)^2,(ac)^2,\\
 &(bd)^6,(bc)^3,(dc)^2,(c(bd)^2b)^2\rangle.
\end{align*}
Adjoining $(a(bd)^2b)^s=1$ gives exactly $E_s=E/sD$, of order $48s^3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The linear maps generate all $48$ signed permutations. Each affine
map satisfies the parity condition, which multiplication preserves.
Direct multiplication gives $u=a(bd)^2b=(1,(1,-1,0))$; its $Q$-conjugates
are the twelve roots $\pm e_i\pm e_j$, generating $D$. Hence the
translation kernel is $D$. Surjectivity to $Q$ proves the description of
$E$. All eleven presentation relators hold.

Identification of the abstract presentation is separate. The complete
program \path{cubic/kernel_certificate.g} forms its epimorphism onto the
specified $48$-element group and computes the actual kernel presentation
by Reidemeister--Schreier rewriting and exact presentation simplification.
It has three generators $f_1,f_2,f_3$ and exactly
\[
 [f_2,f_3],\qquad f_3f_1^{-1}f_3^{-1}f_1,\qquad [f_1,f_2]
\]
as relators. The full preimage words are emitted and independently
evaluated by \path{cubic/certificate.py}. Their translations are
$(0,1,1),(1,0,-1),(-1,0,-1)$, of determinant of absolute value $2$.
Thus this is the whole free abelian kernel, not its abelianization,
and its image is $D$. AL.2 identifies the presented group with $E$.
The exact GAP rewriting and simplification are declared software trust.
Conjugating $u^s$ generates exactly the already normal lattice $sD$,
proving the quotient statement and its order.
\end{proof}

\section{All parabolics and uniform intersections}
Write $E_J=\langle\rho_i:i\in J\rangle$. A mask $m$ denotes the set of
positions of its nonzero binary digits. The only infinite proper
parabolic is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fiber}
 E_{012}=\{(q,v):q\in S_3\times\{\pm I\},\ \sum v_i=\epsilon(q)\},
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon$ is zero on permutations and one on their negatives.
Indeed each of $a,b,d$ satisfies this formula, their linear image has
order $12$, and the conjugates of $u$ by $a,b$ generate $A$. This proves
equality of every fiber. In particular $A\cap sD=sA$.

All other proper parabolics inject into their linear images. Complete
closure of the actual affine transformations gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrrrrrrr}
m&0&1&2&3&4&5&6&8&9&10&11&12&13&14\\\hline
|E_J|&1&2&2&6&2&4&12&2&4&6&24&4&8&48 .
\end{array}
\]
The maximal finite groups fix respectively $p=(-1/2,1/2,1/2)$ for
$E_{123}$, $p_1=(0,0,1/2)$ for $E_{023}$, and $0$ for $E_{013}$.
The first has linear image $Q$, and the last is the even-sign subgroup
$S_4$.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:IP}
For every $s\ge3$ all $256$ marked subgroup intersection identities hold
in $E_s$. Its maximal parabolic orders, in omitted-label order $0,1,2,3$,
are $48,8,24,12s^2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the fourteen finite supports the certificate checks all $196$ exact
intersections $E_J\cap E_T=E_{J\cap T}$. For any two elements of these
groups with equal linear part, each coordinate of the translation
difference has absolute value at most $1$. Such a difference belongs to
$sD$ only if zero for $s\ge3$, so these meets survive the quotient.

For every finite support $J$ the certificate also checks
$Q_J\cap Q_{012}=Q_{J\cap012}$, and that each actual affine element of
$E_J$ with linear part in $Q_{012}$ satisfies \eqref{eq:fiber}.
These are exactly the elements of $E_{J\cap012}$, by injectivity of the
finite linear images. Any element of the image of $E_J$ also lying in
the image of $E_{012}$ has its linear part in this intersection; it
therefore already comes from $E_{J\cap012}$. Identical supports and the
full group give the remaining immediate meets. The orders follow from
the table and $[A:sA]=s^2$.
\end{proof}

The toroidal maximal sections are exactly $\{3,6\}_{(2,0)}$ on $(c,b,d)$
and $\{3,6\}_{(s,0)}$ on $(a,b,d)$, not just maps with their diagrams.
The standard Euclidean reflection group $[3,6]$ has translation lattice
$A$, generated by the conjugates of $u=a(bd)^2b$; the imposed lattice is
exactly $sA$. This is the usual ordered parameter $(s,0)$.
For the first section the corresponding word with $a$ replaced by $c$
has order two. It is a quotient of $[3,6]_{(2,0)}$, whose lattice
calculation gives order $12\cdot2^2=48$. Our subgroup has order $48$,
so the marked epimorphism is an isomorphism. The Euclidean reflection
presentation and its torus parameter convention are explicitly retained
classical facts. The other maximal sections are $C_2^3$ and the
tetrahedral string group $S_4$.

\section{A faithful action and the literal CPR diagrams}
Let
\[
w_0=(1,1,1),\ w_1=(1,-1,-1),\ w_2=(-1,1,-1),\ w_3=(-1,-1,1).
\]
The $4s$ labels $(i,k)$, with $k\in\Z/s\Z$, represent plane families
$w_i\cdot x=2k+1/2$ modulo period $2s$ in the constant. If
$qw_i=\eta w_j$, then $\eta=(-1)^{\epsilon(q)}$ is independent of $i$.
An element $(q,v)$ acts by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:action}
(i,k)\longmapsto\left(j,\eta k+\frac{\eta-1+2w_j\cdot v}{4}\right).
\end{equation}
The parity condition makes the fraction integral; translation by $sD$
is trivial. The generators $a,b,c$ transpose family pairs $(1,2),(2,3),(0,3)$ respectively, preserving $k$. The map $d$ swaps families $1,2$
with $k\mapsto-k-1$, and preserves $0,3$ with $k\mapsto-k$.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:faithful}
For $s\ge3$ this is a faithful action of $E_s$, with exactly the two
parity-dependent CPR diagrams on \cite[p.~6]{source}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An element fixing all four family labels has $q=I$ or $-I$: these are
the kernel of the signed-permutation action on the four diagonal lines.
For $q=-I$, fixing both levels $0,1$ forces $s\mid2$, impossible.
For $q=I$ triviality says $w_i\cdot v\in2s\Z$ for every $i$.
The vectors $(w_0+w_i)/2$, $i=1,2,3$, are the coordinate vectors,
so $v\in s\Z^3$. The condition on $w_0$ then gives $v/s\in D$.
Thus the kernel in $E$ is exactly $sD$.

The following explicit labeling was read against the source graphics.
Begin with $(0,0)$--$(3,0)$, an edge of label $3$. Append $s-1$ alternating
four-vertex blocks, each joined to the preceding last vertex by label $1$:
\begin{align*}
\text{diamond }h:&\quad(2,h),(1,h),(1,-h-1),(2,-h-1),\\
\text{square }h:&\quad(3,-h-1),(0,-h-1),(0,h+1),(3,h+1).
\end{align*}
In each diamond label $0$ joins first--second and third--fourth, and
label $2$ joins first--third and second--fourth. In each square label $3$
joins first--second and third--fourth; label $2$ joins first--fourth and
second--third. For $s=2t$, finish with label $1$ to $(3,t)$ then label $3$
to $(0,t)$. For $s=2t+1$, finish with label $1$ to $(2,t)$ then parallel
labels $0,2$ to $(1,t)$. All levels are modulo $s$.
The alternating levels $0,-1,1,-2,2,\ldots$ show every label occurs once.
The explicit generator formulas give precisely the source's two drawings.
This is uniform for both parities; the separately run examples are
checks of the transcription, not a proof of the family. IP makes these
faithful permutation graphs CPR graphs.
\end{proof}
The range matters: at $s=2$ the nontrivial element $(-I,(1,1,-1))$
fixes all eight labels. No faithful degree-eight claim follows.

\section{Flag transitivity}
Let $P_i=(E_s)_{I\setminus\{i\}}$. For three subgroups $X,Y,Z$, Tits'
elementary coset criterion says that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:helly}
(X\cap Y)(X\cap Z)=X\cap YZ
\end{equation}
is equivalent to the three corresponding coset types having the Helly
property. First, each $P_i$ has this local property on its three maximal
parabolics. The certificate checks \eqref{eq:helly} on the finite pair
subgroups. For supports $7,11,13,14$ the product sizes are $12,12,8,36$
and the intersection size is $4$ in each case. The equal-linear-part
translation-difference bounds are $1,0,0,0$, so all four identities
survive modulo $sD$ for every $s\ge3$.

For triples containing $P_1=\langle a,d,c\rangle\cong C_2^3$, use
\[
(X,Y,Z)=(P_0,P_1,P_2),\ (P_0,P_1,P_3),\ (P_2,P_1,P_3).
\]
Indeed $P_1P_2=P_2\cup dP_2$ and $P_1P_3=P_3\cup cP_3$.
The extra representative belongs to $X\cap Y$ in each chosen triple,
so IP immediately gives \eqref{eq:helly}.

For $(X,Y,Z)=(P_0,P_2,P_3)$ the complete finite $S_4$ coset decomposition is
\[
P_2P_3=P_3\cup cP_3\cup bcP_3\cup abcP_3.
\]
The first three representatives lie in $P_0\cap P_2$, so their
intersections with $P_0$ are in the required product. The last coset has
empty intersection: $P_3$ fixes $(0,0)$ in the proved action, whereas
$abc$ sends it to $(1,0)$. Its $P_0$-orbit is exactly
$\{(0,0),(3,0),(2,0),(1,-1)\}$, by the three explicit generator formulas.
For $s\ge3$ this excludes $(1,0)$. Hence \eqref{eq:helly} holds for every
triple of maximal parabolics.

For completeness these global and local triple properties imply full
four-coset Helly. Normalize one coset to $P_0$. Its intersections with
the other three cosets are cosets in $P_0$. Any two meet by the global
triple property, and the local triple property in $P_0$ gives a common
point of all four. Smaller families are already covered. UA.Geometry
therefore gives thinness, residual connectivity and flag transitivity,
and identifies $E_s$ with the full type-preserving automorphism group,
acting regularly on chambers.
\begin{theorem}
For every $s\ge3$, the literal presentation in \cite[Theorem~3.1]{source}
defines $E/sD$ of order $48s^3$. Its coset system is the universal locally
toroidal regular rank-four hypertope specified there, with exact toroidal
parameters $(2,0)$ and $(s,0)$ and the source's faithful degree-$4s$ CPR graph.
\end{theorem}
Universality means the exact diagram presentation with the stated local
relations: Lemma~\ref{lem:group} proves a marked isomorphism, not merely
the existence of a finite image.
