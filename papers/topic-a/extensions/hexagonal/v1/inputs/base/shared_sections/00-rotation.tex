\section{Exact rotation presentations and coset certificates}
The following certificates isolate the proof shared by the finite regular and chiral rows. It does not
identify a finite image with a presented group, and it does not infer flag
transitivity from subgroup intersections alone. Every source-specific table row remains a separate certificate obligation.

\begin{proposition}[Exact regular completion]
Let $r\geq3$, let $P=\gen{a_1,\ldots,a_{r-1}}$ be a finite group with its
specified marking, and put $a_0=1$. Suppose that simultaneous inversion of the
$a_i$ extends to an automorphism $\sigma$ of $P$. Form
$H=P\rtimes\gen t$, with $t^2=1$ and $t a_i t=a_i^{-1}$, and put
$\rho_i=t a_i$ for $0\leq i<r$. Suppose that these involutions have the full
marked subgroup intersection property and that their maximal parabolics
$H_i=\gen{\rho_j:j\ne i}$ have typed coset Helly.
Then the standard $H$-coset geometry is a regular hypertope with full
type-preserving group $H$, of order $2|P|$. It is isomorphic to the source
rotation coset system with parabolics
\[
 P_i=\gen{a_j^{-1}a_k:j,k\ne i}.
\]
Thus $P$ has exactly two orbits on its chambers, and $H$ is its regular
completion. For $i\geq1$ the displayed $P_i$ equals
$\gen{a_j:j\ne i}$, and $P_0=\gen{a_1^{-1}a_j:j\geq2}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The $\rho_i$ are involutions and generate $H$, since $\rho_0=t$ and
$\rho_0\rho_i=a_i$. The sign homomorphism $H\to C_2$ has kernel $P$.
Its restriction to $H_i$ is onto, and its kernel is generated by the even
words $\rho_j\rho_k=a_j^{-1}a_k$. Hence $H_i\cap P=P_i$ and
$[H_i:P_i]=2$.
Every right coset of $H_i$ has an even representative, so $P_i g\mapsto H_i g$
is a bijection on elements of each type. It also preserves incidence.
One implication is immediate. Conversely, if two $H$-cosets have a common
odd element $h$, choose a label $k$ different from their two types. Then
$\rho_k h$ is an even common element. This uses $r\geq3$ and proves the
claimed incidence isomorphism without assuming equality of arbitrary products
or quotient parabolics.

Full marked IP and typed coset Helly give the regular hypertope by the common
chamber construction: Helly gives flag completion and flag transitivity; IP gives
trivial chamber stabilizer and two elements in every panel; the generating
property in each marked subgroup gives residue connectivity. A type-preserving
automorphism fixing a chamber fixes each adjacent chamber by thinness, and then
all chambers by gallery connectivity. Consequently the displayed chamber-regular
$H$ is the full type-preserving automorphism group. Its index-two subgroup $P$
has exactly two chamber orbits. The formulas for $P_i$ follow by including
$a_0=1$ when $i\ne0$, and by taking differences from $a_1$ when $i=0$.
\end{proof}

An exact presentation certificate must first identify $P$ itself. In the finite
rows, a completed coset enumeration or an exact fp-group isomorphism supplies
an upper bound; an actual permutation image supplies the matching lower bound.
A numerical image of the expected order by itself supplies only the lower bound.
The regularity test then checks all marked subgroup intersections and the separate
normalized coset-Helly identities in $H$. Neither a group order nor a source
structural abbreviation replaces these checks.

For a chiral row the last proposition is not applicable. We must instead certify
the rotation coset geometry itself, including thinness, residue connectivity,
two chamber orbits with adjacent chambers in opposite orbits, and the absence
of simultaneous inversion. Its full automorphism order is then $|P|$, whereas
a regular row has full order $2|P|$. The source tables and the literal rotation
presentations must retain this distinction.

\section*{A bounded certificate for chiral rows}
Here all cosets are right cosets. For a finite group $P$ with its three
rotation generators, use the four subgroups $P_i$ above and write
$K_J=\bigcap_{j\in J}P_j$. For $i\notin J$, choose $j_0\in J$ and a
right transversal $T$ of $P_i\cap P_{j_0}$ in $P_{j_0}$. Set
\[
 c(i,J)=\#\{g\in T:g\in P_iP_j\text{ for every }j\in J\}.
\]
This counts exactly the type-$i$ elements incident with the base flag of
types $J$, provided that base flag has been fixed; the representatives lie
in one finite proper parabolic, not in the entire ambient group.

\begin{proposition}[Two chamber orbits, with independent connectivity checks]
Suppose the marked pair-difference subgroups have the full $C^+$ intersection
property. Suppose additionally that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $c(i,J)=[K_J:K_J\cap P_i]$ for every $|J|=2$, and
$c(i,J)=2$ for every $|J|=3$;
\item $P$ is generated by the four $P_i$, and each $P_f$ is generated by
its subgroups $P_f\cap P_j$, $j\ne f$;
\item for each pair of types $F$, the graph with two vertex sets indexed
by $K_F$, one for each remaining type $i,j$, and edges
\[
 h\sim k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad hk^{-1}\in P_iP_j
 \qquad(h,k\in K_F)
\]
is connected.
\end{enumerate}
Then the rotation coset system is a hypertope. The group $P$ acts freely
with two chamber orbits, and adjacent chambers lie in opposite orbits.
It is chiral with full type-preserving group $P$ if simultaneous inversion
of the three marked generators does not extend to an automorphism of $P$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Pair flags are transitive in any coset system. The $|J|=2$ equalities say
that all eligible cosets are supplied by $K_J$, so all three-type flags are
transitive as well. The $C^+$ intersection identities give trivial
three-type stabilizers: they are the pair-difference group on the one
remaining label. The $|J|=3$ equalities therefore give two completions of
each panel, with trivial chamber stabilizer. Every shorter flag extends
successively to a chamber, so this is a thin geometry with exactly two
$P$-orbits on chambers. Two adjacent chambers cannot lie in the same orbit,
since an element mapping one to the other would fix their panel and hence
would be the identity.

The incidence graph of a coset system is connected when its defining
subgroups generate its ambient group. Applied first to $P$, and then to
$P_f$ with its subgroups $P_f\cap P_j$, the second condition proves
connectivity of the whole geometry and of every rank-three residue.
The latter residue identification uses transitivity on three-type flags,
so incidence of two elements through the fixed type-$f$ element is
represented by an intersection inside $P_f$. For a rank-two residue, the
same transitivity represents each of its two vertex types by $K_F$;
three-type stabilizers are trivial, so these labels are unique. Its actual
incidence is exactly the graph in the third condition. This proves
residual connectivity independently of the intersection checks.

Thinness and chamber connectivity make every chamber-fixing automorphism
trivial. Thus the full type-preserving automorphism group has order at
most $2|P|$. If it properly contains $P$, the latter has index two and
is normal. Composing an extra automorphism with an element of $P$ gives
an involution $t$ sending the base chamber $C$ to its type-zero neighbor
$C^0$: its square fixes $C$.
For the source coset conventions,
\[
 C^0=(P_0a_1^{-1},P_1,P_2,P_3).
\]
Indeed $P_0a_i^{-1}=P_0a_1^{-1}$ for every $i>0$; these cosets meet all
three other base elements, and $C^+$ intersection excludes $a_1\in P_0$.
Right multiplication gives $C^0 a_i=C^i$, whence
$C a_i^{-1}=(C^0)^i$. As type-preserving automorphisms commute with
adjacency, $t a_i t$ and $a_i^{-1}$ have the same effect on $C$ and are
equal. Thus an extra automorphism would induce simultaneous inversion on
$P$. Its failure proves that $P$ is the full group and the geometry is
chiral.
\end{proof}

Every test above is finite and exact. The rank-two connectivity tests use
only two copies of an intersection of proper parabolics. A bounded count
is not extrapolated to untested presentations. For each literal finite
presentation, simultaneous inversion can be decided by evaluating its full
relator list on the inverse marked images: if all relations hold, the
assignment defines an endomorphism whose square fixes every generator,
hence an automorphism; a failed relation excludes it.
