\begin{abstract}
We recover the principal classification of Fernandes and Leemans for
C-groups of ranks $n-1$ and $n-2$ in degree $n$, with the original
bounds $n\geq7$ and $n\geq9$. The common theory supplies a uniform
intransitive-parabolic reduction and a finite-core attachment theorem.
We prove the complete fracture-graph converse, including the local
splitting obstructions, and realize every flag by a decorated four-point
core and a laminar tree partition. This gives all the asserted regular
hypertopes. Exact changes of marking give presentations for arbitrary
branching. One clause of the accepted manuscript's printed presentation
is incompatible with such branching; we exhibit the literal obstruction
and give the corrected general rule. No conjecture solution or new
historical classification is claimed.
\end{abstract}
We use the C-group convention of the companion common paper [U]. The subgroup $G_i$ denotes the maximal parabolic obtained by omitting
$i$; other generated subgroups are written with their full generating
sets. The permutation graph has one labelled edge for
each transposition factor of a distinguished involution. Its graph
may have parallel edges with different labels. An action on cosets
will be represented by images of its explicitly described stabilized
objects, so left cosets are used throughout.

The source is the accepted 2018 institutional manuscript [FL], with
20 authored pages and a publisher cover. Its five principal targets are
Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, Proposition 3.1, Corollary 6.5 and Theorem 6.6.
The first, second, third and fifth conclusions are recovered in their
full stated ranges. The general presentation conclusion is recovered
by an exact corrected rule; its false branching subclause is not
endorsed. The ledger records auxiliary statements and exclusions
separately from this principal contract.

\section{Boundary example and exact recovery scope}
The restriction $n\geq9$ in the rank-$n-2$ assertion cannot be
removed unchanged. The accepted source displays the following six
involutions on eight letters:
\[
 (12),\ (12)(34),\ (12)(78),\ (12)(56),\ (13)(68),\ (18)(36).
\]
For the finite flag check we use the following elementary criterion.
If $H_i$ are subgroups, all coset flags have common representatives
if and only if, for every anchor $a$ and set $J$ of other types,
$\bigcap_{j\in J}H_aH_j=H_a(\bigcap_{j\in J}H_j)$.
Indeed, inductively realize the flag on $J$ and translate its common
representative to the identity. Its objects are then the subgroups
$H_j$. A right coset $H_ax$ is incident with each of them exactly when
$x$ belongs to the intersection on the left; it meets their common
intersection exactly when $x$ belongs to the right side. This proves
both necessity and the induction step. It is the product-set criterion
recalled in [FL, Theorem 2.5], here with the coset convention reversed
by inversion. The program represents $H_aH_j$ by the orbit of $H_a$
under $H_j$ on right cosets, so its test is exactly this criterion.

The complete auxiliary program \texttt{boundary\_example.g} verifies
that they generate $S_8$, that all $64^2=4096$ subset intersections
satisfy the C-group condition, and that their coset system is
flag-transitive. It checks every product-set condition using all
156 choices of anchor and at least two remaining types. The maximal
parabolic obtained by omitting the first generator is transitive of
order $1344$. This bounded example is a computational recovery of the
source's stated boundary example, not a premise in either uniform
classification proof. The same program verifies the two order-three
bridge products in the branching correction above; its all-subset
intersection and geometry assertions already follow from the uniform
written theorems.

The three families, their geometric conclusions and the historical
fracture method belong to Fernandes--Leemans and the prior work they
cite, including Fernandes--Leemans--Mixer. The maximum independent
length and omission alternatives retain Whiston through the exact
Cameron--Cara interfaces listed in [U]. The new proof organization
uses the repeated normal-module argument, a simple-factor chain bound,
one finite attachment interface and the explicit decorated-flag
completion. These arguments are written in full; no earlier combined
manuscript is an unpriced mathematical premise.

The source's auxiliary table of primitive C-ranks is not recovered as
a table of exact ranks. The required program proves only the complete
primitive bounds and exact finite group facts needed by our converse.
We do not infer that a string C-rank equals an unrestricted C-rank.
The unnumbered statement about three times a number of leaves is also
not made a counting contract: after forgetting generator labels one
must specify whether leaves are distinguished individually or only
up to automorphisms of the tail. The classification of all markings
up to relabelling is proved, but a separate numerical convention for
that ancillary sentence is left explicit rather than assumed.
The background results on general incidence systems and on arbitrary
hypertopes are used only in the elementary coset form proved here;
this paper does not claim to replace every historical background
assertion or every maximal-parabolic case description verbatim.

The entire common paper is a dependency of this application. The
primitive catalogue program is a required finite input for its
necessity proof. The four-point identities and all local splitting
cases have complete written proofs; their two full programs are
validation. The boundary example program is required only for that
additional finite source example and is not required for the five
principal results. All four programs are printed without pruning.
Operational drivers and the eight-declaration Lean certificate are
separate evidence. The certificate covers the generic normalization
leg; it does not formalize this classification, the catalogue, the
finite-core theorem or flag completion. No compression ratio, additional
source admission or question credit is inferred before independent
review of the complete packet.
