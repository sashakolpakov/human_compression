\title{Five primitive-geometry counts and the Rudvalis correction}
\author{}\date{}
\begin{document}\maketitle
Leemans and Rodrigues \cite{LR} count primitive rank-two coset geometries
up to automorphisms of their distinguished group, allowing the two types
to be interchanged. Their Table~1 prints $1511$ for the Rudvalis group.
The accepted principal correction already disproves that number by the
lower bound $50669$. Here we obtain an exact total, conditional on the
classical group classification and library inputs specified below. Four
other entries are recovered by the same calculation.

\begin{theorem}
Assume the named group and library premises stated below. For
the groups $M_{11}$, $M_{23}$, $M_{24}$, $J_1$ and $\mathrm{Ru}$, the numbers of firm,
connected, flag-transitive primitive rank-two coset geometries, under the
source's specified equivalence, are respectively
\[
                  37,\quad170,\quad5074,\quad669,\quad556287.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All five groups are nonabelian simple and have trivial outer
automorphism group. These classical group facts are retained from the
Atlas \cite{Atlas}. A maximal subgroup $M$ is self-normalizing: its
normalizer in $G$ is either $M$ or $G$, and the latter contradicts
simplicity. Thus the conjugation action on all maximal subgroups is the
disjoint union of the actions on $G/M$, taking one $M$ from each
conjugacy class. The source's geometries correspond exactly to the
orbits on two-element subsets of this disjoint union. The full
geometric and equivalence arguments are given in the previously accepted
principal correction; the companion common proof states the same
reduction explicitly.

Write $\psi$ for the fixed-point function of this union and set
\[
 d=\psi(1),\quad s=\sum_{[g]}\frac{\psi(g)}{|C_G(g)|},\quad
 r=\sum_{[g]}\frac{\psi(g)^2}{|C_G(g)|},\quad
 u=\sum_{[g]}\frac{\psi(g^2)}{|C_G(g)|}.
\]
The common proof derives each coset character from the subgroup and
parent centralizer orders and the compatible fusion of classes. Its
Theorem~2 then gives $(r-2s+u)/2$ for the required unordered-pair count.
No orbit representative or target count enters that calculation.

The retained library export contains all these integer inputs. The
independent Python program evaluates the formulas using rational
arithmetic, checks integrality of every fixed-point value, and obtains
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}\toprule
$G$ & $s$ & $d$ & $r$ & $u$ & $(r-2s+u)/2$\\\midrule
$M_{11}$ & 5 & 309 & 67 & 17 & 37\\
$M_{23}$ & 7 & 44414 & 316 & 38 & 170\\
$M_{24}$ & 9 & 1507537 & 9851 & 315 & 5074\\
$J_1$ & 7 & 13016 & 1230 & 122 & 669\\
$\mathrm{Ru}$ & 15 & 398283420 & 1107296 & 5308 & 556287\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The accompanying report gives every maximal-subgroup permutation
character and all scalar products, so the displayed totals can be
recomputed directly from the supplied inputs. The identity centralizers,
class-size sums, subgroup indices, centralizer inclusions and fusion
ranges are checked. Each coset character has orbit average one and
values between zero and its degree. For every element class, the
fixed unordered-pair count is a nonnegative integer. These checks and
the exact final divisions establish the displayed arithmetic.
\end{proof}

\section*{Data premises and attribution}
The input is the installed GAP Character Table Library 1.3.11
(25 May 2025), read by GAP 4.15.1. Its documented \texttt{Maxes}
contract supplies one representative of every conjugacy class of
maximal subgroups, their ordinary character tables, and compatible
class fusions to the parent. The complete lists, the validity of the
named tables, the stored square maps and the compatible fusions are
external classification/data premises. The checks above do not prove
those premises from internal numerical consistency. The exact library
files, exporter and execution receipt are bound separately. The export
terminated normally in less than one second; its retained output is
10788 bytes, with no standard-error output.

The second program does not call GAP: it derives the characters and
averages independently from that export. Its five damage controls
reject altered identity centralizers, out-of-range fusion and square
maps, a duplicate maximal identifier, and a changed parent order.
These are tests of the certificate interface, not substitutes for the
general proof. The raw earlier aggregate probe is not needed as a
premise or as an expected answer.

Burnside's lemma, induction of the trivial character, and the elementary
coset realization are classical. The source supplies the problem and
its equivalence convention. Our contribution is this uniform application,
the exact independent arithmetic, and the correction of the Rudvalis
entry under that convention. We make no claim that these formulas or
the four agreeing totals are new.

\section*{The already established $M_{22}$ total and remaining claims}
The complete corrected L073 application proves that $M_{22}$ has
81 pair classes under precisely the equivalence used here: an
automorphism of the distinguished group and interchange of types.
Its common Lemma~1 and Theorem~2 identify these pairs with the firm,
connected primitive geometries, and its application Theorem~4 gives
the exhaustive 81-class result. Thus the $M_{22}$ entry of the present
source is imported with its existing named-group, catalogue and exact
algorithm premises. Its enumeration is not rerun and earns no new
original-manuscript or measurement credit.

The other six outer-automorphism cases, all five printed suborbit
tables, and the individual $M_{11}$ incidence and design tables remain
separate obligations. In particular, this calculation alone does not
complete L036. It proves no recorded open conjecture, supplies no Lean
certificate, and asserts no compression factor.
