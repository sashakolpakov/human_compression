Module A: Construction and classification of two-group polytopes and hy-
pertopes
Abstract
We develop construction and classification methods for regular polytopes and hypertopes whose auto-
morphism groups have two-power order. A forest of noncommuting generators determines a canonical
class-two quotient. Its square-lattice sections provide local kernels that can be attached independently
by the supported-kernel theorem of Marked groups, kernels and intersections . This yields prescribed
edge orders, square sections and three-generator hypertope types, with exact orders and complete pre-
sentations. Conversely, a fixed class-two quotient reduces bounded-order classification to an exhaustive
invariant-kernel problem. We combine this reduction with cyclic-core and central-descent arguments to
recover the high-type rank-three families and the full order-1024 classification, including named facets
and corrected presentations. The same local models also support regular, chiral and alternating exten-
sions. Source conclusions, retained geometric inputs and corrections are stated explicitly.
Introduction
Local sections serve two complementary purposes in finite incidence geometry. One may prescribe them
and construct a marked group realizing those prescriptions, or constrain their orders and classify every
group that can occur. The same kernel coordinates can organize both tasks. In the construction direction,
a local quotient is attached to a subset of the marker labels. In the classification direction, a canonical
nilpotent quotient confines the search to normal subgroups of one finite group.
The starting point is a vertex-and-edge model for a forest diagram. The intersection property forces
independent edge commutators in degree two, so every finite marked two-group with that diagram has
the same class-two quotient (§2). The affine square group supplies exact local kernels and two useful
quotient decompositions (§3). The supported-kernel theorem of the companion paper [U] then assembles
prescribed edge orders and consecutive square sections. Its coordinate-kernel Helly theorem upgrades
three dihedral attachments to an actual regular hypertope. A separate sharp order bound identifies the
source’s complete presentation (§4).
The reverse direction begins with a bounded cyclic core, reducing the high-type rank-three problem to
eight explicit models (§5). Central descent and complete binary lifting link the bounded quotients (§6).
One invariant-kernel census, followed by complete presentation and marked-isomorphism checks, yields
the order-1024 classification (§7). Finally, free geometric extensions use the amalgam theorem of [U]
and precisely stated earlier constructions (§8).
The six applications are the prescribed-order theorem of Hou, Feng and Leemans [HFLa], their high-
type rank-three classification [HFLb], their three-generator hypertope construction [HFLh], the common-
quotient theorem of Hou and Schulte [HS26], the square-section and extension theorems of Cunning-
ham, Feng, Hou and Schulte [CFHS25], and the order-1024 classification of Gomi, Loyola and De Las
Peñas [GLP18]. Their original results retain that attribution. The general algebraic proofs occur once
in [U]; this paper supplies the actual marked maps, local injectivity, geometric hypotheses and source-
presentation identifications needed to use them. Earlier cyclic-core, geometric-assembly and infinite-
decomposition theorems remain explicit retained inputs. All finite classification data and the complete
certificate interface are specified in Appendix C.
1. Markings, geometric inputs and the interface to U

do not commute. The type is their consecutive product orders. A regular hypertope is a thin, residually

the group by two relevant face stabilizers makes every face of the first kind incident with every face of
the second kind. It is never inferred merely from IP.
