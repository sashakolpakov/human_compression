# Two marking operations and the six rank-four J1 constructions

10 October 2026. Original uniform reduction and exact verification of the construction results in Hartley–Leemans, *On the thin regular geometries of rank four for the Janko group J1*, **Innovations in Incidence Geometry 1** (2005), 181–190. [Primary full text](https://msp.org/iig/2005/1-1/iig-v1-n1-p08-s.pdf).

This note recovers the six construction theorems, numbered 3.2, 4.2, 5.2, 5.4, 6.2 and 7.2, through two marking operations and one common recognition test. It supplies every subgroup intersection and typed coset-intersection check, including those left as exercises in the paper. It does **not** independently classify every thin regular geometry of J1 or rule out all higher ranks. The paper's introductory assertion that its six geometries exhaust the rank-four possibilities remains a published computational classification input when an exhaustive statement is made.

There is a source-label issue: the Section 5 definitions of Γ3 and Γ4 correspond respectively to the diagrams labelled Γ4 and Γ3 in the printed figures. We keep the definitions and record the discrepancy explicitly in Section 5 below.

## 1. Uniform odd-endpoint doubling

Let `(G; a,b,r2,...,r(n−1))` be a C-group marking: the generators are independent involutions, and for every two sets of marked generators, their generated subgroups intersect in the subgroup generated by their common markers. Assume `a` commutes with each `ri`, and `|ab|=m` is odd. Write `R=<r2,...,r(n−1)>`, `P=<b,R>`, and use `x^g=g⁻¹xg`. Put `a'=b^a=aba`.

**Theorem A.** The tuple `(a',b,r2,...,r(n−1))` generates `G`. It is a C-group marking if and only if

\[
 P\cap P^a=R. \tag{1}
\]

Its maximal parabolics, in the same order, are

\[
 P,\quad P^a,\quad P_2,\ldots,P_{n-1},
\]

where `Pi` are the old maximal parabolics. There is a symmetric statement for the opposite endpoint.

**Proof.** Since `a'b=(ab)²` and `m` is odd, `<a',b>=<a,b>`: explicitly `a=(a'b)^((m+1)/2)b`. The full groups and all parabolics with index at least two therefore agree. Deleting `a'` gives `P`; deleting `b` gives `P^a`, since `a` centralizes every remaining `ri`.

Here is a direct proof of the intersection assertion for **all** marked subsets. Classify a new marked subgroup into three cases:

1. If it omits `a'`, it is its corresponding old subgroup, contained in `P`.
2. If it contains both `a'` and `b`, it is the old subgroup with `a` replacing `a'`. It contains `a` and is normalized by conjugation by `a`.
3. If it contains `a'` but omits `b`, it is `<b, selected ri>^a`, contained in `P^a`.

Intersections of cases 1–1, 1–2 and 2–2 follow from the old intersection property. Cases 3–3 follow by conjugating the old property. For cases 2–3, conjugate both subgroups by `a` and use that the case-2 subgroup is unchanged; the old common marker `b` conjugates back to `a'`. Finally, in cases 1–3, condition (1) confines the intersection to `R`. The old intersection property removes every marker outside the common selected `ri`, and conjugation by `a` fixes their subgroup. These are all cases. Conversely, the new intersection property applied to its first two maximal parabolics gives (1). ∎

This is a substantive reduction of the intersection property to one specified subgroup meet. Generation alone is not enough. Nor does Theorem A assert flag transitivity for a nonstring diagram.

## 2. Uniform central twist

Let a group be generated by involutions including `b,c`, with `|bc|=m` odd and at least three. Let `w` be a nonidentity involution in that group which centralizes `b` and `c`. Replace `b` by `b'=wb`, leaving every other marker unchanged.

**Lemma B.** The new tuple still generates the group, and `|b'c|=2m`.

**Proof.** Since `w` commutes with `bc`, `(b'c)^m=w`. Thus the new tuple recovers `w`, then `b=wb'`. The order is exactly `2m`, since the commuting factors `w` and `bc` have coprime orders two and `m`. The new marker is a nonidentity involution; equality `wb=1` would force `b` to commute with `c`, contrary to odd `m≥3`. ∎

If `w` lies in and centralizes a parabolic `P` containing `b,c`, that parabolic is unchanged by the twist. The parabolic obtained by deleting `b` is also unchanged. Intersections involving other parabolics require proof or exact verification. No automatic preservation of the intersection property or flag transitivity is being assumed.

## 3. A common, exact coset-recognition theorem

For a finite group `G` and maximal parabolics `P0,...,P(n−1)`, define incidence between right cosets by nonempty set intersection. For each `i` and set `J` of other types, put

\[
 X_{ij}=\{P_i x:x\in P_j\},\qquad
 P_J=\bigcap_{j\in J}P_j.
\]

**Lemma C.** The coset system has the typed Helly property, and consequently is a flag-transitive geometry, if and only if, for every `i` and every `J` of size at least two avoiding `i`,

\[
 \left|\bigcap_{j\in J}X_{ij}\right|
   =[P_J:P_J\cap P_i]. \tag{2}
\]

The right-hand side counts the cosets `Pi x` with `x∈PJ`; these form a subset of the set on the left. Thus (2) is an exact set equality, not a probabilistic or numerical heuristic. To prove sufficiency, induct on the size of a flag. Normalize its previously chosen objects to base cosets. Every additional object incident to them lies on the left of (2), and hence has a representative in their common subgroup `PJ`. It consequently has a common point with all the base cosets. Translating this common point normalizes the whole flag. Pairs and singletons start the induction. Necessity follows by normalizing any incident flag of the indicated types. Extending a common coset point to all types proves the geometry axiom. ∎

If the parabolics come from a C-group marking and (2) holds, the geometry is thin, residually connected and chamber-regular. Indeed, the chamber stabilizer is the intersection of all maximal parabolics, which is trivial by the intersection property. A panel stabilizer is a single generator group of order two. For any residue of rank at least two, its stabilizer is the subgroup generated by the remaining markers, and it is generated by its proper maximal parabolics. The usual coset-graph argument then gives connectivity: a word in their union supplies an incidence walk. The chamber graph is also connected directly: all chambers lie in the base chamber's `G`-orbit, and the marked generators move across its panels. These facts show that the full type-preserving automorphism group is `G`: an automorphism fixing a chamber fixes adjacent chambers by thinness, then every chamber by chamber-graph connectivity.

The checker verifies (2) both globally and in every rank-three marked residue. The latter checks are redundant for the global theorem, but make the geometric scope visible. It separately checks all 256 subgroup meets for each rank-four marking and all eleven residue-generation equalities. Subgroup intersection and typed coset Helly are different assertions, both checked here.

## 4. One exact seed and five derived markings

The independent literal seed is a tuple `(a,b,c,d)` of permutations of degree 266, saved in `phase24_L088_seed.json`. It was found inside the retained TomLib J1 permutation group by a short centralizer search, not read from a published geometry list. Fixing its first involution leaves a centralizer of order 120 with 31 involutions; the eleventh tested compatible tuple satisfies the source relations and generates the full group of order 175,560.

The seed has pair orders, in the order `ab,ac,ad,bc,bd,cd`,

\[
 (5,2,2,3,2,5),
\]

and also satisfies `(abc)^5=1` and `((dcb)^5a)^3=1`. These are exactly the additional relations in Section 3 of the source. A separate completed Todd–Coxeter calculation gives index 1,463 over `<b,c,d>` and a coset image of order 175,560. The subgroup `<b,c,d>` is a quotient of the finite Coxeter group `[3,5]`, of order 120. Consequently the presentation has order at most `1463·120=175560`; the literal generating tuple supplies a quotient of exactly that order. The presentation therefore gives J1, conditional on the retained exact coset-enumeration and permutation-group algorithms. This verifies the initial presentation identification rather than merely fitting its product orders.

Put `w=(bcd)^5`. The checker verifies that it is a nonidentity central involution of `<b,c,d>`. Use the following **source-defined** markings:

| Geometry | Marking | Construction |
|---|---|---|
| Γ1 | `(a,b,c,d)` | Seed |
| Γ2 | `(a,wb,c,d)` | Lemma B |
| Γ3 | `(a,b,c,c^d)` | Right-end Theorem A |
| Γ4 | `(b^a,b,c,d)` | Left-end Theorem A |
| Γ5 | `(b^a,b,c,c^d)` | Both endpoint operations |
| Γ6 | `((wb)^a,wb,c,d)` | Left-end operation after the twist |

The two endpoint conditions for the seed are checked as actual subgroup equalities:

\[
 \langle b,c,d\rangle\cap\langle b,c,d\rangle^a
    =\langle c,d\rangle,\qquad
 \langle a,b,c\rangle\cap\langle a,b,c\rangle^d
    =\langle a,b\rangle.
\]

Both intersections have order ten. The first unchanged parabolic also applies to Γ3 and Γ2, so the same left-end equality proves the intersection property for Γ5 and Γ6 after the preceding step. Thus, beyond the seed and central-twist checks, four construction proofs reduce to these two meets. The independent direct full-meet certificates confirm this reduction on every derived tuple.

## 5. Exact source comparison and failure boundary

All ten primary pages were read. The figures on PDF pages 9 and 10 were also rendered and visually inspected; the extracted text alone was not used to assign the diagram labels. In Section 5, Γ3 replaces `d` by `c^d`. Theorem A immediately makes its two corresponding parabolics conjugates of `<a,b,c>≅A5`. However, the figure labelled Γ3 has two parabolics `C2×A5`. The Section 5 definition of Γ4 instead duplicates `<b,c,d>≅C2×A5`, whereas the figure labelled Γ4 duplicates `A5`. Thus the two figure labels are transposed relative to the definitions. This is independent of type orientation and is confirmed by the exact certificates. The other four diagram/parabolic descriptions agree.

The paper warns that the opposite-end doubling of Γ2 fails thinness. The checker verifies that exact tuple `(a,wb,c,c^d)`: it still generates J1 and passes the coset-Helly tests, but has eight failing subgroup-meet pairs and panel orders `2,4,2,2`. A second source-mentioned failure, `(a,b,c,wd)`, has the same group and passes Helly but has eight failing meet pairs and panel orders `2,2,2,4`. These examples show why neither odd-order generation nor an unchanged ambient group can substitute for the intersection and thinness arguments.

This package does not enumerate all possible central modifications mentioned in the final discussion, and it does not rederive the introductory six-object completeness assertion. It rederives all six labelled principal **construction** statements, their mutual distinction, and the two specific failure examples. The two rank-four records in the older phase6 RWPRI census both have Borel order two; they are not thin seeds and are not used as a thin-geometry classification here.

```sh

```

Alternatively, the underlying standalone literal-seed checker and presentation certificate run from this directory:

```sh

```

The main check requires GAP and TomLib. TomLib is used only to identify the generated literal permutation group as its J1 model; the previous full geometry census and its representative list are not read. The optional extraction script records how this original seed was found; its use of the earlier ambient group file supplies no geometry-classification inference. GAP's exact group/intersection/coset algorithms and the named TomLib group model remain explicit computational inputs. A separate Python standard-library checker independently verifies the permutation relations, transformed pair orders, and validity and transitivity of the saved coset action. It also checks every saved numerical recognition row. It does not re-enumerate subgroup intersections or replay the Todd–Coxeter proof of the upper bound: a valid transitive coset table alone is not such an upper-bound proof. The common marking lemmas and recognition proof are written proofs, not Lean theorems.

No open conjecture or publication-priority claim is made. These are new reproductions and a uniform proof adapter for established 2005 construction results. Internal reviews are not external peer review.
