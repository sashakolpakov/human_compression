# All source parameters from bounded tensor membership tests

This note completes the geometric step for the integral tensor marking in `phase35_L003_tensor_model.md`. The new conclusion is finite regular rank-four hypertopes for **every integer source parameter $s\geq2$**, with the four prescribed local toroidal parameters. It does not assert that the source's universal groups are finite. Bibliographic priority remains a separate question.

## 1. Marking and theorem

Let
$$
N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
D=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
R=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad
S=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Use the tensor-permutation convention of the retained note: a factor in position $i$ is sent to position $p(i)$. Put
$$
x=-[(D,R,R);(132)],\quad y=[(I,I,I);(132)],\quad
z=[(S,Q,S);1],\quad a=[(I,I,I);(13)],
$$
and $r_0=a,r_1=ax,r_2=ay,r_3=az$. These are integral $8\times8$ involutions. For an integer $n\geq2$, let $G(n)$ be their subgroup in $\operatorname{GL}_8(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)$, with this specific marking.

**Theorem.** For every $n\geq5$, and also for $n=3$, this marking has the full subgroup intersection property and typed coset Helly. Its standard coset geometry is a regular hypertope. In particular, taking $n=F_s$, the $s$th Fibonacci number, gives a finite regular hypertope with source parameters
$$
((3,0),(2,0),(1,1),(s,0))
$$
for every $s\geq4$. The previously certified parameter-two and parameter-three examples complete finite existence for every $s\geq2$.

The theorem for all $n\geq5$ concerns geometry only; the source parameter at a general modulus is not asserted here. At Fibonacci moduli the retained tensor theorem supplies its exact value and all four local marked identifications. In omitted-type order $0,1,2,3$, their orders are $36,12s^2,48,108$.

## 2. The three protected subgroups

All groups in this section are first formed over the integers. Write $P_i=\langle r_j:j\ne i\rangle$. The retained literal tensor certificate, also recomputed by the new checker, gives
$$
|P_0|=36,\qquad |P_2|=48,\qquad |P_3|=108.
$$
Let $B=P_0\cup P_2\cup P_3$ and $W=BB$. The new exact certificate enumerates the complete groups and all products, obtaining
$$
|B|=172,\qquad |W|=1928,\qquad
\max_{w\in W}\max_{u,v}|w_{uv}|=2.                 \tag{1}
$$
These are actual $8\times8$ matrices, so (1) implies that reduction is injective on $W$ for every $n\geq5$. In particular it is faithful on each protected subgroup, their intersections, and every comparison between two protected products. This is an entry bound, with no assertion about the kernel of reduction on the whole ambient group.

Every proper marked subgroup other than $P_1$ is contained in at least one protected subgroup: a proper subset of the four generators either omits a type different from 1, or is exactly the subset defining $P_1$. Thus $P_1$ is the only variable subgroup needed in the argument.

## 3. A necessary membership test that is exact on $W$

The pure factor permutations generated by $r_0,r_2$ form $S_3$. Since $r_3=r_0z$,
$$
P_1=\langle S_3,z\rangle.                         \tag{2}
$$
For $i=1,2,3$, let $J_i$ denote $N$ in tensor position $i$ and identities elsewhere. Direct $2\times2$ multiplication gives
$$
S^{-1}NS=Q^{-1}NQ=I-N.
$$
Consequently every element of (2) conjugates the three $J_i$ by a permutation and one **common** choice between $J_i$ and $I-J_i$. This assertion is valid over the integers and modulo every $n\geq2$.

Consider a tensor-permutation word $g=[(A_1,A_2,A_3);p]$. If its reduction belongs to $P_1(n)$, remove its pure permutation, which lies in (2). A pure tensor conjugates the full matrix algebra of each tensor factor to itself. These three factor algebras have pairwise intersection equal to the scalar matrices, over any nonzero coefficient ring: comparing entries on pairs of basis vectors differing in just one factor proves this directly. Since each $J_i$ and $I-J_i$ is nonscalar, the permutation in its conjugation action must now be the identity. If the common choice is the second one, multiplication by $z$ switches it to the first. Therefore, for one $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}$, **all three factor matrices of $gz^\epsilon$ commute with $N$ modulo $n$**. Removing or reinstating the pure factor permutation does not change this statement; multiplication by $z$ supplies the appropriately permuted factors automatically.

The finite certificate evaluates this necessary test for every $g\in W$ and both values of $\epsilon$. It proves:

1. Every entry of each factor commutator $A_iN-NA_i$ has absolute value at most 4.
2. Exactly 52 of the $3856$ pairs $(g,\epsilon)$ have all three integral commutators zero.
3. In every such pair, the factors are explicitly $A_i=\delta_iN^{k_i}$, with $\delta_i\in\{1,-1\}$ and $|k_i|\leq2$, and
   $$k_1+k_2+k_3=0,\qquad \delta_1\delta_2\delta_3=1.       \tag{3}$$

The report lists these witnesses. There is no unproved classification of units of a quadratic ring in this step: only the five literal powers $N^{-2},\ldots,N^2$ and their negatives are compared with the finitely enumerated factors.

Conversely, each pair satisfying (3) gives an element of $P_1$ over the integers. Indeed the retained identity
$$
T=y(zy)^2=N^{-1}\otimes I\otimes N
$$
and its factor-permutation conjugates generate every tensor $N^{k_1}\otimes N^{k_2}\otimes N^{k_3}$ with exponent sum zero. The total scalar in (3) is 1. Reinsert the pure permutation and, if needed, $z$; all belong to (2).

For $n\geq5$, a bounded commutator entry vanishes modulo $n$ precisely when it is zero as an integer. The necessary test is therefore sufficient on $W$, and proves the exact equivalence
$$
g\bmod n\in P_1(n)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g\in P_1
\qquad(g\in W,\ n\geq5).                         \tag{4}
$$
In particular these decisions are identical at every such modulus, including the base modulus 5. This reasoning does not require a factorwise scalar decomposition of tensor identities over a composite ring.

## 4. Why these finitely many decisions suffice

At modulus 5 the checker closes all 15 proper marked subgroups. It checks all $15^2=225$ proper-subset meets. The other 31 of the full 256 meets involve the ambient marked group and are tautological. Equation (1) preserves all meets of subgroups contained in protected groups. Equation (4) preserves a meet involving $P_1$ and any other proper subgroup. Its self-meet is tautological. Thus full subgroup IP holds for every $n\geq5$.

Here is the separate flag test; it is not inferred from IP. For distinct types, use right cosets. Fix a type $i$ and a set $J$ of at least two other types, and choose $j_0\in J$ with $j_0\ne1$. Such a protected choice always exists. Among cosets $P_i g$ with $g\in P_{j_0}$, count those incident with every base coset $P_j$, $j\in J$. They are indexed by cosets $(P_i\cap P_{j_0})g$ inside $P_{j_0}$; a representative $g$ is eligible exactly when
$$
g\in P_iP_j\quad\text{for every }j\in J.          \tag{5}
$$
The cosets supplied by $\bigcap_{j\in J}P_j$ always form a subset of these eligible cosets. Its cardinality is
$$
\left[\bigcap_{j\in J}P_j:
P_i\cap\bigcap_{j\in J}P_j\right].               \tag{6}
$$
Equality between the count in (5) and (6) says that every such eligible coset has a common element with all the base parabolics. Checking this for all 16 choices $(i,J)$ is equivalent to typed coset Helly: induct on flag size, carry all but one coset to the base flag using their common element, and apply the equality. Flags of size at most two need no test.

All ingredients of these 16 counts are stable from modulus 5 to any $n\geq5$. The representatives lie in a protected group. Their coset identifications and (6) are determined by intersections inside a protected group. If neither factor in (5) is $P_1$, the decision compares matrices in $W$, so (1) applies. If one factor is $P_1$, test (5) by multiplying $g$ on the appropriate side by the inverses of elements of the other, protected, parabolic. Every resulting word belongs to $W$, so (4) applies. This explains why no ambient group enumeration or unbounded list of exceptional-parabolic elements is required for the uniform argument.

The checker verifies equality in all 16 cases at modulus 5. The resulting IP and Helly therefore hold uniformly. At modulus 3, it performs the full proper-subgroup and flag tests directly, independently of the bound (1).

## 5. From the marking to the source geometries

Typed coset Helly gives flag transitivity and flag completion in the standard coset system. Full marked IP gives a trivial chamber stabilizer and panels of size two. Residues are the standard geometries of the appropriate marked subgroups and are connected because those subgroups are generated by their marked involutions. Thus the geometry is thin and residually connected. The group acts regularly on chambers, and every type-preserving automorphism fixing a chamber is trivial by chamber-gallery propagation. Hence $G(n)$ is its full type-preserving automorphism group.

The retained tensor theorem proves the first-nine source relations, all three fixed toroidal local identifications, and exact parameter order $s$ modulo $F_s$. It also proves that the exceptional maximal parabolic has order $12s^2$, attaining its source torus bound. Combining that result with the present theorem gives the stated exact local parameters for every $s\geq4$. The frozen even and multiple-of-three packages give the cases $s=2$ and $s=3$, respectively.

This answers finite regular existence at every integer parameter in the displayed second source family. It does not identify each finite constructed group with the corresponding universal presentation. The already proved infinitude at multiples of six prevents a claim of universal finiteness for all parameters. Likewise this is not an assertion that every assertion or application in the source paper has been rederived.
