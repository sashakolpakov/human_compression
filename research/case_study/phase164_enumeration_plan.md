## The actual common theory

The organizing object is a finite action on admissible marked configurations, together with a map forgetting part of the marking. U.Orbits classifies finite actions by transitive-orbit multiplicities and their kernels and normalizers. U.ExtensionOrbits classifies extensions over one orbit representative by its stabilizer acting on the actual fiber. Each application supplies its own invariant admissibility predicate and geometric recognition; no group order, linear representation or orbit count substitutes for that predicate.

The shared use is concrete:

| Application | Acting group | Retained configuration | Extension fiber | Required local tests |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L066, all degrees | Marked four-group and endpoint reversal | Multiplicity vector of its five transitive actions | Parity/degree constraints | Faithfulness, sign character, endpoint distinctness |
| L066, degree six | Full Aut(S6) or Aut(A6) | Unordered commuting endpoint pair | Middle involutions | Generation and all 64 subgroup meets |
| L074 | Frobenius group, then commuting duality | First normalized field coordinate | Nonzero second field coordinate | Exact joint field of definition, generation and rank-three IP |
| L104 | Normalizer of the Borel, acting on actual panel subgroups | Accepted lower-rank panel set | Panels in a maximal overgroup of the generated subgroup | All meets, typed Helly, RWPRI, local two-transitivity and rank-two partial-linearity |

In L074 this is an actual reuse of U.ExtensionOrbits in the published nested lambda/psi formula: fixing a degree-n first coordinate leaves the subgroup generated by the n-th Frobenius acting on the second coordinate. In L104, replacing the normalizer by its permutation image on panels preserves both orbit equivalence and every subgroup test, since every image permutation has an ambient lift. The source-specific subgroup classification and exact panel recognition remain paid inputs.

Three additional local theorem legs are needed, and must be proved in the application rather than hidden inside the shared theorem:

1. Exact-support orbit enumeration: Möbius inversion on a finite support poset, combined with fixed-point orbit counting. Field degree is the application in L074; no claim is made that the current J2 implementation computes a Möbius sum.
2. Arbitrary-Borel recognition: the typed-Helly argument from U applies to generated joins of panel subgroups, including nontrivial Borel. The meet diagram, local indices and generation of residues are checked explicitly. U.Geometry's thin-involution specialization is insufficient for this use.
3. Maximal-residue augmentation: a complete list of subgroup/maximal-inclusion classes and exact transporters enumerates every next overgroup. RWPRI supplies a suitable maximal deletion; the proof does not assume every deletion is maximal. A subgroup-index chain gives termination, and a recorded empty level is required.

These legs are reused mathematical deductions, not a claim that three classifications follow merely from having a group action. The finite degree-six branch, the Suzuki subgroup-position argument and the J2 catalogue remain substantial separate application costs.

## First expansion target: L073

L073, Leemans–Rowley (2010), *Rank three residually connected geometries for M22, revisited*, has nine primary pages. Its principal Theorem 1 gives 431 rank-three flag-transitive residually connected geometries with all object stabilizers maximal, up to Aut(M22) conjugacy. The table describes all 431 representatives using the natural 22-point action, an M24 Steiner-system realization, eight maximal-subgroup classes, pairwise shadow intersections, additional source orbit labels and the Borel-group type. It also corrects the rank-two list to 81 classes.

The source's only claimed mathematical main theorem is not enough to excuse ignoring its classification table. Before census the exact contract will include (i) exhaustive 431-class enumeration and inequivalence, (ii) the 81-class rank-two boundary, (iii) the eight geometric maximal-class identifications, and (iv) the extent to which all 431 tabulated shadow/Borel labels are recovered. Some secondary orbit-index notation is delegated by the source to Kilic–Rowley (2003); that dependency must be read or explicitly remain a label-matching gap. No source-data list will be used as an exhaustion premise.

A suitable search is a top-down stabilizer-fiber enumeration of unordered maximal-subgroup triples, followed by exact typed Helly and residue-generation tests. It differs from J2's RWPRI panel augmentation: maximal object stabilizers do not imply all the J2 local axioms. Every distinct maximal pair gives a connected firm rank-two geometry; U.ExtensionOrbits first classifies pairs and then third maximal subgroups under their setwise stabilizer. Full Aut(M22) and type equivalence must be explicit. Reaching 431 alone is not a certificate without this completeness proof and exact witnesses.
