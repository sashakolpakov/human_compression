# An arbitrary-rank reduction of the older finite-group geometry classifications

Status: the independent thin-geometry search is complete, conditional on the imported subgroup catalogue. It recovers exactly the two thin rank-four geometries and excludes all other ranks. Separately, the source-data checks recover 19 rank-three classes and the one thick rank-four representative. Nonthin exhaustiveness has not been independently rederived.

## 1. The target and the source boundary

D. Leemans, *The residually weakly primitive geometries of J2*, Note di Matematica 21(1) (2002), 77–81, is catalogue L104. Its five pages have been read in full. The [primary published PDF](https://ese-journals.unisalento.it/index.php/notemat/article/download/i15900932v21n1p77/585) is archived as `sources/catalogue_L104.pdf`.

Section 1 requires flag transitivity (FT), firmness, residual connectedness (RC), residual weak primitivity (RWPRI), locally two-transitive panels `(2T)1`, and `(IP)2`: each rank-two residue is a partial linear space or a generalized digon. Section 3 reports **8, 19, 3, 0** geometries of ranks 2, 3, 4, and at least 5. The two new thin rank-four geometries have Borel subgroup 1; the third has Borel subgroup D8. The paper explicitly describes maximal-subgroup recursion as its computational method in Section 2. That idea is not claimed as new here.

There are three distinct conditions in the surrounding literature:

* `(IP)2` in L104 is a condition on rank-two incidence residues.
* The subgroup intersection equalities for a marked generating system are algebraic equalities in the subgroup lattice.
* The shadow intersection property studied in L096 is another geometric condition.

None is silently substituted for another below.

The 2004 L096 Appendix B archive was recovered from [Cambridge's primary supplementary archive](https://static.cambridge.org/content/id/urn:cambridge.org:id:article:S1461157000001133/resource/name/S1461157000001133sup002.tar). Its `j2.rk3` file defines 30 subgroup triples and finally selects 24 of them. Its `j2.rk4` file defines only one subgroup quadruple. The 24 rank-three entries fuse to exactly 19 classes under Aut(J2) and type permutations. The quadruple is the thick rank-four geometry; the archive omits the two thin entries. Thus the difference in literal counts does not contradict L104. All original statements are parsed as data with a restricted grammar; no downloaded Magma code is executed.

## 2. Common-torsor recognition in arbitrary finite rank

Let G be a finite group, let I be a finite type set, and let P_i be subgroups containing a fixed subgroup B. For S contained in I set

    K_empty = B,      K_S = <P_i : i in S>  (S nonempty).

Require K_I=G and the following conditions.

1. **Exact meet diagram:** K_S intersect K_T = K_(S intersect T) for every S,T.
2. **Proper panels:** [P_i:B] is at least two.
3. **Typed Helly:** for H_i=K_(I minus {i}), every i and every J contained in I minus {i} satisfy

       intersection_(j in J) (H_i H_j) = H_i (intersection_(j in J) H_j).

   For J empty this is interpreted in the usual trivial way and need not be tested.

Then the cosets of H_i form a firm, flag-transitive, residually connected geometry with Borel B. Conversely every finite-rank firm FT RC coset geometry is recovered in this manner by taking its panel stabilizers P_i.

**Proof.** For the forward direction, the meet diagram identifies intersections of the H_i with the corresponding K_S. Typed Helly says that every coset incident with a partial base flag lies in the orbit of the intersection stabilizer of that flag. Induction on flag size therefore proves that every flag has a common group element in all its cosets, and proves flag transitivity and the geometry axiom together. Every residue is the coset geometry of K_S on the subgroups K_(S minus {i}). Its incidence graph is connected because those subgroups generate K_S; for rank two this says K_{i,j}=<P_i,P_j>, and for larger rank every P_i occurs in a proper deletion. Panel indices give firmness.

For the reverse direction, write K_S for the stabilizer of the complementary base flag. Intersections of flag stabilizers give the meet diagram. Residual connectedness implies that K_S is generated by its maximal parabolic subgroups. Induction on |S| reconstructs K_S from the panel stabilizers, with K_empty=B. Flag transitivity gives typed Helly by the same common-coset argument. ∎

The torsor partition matrices in `torsor_matrix_theory.md` express this theorem without changing its content: subgroup intersections are Hadamard meets and generated joins are Boolean transitive closures. The nontrivial geometric condition remains typed Helly. Matrix notation alone does not remove that test.

The remaining source axioms are exact local tests on the same diagram:

* **RWPRI:** for every nonempty S, some K_(S minus {i}) is maximal proper in K_S.
* **(2T)1:** the action of P_i on P_i/B has two orbits on ordered pairs, equivalently `|B\P_i/B|=2`.
* **(IP)2:** for each pair {i,j}, the coset graph of K_{i,j} on P_i,P_j is a partial linear space or a digon. If the local actions are two-transitive, it suffices to count common neighbours for one pair of distinct points with a common line. Without that hypothesis, all nonidentity B-double-coset representatives in the other panel group must be checked.

The recognition theorem is independent of the particular finite group. Its hypotheses are checkable and cannot be dropped merely because the subgroup diagram looks Boolean.

## 3. A complete maximal-residue augmentation theorem

Suppose a complete list of conjugacy classes of subgroups K of a fixed finite ambient group G is supplied, together with the K-conjugacy classes of their maximal subgroups H. For each such inclusion H0<K0, transport H0 to a specified H whenever possible. If H0^c=H, then all maximal overgroups of H in that ambient conjugacy class are

    (K0^c)^n,      n in N_G(H).

Taking the union over the supplied inclusion classes gives **every** maximal overgroup of H. Duplicates may be removed as actual subgroups.

Indeed any inclusion H<K can be conjugated into some representative K0. Its image of H is conjugate within K0 to one of the supplied maximal subgroups H0. The resulting transporter differs from c by an element normalizing H. The converse is immediate because maximality is preserved by conjugation. ∎

This replaces trials with arbitrary elements of G by trials inside certified maximal overgroups. It is more than an order filter: every candidate extension outside H and inside such a K generates K together with H.

For general B, enumerate locally two-transitive overgroups P of B using the same transport-and-normalizer construction. Classify marked tuples (P_i) modulo N_G(B) and type permutation. Every RWPRI geometry has a maximal parabolic H; deleting its complementary panel produces a lower-rank geometry with the same B. Induction therefore gives a complete augmentation procedure: extend every classified lower-rank tuple inside every maximal overgroup H<K, then apply the recognition tests of Section 2. The search terminates because every augmentation strictly increases the generated subgroup and G is finite.

**Thin specialization.** If B=1, `(2T)1` forces each P_i to have order two; write P_i=<rho_i>. Rank-two RWPRI residues force each product rho_i rho_j to have prime order (including order two for a digon). Every rank-two residue is already a polygon or digon, so `(IP)2` is automatic. Thus only the exact subgroup meet diagram, typed Helly, and the maximal-deletion RWPRI tests remain. This specialization is what the new exhaustive J2 search implements.

At each stage its stabilizer `C_G(<rho_i>)` acts on possible new involutions. Choosing representatives of these orbits is sufficient; it need not equal the larger setwise stabilizer of the old marked tuple. Final equivalence tests try type permutations and successive conjugators in centralizers of the already matched generators. Thus the use of the smaller stabilizer costs time but loses no objects.

## 4. Exact computations and limits

The current scripts are:

* `phase4_thin_j2.g`: an independent exhaustive augmentation from the two J2 involution classes, through all subgroups and until an empty next rank. It imports TomLib's complete subgroup representatives and their maximal subgroup inclusions. This imported completeness is a trust boundary.
* `phase4_parse_j2.py`: a restricted parser for the Cambridge archive, accepting only explicit permutation cycles, subgroup definitions, list appends, and the final numeric reindexing.
* `phase4_verify_j2.g`: arbitrary-rank checks of all source-data triples and quadruples, including exact typed-Helly orbit cardinalities, every RWPRI residue, every panel action, every rank-two partial-linearity test, and reconstruction from panels.

The old rank-two computation in `classification_compression.md` remains independent and has already recovered all eight L104 rank-two classes. The higher-rank work must not be described as a complete reproduction of L104 until both the nonthin completeness search and the thin search are complete. Merely verifying the author's listed representatives would establish existence, not exhaustion or inequivalence.

The rank-five groups have orders 240 or 600. Their absence of extensions proves termination, so the result is not a search stopped at a chosen rank. The four rank-four J2 classes fuse in pairs under Aut(J2), whose order is 1,209,600. The two resulting representatives have maximal parabolic orders

    [150, 12, 60, 60]       and       [150, 150, 60, 60].

These are exactly the two thin geometries displayed in L104. The independently generated representatives are in `phase4_thin_j2_witnesses.g`. `phase4_verify_thin_witnesses.g` checks them through the arbitrary-rank parabolic recognition route and does not load TomLib.

The source-data verifier checks all 30 raw rank-three triples. Precisely its 24 selected entries satisfy every L104 axiom. The other six fail local two-transitivity; two also fail `(IP)2`. Aut(J2)/type fusion gives 19 classes. In terms of original raw indices, the five nonsingleton classes are

    {20,23}, {21,24}, {22,25}, {27,30}, {28,29}.

The single thick rank-four source representative passes all checks and has Borel order 8 and parabolic orders `[6048,6048,384,384]`. Adding the two independently generated thin representatives recovers all three displayed L104 representatives. Existence and inequivalence are certified; the old nonthin exhaustion result is still imported.

The source group is identified by more than its order: `phase4_source_isomorphism.g` gives explicit generator images, and `phase4_identify_source.g` constructs and checks a bijective homomorphism onto TomLib's named J2 group. Both source files use the same source group.

The substantive reduction is a single exact maximal-overgroup search followed by the same finite-rank recognition tests. It independently recovers the entire thin contribution of this older paper, including its higher-rank exclusion, without importing the author's geometry list. It retains the complete 146-class subgroup catalogue and GAP's exact group algorithms. It does not eliminate the group-specific subgroup classification, and it does not by itself compress all nonthin sporadic classifications into a proof without data.

The final five-stage pipeline, script hashes, input hashes, exact counts, and per-entry checks are bound in `phase4_j2_certificate.json` and `phase4_j2_runs.json`. Earlier development runs are excluded. No part of this enumeration is claimed to be formalized in Lean.
