## Mathematical review

With right conjugation, bb^a=(ba)^2. The prescribed order of ab is four,
so the two distinct involutions b,b^a commute and generate V4. This
factor cannot have the printed order 2s except when s=2. The error
also occurs at the positive boundary s=1.

Root read the entire correction, the affected Module A §7.4 and its
center argument. In the source convention a(x,y)=(1-x,y), b(x,y)=(y,x),
c(x,y)=(x,-y), put S(x,y)=(y,x). Direct composition gives

    b^(ac) = ((1,-1), S),
    b^a    = ((1,1), -S),
    b^c    = ((0,0), -S).

Consequently the corrected factors <b,b^(ac)> and <b^a,b^c> have
rotation directions (-1,1) and (1,1). Modulo the lattice generated by
(s,s),(s,-s), both rotations have exact order s. Their reflections have
nonidentity integral linear parts, including at s=1; no faithfulness of
the action on the finite translation set is assumed. The distinct linear
parts and independent lattice directions prove that the factors intersect
trivially. Direct affine multiplication proves cross-commutation.

Conjugation by a or c exchanges the factors. Their product contains no
a or c by its linear parts, and contains no ac by the parity of its
translation coordinates. These observations prove the complement and
the complete internal semidirect product. The square Coxeter normal form
and normal closure of the diagonal translation identify the exact
presentation, rather than just an image of the claimed order. The written
argument works for every positive integer s.

The center proof uses affine translations and the signed-permutation
action, not the erroneous factors. The existing four census and
