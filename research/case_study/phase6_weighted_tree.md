# Weighted trees: one model for every tree-geometry truncation

10 October 2026. A written all-parameter derivation, with finite checks and
independent review recorded separately. This extends the known tree model;
it is not a claim of novelty or a solution to a currently open conjecture.

## Source and exact scope

The target is P. Cara, *Truncations of inductively minimal geometries*,
Discrete Mathematics 267 (2003), 63–74,
[DOI 10.1016/S0012-365X(02)00603-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(02)00603-9).
The primary abstract identifies the classification of truncations with RWPRI
and rank-two intersection, and the description of their residues and diagrams.
At the start of this derivation the full paper had not been obtained. Until
an exact statement comparison is recorded, the results below are a proved
model-level answer to that advertised target, not a whole-paper reduction.

The retained bridge to all inductively minimal pairs is the foundational
1997 classification specified in [the unweighted derivation](phase5_tree_cuts.md).
The transposition-tree model is already in Cameron–Cara (2002), whose full
author manuscript was read there. None of those historical facts is asserted
to be a new discovery here. All geometry statements below refer to the
specified symmetric-group action and the reflexive incidence convention.

## 1. Truncation is contraction with positive vertex weights

Let T be a finite tree. Give its vertices positive integer weights w(v), and
replace each vertex v by a labelled block B_v of w(v) points. Let Ω be the
disjoint union of these blocks. For an edge e, let A_e be the union of the
blocks in the component of T−e away from a chosen root. Set

    G = Sym(Ω),   H_e = Sym(A_e) × Sym(Ω \ A_e).

The geometry Γ(T,w) has the left cosets of H_e as vertices of type e, with
incidence given by nonempty coset intersection. Equivalently its type-e
vertices are the |A_e|-subsets X of Ω, and incidence of different types is

    |X ∩ Y| = |A_e ∩ A_f|.                                  (1)

The proof of (1) compares the four Boolean atoms of two subsets. Because
the base cuts are laminar, pairwise incidence forces the same containment
and disjointness relations. Subtract maximal selected children from each
selected container. The resulting atoms have equal sizes in the base and
target families, so bijections of corresponding atoms combine into one
permutation. Thus every flag is a translate of the corresponding base flag:
the coset system is a flag-transitive geometry, in every rank and for every
positive weighting.

For I⊆E(T), put K_I equal to the product of the symmetric groups on the
unions of blocks in components of the forest (V(T),I). Then

    K_∅ = ∏_v Sym(B_v),     K_I ∩ K_J = K_(I∩J).              (2)

Unique tree paths prove the intersection formula, just as for unit weights.
Also K_I is generated by K_∅ and the groups K_{e}, e∈I: transpositions
inside merged adjacent blocks connect every component. Residues on the
types I are the join of Γ(C,w|C) over the nontrivial components C of that
forest. The other factors act trivially on that residue. In particular the
geometry is firm and residually connected. A panel on edge uv has

    binomial(w(u)+w(v), w(u))                               (3)

elements, hence at least two. Formula (2) is subgroup intersection, not the
stronger geometric shadow intersection property considered below.

Now start with an unweighted tree T₀ and retain only a nonempty set J of
edge types. Contract the edges outside J. Give each quotient vertex weight
equal to the number of original vertices contracted into it. For each
retained edge its bipartition of Ω, and hence its parabolic H_e, is unchanged.
Its J-truncation is exactly Γ(T,w), including incidence and the group action.
Conversely, replace a vertex of weight a by any tree on a unit vertices and
attach its incident edges to chosen vertices of that tree. The expanded
graph is a tree; retaining only the edges between these blocks recovers
Γ(T,w). Therefore positive weighted trees describe **every truncation in
this family**, not just some examples. Different expansions of a block
produce the same truncated geometry with its specified action. This does
not assert uniqueness up to abstract geometry isomorphism of weighted-tree
parameters.

## 2. Primitivity and local two-transitivity

For positive a,b the action of Sym(a+b) on the a-subsets is primitive exactly
when a≠b or a=b=1. Indeed the two-block stabilizer H=Sym(a)×Sym(b) contains
all transpositions inside both blocks. If an overgroup contains a permutation
not preserving the unordered two-block partition, these transpositions and
their conjugates have connected support, and generate Sym(a+b). The only
possible proper overgroup is therefore the unordered block stabilizer. It
strictly contains H precisely when a=b≥2; it is then Sym(a) wr C₂ and has
index two over H. For a=b=1 the original H is already maximal in Sym(2).

Any positive weighted tree with at least two edges has an unbalanced edge.
In fact at most one edge can split its total mass into equal halves: if e
does so, the side of any other edge f away from e is a proper positive-mass
subset of one half and has mass less than half. Apply this to each component
of each residue. Components with at least two edges always have a primitive
type; a one-edge component has one exactly under the preceding a,b test.
It follows that

    Γ(T,w) is RWPRI
      ⇔ for every edge uv, w(u)≠w(v) or w(u)=w(v)=1.          (4)

For necessity take the rank-one residue on uv. For sufficiency every
nonempty residue is a join of components, and a primitive type of any
component is a primitive type for the residue group; the other factors
are merely in the kernel of that action. This also explains why no
faithfulness assumption may be inserted into this step.

The same panel action is two-transitive exactly when min(a,b)=1. For the
reverse implication replace a by min(a,b) using complementation. If a,b≥2,
the stabilizer of an a-subset has other subsets meeting it in a−1 and a−2
points, so is not transitive on the remaining vertices. If min(a,b)=1 the
action is the natural symmetric action. Thus

    Γ(T,w) satisfies (2T)₁
      ⇔ every edge has an endpoint of weight one.            (5)

These formulas include rank one. For global PRI rather than RWPRI, test
each full-tree edge cut for unequal total mass (with the 1+1 exception).
For RPRI perform that same test in every connected subtree. A balanced cut
also gives the exact two-step overgroup chain above, so QPRI has the same
uniform explanation as in the unweighted case.

## 3. Rank-two intersection is a local vertex condition

Two nonadjacent edges give a rank-two residue that is a digon. Two adjacent
edges give a three-vertex path of weights a,b,c, with the middle weight b.
Its geometry has a-subsets and c-subsets of an (a+b+c)-set as its two types,
with incidence meaning disjointness. It is not a digon: there are both
incident pairs and overlapping pairs.

Take distinct c-subsets C,D with |C\D|=d≥1. Their common a-neighbors number

    binomial(a+b−d, a),                                    (6)

interpreted as zero if a+b−d<a. There are C,D with d=1. If b≥2 their number
of common neighbors is at least a+1≥2. If b=1, every such pair has at most
one neighbor. Interchanging a and c proves the corresponding other-side
statement. The rank-two shadow-IP criterion is precisely absence of a
four-cycle or being a digon, as independently derived in the
[shadow audit](phase4_shadow_ip.md). Consequently

    Γ(T,w) satisfies (IP)₂
      ⇔ every vertex of degree at least two has weight one.  (7)

In rank at least two, each edge has a nonleaf endpoint. Hence (7) implies
both (4) and (5). The advertised simultaneous RWPRI/(IP)₂ truncation target
therefore reduces, in these ranks, to **arbitrary positive weights on the
leaves and weight one everywhere else**. Rank-one truncations have vacuous
(IP)₂ and must separately satisfy (4); their two-transitivity is governed
by (5). These small-rank distinctions cannot be silently discarded.

For an adjacent pair with weights a,1,c, complementing the c-subsets
identifies the residue with inclusion between the a- and (a+1)-subsets
of an (a+c+1)-set. Its two valencies are c+1 and a+1. Its girth is six:
there is no four-cycle by (6), and three a-subsets obtained from a fixed
(a−1)-subset by adding one of three different points give a six-cycle.
Thus the basic diagram is the line graph of T, with these explicit
consecutive-level Boolean residues attached to its edges. Nonadjacent
types have the digon residues already described. This determines all
rank-two incidence data without tables of separate constructions.

## 4. Full shadow intersection: consecutive Boolean ranks

For rank at least two, the geometric shadow-IP in all residues holds exactly
when T is a path and every internal vertex has weight one. For rank one
it always holds, independently of primitivity.

Necessity of the weights follows from (7). If T branches, take three edges
at a branching vertex. The corresponding residue has a center of weight
one and three leaves of weights a,b,c≥1. The types A,B,C are respectively
the a-, b-, c-subsets of an (a+b+c+1)-set, pairwise incident by disjointness.
Take two B-subsets X,Y with |X∩Y|=b−1 and put Q=Ω\(X∪Y), so |Q|=a+c.
Their common A-shadow is every a-subset of Q, and is nonempty with more
than one element. This is a permissible x-plus-one-element-flag query.

A flag witnessing that shadow cannot contain an A-vertex, since this would
give at most a singleton A-shadow. It cannot contain a B-vertex incident
with both distinct same-type X,Y. A C-vertex incident with X,Y must lie
inside Q, but incidence with every a-subset of Q would require disjointness
from their union Q, an impossibility because c>0. The empty flag has the
entire A-type as shadow, strictly larger than the a-subsets of Q. Therefore
no witness exists. This obstructs every weighted branch, not only a thin
four-point claw.

Conversely take a path of r edges, with endpoint weights a,c and all r−1
internal weights equal to one. Rooting at an endpoint identifies its cut
geometry with the consecutive ranks

    a, a+1, …, a+r−1       in the Boolean lattice on
    N = a+c+r−1 points.                                    (8)

For any family U of vertices, the common shadow at a retained rank k has
the interval form {Y: |Y|=k, L⊆Y⊆V}, where L is the union of the members
of U below k, and V is the intersection of those above k. A member at
rank k forces a singleton when the shadow is nonempty. A boundary of
size k similarly gives the singleton witness. In all other nonempty cases
|L|<k<|V|. If there is any lower member, |L|≥a, so L is a retained rank;
otherwise L=∅ is omitted. If there is any upper member, |V|≤a+r−1, so V
is a retained rank; otherwise V=Ω is omitted. Consecutiveness in (8) is
what ensures these witnesses exist in the truncation. The remaining set
{L,V} is an incident flag of at most two vertices, incident with every
member of U, and has exactly the required shadow.

Every component of a residue is a subpath whose internal weights are again
one. Repeat the argument there; joins preserve this property because a
shadow depends only on its target factor. Rank-one factors have only the
empty, entire or singleton possibilities. This proves full residual IP.
The Lean BooleanShadow module proves the general interval/witness lemma.
The new [ConsecutiveShadow module](formal/ConsecutiveShadow.lean) proves that
the witnesses can be chosen inside any consecutive interval of proper ranks,
for every finite universe and arbitrary query family in that interval. Both
modules were freshly compiled with the pinned dependencies. Tree realization,
the weighted-claw obstruction and residue joins remain written arguments.

## 5. What is compressed, and what still needs source comparison

One contraction operation and the forest/laminar lemmas determine the
chambers, Borel, all residues, primitivity conditions, local two-transitivity,
rank-two intersection, diagrams and full shadow intersection. The 1998
property tests and 2005 unweighted path criterion are the special case
w≡1. Positive weights incorporate arbitrary truncations with no new group
classification input beyond the retained identification of the original
family. Neither a description-length ratio nor a new-resolution infinity
marker is assigned by this derivation alone.

Finite certificates validate examples and independent implementations; the
all-parameter argument is the written proof above. Source comparison,
verification actually executed, and any subsequent corrections belong in
separate dated records. In particular no inaccessible original theorem,
full automorphism group, abstract isomorphism classification, or previously
open statement is declared solved on the strength of an abstract.
