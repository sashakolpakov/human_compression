# An exact rank-three quotient and powerful eighth powers

10 October 2026. Private structural continuation. This note gives necessary
conditions on a minimum-order counterexample to cyclic subgroup TPP in
finite 2-groups. It does not prove that no such counterexample exists.
Earlier frozen proofs are unchanged.

**Theorem.** Let A=<a>, B=<b>, C=<c> be a violating cyclic TPP triple
in a finite 2-group P of minimum order among all violating 2-groups. Put
K=Phi(P), S=Phi(K), T=Phi(S). Then:

1. K and S have minimum generator number three.
2. K=<a^2,b^2,c^2>=P^2 and S=<a^4,b^4,c^4>=P^4.
3. P/P^4 is the order-32 group described in section 3 below.
4. T=<a^8,b^8,c^8>=P^8 is powerful and has index 256 in P.
5. For every i>=1, Phi^i(P)=P^(2^i)=<a^(2^i),b^(2^i),c^(2^i)>.
   For i>=3, this is an ordered product of these three cyclic subgroups,
   and that ordered multiplication map is bijective.

The last assertion concerns the deep power subgroups, not the original
triple A,B,C. Some cyclic factors in T may be trivial.

The ingredients retained from phase11 are the minimum-counterexample
reductions, the binary square-action lemma, and the powerful-Frattini
obstruction and powerful-group coordinate results. The lower bound
min(|A|,|B|,|C|)>=8 used in section 4 is the frozen small-factor theorem
of [phase10_cyclic_tpp.md](phase10_cyclic_tpp.md). Its published marked
isobicyclic classification input is retained explicitly; the rank-three
obstruction in sections 1-3 does not need that classification.

## 1. An elementary weak-embedding lemma

**Lemma.** Suppose N is normal in a finite 2-group G, with

    N <= G^2,  [N,G] <= N^2.

Then [N,G^2] <= N^4. In particular N is powerful.

Here powers denote subgroups generated by powers; the claim does not
silently identify (N^2)^2 with N^4.

**Proof.** Induct on |G|. The assertion is trivial if N=1. If N^4 is
nontrivial, apply induction in G/N^4: both hypotheses pass to quotients,
and the required commutator containment follows. We can therefore assume
N^4=1.

Choose a central order-two subgroup Z of G contained in the nontrivial
normal subgroup N. Induction in G/Z says that N/Z is powerful. Its
fourth-power subgroup is trivial, so N'<=Z. Thus N has class at most
two and exponent dividing four. Consequently N^2 is elementary abelian
and central in N: the class-two fourth-power formula makes every
commutator have square one, and [u^2,v]=[u,v]^2=1.

For n in N and g in G write n^g=nw with w in N^2. Since w is central
in N and has square one, (n^2)^g=(nw)^2=n^2. Hence G centralizes N^2.
It follows that n^(g^2)=(nw)^g=nww=n. Every square of G centralizes
N, so [N,G^2]=1. Since N<=G^2, N is abelian as well. This proves the
induction and the asserted containment. QED.

## 2. The low-dimensional action obstruction

The minimum-counterexample reductions give

    P=<a,b>=<a,c>=<b,c>,  P/K=C2^2,
    K=<x,y,z>,  x=a^2, y=b^2, z=c^2,
    |<x>||<y>||<z>|=|K|,
    K is not powerful.

The images of a,b,c are the three nonzero elements of P/K, so c=abk
for some k in K. On V=K/Phi(K)=K/K^2 let S0,T0 denote right
conjugation by a,b; put N0=S0-I and M0=T0-I. With d the image of
[b,a] and h the image of k, the square identities from phase11 give

    N0*x=0, M0*y=0, M0*x=N0*d, N0*y=M0*d,
    z=x+y+T0*d+(I+S0*T0)*h.                        (1)

The maps S0,T0 commute and are involutions: their squares and
commutator come from inner automorphisms of K. The vectors x,y,z span
V, whose dimension is at most three.

If dim V<=1, the action is trivial. If dim V=2, any nonidentity
involution has image and kernel, after subtracting I, equal to a line L.
Two commuting nonidentity involutions in GL2(F2) coincide: this follows
by writing the first in its Jordan basis, where its invertible centralizer
consists of itself and I.

Suppose first S0=T0!=I. The first two equations of (1) put x,y in L,
the third puts d in L, and the last puts z in L because S0*T0=I.
This contradicts spanning. If S0=I and T0!=I, the first four equations
put x,y,d in L, and the correction (I+S0*T0)h also lies in L, again
putting z there. The case S0!=I,T0=I is the same with roles exchanged.
Thus in dimension at most two S0=T0=I.

In that case [K,P]<=K^2. As K=Phi(P)=P^2 for every 2-group, the weak
embedding lemma makes K powerful, a contradiction. Therefore

    d(K)=3.                                        (2)

The same weak-embedding argument rules out S0=T0=I in dimension three.
The retained binary action lemma then gives nontrivial actions with
common image under subtraction of I equal to one coordinate line.

## 3. The forced quotient of order 32

Relabel the three cyclic factors so that the common image line is <x>.
Write N0=x*phi and M0=x*psi for nonzero linear functionals. We have
phi(x)=psi(x)=psi(y)=0. Nontriviality of M0 forces psi(z)=1.
Reducing the last identity of (1) modulo <x> gives d=y+z modulo <x>.
The identity M0*x=N0*d=0 forces phi(y)=phi(z); nontriviality of N0
makes both values one. Thus

    S0(x)=x, S0(y)=y+x, S0(z)=z+x,
    T0(x)=x, T0(y)=y,   T0(z)=z+x,
    d=y+z+epsilon*x  for epsilon in F2.              (3)

By (2), |P:Phi(K)|=4*8=32. In Q=P/Phi(K), the subgroup K/Phi(K)
is elementary abelian, and (3) says that its commutators with Q lie in
the fixed central line <x>. In particular Q has class three. If a,b
also denote the induced generators, Q has presentation

    <a,b | a^4=b^4=d^2=1, [d,a]=1, [d,b]=a^2>,
    d=[b,a].                                        (4)

Indeed (3) gives all these relations. Conversely the group defined by
(4) has at most 32 elements: d commutes with a, d^b=d a^2, and
[a^2,b]=1 follows from [d,a]=1 and d^2=1. Collection gives normal
forms a^i b^j d^k with 0<=i,j<4 and 0<=k<2. Since it maps onto the
order-32 quotient Q, the presentation has exactly that order and
identifies Q. No catalogue identification is required.

The equality triple in K can now be squared. Since its three factors
are nontrivial and d(K)=3, their squared order product is |Phi(K)|.
Minimality forces these squares to generate Phi(K); otherwise they
give a violating triple in a smaller generated subgroup. Hence

    S=Phi(K)=<a^4,b^4,c^4>=P^4.                    (5)

For the last equality, one inclusion is by the displayed generators;
the other uses that every fourth power in P is a square of an element
of K, and therefore belongs to Phi(K). Thus the quotient in (4) is
P/P^4, as claimed.

## 4. The next rank and a powerful subgroup of index 256

We use the previously proved lower bound |A|,|B|,|C|>=8. The three
factors <a^4>,<b^4>,<c^4> in S are therefore nontrivial. They generate
S, retain TPP, and have order product |S| by (5) and minimality.

Suppose d(S)<=2. The conjugation image of P on S/Phi(S) is a 2-subgroup
of GL_d(F2), d<=2, and has exponent at most two. Therefore P^2=K
centralizes this quotient. Apply the weak-embedding lemma with ambient
group K and normal subgroup S=K^2: it gives that S is powerful.

The reviewed powerful coordinate argument now contradicts d(S)<=2.
In detail, ordered factorization makes S=<a^4><b^4><c^4>, and equality
of the product of orders makes this map bijective. The same is true
after squaring, using powerful power-subgroup generation. Because all
three factors are nontrivial, |S:S^2|=8. Thus d(S)=3. We have proved

    d(S)=3.                                        (6)

Squaring this equality triple gives

    T=Phi(S)=<a^8,b^8,c^8>=P^8,
    |<a^8>||<b^8>||<c^8>|=|T|,
    d(T)<=3.                                       (7)

The proof is the same generation-by-minimality argument used for (5).
The equality with P^8 follows because each eighth power is a square
of an element of P^4=S. A factor in (7) can be trivial; no nontriviality
assumption is needed for this generation step.

Every 2-subgroup of GL_d(F2), d<=3, has exponent dividing four. One
elementary justification is an invariant full flag: a 2-group acting on
a nonzero F2-space has a nonzero fixed vector by orbit counting; repeat
in the quotient. The action is then upper unitriangular, and
(I+U)^4=I since U^3=0. Consequently P^4=S centralizes T/Phi(T).
Apply the weak-embedding lemma with ambient S and N=T=S^2. This proves
that T is powerful. Finally

    |P:T|=|P:K| |K:S| |S:T|=4*8*8=256.             (8)

## 5. The remaining power layers and the centrality boundary

For i>=3, powerful power-subgroup generation in T gives

    T^(2^(i-3))=<a^(2^i),b^(2^i),c^(2^i)>.

This equals P^(2^i): the displayed generators prove one inclusion;
the other follows by raising each eighth power of an element of P to
the appropriate further power. Powerful Frattini iteration identifies
the same subgroup with Phi^i(P). The equality in (7) and ordered
factorization make the multiplication map for T bijective. Restriction
to powers preserves injectivity, while powerful factorization supplies
surjectivity at each deeper level. This proves the theorem's final
claim with its stated scope.

This does not make a marked top involution central in P. The apparent
shortcut using only three fixed marked coordinates is false. On F2^3,
with columns encoded as bitmasks, take

    S0=(1,5,3),  T0=(6,2,3).

These are commuting involutions, S0 fixes x, T0 fixes y, and S0*T0
fixes z, for x=1,y=2,z=4. They generate C2^2, so its Frattini subgroup
is trivial. Nevertheless no coordinate is fixed by both; their common
fixed line is <x+y+z>. Thus even allowing c's action to be precisely
S0*T0 does not justify the desired fixed-coordinate conclusion.
Additional extension or commutator identities would be required.

The unrestricted 2-group cyclic TPP problem remains open in this work.
The present result supplies exact small-quotient and power-layer
constraints. The new weak-embedding and dimension-two arguments are
elementary; previously reviewed powerful structure and the small-factor
theorem are retained where explicitly indicated. No finite quotient
enumeration or global triple-product injectivity is assumed.

## 6. Focused checks and reproduction

The standalone [Python check](phase12_power_layers_check.py) exhausts
the small linear identities, with [saved output](phase12_power_layers_check.json).
In dimensions 0,1,2 it finds respectively 1,14,168 spanning solutions,
all with trivial actions. In dimension three with the marked basis it
finds 32 solutions and exactly four action pairs: the trivial pair and
the three relabelings of (3). The same script verifies the explicit
counterexample to the weaker fixed-coordinate condition.

The separate [GAP/NQ check](phase12_rank32_check.g), with its
[transcript](phase12_rank32_check.log), constructs presentation (4)
and verifies order 32, class three, elementary abelian Frattini subgroup
of order eight, and the canonical action relations. No SmallGroups
catalogue is used. Run

    python3 phase12_power_layers_check.py
    gap -q phase12_rank32_check.g

The Python script uses only the standard library. The GAP run records
GAP 4.15.1 and NQ 2.5.11. These focused checks support the indicated
finite algebra and presentation calculations; they do not replace the
uniform induction, prove nonexistence of the proposed counterexample,
or constitute a full Lean formalization. No publication-priority claim
is made.
