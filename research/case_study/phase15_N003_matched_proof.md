# Matched forest-and-parity proof: the eight numbered 2002 results

10 October 2026. Proposed compact replacement for Cameron--Cara,
*Independent generating sets and geometries for symmetric groups*,
[author manuscript](https://maths.qmul.ac.uk/~pjc/preprints/igsgsn.pdf).
The exact targets are Theorems 1.1, 2.1, 3.1, 3.2 and Corollaries
2.2--2.4, 3.3: finite-group bounds and the stated n>=7 classifications.
Small-degree exceptions, unnumbered remarks and open questions are excluded.
The longer reviewed derivation is [phase12_N003_reduction.md](phase12_N003_reduction.md).

The common unexpanded background is elementary finite group/permutation
theory, Cayley's labelled-tree formula, and the source's coset criteria
(G1)--(G4). Retain Whiston's two exact inputs: (W1) an independent family
in S_m has size at most m-1, with every family of that size generating S_m;
(W2) for n>=7, every omission subgroup of an independent (n-1)-element
generating family of S_n is intransitive, imprimitive with two-point blocks,
or A_n. W2 is a separate retained consequence of Whiston's proof, with its
CFSG dependence. It is not deduced from W1 here.

Use the source's definitions of mu, mu-prime, independence relative to B,
and A-independent families. Both sides of the measurement use the same
definitions and background. Marker-delimited text below is the replacement
proof; target statements and this accounting preamble are not counted.

<!-- proof:quotient:start -->
For N normal in G, take an independent generating family S and a subset
T whose images minimally generate G/N. For each s outside T choose a
word w_s in T representing sN. The elements v_s=s w_s^-1 in N are
independent: an expression using the other v's would express s using
S minus s. Hence mu(G)<=mu(G/N)+mu-prime(N). If N is abelian, its
G-action is already the action of <T>, since G=N<T>. The same substitution
works for expressions in conjugates of the other v's, proving the stronger
bound with mu-prime_G(N). This proves Theorem 1.1.
<!-- proof:quotient:end -->

<!-- proof:forest:start -->
For a tree T on Omega and an edge set F, its transpositions generate
K_F=product Sym(C), over the components C of (Omega,F): path conjugation
gives all transpositions in each component. Unique paths give
K_F intersect K_D=K_(F intersect D). Equivalently, intersecting stabilizers
of edge cuts gives the symmetric groups on the forest left by those cuts.

Distinguish an edge d with transposition s. In any forest containing d,
the even subgroup of K_F is generated by s tau_e for e in F minus d:
pair every even word using tau_e tau_f=(s tau_e)^-1(s tau_f), with
tau_d=s. Thus a subset I of a marking
{s} union {(s tau_e)^epsilon_e:e not d}, epsilon_e=+1 or -1, generates
K_(F(I)) if s is retained and K_(F(I)) intersect A_n otherwise, where
F(I) consists of d and its retained edge labels. Inverting a generator
does not change its cyclic subgroup. These formulas give the full subset
intersection property, including the empty subset. They also prove
generation and independence for both this marking and the ordinary edge
marking: omitting an edge splits its cut, while omitting s leaves only even
permutations. This supplies both converses and Corollary 2.3.
<!-- proof:forest:end -->

<!-- proof:classification:start -->
First exclude W2's imprimitive case. If an omission subgroup H is
transitive on 2m points in C2 wr S_m, m>=4, its block kernel N is a
subspace of F2^m. The quotient bound gives
2m-2<=mu(H)<=mu(H/N)+mu-prime_H(N)<=2m-2.
The last bound is m-1 for each term: W1 handles H/N; if N is the full
space, an H-independent basis would make every hyperplane obtained by
omitting a basis vector invariant, hence every basis line invariant,
contradicting the nontrivial block action. Proper N has dimension <=m-1.
Equality and W1 force H/N=S_m. A nonconstant vector and a transposed
copy generate a vector e_i+e_j; its S_m-span contains the even-weight
subspace. Any S_m-independent family in N therefore has at most two
members, since the quotient by that subspace has dimension one; constants
alone give at most one. Now 2m-2<=m+1, impossible for m>=4.

At most one omission group G_i can be A_n: its omitted generator is the
unique odd generator. Label this possible exception 1. For each i>1
choose e_i={x,s_i(x)} crossing G_i-orbits; choose e_1 from any point moved
by s_1. Every e_j, j not i, stays within a G_i-orbit. Thus no cycle can
contain e_i for i>1, and no cycle consists of e_1 alone. These n-1 edges
form a tree. Its two components after deleting e_i are exactly the two
G_i-orbits. Hence every s_j preserves cut i when j not i. The forest
formula gives s_1 in K_{e_1} and s_i in K_{e_1,e_i}. Therefore s_1 is
the transposition on e_1. If every G_i is intransitive, choose e_1
crossing its own omission orbits too; then s_i is the edge transposition
for every i. Otherwise G_1=A_n, and the other generators are nonidentity
even elements of S3 or C2 times C2 on the two edges. They are precisely
(s_1 tau_(e_i))^+1 or ^-1. This proves Theorem 2.1.
<!-- proof:classification:end -->

<!-- proof:counts:start -->
Cayley gives n^(n-2) ordinary markings. A twisted set has a unique odd
member s=(a b). Multiplying its other actual generators h by s recovers
the edges of a unique tree with distinguished edge (a b). Inverting a
3-cycle switches its edge endpoint between a and b; contraction of (a b)
is unchanged, so every such switch preserves being a tree. Conversely
each distinguished-edge tree gives the actual set {s} union {s tau_e}.
Thus there are (n-1)n^(n-2) actual twisted sets. On identifying inverse
3-cycles, only the pair {a,b} and the tree on the n-1 contracted blocks
remain, giving binomial(n,2)(n-1)^(n-3). This proves Corollary 2.2.
<!-- proof:counts:end -->

<!-- proof:overgroups:start -->
For H=Sym(A) times Sym(A-complement), any overgroup element that fails to
preserve the unordered two-block partition supplies, by conjugation, a
transposition joining the original blocks. Together with H it generates
S_n. Thus H is maximal unless the blocks are equal and have size at least
two; then its sole proper overgroup is their unordered stabilizer, with
index two over H. The 1+1 case is H=1 in S2 and is maximal. A tree has
at most one balanced cut: relative to one such cut, the far side of any
other cut is a proper subset of one half.

For n-1 generators independent relative to B, W1 supplies ordinary
generation and the classification above. Their omission groups are the
tree-cut stabilizers, with A_n replacing the distinguished cut in the
twisted case. Independence forces B into every maximal omission group.
A pendant edge other than d gives a point stabilizer, so B fixes a point.
At a balanced cut, B must lie in its only proper overgroup; elements
outside the cut stabilizer interchange the halves and fix no point.
Hence B lies in that omission group too. The full subset intersection
property gives B=1. This proves Corollary 2.4 even with mu-prime(S_n,B).
<!-- proof:overgroups:end -->

<!-- proof:rank:start -->
Write H_J=intersection of H_j for j in J, with H_empty=G and H_I=B.
Source (G1) lets us choose s_i in H_(I minus i) outside H_i. All other
s_j and B lie in H_i, so this family is independent relative to B;
rank<=mu-prime(G,B). Under RWPRI, (G4) selects, for each proper J,
a maximal H_(J union k) in H_J. Induction from J=I then gives
H_J=<B,s_i:i not in J>, because s_k lies outside that maximal subgroup.
This proves rank<=mu(G,B) and strong relative independence, Theorem 3.1.
At rank n-1 in S_n it gives B=1, the two classified markings and all
their parabolics. Their panel groups are cyclic of order two or three,
so their generators are unique up to inversion. Changing the base chamber
conjugates the marking; renaming types permutes it. This gives the forward
and uniqueness assertions in Theorem 3.2.
<!-- proof:rank:end -->

<!-- proof:geometry:start -->
Root the tree; its edge cuts away from the root are laminar. Cosets of a
cut stabilizer identify with subsets of the cut's size. Pairwise incidence
fixes intersection sizes, forcing exactly the original nesting and
disjointness. In each selected container, subtract its maximal selected
children, including Omega as the top container. These atoms have the same
sizes as the base atoms. Bijections on atoms assemble to one permutation
realizing the entire partial flag. This proves typed Helly (G3).
The forest formula gives (G1) and residual generation (G2). A leaf in a
nontrivial forest component gives a maximal point stabilizer in its
symmetric factor, proving RWPRI for the ordinary family.

For the twisted family contract d to a vertex of weight two. Its other
parabolics are exactly the resulting cut stabilizers; add the parity
parabolic A_n. Every intersection of cut parabolics contains the odd
transposition s. A common flag representative can therefore have its
parity adjusted without changing the cuts, proving (G3) also when that
type is present. The forest/sign formulas give (G1) and (G2).
For RWPRI, if parity is unfixed, fix it: the stabilizer has index two.
If parity is fixed, the residue group is the even part of a product of
forest symmetric groups, with d still in the forest. A nontrivial
component away from d admits a leaf cut with maximal point stabilizer:
the d-component supplies parity correction, so projection onto the chosen
factor is the full symmetric group. If only the d-component is nontrivial
and a cut remains, it has at least three vertices and a leaf other than d;
its alternating group acts primitively, including A3's regular action of
prime degree. This proves (G4) and completes Theorem 3.2.
<!-- proof:geometry:end -->

<!-- proof:arbitrary:start -->
Finally take any source S_n coset geometry of rank n-1, without assuming
RWPRI. The independent family chosen above generates S_n by W1, and the
relative bound gives B=1. Put G_i=<s_j:j not i>; then G_i<=H_i<S_n.
By the overgroup calculation, H_i=G_i except possibly at one balanced
cut k, where H_k could be its unordered stabilizer W_k. Every other
tree cut has a nonempty side wholly in one k-half; its stabilizer cannot
interchange those halves. Hence W_k intersect G_j<=G_k for those j.
In every panel intersection, either k is omitted or another such j is
present: n-1>=6 and there is at most one parity type. Replacing W_k by
G_k therefore leaves every panel unchanged, giving panel i equal to
<s_i>. Repeated residual generation (G2) recovers H_J=<s_i:i not in J>
and hence all H_i=G_i. The geometry is one already proved RWPRI.
This proves Corollary 3.3.
<!-- proof:arbitrary:end -->
