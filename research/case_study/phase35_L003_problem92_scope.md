# Exact source scope of Problem 9.2

Date: 2026-10-10. This is a fresh reading of the cached primary PDF, not a new current-literature search.

The inspected source is Fernandes–Leemans–Weiss, *Hexagonal extensions of toroidal maps and hypermaps* (2018), [author/institutional PDF](https://ria.ua.pt/bitstream/10773/23862/1/2018DFW.pdf). The private PDF is bound by its SHA-256 in the adjacent JSON. Printed page numbers and PDF page numbers agree for the locators below.

On page17, section8 gives the rotation presentation with eight ordered local parameters. Page18, Table8, rows5–8 gives the four regular examples with ordered local parameters $((3,0),(2,0),(1,1),(s,0))$, for $s=2,3,4,5$, and full group orders $1296,13824,165888,5184000$. The table caption identifies its entries as finite universal hypertopes.

Page18, the paragraph immediately preceding section9, describes those rows as suggesting an **infinite family of finite hypertopes** and explicitly leaves both group size and structure unspecified. The numbered Problem9.2 on page19 asks whether the four rows are part of an “infinite family of hypertopes” with the displayed ordered parameters and an integer $s\geq2$.

The numbered problem itself does not include the word *finite*, the word *universal*, or the phrase *for every integer*. The preceding paragraph supplies a finite-existence context; the table and wider section also emphasize universal examples. These different assertions should therefore be stated separately:

| Assertion | What the present work establishes |
|---|---|
| Infinitely many finite regular hypertopes with the displayed exact local parameters, extending the known examples | Yes. The all-parameter finite construction is stronger than existence of an unbounded set of parameters. If literal inclusion of the four particular table entries matters, choose the source's examples at $s=2,3,4,5$ and the new finite examples thereafter. |
| A finite regular example for every integer $s\geq2$ | Yes, by the tensor construction and the retained small bases. |
| An actual universal marked regular hypertope for every integer $s\geq2$ | Yes, by the separate all-parameter universal corollary and the attained local upper bounds. |
| Finite universal groups for every integer parameter | No. The universal group is already certified infinite at $s=6$ and at all its positive multiples. |
| Exact orders and structures of all universal groups | Not established. |

Thus it is accurate to report a construction answering the finite-existence reading of Problem9.2, with a precise all-parameter statement. It would be inaccurate to advertise universal finiteness or a uniform formula for the universal automorphism groups.
