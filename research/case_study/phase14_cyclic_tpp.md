# Cyclic subgroup TPP in finite 2-groups

10 October 2026. Private proposed proof for independent review. Frozen
phase13 and earlier artifacts are unchanged. This proof retains standard
powerful-group structure, but no finite-group catalogue, marked-isobicyclic
classification, order cutoff, or quadratic-map enumeration. Publication
priority and full formal verification have not been established.

**Theorem.** If A, B, C are cyclic subgroups of a finite 2-group P with
the triple product property, then

    |A||B||C| <= |P|.

The triple product property (TPP) means that xyz=1, with x in A, y in B,
z in C, forces x=y=z=1. It does not say that the ordered multiplication
map A x B x C -> P is injective. That stronger assertion is never assumed.

We give the complete reduction and square-layer argument below. Earlier
local proofs are cited for provenance, but the argument here spells out
their needed elementary content. The new uniform proof uses only the
published powerful-group statements in section 1 as external structural
input.

## 1. Retained powerful-group structure

A finite 2-group H is powerful if [H,H]<=H^4. Here H^r denotes the
subgroup generated by r-th powers; H^1=H. We retain:

* Power subgroups of a powerful group are powerful, and
  (H^(2^i))^(2^j)=H^(2^(i+j)).
* If H=<h1,...,hd> is powerful, then H^(2^i) is generated by
  h1^(2^i),...,hd^(2^i).
* For that given generating list, H=<h1>...<hd> as an ordered set
  product. The list need not first be replaced by a specially chosen
  basis.

These are the 2-group forms of Lubotzky--Mann,
[*Powerful p-groups. I. Finite groups*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(87)90211-0),
J. Algebra 105 (1987), 484--505: statements 4.1.1, 4.1.3, 4.1.9 and
4.1.11, printed pages 499--501. Exact primary-source reading and the
arbitrary-generating-list issue were recorded in the frozen
[phase11 powerful proof](phase11_powerful_frattini.md), section 2,
and its independent audit. No third-party full text is included here.

We use the following direct consequence. Suppose H is powerful and has three marked
generators h1,h2,h3, all of order 2^r>=2, and their cyclic subgroups
give a bijective ordered product on H. Then

    H/H^2 -> H^(2^(r-1)),
    h H^2 |-> h^(2^(r-1))                            (1)

is a linear, automorphism-equivariant isomorphism. Indeed the given
coordinate bijection restricts injectively to every simultaneous power
subgroup, and the retained factorization supplies surjectivity. The
source and target in (1) consequently both have dimension three, with
the specified marked bases. The adjacent-layer maps are linear because
L/L^4 is abelian for each powerful power subgroup L; thus squaring
induces a well-defined surjection L/L^2 -> L^2/L^4. Composing these
maps proves (1). They all commute with automorphisms. For r=1 the map
is the identity on the elementary abelian group H.

## 2. Three elementary action lemmas

### 2.1 Weak embedding

If D is normal in a finite 2-group H, D<=H^2, and [D,H]<=D^2, then

    [D,H^2]<=D^4; in particular D is powerful.        (2)

Induct on |H|. If D^4 is nontrivial, apply induction in H/D^4. It
therefore suffices to handle D^4=1. If D=1 there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise choose a central order-two subgroup Z of H contained in D;
every nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite p-group meets its center.
Induction in H/Z makes D/Z powerful, so D'<=Z. Thus D has class at
most two and exponent at most four. The class-two power formula then
gives D' of exponent at most two, and D^2 is elementary abelian and
central in D.

For d in D and h in H, write d^h=dt with t in D^2, using right
conjugation d^h=h^-1 d h. Since t is central in D and t^2=1,
(d^2)^h=(dt)^2=d^2. Therefore H centralizes D^2. It follows that
d^(h^2)=(dt)^h=dtt=d. Every square of H centralizes D, proving (2)
in the reduced case. Since D<=H^2, this also makes D abelian there.
This proves the induction. No equality of iterated and ordinary power
subgroups of an arbitrary group is used.

### 2.2 Binary square compatibility

Let V be spanned by x,y,z over F2, with dimension at most three. Let
T_a,T_b be commuting involutory linear maps. Suppose d,f in V satisfy

    T_a x=x, T_b y=y,
    T_b x=x+(I+T_a)d,
    T_a y=y+(I+T_b)d,
    z=x+y+T_b d+(I+T_a T_b)f.                       (3)

If dim V<=2, both maps are the identity. If dim V=3, at least one
marked basis vector is fixed by both maps. More precisely, either
both are the identity, or their images after subtracting I coincide
in a fixed coordinate line.

Here is an algebraic proof. Put E=T_a-I and F=T_b-I. In dimension
at most two, two commuting nonidentity involutions coincide, with
image and kernel a single line. If both are that involution, (3)
places x,y,d,z in that line. If only one is nonidentity, the same
equations again place x,y,d,z in its line. Both contradict spanning.
Dimensions zero and one are immediate.

In dimension three, if one map is the identity, equations (3) make
the other fix x,y,d,z, so it too is the identity. If the maps
coincide, (3) again fixes the whole basis. Otherwise E,F are distinct
nonzero rank-one square-zero maps. They satisfy EF=FE=0: a nonzero
commuting product would force their image lines to coincide, but
both maps kill that common line. If their image lines were distinct,
both would lie in each kernel. The kernels would be the same plane;
(3) would put x,y,d,z in it, a contradiction.

Thus E=u*phi and F=u*psi with a common nonzero image u, and distinct
nonzero functionals phi,psi annihilating u. Write

    r=psi(x)=phi(d), s=phi(y)=psi(d).

The pairs of functional values on x,y,z are respectively

    (0,r), (s,0), (r+s,r+s).

Since the functionals are independent and x,y,z are a basis, (r,s)
is not (0,0). In each of the other three cases their common kernel
is one coordinate line. This is also <u>, proving the assertion.
This is the analytic lemma recorded in the
[phase11 binary proof](phase11_abelian_frattini.md), with its
low-dimensional extension from phase12; no enumeration is needed.

### 2.3 A marked three-dimensional module

Let a finite 2-group P be generated by a,b, with c in ab Phi(P).
Let V be a three-dimensional F2 P-module with basis x,y,z, where a
fixes x, b fixes y, and c fixes z. If no marked basis vector is fixed
by all of P, then Phi(P) acts trivially on V.

To prove this, a 2-subgroup of GL_3(F2) has an invariant complete
flag, by successively choosing nonzero fixed vectors in quotient
spaces. Hence the image R lies in a conjugate UT_3(F2), which is
dihedral of order eight. An element of order four in GL_3(F2) is
regular unipotent and has a unique fixed line. Since a finite 2-group
has a nonzero common fixed vector on an F2-space, that line would
also be globally fixed. Thus none of a,b,c can have an order-four
action: its individually fixed marked vector would be globally fixed.

If R were nonabelian, it would be dihedral of order eight. The
involutory images of a,b generate it, so their product has order
four. Both elements in its coset modulo Phi(R) have order four.
Surjections of finite p-groups map Frattini subgroups onto Frattini
subgroups, so c in ab Phi(P) would also have order-four action,
a contradiction. Therefore R is abelian and generated by involutions.
It is elementary abelian, and Phi(P) acts trivially, as claimed.
This argument does not require V to be a power subgroup or require
an exact choice of matrices for its action.

## 3. Minimum-counterexample reductions

Suppose the theorem fails, and choose a violating triple in a group P
of smallest possible order. Write A=<a>, B=<b>, C=<c>.

Each factor avoids Z(P). For example, if a nontrivial central subgroup
Z were contained in A, the quotient triple (A/Z,BZ/Z,CZ/Z) would
have TPP: a product in Z can be absorbed into the A-factor before
testing TPP in P. Its order-product ratio would be unchanged, since
B and C meet Z trivially. This contradicts minimality.

Every pair of factors generates P. If H=<A,B> were proper, minimality
applied to A,B,C intersect H would give

    |A||B||C intersect H|<=|H|.

The C-orbit on the cosets of H has size |C:C intersect H|<=|P:H|.
Multiplication yields the desired inequality in P, a contradiction.
The other pairs are the same. Thus P/Phi(P)=C2^2 and a,b,c have its
three distinct nonzero images. In particular c in ab Phi(P).

The violating ratio is exactly two. In the maximal subgroup containing
A, the intersections with B and C are their square subgroups. The
resulting triple has half the original ratio in a smaller group. A
ratio at least four would violate minimality; a violating ratio is a
positive power of two. Therefore

    |A||B||C|=2|P|.                                 (4)

Every cyclic factor has order at least four. For a center-free cyclic
factor D, choose a conjugate whose involution is different. Then
D intersect D^g=1. Both lie in a maximal subgroup containing D,
which is normal in P. Hence |D|^2<=|P|/2. For cyclic orders
alpha>=beta>=gamma, (4) gives 4alpha<=beta*gamma, and so gamma>=4.

Put K=Phi(P)=P^2. The nontrivial cyclic groups generated by
a^2,b^2,c^2 retain TPP and have order product |K|. They generate K:
otherwise they violate the inequality in their smaller generated
subgroup. At this point no product-map bijectivity has been inferred.

On V=K/Phi(K)=K/K^2, the images x=a^2, y=b^2, z=c^2 span V.
Conjugation by a,b gives commuting involutions T_a,T_b, since their
squares and commutator act by inner automorphisms of K. In P/K^2,
the image of K is elementary abelian. With d=[b,a] and c=abf,
the identities b^a=bd, a^b=ad^-1 and (ab)^2=a^2 b^2 d^b give exactly
the square equations (3). Thus section 2.2 applies whenever the
appropriate dimension has been established.

## 4. Normal powerful coordinates and the first two ranks

We first record a short observation. Suppose D is a nontrivial normal
powerful subgroup of this P, generated by a^m,b^m,c^m, with bijective
ordered cyclic coordinates. Then all three coordinate orders are equal
and nontrivial. To see this, let 2^r be the largest coordinate order.
The characteristic subgroup D^(2^(r-1)) is elementary abelian, and
its basis consists exactly of the nontrivial marked top powers, by
powerful generation and the restricted coordinate bijection.

If there is one largest factor, its top involution generates a normal
subgroup of order two, making it central in P. If there are two,
relabel them a^m,b^m. On their two-dimensional top space, the image
of P=<a,b> is a 2-subgroup of GL_2(F2), of order at most two.
The image of a fixes its own marked vector, and that of b fixes the
other. If both images were nontrivial, they would coincide and fix
both basis vectors, hence be trivial. If one is trivial, the vector
fixed by the other is fixed by all of P. Both cases contradict the
center-free cyclic factors. All three factors must therefore be largest.

The subgroup K is not powerful. If it were, retained ordered
factorization and the order equality in section 3 would give a
coordinate bijection. Restricting it to squares shows |K:K^2|=8.
Section 2.2 then supplies a globally fixed marked vector in K/K^2.
The preceding observation makes all three coordinate orders equal;
the isomorphism (1) maps that fixed coordinate to the nonidentity
top involution of one original factor. It would be central in P,
a contradiction.

If d(K)<=2, section 2.2 makes P act trivially on K/K^2. Weak
embedding (2), with D=K=P^2 and H=P, would make K powerful.
Therefore

    d(K)=3.                                        (5)

Put S=Phi(K)=K^2. Squaring the equality triple in K now gives a TPP
triple generated by a^4,b^4,c^4 whose order product is |K|/8=|S|.
It generates S by minimum order, just as above. Some of these factors
are allowed to be trivial at this stage.

Suppose d(S)<=2. The P-action on S/S^2 is a 2-subgroup of
GL_d(F2), d<=2, and has exponent at most two. Thus P^2=K
centralizes this quotient. Applying weak embedding with ambient K
and D=S=K^2 shows that S is powerful. If S=1 then K^2=1,
making K elementary abelian and contradicting its nonpowerfulness.
Otherwise the order equality and powerful factorization give a
coordinate bijection on S. The normal-powerful observation forces
all three coordinates to be nontrivial (and equal). Restricting this
bijection to squares then gives |S:S^2|=8, contrary to d(S)<=2.
Consequently

    d(S)=3.                                        (6)

In particular a^4,b^4,c^4 are all nontrivial, so the original cyclic
orders are at least eight. This is an analytic deduction; it uses no
marked-isobicyclic classification.

## 5. A powerful third layer

Put N=Phi(S)=S^2. By (6), squaring the equality triple in S gives
the TPP triple generated by a^8,b^8,c^8, with order product |S|/8=|N|.
It generates N by minimality. The factors may again be trivial.
In particular d(N)<=3.

Every 2-subgroup of GL_d(F2), d<=3, has exponent dividing four.
An invariant full flag makes it upper unitriangular, and (I+E)^4=I
for a strictly upper triangular three-by-three matrix E. Thus a^4,
b^4,c^4 act trivially on N/N^2. These elements generate S, so
[N,S]<=N^2. Weak embedding, now with ambient S and D=N=S^2,
shows that N is powerful.

If N=1, then S is elementary abelian of order eight. Its marked
basis is a^4,b^4,c^4, the top involutions of the original cyclic
factors. None is globally P-fixed, since those factors avoid Z(P).
The marked-module lemma of section 2.3 says that K=Phi(P)
centralizes S. Therefore squaring defines a well-defined P-equivariant
map

    K/S -> S, kS |-> k^2.

Indeed S is central in K and has exponent two. Section 2.2 gives
a globally fixed marked coordinate of K/S; its square is the
corresponding marked vector of S, which is not globally fixed.
This contradiction excludes N=1 without an order-256 computation.

We may therefore assume N is nontrivial. Powerful ordered
factorization and its order equality supply bijective marked
coordinates. The normal-powerful observation in section 4 makes
their orders equal and nontrivial. The map (1) identifies N/N^2,
as a marked P-module, with its actual top involution subgroup U.
None of the three marked vectors is globally fixed, by center-freeness.
Apply section 2.3 to U. It follows that K acts trivially on U,
and hence also on N/N^2:

    [N,K]<=N^2.                                    (7)

## 6. Two equivariant square maps give the contradiction

Write W=N/N^2 and V=S/N, each with its marked basis from the
eighth and fourth powers, respectively. The square map

    q: V -> W,  sN |-> s^2 N^2                     (8)

is well defined and P-equivariant. For well-definedness, if t in N,
then replacing s by st changes its square only by t^2 and a
commutator in [S,N], both in N^2 by (7), since S<=K.
It maps each marked vector to the corresponding marked vector.
No linearity or multiplicativity of this square map is asserted.

No marked vector of V can be globally fixed: its image under (8)
would be a globally fixed marked vector of W, which has just been
excluded. On V, a,b,c fix their own respective marked basis vectors.
Apply the marked-module lemma again. It yields

    [S,K]<=N.                                      (9)

Now S^2=N and (9) make the second square map

    r: K/S -> S/N,  kS |-> k^2 N                   (10)

well defined and P-equivariant. Explicitly, S/N is central in K/N
and has exponent two, so changing the representative k by an element
of S leaves its square unchanged modulo N.

By (5) and section 2.2, some marked coordinate a^2 S, b^2 S or
c^2 S of K/S is fixed by all of P. Its image under (10) is the
corresponding marked coordinate a^4 N, b^4 N or c^4 N of S/N.
Equivariance makes that coordinate globally fixed, contradicting the
conclusion after (8). No minimum counterexample exists. QED.

## 7. Consequences and precise trust boundary

Combining this 2-group theorem with the reviewed odd-prime theorem
in [phase11_odd_nilpotent.md](phase11_odd_nilpotent.md) gives the
cyclic-TPP size bound for every finite nilpotent group. Decompose the
three cyclic factors into their Sylow components, apply the p-group
bound to each component triple, and multiply. This is not a theorem
for arbitrary nonnilpotent groups or arbitrary noncyclic subgroup triples.

The retained geometric reduction in
[phase3_order_bound.md](phase3_order_bound.md), section 3, then gives
the lower bound of Hou--Feng--Leemans
[*Existence of regular 3-hypertopes with 2^n chambers*, Conjecture 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09034).
For the source regular hypertope, P=G' has order |G|/8. The three
square-rotation cyclic subgroups have TPP and orders half the three
distinguished pair orders. Their product bound is exactly the
conjectured hypertope order inequality. Flag transitivity and the
typed Helly identity remain actual inputs to that geometric adapter;
subgroup intersection alone does not suffice.

The present proof uses neither the earlier SmallGroups certificates nor
the marked-isobicyclic classification. It also does not use the
order-32 presentation, the exact matrices of the phase13 action, a
quadratic-map enumeration, or the exploratory order-2048 extension
probes. Its structural external input is precisely the powerful-group
theory listed in section 1, in addition to elementary finite-p-group
facts (Frattini/Burnside basis, central normal intersections and Sylow
decomposition). All coordinate bijections are justified by powerful
factorization plus a separate equality of orders.

This remains a private proposed proof pending final independent review.
It is not a fully formalized group proof, external peer review, or a
determination of priority. Any Lean certificate for the finite-action
or equivariant-transport portion has that narrower scope.
