# Homology covers and infinitely many minimal regular covers

Private manuscript, phase211, 10 October 2026. The general conjecture
about arbitrary polytopes is not proved here. The main result below gives a
uniform construction from a finite group and three explicit tests on a finite
surface. Its applications strengthen the previous four 2-primary family
results from two minimal covers to infinitely many. Nothing in this manuscript
changes the frozen source presentations or their previously reviewed maps.

## 1. The question and the kernel order

Let W_n be generated by involutions r_0,...,r_(n−1), subject only to
[r_i,r_j]=1 when |i−j|>1. For a polytope P, finite or infinite, let K be the
kernel of its flag action. A regular polytopal cover corresponds to a normal
subgroup N of W_n contained in K for which W_n/N has the intersection property.
The standard correspondence between string C-groups and regular polytopes is
used throughout. Kernels are compared with the marks fixed.

The question in Cunningham–Pellicer, *Open problems on k-orbit polytopes*,
Problem5, and Cunningham, *Chiral polytopes whose smallest regular cover is a
polytope*, §4, is whether a nonpolytopal W_n/K can nevertheless have a unique
minimal regular polytopal cover. Neither formulation imposes finiteness in its
general question. F005, Cunningham–Hubard2025, supplies criteria for specified
mixes and ends with the distinct question of recognizing when W_n/K itself is
polytopal. Its mix criterion does not turn an intersection of kernels into a
join of kernels.

There is a useful extension of the finite order argument in phase44. The union
of a nonempty chain of admissible normal subgroups is admissible. To see this,
write the intersection property in the following equivalent form, with
W_I=⟨r_i:i∈I⟩:

    u∈W_I, v∈W_J, uv^−1∈N
       ⇒ there is w∈W_(I∩J) with uw^−1∈N.                 (1)

If uv^−1 lies in the union, it lies in one member of the chain, which supplies
w. The union is normal and still contained in K. The marks remain nontrivial
because no r_i belongs to a polytope's flag-action kernel. Zorn's lemma
therefore supplies an admissible maximal overgroup of every admissible N.
Consequently every regular polytopal cover has a minimal regular polytopal
quotient over P, without a finiteness assumption. A unique minimal cover would
be covered by every regular cover: every admissible N is contained in some
maximal member, and uniqueness identifies all these maximal members.

Let J_all be the join of all admissible normals, without restricting their
indices. The following are equivalent: unique minimal cover; a greatest
admissible normal; admissibility of J_all. Thus J_all=K suffices for
multiplicity when W_n/K is not polytopal. We do not prove this equality in
general. For finite P, phase44 gives the separate analogous statement using
only finite-index admissible normals. The construction below produces finite
witnesses, so its conclusions do not depend on classifying infinite covers.

## 2. A surface belonging to any suitable group of rank four

Let G=⟨s_0,s_1,s_2,s_3⟩ be a finite string group generated by involutions.
Assume that its four generators are distinct and nonidentity, the groups generated
by the pairs02,03,13 have order four, and q=ord(s_1s_2)≥2. These conditions
hold for monodromy groups of polytopes. They also hold for some groups which
are not monodromy groups of any polytope; that distinction will matter below.

Form a finite polygonal complex X as follows. Its vertices are G. Join g to
gs_i by an undirected edge of color i. For each left coset of each of

    G02, G03, G13, G12,

attach one disk along its alternating two-color cycle. The first three kinds
of disks are squares; the last kind has 2q sides. Each such cycle has distinct
vertices. Each edge lies in exactly two disks, and the link of each vertex is
the four-cycle

    0 — 2 — 1 — 3 — 0.

Thus X is a connected closed surface. It need not be orientable. With g=|G|,
its cell numbers and Euler characteristic are

    V=g,  E=2g,  F=3g/4+g/(2q),
    χ(X)=g/(2q)−g/4.                                  (2)

The surface is orientable exactly when the Cayley graph is bipartite. Indeed,
the four-color cyclic link fixes a local orientation at every vertex; crossing
any edge reverses the choice. A consistent choice is therefore exactly a
two-coloring of the vertices. Equivalently, the assignment s_i↦−1 is a
homomorphism G→C₂. This assertion can also be checked directly by orienting
the disk boundaries and propagating their orientations across shared edges.

For I⊆{0,1,2,3}, let X_I be the component through the identity of the
subcomplex consisting of edges of colors in I and disks whose two colors
lie in I. Its vertex set is G_I. Define

    S_top = X012 ∪ X123,
    S_left = X01 ∪ X12,
    S_right = X12 ∪ X23.                               (3)

All three subcomplexes are connected. The components of X012∩X123 are the
G12 cosets contained in G012∩G123, each with its attached disk. Hence they
are disjoint closed disks. The components of X01∩X12, and of X12∩X23,
are single edges of color1 and2, respectively. In particular all three
intersections have zero first homology over every field. The component
through the identity is the indicated common marked subcomplex in each
case. These assertions use actual cells, not merely equalities of orders.

## 3. A homological intersection lemma

Let X be a connected cell complex and let A,B be connected subcomplexes
with a common base vertex x. Let C=A∩B, C₀ its component through x, and
S=A∪B. Fix a field F. Suppose

    H₁(C;F)=0,
    H₁(S;F) → H₁(X;F) is injective.                    (4)

Consider the connected cover of X corresponding to the kernel of
π₁(X,x)→H₁(X;F), and choose a lift of x. Let A~,B~,C~₀ be the components
over A,B,C₀ through that lift. Then

    A~ ∩ B~ = C~₀.                                    (5)

Here and below a homology cover over F_p means this ordinary topological
cover; it is finite when H₁(X;F_p) is finite-dimensional.

**Proof.** The inclusion from right to left is immediate. Suppose y~ belongs
to the left side and maps to y∈C. Choose paths α in A and β in B from x to y
whose lifts end at y~. Then [αβ^−1]=0 in H₁(X;F). Injectivity in (4) says
that this class is already zero in H₁(S;F).

The connecting homomorphism of the Mayer–Vietoris sequence for A∪B sends
this class to the difference between the components of y and x in H₀(C;F).
Distinct components give independent basis elements over every field.
Consequently y lies in C₀. Choose a path γ in C₀ from x to y.

The pair of cycles (αγ^−1,−βγ^−1) in A and B maps to zero in H₁(S;F).
Mayer–Vietoris and H₁(C;F)=0 imply that
H₁(A;F)⊕H₁(B;F)→H₁(S;F) is injective. Both cycles therefore have zero
homology in their own subcomplexes. In particular α and γ have the same
lift endpoint in the homology cover of X. The lift of γ lies in C~₀, proving
(5). The argument proves equality of all lift vertices, and then equality of
the relevant colored subgraphs and cells follows. ∎

This proof includes both possible obstructions. It separates different
components of C, and it excludes extra elements in the common covering
kernel. Testing only the projected intersection would not suffice.

## 4. Computing the injectivity condition on a surface

Let X be a connected closed surface with a finite polygonal cellulation and
S a connected proper subcomplex. The components below are components of
X\S, equivalently of the complement of a sufficiently small regular
neighborhood of S.

Over F₂, the map H₁(S;F₂)→H₁(X;F₂) is injective if and only if X\S is
connected. Over a field of odd characteristic it is injective exactly in
the following cases:

* X is orientable and X\S is connected;
* X is nonorientable and every component of X\S is nonorientable.

The nonorientable case does **not** follow from connectedness alone.

**Proof.** The exact sequence of the pair gives

    H₂(X;F) → H₂(X,S;F) → H₁(S;F) → H₁(X;F).         (6)

A relative cellular two-cycle assigns coefficients to the disks outside S.
For each edge outside S, the coefficients on its two incident disks must
cancel along that edge. Propagating these equations gives one free
coefficient on each orientable component of X\S. On a nonorientable
component it imposes a=−a. Thus, in odd characteristic such a component
contributes zero, whereas in characteristic two every component contributes
one dimension. This calculation also proves the claimed interpretation of
complement components: disks are adjacent precisely across edges outside S;
passing through a vertex outside S adds no further component because all
its incident edges are outside S.

For orientable X, H₂(X;F) has dimension one, and its fundamental cycle
restricts nontrivially to every complementary component. Its image in (6)
is the diagonal line of these coefficients. In characteristic two the same
statement holds whether or not X is orientable. Surjectivity onto relative
two-cycles is therefore equivalent to having one complementary component.
For nonorientable X in odd characteristic, H₂(X;F)=0. Surjectivity is then
equivalent to the absence of orientable complementary components. Exactness
of (6) proves every assertion. ∎

There is an integral version when X is orientable: the same calculation
over Z gives injectivity of H₁(S;Z)→H₁(X;Z) if and only if the complement
is connected. We do not infer an integral analogue in the nonorientable
case from the odd-characteristic calculation. Its two-torsion must be
handled separately; no such analogue is used here.

## 5. A general construction of coprime covers in rank four

**Theorem.** Let G and X satisfy §2. Suppose either

(a) X is orientable and each of the three complements X\S in (3) is
connected; or

(b) X is nonorientable and every component of each of those complements
is nonorientable.

For every odd prime ℓ there is a finite marked string C-group H_ℓ mapping
onto G with elementary abelian kernel

    ker(H_ℓ→G) ≅ F_ℓ^b,
    b = 2−χ(X) in case(a),  b = 1−χ(X) in case(b).     (7)

In case(a) the assertion also holds for ℓ=2. In either orientability case,
connectedness of all three complements separately suffices for ℓ=2,
with b=2−χ(X). The middle product has order exactly q in every case.

If G is not a string C-group and is the monodromy group of a polytope P,
then P has infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic finite minimal regular
polytopal covers. For each odd prime ℓ one can choose such a minimal cover
whose degree over G is ℓ^e with 1≤e≤b. The universal kernels of any two
constructed covers for distinct odd primes have product K. In particular
the join of all finite admissible universal kernels is K.

**Proof.** Put

    W(q)=W₄ / ⟨⟨(r₁r₂)^q⟩⟩,
    Π=ker(W(q)→G).

The usual path presentation identifies π₁(X,1) with Π. Explicitly, paths
in the Cayley graph spell words in the r_i; backtracking accounts for
r_i², and the attached disks account for exactly the three nonadjacent
commutations and the middle relation. Closed paths at the identity spell
precisely the kernel of the map to G. Homotopies across the disks give
exactly those defining relations. More formally, Reidemeister–Schreier
rewriting uses one copy of each defining relation at every Cayley vertex.
The involution relations are backtracks. Starting a two-color relator at a
different vertex on the same polygon only conjugates that polygon's boundary
word, so one disk per polygon gives the same normal subgroup of the graph's
fundamental group. The exact pair orders ensure that those polygons have
the stated embedded boundaries. No asphericity assertion is needed.

Let Φ_ℓ=ker(Π→H₁(X;F_ℓ)). It is characteristic in Π, hence normal in
W(q). Set H_ℓ=W(q)/Φ_ℓ. Then H_ℓ maps onto G with kernel H₁(X;F_ℓ).
The lifted Cayley graph is precisely the connected homology cover of X.
The cell count (2), H₀(X;F_ℓ)=F_ℓ, and the two-cycle calculation in §4
give (7), so H_ℓ is finite of order |G|ℓ^b.

Apply §4 and then §3 to the three pairs in (3). Their common components
have zero first homology by §2. We obtain the three marked intersections

    (H_ℓ)012 ∩ (H_ℓ)123 = (H_ℓ)12,
    (H_ℓ)01 ∩ (H_ℓ)12 = ⟨r₁⟩,
    (H_ℓ)12 ∩ (H_ℓ)23 = ⟨r₂⟩.                       (8)

The involution and string relations hold because their cycles bound
disks or are backtracks. Adjacent generators remain distinct because
their images in G are distinct. Rank-two groups generated by distinct
involutions have the required intersection property. The last two
equalities in (8) therefore prove that both rank-three ends are string
C-groups; the first proves the full rank-four intersection property by
recursive recognition. The middle relation holds upstairs and has exact
order q downstairs, so its order upstairs is exactly q.

Each H_ℓ covers P by the retained marked group/polytope correspondence.
Choose a minimal regular polytopal quotient Q_ℓ over P by finite descent.
Its group order divides |H_ℓ|, and the map to G makes its covering degree
a divisor of ℓ^b. Degree one would make G polytopal, contrary to the
hypothesis. The degree is therefore ℓ^e for 1≤e≤b. Distinct primes give
different degrees, hence different marked kernels and nonisomorphic
rank-preserving regular polytopes. A regular-polytope isomorphism can
always be followed by an automorphism aligning base flags, so forgetting
the base flag introduces no additional identifications here. Minimality
also excludes covering relations between two different Q_ℓ.

For two distinct primes, let N_ℓ,N_m be the kernels of W₄→H_ℓ,H_m and
K the kernel of W₄→G. The finite index [K:N_ℓN_m] divides both
ℓ^b and m^b, hence is one. Thus N_ℓN_m=K. This proves the claimed join
statement and also provides the direct coprime witness of multiplicity.
The minimal descendants themselves likewise have coprime nontrivial
degrees, although their precise exponents are not selected. ∎

The theorem is a conditional result for arbitrary finite marked groups of
rank four, not a theorem that its surface condition holds for every
polytope monodromy group. It imposes no source-family presentation,
centrality of a defect, local injectivity, prescribed group order, or
semisimplicity assumption on an existing cover.

## 6. The common group of order256 and all four 2-primary families

Retain the literal permutation model D from frozen phase109. Its exact
orders are

    |D|=256, |D012|=|D123|=64, |D12|=8,
    |D012∩D123|=16, ord(s₀s₁)=ord(s₁s₂)=ord(s₂s₃)=4.

The cellulation X has (V,E,F)=(256,512,224), is orientable, and has
χ=−32. A direct enumeration of the literal cells gives:

| Subcomplex S | Number of its disks | Disks in its complement | Components |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| X012∪X123 | 46 | 178 | 1 |
| X01∪X12 | 1 | 223 | 1 |
| X12∪X23 | 1 | 223 | 1 |

This is a finite combinatorial certificate for the hypotheses of §5. The
proof therefore gives a cover \bar H_ℓ→D with kernel F_ℓ^34 for **every**
prime ℓ. Checking only ℓ=3,5 is unnecessary for this conclusion.

The restrictions over D012 and D123 have kernels of dimension nine.
Indeed each indicated subcomplex has 64 vertices,96 edges,24 disks, hence
Euler characteristic−8 and first Betti number nine. It is an orientable
surface with nonempty boundary. Its H₁ injects into H₁(X), by the
injectivity of H₁(S_top) and Mayer–Vietoris; the common disks have zero H₁.
The middle restriction is injective, since X12 is a disk.

Each end has eight boundary circles, the cosets of D01 or D23. In an
orientable surface with at least two boundary components, each boundary
circle represents a primitive nonzero homology class: the subgroup
generated by the boundary classes has the single relation that their
oriented sum is zero. This also follows by arcs from one selected boundary
to each of the others and their intersection numbers. Consequently the
lift of each boundary cycle has order ℓ in the deck group. It follows that
the exact type of \bar H_ℓ is {4ℓ,4,4ℓ}.

We use the following form of the coprime pullback lemma, which does not
require a choice of a splitting. Let π:G→D be an epimorphism of marked
groups with kernel N, and let E→D have elementary abelian ℓ-kernel V,
where ℓ does not divide |N|. In E×_D G let H be generated by the matching
marked lifts. For every mark set I, H_I is the full fiber product
E_I×_(D_I)G_I. For if n∈N∩G_I, choose m∈⟨n⟩ with m^ℓ=n and lift m
to (v,m)∈H_I. The first coordinate lies in V, so (v,m)^ℓ=(1,n).
Thus H_I contains all standard N∩G_I; it then contains every pair in
the fiber product by adjusting one actual lift. This proves the claim.
If E is a string C-group, its parabolic intersections consequently give

    H_I∩H_J = (N∩G_I∩G_J) H_(I∩J),

where N is identified with the standard subgroup in the fiber product.
Hence H has the intersection property precisely when

    N∩G_I∩G_J ≤ G_(I∩J) for every I,J.                (9)

For the four frozen 2-primary source families Γ₂,Γ₃,Γ₄ and dualΓ₄,
phase109 proves the marked quotient G→D for every stated source parameter.
More explicitly, put q=2^β with every integer β≥5. The four rows have

| Family | p | r |
|---|---:|---:|
| Γ₂ | 8 | 8 |
| Γ₃ | 2^(β−1) | 2^(β−1) |
| Γ₄ | 8 | 2^(β−1) |
| dual Γ₄ | 2^(β−1) | 8 |

Both signs in the source presentations vary independently. All four sign
choices are included, with the dual interpreted by reversal of the marks.
The source supplies the chiral polytope of type {p,q,r}; phase109 proves
that its full monodromy group G has order4pqr.
It proves that N is a 2-group, that both maximal ends of G are string
C-groups, and that N∩G012∩G123=N∩G12. Therefore (9) holds within
each end and at the top intersection, and recursive recognition applies.
No new parameter interpolation is being made here.

For every odd prime ℓ, the resulting H_ℓ→G has kernel F_ℓ^34 and both
end kernels F_ℓ^9; the medial kernel is trivial. If G comes from a source
polytope of type {p,q,r}, its type is exactly

    {ℓp,q,ℓr}.

This follows from the orders of the two coordinates in the fiber product:
the outer orders are lcm(p,4ℓ)=pℓ and lcm(r,4ℓ)=rℓ because p,r are
powers of two divisible by four, and the middle order is lcm(q,4)=q.

**Corollary.** Every polytope in those four source families has infinitely
many pairwise nonisomorphic finite minimal regular polytopal covers.
For each odd prime ℓ one may choose one of degree ℓ^e over its smallest
regular maniplex cover, with 1≤e≤34. Any two different prime choices have
no common regular polytopal quotient over the original polytope. The last
assertion follows because a common quotient would have degree dividing
two coprime powers, hence degree one and the nonpolytopal group G.

This strengthens the prior two-prime conclusion. It does not identify the
minimal descendants, classify every minimal cover, or establish that the
general multiple-minimal-cover conjecture is now settled. The source's
atomic classification, presentations, chirality and group/polytope
correspondence remain borrowed inputs with the attributions in phase109.

## 7. A nonorientable application and a failed stronger claim

As a separate test of the general method, reconstruct the Tomotope using
the four literal automorphisms in Monson–Pellicer–Williams, *The Tomotope*,
Example4.3. The Wythoff cosets in Definition3.1 give four vertices, twelve
edges, sixteen triangles and eight facets of two kinds. Enumerating the
two orbits of flags gives192 flags; changing one entry gives the four
monodromy generators. Their generated permutation group has order18432.
Its middle order is12 and the surface X is nonorientable. It has

    (V,E,F)=(18432,36864,14592), χ(X)=−3840.

For S_top,S_left,S_right the complements have respectively14090,14591,
14591 disks. Each is connected and nonorientable. Thus §5 gives elementary
abelian covers of degree ℓ^3841 for every odd prime ℓ. This recovers
infinitely many minimal covers by the same construction, without the
source's separate geometric family of covers. The source already proves
the Tomotope's multiplicity in Theorem5.9; there is no claim of a newly
solved open problem for that example.

The surface criterion is not automatic for independent marked generators.
Three quotients of D, of orders32,64,64, still have independent marked
involutions and orientable surfaces. Their top complements each have two
components, with disk counts(9,9), (17,17), (17,17). The corresponding
mod-three homology covers retain respectively4,8,8 unwanted projected
intersection elements. An exhaustive search of core-free flag stabilizers
in these three groups found no polytopal action. They are therefore
obstructions to an overstrong statement about arbitrary marked groups,
not counterexamples to the question about monodromy groups of polytopes.
The exploratory finite search must be independently verified before this
last nonexistence assertion is used as a theorem.

The remaining rank-four problem is now concrete: prove that a genuine
polytope monodromy group has the required complement behavior, or produce
a genuine monodromy example that fails it and change the construction.
Neither the three failed quotients nor the successful Tomotope settles
that problem. Higher rank requires an additional construction; the
four-color closed-surface argument does not extend by renaming ranks.

## 8. Verification and attribution

The uniform arguments in §§3–6 are written proofs, not Lean theorems.
The file `formal/CoverKernelChains.lean` verifies six algebraic theorems:
closure of the word intersection property under directed unions and chains;
existence of maximal admissible overgroups at arbitrary index; uniqueness
implying a greatest admissible kernel; and the two coprime-power membership
and join statements. Its definition of admissibility includes normality,
containment in the fixed target kernel and the word intersection property.
The marks' nontriviality and the group/polytope correspondence are written
premises of the application, rather than part of this abstract Lean interface.
All six theorems use only Lean's standard reported axioms.

The exact finite cell counts and dual-graph component and
orientation calculations concern the literal permutation inputs. Their
role is to verify the hypotheses of a theorem valid over every indicated
field, rather than to extrapolate from a few characteristics.

The standard ingredients are the homology-cover correspondence, cellular
homology, Mayer–Vietoris, the exact sequence of a pair, recursive string
C-group recognition, and the marked group/polytope correspondence. The
specific surface construction, complement test and its use in the four
families are the proposed contribution of this packet. Historical
priority has not been established. A bounded search of later primary
literature did not locate a proof or counterexample to the fully general
minimal-cover question; this is a report of the search, not a definitive
statement that no such work exists.

The reproducible command is `python3 phase211_check.py --out /path/to/NEW`.
It runs the exact finite calculations under optimized Python, compiles the
six Lean theorems against the pinned library, and rejects a version of the
coprime-power theorem with its hypothesis weakened to a mere inequality.
The finite controls reject an identity generator and detect a disconnected
complement. The accompanying scope record identifies every retained source
and proof dependency. No source classification or additional manuscript is
credited as compressed by the present extension.
