## A complete description of the image's translation rank

Let H be the group generated by the three certified integer-voltage
transformations. Forgetting voltages maps H onto a finite permutation group
Q. The independent checker exhausts this permutation group and finds
|Q|=216. During the enumeration it retains a lifted representative T_q for
every q∈Q, with T_1=1.

The kernel L consists exactly of pure translations, identified with integer
vectors in Z^216; composition adds these vectors. For each q and each
positive generator a, form

    T_q a T_(qa)^(-1).

These648 loops have identity permutation. They generate L: insert the
chosen representatives between successive letters of a kernel word and
telescope. A negative-generator loop is the inverse of the positive loop
based at its neighboring quotient element. Thus the648 positive loops
already suffice. This is the elementary finite-index Schreier argument,
retained standard group theory rather than a new enumeration principle.

The checker computes every resulting integer vector, then performs exact
rational elimination. Six original rows are independent and all648 rows
reduce to their span. Consequently the generated integer lattice has rank6.
A finitely generated subgroup of a free abelian group is free abelian, so

    1 → Z^6 ≅ L → H → Q → 1,        |Q|=216.

This describes the certified monomial image. It does not prove that P_6
itself is isomorphic to H or that the extension splits. No claim about the
lattice's saturation in Z^216 or the index in its rational span is needed.
The six independent rows and all elimination pivots are in the report.

## Arbitrarily large finite quotients, with an explicit limit

For every integer m≥1, the subgroup mL is characteristic in L and therefore
normal in H. The quotient H/mL is a finite quotient of every P_s with6|s,
of exact order

    |H/mL|=216m^6.

Indeed, its translation kernel is L/mL≅(Z/mZ)^6 and its permutation quotient
is still Q. Every finite subgroup of H embeds in H/mL, since its intersection
with the torsion-free subgroup mL is trivial. This does not establish that
the images of the source's parabolics have the advertised orders, that their
intersections and coset Helly conditions hold, or that the finite quotients
give the required hypertopes. Those are separate remaining obligations.

The finite-quotient consequence makes the limitation of the infinite-image
obstruction concrete. The source groups cannot all be finite, yet they
have finite quotients of unbounded order. Neither conclusion alone settles
the geometric problem or earns an infinity-resolution marker.
