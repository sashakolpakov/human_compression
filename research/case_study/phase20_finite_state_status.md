# Fixed-class finite-state theorem: bounded literature check

10 October 2026. **Priority unverified; no named open problem claimed
solved.** This note records a bounded search, separately from mathematical
review of the proposed theorem. Twelve queries were made; their exact
strings and reading boundaries are in the accompanying JSON.

No matching complete theorem about bounded windows, deletion-kernel
states and rational counting across all ranks was located in those
results. That negative search result does not establish novelty.

The proof retains the regular-polytope/string-C-group correspondence.
Schulte's [2017 primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02297),
*Regular Incidence Complexes, Polytopes, and C-Groups*, was checked in
the [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.02297): Lemma 3.1 identifies subflag
stabilizers, and Theorems 4.1–4.2 with section 5 give the correspondence
used here. The proof's coset Helly step is a consequence of this
established theory, not a new correspondence. Reading was limited to
these passages and front matter; no complete reduction of that paper
is claimed. The publisher chapter endpoint was inaccessible in this
check, so the preprint supplies the exact reading version.

The [2011 Fields Institute abstract](https://www.fields.utoronto.ca/programs/scientific/11-12/discretegeom/talks/october.html)
on nilpotent regular maps announces low-class classifications and a
finiteness result. The [earlier status note](phase18_class_three_status.md)
records the associated later publication and unpublished-class-three
lead. Those map results require comparison of the marked full group
with orientation-preserving groups before claiming an overlap or a
new low-class classification. The new theorem concerns all ranks at
each fixed class, with the actual involutory marking retained.

The fixed-excess corollary uses the already reviewed canonical quotient
and lower-central descent. It is restricted progress toward the regular
two-group component of the [2006 Problem 30](phase18_schulte_weiss_intake.md).
It does not characterize unmarked groups, cover the chiral cases or
the mixed orders in that question, or settle the whole problem. No
matched proof-length ratio or infinity marker is assigned.
