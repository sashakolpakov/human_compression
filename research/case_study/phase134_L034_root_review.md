## Review of the new common argument

The typed-coset Helly transfer is valid. Pairwise intersecting cosets map
to a family with a common base point b. For any pair, start with their
actual common lift x, whose base is z. Base IP puts bz⁻¹ in the base
parabolic on the intersected label set. A marked preimage h in that
parabolic makes hx a common lift at b. This step proves the necessary
pairwise fiber intersections without assuming surjectivity of an arbitrary
subgroup intersection.

Right multiplication by a chosen lift of b identifies the fiber with the
kernel. Its intersections with the given cosets become cosets of products
of full kernel coordinates. Such a coset fixes some coordinates and leaves
the others free. Pairwise intersection makes every repeated coordinate
constraint agree, supplying a common tuple. Neither an individual
nonabelian factor nor an infinite group causes a problem. IP in the total
group is not a premise of this transfer argument.

The geometric corollary originally inferred that generators were
involutions merely from their involutory base images. That inference is
false. A singleton support with C4→C2 satisfies U.SK but lifts the marker
to order four. The mandatory erratum adds involutory local markers and
proves their tuple markers have order exactly two. Alternatively, support
size at least two makes proper-singleton injectivity force this condition.
These alternatives are not equivalent. The L034 application has three
pair supports and marked dihedral groups, so it satisfies the correction.

## Review of the full presentation and structure

In the affine square group, the two required commutators are translations
by (0,−2) and (−2,−2), with the source's x⁻¹y⁻¹xy convention. Their
signed-permutation orbits generate exactly the stated square and diagonal
lattices. Thus adding the parity relator identifies the full quotient;
checking a finite image's order alone is not used.

In the square case the two coordinate-axis dihedral subgroups commute,
intersect trivially and are interchanged by the coordinate swap. In the
diagonal case their translation directions are (1,1) and (1,−1),
independent modulo q times their span. Their reflection linear parts are
distinct, so their intersection consists only of translations and is
trivial. The complement generated by a and c has no nontrivial intersection
with their product: linear parts exclude a and c, and the parity of the
translation coordinates excludes ac. This parity condition is unchanged
by every vector in the diagonal lattice, including odd q. These arguments
prove the stated semidirect decompositions for all q≥2.

Each of the three dihedral attachments has the exact marked quotient and
is injective on proper parabolics. The base square group has the required
deletions and geometry by the retained A arguments. U.SK and its antichain
corollary therefore give the full group H, its order and exact pair orders;
the corrected geometric corollary supplies flag transitivity as well as IP.

Every relator in the source presentation holds in H. Conversely, in the
presented group the three indicated pair-power cyclic subgroups are normal:
their endpoints invert them and the remaining marker centralizes them.
Their normal product has order at most the product of the three cyclic
bounds; commutation is not assumed to obtain this inequality. Quotienting
by that product leaves exactly the affine square presentation and the
parity relator. The resulting sharp upper bound equals |H|, proving that
the marked epimorphism onto H is an isomorphism. Only then are the three
subgroups identified with the pure coordinate kernels, proving their
independence, commutation and exact extension structure.
