## Written proof review

**The downward quotient criterion.** For a quotient with kernel N and arbitrary subgroups A,B, K(d) proves

`q(A) ∩ q(B) = q(A ∩ B)` if and only if `N ∩ AB = (N ∩ A)(N ∩ B)`.

Here AB is a set product; neither subgroup is assumed normal. Factoring an element of the left side through a common representative proves one direction, and factoring `ab^{-1}` through the two kernel intersections proves the other. The special cases N contained in one end and injectivity on both ends give exactly the quotient interfaces used later. This is a downward criterion and is not confused with K(a)'s upward transport.

**Finite universal quotient Q5.5.** For a forest T with d vertices, m edges and excess k, the involution/commutation group modulo `gamma_(k+3)` is finite. The lower-central graded Lie ring is generated by degree-one elements of order two, giving elementary 2-layers and the explicit bound `2^(sum_(j=1)^(k+2) d^j)`. Finiteness is therefore a written theorem, not an inference from NQ terminating. Boolean deletion maps establish its intersection property. Its class-two quotient is the exact order-`2^(d+m)` forest group, with each edge coordinate present, and the kernel restriction is `K ∩ U_S = gamma_3(U_S)`.

The preceding forest/excess theorem Q5.3 gives `|gamma_3(G)|=2^k` for every target. Strict descent in a finite p-group implies the necessary class bound, so every ordered marked target is uniquely a quotient U/N with N normal in U, N contained in K, and `[K:N]=2^k`. Conversely, K(a) tests the actual intersection property from the small quotient K/N and its precise parabolic restrictions. Quotient intersection properties are not assumed automatically. Ordered marked isomorphism means equal kernels; the source's generator-set convention is then the path-reversal action.

The full supporting Section 11 was reread, including the graded support argument and the deepest-edge-cut proof of Q5.3. The latter uses actual intersection in G and the written descending lift; it does not import intersection properties for arbitrary intermediate quotients. The k=0 boundary in Q5.5 is proved even though the four required census instances have positive k.

**Invariant ladder Q5.6.** The U-normal index-two subgroups of M are exactly the preimages of hyperplanes in `M/(Phi(M)[M,U])`. Completeness of repeated descent does not require M or K to be abelian: the finite 2-group action on the odd-sized set of nonzero Frattini-dual characters has a fixed character. This supplies a U-normal index-two step containing any required terminal N, and induction supplies the complete ladder. The program enumerates these hyperplanes and deduplicates actual subgroups. Its binary encoding is used only after checking relative pc orders two.

**Central rank-three reduction.** K(d) supplies the two quotient lemmas. The central candidates in the product of the dihedral ends are explicitly derived. A central product of half-rotations forces the individual half-rotations to be central. The remaining mixed middle-reflection candidate would replace the middle Frattini-basis generator by a central involution and force the group to be elementary abelian, contradicting nondegeneracy. Thus a central involution gives a safe quotient, possibly by a half-rotation. Degenerate products are handled separately, and descent ends at C2^3. No rank-three group of order four is asserted.

**Extension presentations.** The proof bounds the three possible adjacent-order lift patterns and the binary lifts of the complete defining relators. Both adjacent orders cannot double, since that would place the kernel involution in both ends in conflict with the intersection property. For a successful central extension, the displayed complete presentation maps onto G and its central quotient is the given base; the order sandwich proves equality. It does not claim that every binary assignment gives a successful extension. The finite program checks all assignments relevant to the source example, including collapses.

**Centers and the exceptional class.** The square-center proof really uses the shared affine square model: endpoint reversal followed by simultaneous conjugation by b gives the displayed marking. Direct affine calculations establish the translation and half-turn words. The fixed translations and faithful linear actions give the generic centers; the two small-parameter exceptions are treated separately. The finite smooth-square classification and its structural dihedral decompositions remain named inputs.

For the exceptional infinite universal group, the precise published marked decomposition `(W1 × W2) ⋊ C2^2` is retained. The Frattini characters of a finite target locate its kernel inside W1 × W2 and preserve the stated complement. The two extra-relation presentations are then independently shown finite of order 1024, have the intersection property, and match the two exhaustive census objects with the required exact ends. Unequal end orders prevent an unintended reversal identification. Neither the infinitude nor the full decomposition is inferred from these finite calculations.
