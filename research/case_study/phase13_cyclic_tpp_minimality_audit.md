## 2. The largest-power-layer lemma

The exponent assertion uses powerful power generation: if the largest
marked order in N is 2^r, then N^(2^r) is generated by three identity
elements. A marked element of that order supplies the reverse inequality.
Thus U=N^(2^(r−1)) has exponent two and is powerful, hence elementary
abelian. It is normal in P because it is characteristic in the normal N.

The marked top powers form a basis for an actual subgroup U, not merely
for a quotient. Their ordered multiplication is injective by restriction
of the assumed bijection on N, and is onto U by the retained powerful
coordinate theorem. A shorter marked cyclic factor contributes the
identity, whereas a maximal one contributes a nonzero basis vector.

With one maximal factor, U has order two, so its nonzero element is
fixed by all of P. With two maximal factors, relabeling them as a and b
is legitimate because every pair generates P. The conjugation image in
GL_2(F_2) is a 2-group and therefore has order at most two. The image of
a fixes its own marked top vector and that of b fixes the other. If both
images are nonidentity they coincide and fix the two basis vectors, a
contradiction. Otherwise one image is the identity and a marked vector
is fixed by both. In either case the vector is central in P and belongs
to one of the three forbidden cyclic factors.

This proves that all three marked orders in nontrivial N must be equal
and nontrivial. It uses no linearity of an arbitrary power map. It also
does not assert the false analogous fixed-coordinate statement for
arbitrary two-group actions in dimension three.

## 3. Restricted minimum order: every reduction stays in the class

Take a minimum-order violating triple having at least one factor of
order at most eight. The relevant condition is on the marked triple;
there is no need to assert that an ambient group property is hereditary.

For a nontrivial central D contained in a factor A, quotient absorption
proves TPP for the three images. The other factors meet D trivially,
because TPP implies pairwise trivial intersection. Consequently the
factor-order product and ambient order both lose exactly |D|, preserving
the violating ratio. Every image has order at most its original order,
so the small-factor condition survives.

If H is a proper subgroup generated by two factors, the third is replaced
by its intersection with H. Each factor is either unchanged or smaller.
Minimality therefore applies inside H, and the elementary bound
|C:C∩H|≤|P:H| transfers its inequality back to P. This forces pairwise
generation in the restricted minimum just as in the unrestricted proof.

The maximal-subgroup ratio reduction replaces two factors by their
squares and keeps the third. Again the small-factor condition survives.
It yields ratio exactly two, distinct nonzero Frattini classes and the
same center-disjointness conclusions. The elementary conjugate-cyclic
bound first gives minimum factor order at least four. The phase-ten
structural theorem is an unconditional theorem about these three marked
Frattini classes and center-disjoint factors; its application does not
require an unrestricted choice of minimum. It excludes order four, so
the smallest factor in the restricted minimum has order exactly eight.

Finally, every generation-by-minimality step for the square, fourth-power
and eighth-power triples replaces all factors by subgroups and passes
to their generated subgroup. Any smaller violating triple so obtained
still has a factor of order at most eight. These are all the uses of
TPP minimality in the retained power-layer argument. Its weak-embedding
lemma has its own unrestricted elementary induction, independent of a
TPP counterexample. The powerful-Frattini obstruction uses only the
already obtained marked relations, genuine powerful coordinates and
center-disjointness; no additional class-closure assumption enters.

Hence the complete phase-twelve conclusion N=P^8, |P:N|=256, with N
powerful and bijective marked coordinates, applies to this restricted
minimum. If N is trivial, the retained finite theorem at order 256
contradicts the violation. Otherwise the new lemma requires every marked
eighth-power factor to be nontrivial, contradicting the order-eight
factor. This establishes the uniform theorem, not merely a statement
about unrestricted minimum counterexamples.

## 4. Unrestricted structure and consequences

For an unrestricted minimum, the same order-256 input makes N nontrivial.
Equality of the three nontrivial eighth-power orders then forces equality
of the original cyclic orders, each 2^e with e≥4. The ratio-two identity
gives |P|=2^(3e−1), so the first possible order is 2048. This excludes
every counterexample at orders at most 1024 by taking a minimum one.
It does not prove the inequality separately for every unequal-order
triple: such a subclass was not shown stable under the reductions.

The hypertope translation uses its actual flag transitivity, typed Helly
identity and subgroup intersection property. Its derived subgroup has
index eight, and the cyclic square-rotation factors have half the three
pair-product orders. Thus a pair order at most sixteen falls under the
uniform theorem, and a hypertope group of order at most 2^13 has derived
group of order at most 1024. No factor of two is lost in either statement.
