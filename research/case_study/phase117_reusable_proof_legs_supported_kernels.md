# A supported-kernel interface for two construction branches

Private mathematical draft, 10 October 2026. This is a proposed reusable proof body with explicit attachments to the fixed twelve-source contract. It is not yet a complete shorter replacement. The entire body and every attachment must be charged before a saving is asserted.

Let I be finite. A marking (r_i:i∈I) generates its group; G_L denotes the subgroup on L⊆I. A deletion is a homomorphism killing specified markers and fixing the others. The existing K(a) transports IP through a marked quotient, and Q2.2 proves IP for a complete Boolean family of deletions. Both hypotheses remain visible below.

**Supported-kernel theorem SK.** Let B have IP and exact deletion retractions p_S:B→B_S for a finite family of distinct supports S⊆I: p_S(r_i)=r_i for i∈S and p_S(r_i)=1 otherwise. For each support let E_S have an S-marking (e_i), and let q_S:E_S→B_S be a marked epimorphism with kernel N_S. In the full fiber product

\[
 H=\{(b,(x_S)_S):q_S(x_S)=p_S(b)\text{ for all }S\}
\]

give generator i its base marker and, in coordinate S, e_i if i∈S and 1 otherwise. Require that q_S be injective on every proper-support parabolic (E_S)_U, U⊊S.

Then the marked generators generate all of H. Its projection π:H→B and every marked parabolic satisfy

\[
 \ker\pi=\prod_S N_S,\qquad
 \ker\pi\cap H_L=\prod_{S\subseteq L}N_S,
 \qquad H_L\twoheadrightarrow B_L.
 \tag{SK1}
\]

Consequently H has IP. If the groups are finite, then

\[
 |H_L|=|B_L|\prod_{S\subseteq L}|N_S|.
 \tag{SK2}
\]

If B has all Boolean deletions, then every E_S and H do also; hence H has every initial/final FAP in the retained geometric interpretation. If N_S=γ_c(E_S) for all S and B has class <c, then kerπ=γ_c(H). If the supports form an antichain, projection gives H_S≅E_S. Finally, if I=L∪M, B=B_LB_M and every support is contained in L or M, then H=H_LH_M.

*Proof.* Let H′ be the subgroup generated by the displayed tuples. It maps onto B, and H′_S maps onto E_S. Induct over the supports by inclusion. For n∈N_S choose a word w in the S-markers whose E_S-value is n. Its B-value is q_S(n)=1. In another coordinate T its value belongs to (E_T)_(S∩T) and has trivial q_T-image; if T is not contained in S, proper-support injectivity therefore makes that value trivial. The only other possible coordinates lie in N_T for T⊊S. By induction their pure copies already lie in H′_T⊆H′_S, so removing those values isolates n inside H′_S. Thus H′ contains ∏N_S=kerπ; together with its surjection to B this proves H′=H. A kernel element of H_L has S-coordinate in (E_S)_(L∩S), hence zero when S is not contained in L. The isolated copies N_S lie in H_S whenever S⊆L. This proves (SK1). The kernel profiles intersect by intersecting supports, so K(a) proves IP. Fiber cardinalities prove (SK2).

Assume B has all deletions. For U⊊S, q_S maps (E_S)_U isomorphically onto B_U. The composite (q_S|_(E_S)_U)⁻¹ p_U q_S deletes E_S onto U; use the identity for U=S. These maps commute because they agree on generators. Coordinatewise deletion on H preserves its compatibility equations, again by agreement on generators, and fixes or kills exactly the required tuples.

For the lower-central clause, γ_c(H)≤kerπ follows from the class bound on B. In the same inclusion induction, choose w∈γ_c(F(S)) mapping to n∈γ_c(E_S); such a lift exists by functorial surjectivity of lower-central subgroups. Its H-value lies in γ_c(H). Removing the smaller pure kernels, already in γ_c(H) by induction, proves the reverse inclusion. In an antichain, the kernel of H_S→B_S is exactly N_S; the S-coordinate detects it, so H_S→E_S is injective and already surjective. In the final clause, H_LH_M maps onto B. Write h(h_Lh_M)⁻¹=n_Ln_M by assigning each pure kernel coordinate to a containing side. Normality of coordinate kernels moves n_M past h_L, giving h∈H_LH_M. ∎

The hypotheses are sufficient rather than asserted necessary. Proper-support injectivity must hold on every proper parabolic, not just individual generators. The full fiber product is essential: a universal amalgam with the same local groups need not have this order. These simplifications use the inclusion-induction argument identified in the independent phase122 challenge; the argument is included and charged here.

## Attachment E: arbitrary even edge inflation

Use the existing Boolean base B from Q2.4; supports are the selected pairs S={i,j}. Write q=|r_ir_j| even and m a multiple of q. Set E_S=D_(2m), with its two generating reflections mapping to those of B_S=D_(2q). Then N_S=⟨(e_ie_j)^q⟩≅C_(m/q). Every proper-support parabolic is trivial or generated by one reflection, and q_S is injective there. Theorem SK gives exactly Q2.4's full kernel, all parabolic kernels, order and IP in one invocation. The antichain clause preserves the selected pairs as E_S, so their orders are m. An unselected pair keeps its base order because every extra coordinate sees either one reflection or identity, and a base odd-order pair cannot project to one reflection under the given deletion. This last assertion is a separate pair-order binding, not supplied merely by IP. Evenness is required for Boolean deletion of the dihedral base; it is no longer a separate kernel-isolation argument.

For L032 Theorem 1.2 put δ=n−1−∑k_i and B=C₂^(d−3)×H_(δ+5), with independent C₂ markers before the square seed. This base and its deletion maps are proved in §9. The first d−3 edge orders are two and the last two four. Bind m_i=2^{k_i}; (SK2) gives

\[
 |H|=2^{d-3+\delta+5}\prod_{i\le d-3}2^{k_i-1}
 \prod_{i>d-3}2^{k_i-2}=2^n.
\]

The marking is string, so the retained correspondence gives the required regular polytope. Setting d=3 gives L035 Theorem 1.2 with no new library body. The latter paper's eight-class completeness theorem is **not** a consequence of this attachment: its cyclic-core classification, models and exact finite seed remain charged.

## Attachment T: arbitrary forest-supported square kernels

Let T be a finite forest with vertex set I. The canonical class-two vertex-and-edge group B(T) from §11 has Boolean deletions and

\[
 |B(T)_L|=2^{|L|+|E(T[L])|}.
 \tag{SK3}
\]

Its central edge coordinates and vertex coordinates remain independent under deletion; thus the same displayed vertex/edge multiplication proves (SK3), not a new classification input. Select any collection of distinct induced three-vertex paths S in T and assign n_S≥5. Identify E_S=H_(n_S) with that path marking, and map it to H₅=B(T)_S. The existing affine square model gives N_S=γ₃(H_(n_S))=2Z²/Λ_(n_S). Every proper parabolic has at most two markers and maps injectively to its identical subgroup of H₅. This proves the local hypothesis and the lower-central hypothesis with c=3; no choice of isolating words is needed.

The one theorem now gives a C-group with Boolean deletions and the entire parabolic order formula

\[
 |H_L|=2^{|L|+|E(T[L])|+\sum_{S\subseteq L}(n_S-5)},\qquad
 \gamma_3(H)=\prod_S\gamma_3(H_{n_S}),\qquad H/\gamma_3(H)=B(T).
 \tag{SK4}
\]

The antichain clause identifies H_S with its prescribed square group, without an additional order comparison. No assertion that an arbitrary nonstring C-group is a regular hypertope is made; geometric recognition still has to be supplied.

Take T to be the d-vertex path and select all consecutive triples, binding n_S=n_(i+3) for S={i,i+1,i+2}. Then (SK4) gives |H|=2^{∑_(j=3)^d n_j−3(d−3)}, the exact local square maps and all deletions/FAP. Put L={0,…,d−2} and M={d−3,d−2,d−1}. All supports lie in one of L,M. Formula (SK3) and IP give |B_LB_M|=|B|, because the vertex and edge counts add with their intersection; hence SK gives H=H_LH_M. The coset-incidence argument of §14 makes every (d−4)-face incident with every facet and then gives flatness. This recovers F016 Theorem 5.1 and its γ₃ identity. Rank three keeps the source's co-(−1)-face convention. The exact universal-presentation theorem still needs the four-marker normalizing identity Q8.1 and its order bound; neither is deleted or imported as a target.

For F016 Theorem 6.1, the two final branches give a tree T with common prefix C and two last leaves. Assign the two final square supports n_(d−1),m_(d−1), retaining the common earlier supports. Let L,M be the two facet-label sets. The two deletion maps give a homomorphism H→H_L×_(H_C)H_M. It is injective: every base vertex/edge coordinate and every attached kernel coordinate is contained in L or M, so the two deletions detect all coordinates. Formula (SK4), with the vertex, edge and support contributions added over L and M and subtracted over C, gives equal finite orders. Thus this homomorphism is an isomorphism onto the whole fiber product. Its deletions split, identifying it with the product of the two facet kernels semidirect the common facet. The marked facets embed by their all-parabolic formulas. Apply the **retained** Monson–Schulte FAP assembly/full-automorphism theorem. Equal final parameters yield the branch swap; unequal parameters have unequal facet orders and forbid it. The result prescribes the old two facet lists, not an extra mixed rank-three coface. For d=4 the prefix is an edge and the common facet is the square, as before.

## What this acquisition does and does not establish

One theorem with proved local obligations now supplies both formerly separate cyclic inflation and square-kernel assembly, as well as a forest extension beyond the string case. Actual bindings are E, T-path and T-fork. It covers two existence statements and several finite F016 clauses, not all twelve papers. No change is proposed to the difficult bounded cyclic classification, J1 catalogue-relative enumeration, Petrial thin obstruction, all-rank lattice identification, or N009 complete table recognition. Those remain irreducible **under this proposed interface**, not proved semantically irreducible mathematics.

Removing SK from this declared proof graph removes these three derivations; it does not prove that the original targets cannot be proved otherwise. The next retention check must substitute the theorem into a complete new manuscript, retain all those other branches and programs, and count the new body plus every application obligation. An apparently small adapter is not a compression claim until the acquisition cost has been included.
