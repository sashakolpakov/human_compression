# A lift extension unifies chamber torsors and shadow monodromy

Private proposed theorem, 10 October 2026. This note identifies the group and cover constructions on the same objects. It uses established covering theory, coset geometry and chamber adjacency; no novelty or whole-paper claim is made. All earlier artifacts remain unchanged.

## 1. Hypotheses and precise conclusions

Let $\Gamma$ be a thin, residually connected incidence geometry of finite rank $n\ge3$, with types $I=\{0,1\}\sqcup J$. Let a type-preserving group $G$ act regularly on its chambers. Assume B1 and B2:

* its type-$\{0,1\}$ truncation is the vertex-edge incidence of a simple graph $X$;
* an edge is incident with a higher object precisely when both its endpoints are incident with that object.

For a higher flag $F$, write $X_F$ for its common vertex-edge shadow; $X_\varnothing=X$. Every $X_F$ is connected and contains an edge. This follows by applying the connected-truncation lemma to the residue $\Gamma_F$. The elementary chamber-gallery proof of that lemma is given in [the cover note, §4](phase28_voltage_shadow_theory.md).

Let $p:Y\to X$ be a connected regular graph cover with deck group $A$. Assume that every graph automorphism induced by an element of $G$ lifts to $Y$. No splitting of a group extension is assumed, and the action of $G$ on $X$ need not be faithful.

Form the graph-incidence lift $\widehat\Gamma$ of [phase28, §2](phase28_voltage_shadow_theory.md): lower objects are the vertices and edges of $Y$; an object over a higher object $x$ is a component $C$ of $p^{-1}(X_x)$, retained together with its label $x$. Higher lifted objects are incident precisely when their base objects are incident and their components share a vertex. This is initially an incidence system, not an assumed geometry.

For each higher flag $F$ and a vertex $v\in X_F$, choose a fiber origin over $v$. Let $H_F\le A$ be the monodromy subgroup of $X_F$; set $H_x=H_{\{x\}}$. Consider the two conditions:

$$\begin{split}
\mathsf H:\quad&\text{every pairwise-intersecting choice of cosets of the }H_x\ (x\in F)
\text{ has nonempty total intersection};\\
\mathsf M:\quad&H_F=\bigcap_{x\in F}H_x.
\end{split}\tag{1}
$$

They are required for every higher flag. Changes of fiber origin conjugate the simultaneous data and leave these conditions unchanged.

**Theorem.** There is a canonical lift extension

$$1\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow\widehat G\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\pi}G\longrightarrow1\tag{2}$$

and a distinguished generating tuple of involutions $\widehat\rho_i$ with the following properties.

1. Before either condition in (1) is imposed, $\widehat G$ acts regularly on all chambers of $\widehat\Gamma$, and $\widehat\Gamma$ is exactly its standard coset pregeometry with maximal parabolics $\widehat G_i=\langle\widehat\rho_j:j\ne i\rangle$.
2. $\widehat\Gamma$ is a geometry if and only if $\mathsf H$ holds. This is also exactly the typed-coset-Helly condition on the parabolics $\widehat G_i$.
3. The marked group $(\widehat G,\widehat\rho_i)$ has the full intersection property if and only if $\mathsf M$ holds.
4. Consequently $\widehat\Gamma$ is a regular hypertope if and only if both $\mathsf H$ and $\mathsf M$ hold. In that case its full type-preserving automorphism group is $\widehat G$.

There is a sharper local identity. For a higher subflag $\widehat F$ of the chosen lifted base chamber, let $K_F$ be its stabilizer, and let $L_F$ be the subgroup generated by the involutions whose types are absent from $F$. With compatible fiber coordinates,

$$K_F=\left(\bigcap_{x\in F}H_x\right)L_F,\qquad
[K_F:L_F]=\left[\bigcap_{x\in F}H_x:H_F\right].\tag{3}$$

These are subgroup products and indices of coset sets; $L_F$ need not be normal in $K_F$. The right-hand index counts the connected components of the common lifted shadow. No finiteness assumption on any group or graph is needed.

## 2. The lift extension and its faithful incidence action

Write $g_X$ for the graph action induced by $g\in G$. Define

$$\widehat G=\{(g,f)\in G\times\operatorname{Aut}(Y):p\circ f=g_X\circ p\}.
\tag{4}$$

Componentwise multiplication makes this a group. Its projection to $G$ is surjective by the liftability assumption; its kernel is exactly $\{(1,a):a\in A\}$, proving (2). This definition does not identify two different base automorphisms merely because they have the same action on $X$.

The pair $(g,f)$ sends $(x,C)$ to $(gx,fC)$ and acts on vertices and edges by $f$. This is a type-preserving incidence action. It is faithful: an element fixing every lifted object has $f=1$ from its vertex action, and its base component $g$ fixes all lower and higher base objects. The base chamber-regular action is faithful, so $g=1$.

Fix a base chamber

$$C=(v,e,x_2,\ldots,x_{n-1}),\qquad e=\{v,w\}.$$

Choose $y\in p^{-1}(v)$, let $\widetilde e=\{y,y'\}$ be the unique lift of $e$ at $y$, and let $C_i$ be the component of $p^{-1}(X_{x_i})$ containing $y$. Each contains $\widetilde e$. These data define a chamber $\widehat C$.

Every full-type chamber of $\widehat\Gamma$ projects to a base chamber and is determined uniquely by that chamber and a lift of its vertex: its edge lifts uniquely and every higher component must contain the chosen vertex. Conversely every such choice gives a chamber. Therefore $\widehat G$ is chamber-transitive, using $G$ on base chambers and $A$ on a vertex fiber. Its chamber stabilizer is trivial: its projection fixes $C$, hence is $1$, and a deck transformation fixing $y$ is $1$.

This proves chamber regularity, **not yet flag transitivity**. A higher-only flag may fail to extend to any chamber. Such flags are exactly why condition $\mathsf H$ remains necessary.

## 3. Canonical chamber involutions and generation

Let $\rho_i$ be the unique element of $G$ taking $C$ to its $i$-adjacent chamber. Thinness and chamber regularity make it a nonidentity involution. Residual connectedness gives the familiar base parabolic formula

$$G_F=\operatorname{Stab}_G(F)=\langle\rho_j:j\notin\operatorname{type}(F)\rangle
\tag{5}$$

for every flag $F\subseteq C$: its residue chamber graph is connected, so the adjacent-chamber generators traverse its entire chamber set.

For $i\ne0$, let $\widehat\rho_i$ be the unique lift of $\rho_i$ fixing $y$. Such a lift exists because $\rho_i$ fixes $v$, and it is unique because a deck transformation is determined by the image of one vertex. Its square is a deck transformation fixing $y$, so its square is $1$.

Let $\widehat\rho_0$ be the unique lift taking $y$ to $y'$. The base element $\rho_0$ fixes $e$ and exchanges its endpoints. The lift consequently reverses $\widetilde e$, and its square is again a deck transformation fixing $y$. These lifts take $\widehat C$ to its unique chamber differing in type $i$. For $i=0$, the higher components remain fixed because both endpoints of $\widetilde e$ lie in each $C_j$; for other $i$, unchanged components contain the fixed vertex $y$.

Every panel contained in a chamber has exactly two chamber completions, even before global flag extension is established. Indeed, it contains a lower object, and the local residue at that vertex or edge is the corresponding base residue. In particular the adjacent chamber just described is unique.

The chamber graph of $\widehat\Gamma$ is connected without assuming (1). At a fixed cover vertex its chambers identify with those of a base vertex residue, hence are gallery connected. Along an edge of connected $Y$, choose a base chamber containing the projected edge and lift it there. A type-0 adjacency moves between its endpoints. Paths in $Y$, interspersed with galleries at fixed vertices, connect any two chambers.

The orbit of $\widehat C$ under the subgroup generated by the $\widehat\rho_i$ is closed under all chamber adjacencies: at a translate, adjacency is obtained by the corresponding translated base adjacency. The chamber graph is connected and $\widehat G$ acts regularly, so

$$\widehat G=\langle\widehat\rho_i:i\in I\rangle.\tag{6}$$

## 4. Exact maximal parabolics

Let $K_i$ be the stabilizer of the type-$i$ object of $\widehat C$.

For $i=0$, a base element fixing $v$ has a unique lift fixing $y$. Thus $\pi:K_0\to G_0$ is an isomorphism. Similarly $\pi:K_1\to G_1$ is an isomorphism: a base element stabilizing $e$ has a unique lift stabilizing $\widetilde e$, choosing its image of $y$ according to whether it fixes or exchanges the endpoints of $e$. A deck transformation cannot nontrivially stabilize $\widetilde e$, since it preserves the distinct base endpoints.

For a higher $i$, every $g\in G_i$ has a lift preserving $C_i$. To see this, choose a path in connected $X_{x_i}$ from $v$ to $g(v)$, lift it from $y$, and adjust any lift of $g$ by a deck transformation to end at that chosen endpoint. The adjusted lift maps $C_i$ to itself. The deck transformations preserving $C_i$ are exactly its monodromy subgroup $H_i$. Consequently

$$1\longrightarrow H_i\longrightarrow K_i\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\pi}G_i
\longrightarrow1\qquad(i\in J).\tag{7}$$

The higher $K_i$ are not generally the full inverse images $\pi^{-1}(G_i)$: that would require $H_i=A$.

To prove the generated-parabolic statement, we give a local argument that will also prove (3). For a higher base flag $F\subseteq C$, let $C_F$ be the component of $p^{-1}(X_F)$ containing $y$, and put

$$L_F=\{(g,f)\in\widehat G:g\in G_F,\ f(C_F)=C_F\}.\tag{8}$$

The chambers containing the chosen lifts of $F$ and having their vertex in $C_F$ form one gallery component of their residue chamber system. At a fixed vertex their chambers are those of the base residue at $F$ and that vertex, so are connected. A path in $C_F$ can be traversed by type-0 moves, choosing incident edge chambers and using the preceding fixed-vertex galleries between steps. Conversely any gallery avoiding the types in $F$ keeps its vertex within $C_F$: only a type-0 step moves that vertex, and its traversed edge projects into $X_F$.

The group $L_F$ acts regularly and transitively on this gallery component. For a target chamber, first use the unique base element in $G_F$ taking $C$ to its projection; then take the unique lift mapping $y$ to its chosen vertex in $C_F$. That lift preserves $C_F$. The adjacency argument from §3 therefore proves

$$L_F=\langle\widehat\rho_j:j\notin\operatorname{type}(F)\rangle.\tag{9}$$

For a flag containing a lower object, its entire residue chamber system identifies with a base residue, so the same formula holds with its actual flag stabilizer in place of $L_F$.

Taking singleton flags in (9), $C_{\{x_i\}}=C_i$ gives

$$K_i=\langle\widehat\rho_j:j\ne i\rangle\qquad(i\in I).\tag{10}$$

The empty flag in (9) also recovers (6), since $C_\varnothing=Y$.

## 5. The stabilizer index is exactly the monodromy defect

Keep a higher flag $F\subseteq C$. Let

$$K_F=\bigcap_{x_i\in F}K_i,\qquad H=\bigcap_{x_i\in F}H_i.$$

A lift preserving $C_F$ preserves every $C_i$ containing it: its image is a component over the same $x_i$ and contains a vertex of $C_F\subseteq C_i$. Thus $L_F\le K_F$.

The path-and-deck-adjustment construction in §4 applies to $X_F$, proving $\pi(L_F)=G_F$. Since $L_F\le K_F$ and every element of $K_F$ projects into $G_F$, we have

$$\pi(L_F)=\pi(K_F)=G_F,\qquad
L_F\cap A=H_F,\qquad K_F\cap A=H.\tag{11}$$

For $k\in K_F$, choose $\ell\in L_F$ with $\pi(k)=\pi(\ell)$. Then $k\ell^{-1}\in H$. Hence $K_F=HL_F$. The map of left-coset sets

$$H/H_F\longrightarrow K_F/L_F,\qquad hH_F\longmapsto hL_F\tag{12}$$

is surjective by that product formula and injective because $H\cap L_F=H_F$. This proves (3), including infinite indices, and gives the equivalence

$$K_F=L_F\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_F=\bigcap_{x\in F}H_x.\tag{13}$$

There is also an exact geometric interpretation of (12). The intersection $Z_F=\bigcap_{x_i\in F}C_i$ is the cover of $X_F$ with fiber $H$ at $v$. Its components are $hC_F$, indexed by $hH_F$. The group $K_F$ is transitive on those components and their stabilizer is $L_F$. Thus (12) identifies both coset sets with the same component set. This is the same monodromy-component calculation as phase28, now inside the lift extension.

This is an application of the elementary subgroup image-and-kernel transport principle, not a new normal-quotient assertion. The subgroup $H_F$ may fail to be normal in $H$, and $L_F$ may fail to be normal in $K_F$.

## 6. Full intersection property, without assuming a geometry

For $S\subseteq I$, put $T_S=\langle\widehat\rho_s:s\in S\rangle$. By §4, if $I\setminus S$ includes a lower type, then

$$T_S=\bigcap_{i\notin S}K_i.\tag{14}$$

If the complement consists only of higher types, (9) and (13) show that (14) holds exactly when $\mathsf M$ holds for that flag. Empty intersections and empty generated subgroups have their usual meanings; for the full base chamber, its stabilizer is $1$.

Assume $\mathsf M$. Formula (14) then holds for every $S$. For any $S,T\subseteq I$,

$$\begin{split}
T_S\cap T_T
&=\left(\bigcap_{i\notin S}K_i\right)\cap
  \left(\bigcap_{i\notin T}K_i\right)\\
&=\bigcap_{i\notin S\cap T}K_i=T_{S\cap T}.
\end{split}\tag{15}$$

This is the full binary intersection property, rather than only a maximal-parabolic test.

Conversely, full binary IP, (10), and finite repeated intersection give

$$\bigcap_{i\in F}K_i
=\bigcap_{i\in F}T_{I\setminus\{i\}}
=T_{I\setminus F}=L_F
$$

for every higher flag in the chosen chamber. Equation (13) gives $\mathsf M$ there. Every higher base flag is $G$-equivalent to such a subflag, and every chosen fiber origin is related by a lift followed by a deck transformation. Thus $\mathsf M$ holds for all higher flags.

This proof never assumes that all lifted flags extend to chambers. It is therefore legitimate to retain the separate condition $\mathsf H$.

## 7. The coset pregeometry and the exact Helly correspondence

Every incident pair of lifted objects extends to a chamber, regardless of $\mathsf H$. For two higher objects, choose a common lifted vertex, extend their base flag with its projected vertex to a chamber, and lift at that vertex. For pairs involving a lower object, use the corresponding local residue identification. Every object also lies in a chamber.

Since $\widehat G$ acts regularly on chambers, it is transitive on incident pairs of any two types. The map

$$gK_i\longmapsto g\widehat x_i\tag{16}$$

is consequently a bijection from cosets to type-$i$ objects, and two objects are incident exactly when their representing cosets meet. Indeed, a common coset element gives a translated base incident pair; an incident pair belongs to a chamber and thus has such a common translating element. This proves the claimed standard coset **pregeometry** description before global flag extension.

For any finite typed family of these parabolic cosets, nonempty total intersection is equivalent to its lifted objects sharing a chamber: a common element translates the base chamber, and every chamber arises in this way. Therefore their typed Helly property is equivalent to $\widehat\Gamma$ being a geometry.

For higher-only lifted flags, choosing a common base vertex converts component incidences into pairwise intersections of the cosets $g_xH_x$ in one deck fiber. Such a flag extends precisely when the whole fiber intersection is nonempty. The proof uses a path in the connected common base shadow to transport any common lifted vertex back to the chosen fiber. Flags already involving a lower object extend automatically. Thus the deck-subgroup Helly condition $\mathsf H$ is equivalent to the parabolic-coset Helly condition, not merely a sufficient analogy.

Under $\mathsf H$, phase28 gives residual connectedness exactly when $\mathsf M$ holds, and local residue identifications preserve thinness. Alternatively, (15), the coset description, and typed Helly give the usual regular-hypertope recognition theorem. This proves assertion 4.

Finally, an automorphism of a thin chamber-connected geometry fixing a chamber fixes its unique neighbors and then every chamber. Since $\widehat G$ is already chamber-regular, under these conditions it is the full type-preserving automorphism group. This conclusion does not identify arbitrary type-permuting automorphisms.

## 8. Bipartite compression and a second exact extension

Assume additionally that $Y$ is bipartite and $X$ is nonbipartite. Let $\varepsilon:\widehat G\to C_2$ record whether the cover automorphism preserves or exchanges the two colors. Its restriction to $A$ is surjective. Otherwise all points of every fiber would have the same color, by deck transitivity, and those colors would define a bipartition of $X$.

Put $\widehat G^+=\ker\varepsilon$ and $A^+=A\cap\widehat G^+$. A lift of any $g\in G$ can be made color preserving by multiplying by a color-exchanging deck transformation. Consequently

$$1\longrightarrow A^+\longrightarrow\widehat G^+
\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow1.\tag{17}$$

The compressed system $\Delta$ has one type for each color class, adjacent lower vertices incident, and the same higher components. Its chambers correspond to unoriented lower edges together with higher chamber objects. The two endpoints supply the two opposite orientations that were separate chambers of $\widehat\Gamma$. Every $\widehat G$-orbit calculation above consequently gives a regular $\widehat G^+$ action on the chambers of $\Delta$; its chamber fibers over base chambers, using the color-0 endpoint as the base vertex, are $A^+$-torsors.

Under $\mathsf H$ and $\mathsf M$, phase28 proves that $\Delta$ is a regular hypertope. Its distinguished marking, after choosing the appropriate order of the lower types, is

$$\widehat\rho_0\widehat\rho_1\widehat\rho_0,\quad
\widehat\rho_1,\quad\widehat\rho_2,\ldots,\widehat\rho_{n-1}.
\tag{18}$$

To check the first generator, start with the chosen lower edge, reverse its orientation, change its edge while retaining that endpoint, and reverse orientation again. This changes the color-0 endpoint and fixes the color-1 endpoint. The second generator changes the latter endpoint while fixing the former. Higher generators fix both endpoints. The group generated by (18) is $\widehat G^+$ because the compressed chamber graph is connected. Its full type-preserving automorphism group is also $\widehat G^+$ by the preceding chamber-stabilizer argument.

For the canonical parity double cover, $A=C_2$ and $A^+=1$, so (17) identifies this group with $G$. The monodromy condition is exactly the parity-compatibility condition of phase27. For larger deck groups, both the Helly test and the surviving kernel $A^+$ remain explicit. Formula (18) is used here only after the required geometry and connectivity conditions have been proved.

## 9. Consequences, limitations, and exact representation

The common mathematical object is the chamber torsor of (2), marked by the partitions into cosets of $K_i$. The graph-cover component labels produce those same partitions via (16). The two defects are different operations on that one object:

* empty intersections of pairwise-intersecting typed blocks obstruct flag completion;
* the excess $K_F/L_F$, as a coset set, is the collection of components of a nonempty common shadow and obstructs marked subgroup IP.

For finite $\widehat G$, the based zero-one matrices of [the torsor note](torsor_matrix_theory.md) give the exact same subgroup test:

$$A_{T_S}\mathbin{\odot} A_{T_T}=A_{T_{S\cap T}}.
$$

This is a consequence of the subgroup/partition identification, not a replacement for proving liftability, generation, $\mathsf H$, or $\mathsf M$.

Several boundaries matter. An arbitrary graph automorphism need not lift. A graph-action kernel in $G$ must be retained in (4). The higher stabilizers are local component-preserving lift groups, not automatically full inverse images. The index in (3) need not be the order of a quotient group. Chamber regularity before Helly does not imply transitivity on nonextendible flags. Nor do B1/B2 alone imply either condition (1). No assertion is made here that regularity of the base automatically supplies $\mathsf M$.

The proof establishes a literal adapter between two existing constructions under explicit hypotheses. It does not classify covers, remove the field or finite-group inputs from unrelated constructions, or establish new priority. The older Lean subgroup and coset kernels apply to parts of the algebra, but this complete lift-extension theorem has not been formalized in Lean.

## 10. Two exact diagnostics and reproduction scope

The standalone standard-library [checker](phase30_torsor_cover_check.py) tests two small examples from literal permutations. It imports no other workspace module and does not use a group catalogue.

1. For the tetrahedron and its canonical parity cover, it constructs $\widehat G=S_4\times C_2$ on eight cover vertices. The lifted graph-incidence system has 12 edges, four higher objects and 48 chambers. Its maximal parabolics have orders $6,4,12$; the last has deck kernel $C_2$. The compressed marking generates the color-preserving $S_4$, has trivial deck kernel, and satisfies all 64 rank-three subgroup meets.
2. For the triangular dihedron with two distinct faces having identical whole-graph shadows, it uses the identity cover and $G=S_3\times C_2$. The graph-action kernel has order two, switching the two faces. The full incidence action remains faithful and has 12 chambers. Replacing the pair group (4) by its graph-automorphism image would incorrectly lose this factor.

For each graph-incidence system, the checker builds all eight marked subgroups and checks all 64 binary meets, all subflag stabilizer formulas, the object/coset incidence equivalence and typed Helly for every flag, and the component-derived monodromy and stabilizer index. There are 145 flags in the first system and 39 in the second, including the empty flag. These are consistency tests of the theorem and its graph-kernel qualification; rank three has no higher flag of size two, so these examples do not test a nontrivial higher-shadow monodromy defect or prove the uniform theorem.

Run `python3 phase30_torsor_cover_check.py --output /tmp/new-torsor-cover-report.json` with a new output path. The saved [report](phase30_torsor_cover_check.json) binds the executed checker bytes. Its output path must not already exist.
