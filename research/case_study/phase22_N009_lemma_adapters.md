# Two remaining numbered lemma adapters in N009

Private proposed rederivation, 10 October 2026. Targets are Gomi–Loyola–De Las Peñas (2018), Lemmas 5.2 and 5.4, printed pp. 17–18, in the primary paper [String C-groups of order 1024](https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/download/62475/46803). These are additional source statements, not new paper counts. The affine square-group description and the source's quoted infinite universal decomposition remain explicit inputs.

## 1. One affine model gives all center formulas in Lemma 5.2

Let W be the affine square reflection group. In coordinates on Z^2, take

    a(x,y)=(1-x,y),   b(x,y)=(y,x),   c(x,y)=(x,-y).

The linear parts generate D8, and t=abcb is translation by (1,0). Its conjugates supply both coordinate translations. Thus W=Z^2 semidirect D8. Also (abc)^2 is translation by (1,1), (bc)^2 has linear part -I and zero translation, and (ab)^2 has linear part -I and translation (1,1).

Retain the square-group/finite quotient identification in the source's quoted Lemma 5.1. Its order-8s^2 family is W/(sZ^2), while its order-16s^2 family is W/L_s, where L_s=< (s,s),(s,-s) >. These identifications can equivalently be obtained by the displayed affine relators and the source's stated orders: the affine images have exactly those orders, hence the maps from the source presentations are isomorphisms. The arithmetic below does not presume that every arbitrary quotient of W has one of these lattices.

Write T=Z^2/L. In a central affine element (v,M), commutation with all translations forces M to act trivially on T. If the D8-action is faithful, M=I and the center is exactly the translation subgroup T^D8. The small nonfaithful cases are checked explicitly below.

**First family L=sZ^2, s=2^m.** For s>=4 the signed-permutation action is faithful. Invariance under the two coordinate sign changes and the swap gives 2x=2y=0 modulo s and x=y modulo s. Thus

    Z(W/L)=<translation(s/2,s/2)>=<(abc)^s>.

When s=2, the kernel of the action on translations consists of the four diagonal sign matrices. For (v,M) to commute with the linear D8 subgroup, M must also lie in Z(D8)={I,-I}, and v must be fixed by D8. The latter translations are 0 and (1,1). Therefore the center is generated by the half-turns (ab)^2 and (bc)^2, exactly the exceptional m=1 formula in Lemma 5.2(i). The source does not assert its displayed first-family formula at m=0; no such extension is needed here.

**Second family L=L_s, s=2^m.** For s>=2 the signed-permutation action on T is faithful. Indeed, if a diagonal sign change acted trivially then (2,0) or (0,2) would belong to L_s, which is impossible for s>=2; a signed swap cannot act trivially because its difference on a coordinate vector has an entry +/-1, not divisible by s. A fixed translation (x,y) must satisfy (2x,0),(0,2y) in L_s. Solving those two equations gives s|x and s|y. Modulo L_s this leaves only 0 and (s,0), and these two classes are also fixed by the swap. Hence

    Z(W/L_s)=<translation(s,0)>=<(abcb)^s>.

For s=1, L_1 consists of vectors of even coordinate sum, so T=C2 and the D8-action is trivial. The group is C2 times D8. Its center is generated by the translation class t and the half-turn -I. The elements ac and (bc)^2 have respectively translation/linear parts (t,-I) and (0,-I); (ab)^2 has the same image as (bc)^2. Consequently the source's generating set <ac,(ab)^2,(bc)^2> is exactly this center. The three displayed generators are not asserted to be independent.

These four cases prove every displayed formula in Lemma 5.2, including both small parameter exceptions. The argument uses actual affine actions, not finite sampling.

## 2. Frattini coordinates give Lemma 5.4

Retain the source's quoted Theorem 5.3: its particular universal group is

    U=(W1 times W2) semidirect <b,d>,   <b,d>=C2^2,

with the stated distinguished generators a,b,c,d and the displayed conjugate-word generators for W1,W2. The coordinate projection U->C2^2 sends b,d to the two basis vectors and a,c to zero; the stated generators of W1 and W2 have even b- and d-parities, as can be checked directly from their conjugate words.

Let pi:U->G be a marked epimorphism to the finite string 2-group in the source class. Actual IP makes the four distinguished generators an irredundant generating set. By the Burnside basis theorem they form a basis of G/Phi(G). Thus G has two homomorphisms to C2 recording, independently, the b- and d-coordinates. Their composite with pi is exactly the above coordinate projection of U, since the maps agree on all four generators.

It follows immediately that ker(pi) lies in W1 times W2. The image of <b,d> still has order four by actual IP and intersects the image of W1 times W2 trivially, either by the same two coordinate maps or by the semidirect quotient construction. Therefore

    G=((W1 times W2)/ker(pi)) semidirect <b,d>.

This is precisely Lemma 5.4. It applies whenever the stated marked universal decomposition and finite 2-group/IP hypotheses hold, not only at order 1024. It does not prove the infinite decomposition itself or assume that quotients generally preserve IP.
