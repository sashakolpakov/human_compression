# A powerful Frattini subgroup cannot occur in a minimum cyclic-TPP counterexample

10 October 2026. Private continuation for review. The preceding abelian-
Frattini proofs and certificates are unchanged. This note retains standard
powerful-group structure results, explicitly listed below. It does not
claim the cyclic-TPP inequality for every group with powerful Frattini
subgroup: that class has not been proved closed under the induction used
here.

## 1. Statements and the induction boundary

Fix a prime p. A finite p-group K is powerful if [K,K]≤K^p for odd p,
or [K,K]≤K⁴ when p=2. Here K^(p^i) denotes the subgroup generated by
all p^i-th powers, and K^1=K.

**Theorem A.** If P is a minimum-order counterexample to the cyclic
subgroup TPP inequality among all finite p-groups, then Φ(P) is not
powerful. Moreover Φ(P) is generated by the p-th powers of the three
distinguished cyclic generators, so d(Φ(P))≤3.

**Theorem B.** Let P be a finite p-group such that Φ(H) is powerful for
every subgroup H≤P. Then every cyclic subgroup TPP triple A,B,C in P
satisfies |A||B||C|≤|P|.

Theorem B's class is closed under subgroups by definition. It is closed
under quotients: a subgroup of P/N is H/N for some H≥N, and
Φ(H/N)=Φ(H)N/N is a quotient of the powerful group Φ(H), hence powerful.
This class includes all groups with abelian Frattini subgroup, since
Φ(H)≤Φ(P). It is not assumed to include all groups with powerful
Frattini subgroup.

For Theorem A, minimum order is taken in the unrestricted p-group class.
For Theorem B it is taken within the stated subgroup- and quotient-closed
class. Thus in both proofs the usual smaller subgroup and central quotient
reductions are legitimate. No classification of finite p-groups is used.

## 2. Retained powerful-group input

The primary source is Lubotzky–Mann, *Powerful p-groups. I. Finite groups*,
Journal of Algebra 105 (1987), 484–505,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(87)90211-0), read in the
[author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265533173_Powerful_p-groups_I_Finite_groups).
The following are retained results, not new theorems of this note:

1. Power subgroups of a powerful group are powerful and obey
   (K^(p^i))^(p^j)=K^(p^(i+j)); Theorems 1.1,1.3 and 4.1.1,4.1.3.
2. If K=⟨g₁,…,g_s⟩ is powerful, then
   K^(p^i)=⟨g₁^(p^i),…,g_s^(p^i)⟩; Corollary 1.9 and Proposition 4.1.9.
3. The same generating list gives K=⟨g₁⟩⋯⟨g_s⟩ as an ordered set
   product; Theorem 1.11 (including its proof for any generating list)
   and Theorem 4.1.11.
4. The power subgroups are powerfully embedded. In particular
   [K^(p^i),K]≤K^(p^(i+1)) for odd p, with the stronger fourth-power
   containment when p=2; Theorem 1.1 and Corollaries 1.2,4.1.2.

For odd p the relevant statements were read on printed pages 486–490;
the corresponding 2-group statements were read on pages 499–501. The
source is not a claim that every subgroup of a powerful group is powerful.
No third-party full text is included in this original proof artifact.

## 3. Every minimum counterexample gives an equality triple in Φ(P)

Use either minimum counterexample described in §1. The elementary
reductions in phase11_nilpotent_frattini.md, §2, do not need abelianness
until its final direct-product step. Before that step they prove

    A∩Z(P)=B∩Z(P)=C∩Z(P)=1,
    ⟨A,B⟩=⟨A,C⟩=⟨B,C⟩=P,
    P/Φ(P)≅C_p²,  |A||B||C|=p|P|,
    min(|A|,|B|,|C|)≥p².                              (1)

These statements also follow directly from central quotient absorption,
the proper-pair coset bound, restriction to the maximal subgroup containing
A, and disjoint conjugates of A with different order-p subgroups.

Write K=Φ(P), x=a^p,y=b^p,z=c^p, and X=⟨x⟩,Y=⟨y⟩,Z=⟨z⟩.
Then X,Y,Z are nontrivial cyclic groups, retain TPP, and satisfy

    |X||Y||Z|=|K|.                                    (2)

They generate K. Otherwise H=⟨X,Y,Z⟩ would be a proper subgroup of K;
the same TPP triple has order product |K|>|H|, contradicting minimum
order in the permitted class. This proves the unconditional generator
bound in Theorem A. It does not yet imply that the ordered multiplication
map is injective.

## 4. Powerful K supplies genuine coordinates

Assume K is powerful. By retained input 3, K=XYZ as an ordered set
product. Its domain X×Y×Z has order |K| by (2), so this particular
surjective multiplication map is bijective. This justification uses
powerfulness in addition to TPP and equality.

The subgroup K^p is powerful and is generated by x^p,y^p,z^p by
inputs 1–2. Apply input 3 again:

    K^p=X^p Y^p Z^p.

The restricted ordered product remains injective, because the original
one is. Each of X,Y,Z is nontrivial, so

    |K^p|=|X^p||Y^p||Z^p|=|K|/p³.

For a powerful group Φ(K)=K^p. Hence V=K/K^p has dimension three,
and x,y,z give a basis. The cyclic orders need not be equal, and K need
not be abelian.

Let p^r be the largest order of x,y,z. Input 2 gives K^(p^r)=1, while
one displayed generator has that order. Thus exp(K)=p^r.

## 5. The top-power map is linear and equivariant

Put K_i=K^(p^i), so K_0=K and K_r=1. Each map

    K_i/K_(i+1) → K_(i+1)/K_(i+2),
    u K_(i+1) ↦ u^p K_(i+2),                          (3)

is a well-defined surjective homomorphism of elementary abelian groups.
Here is a proof of the needed linearity rather than an assumption that
arbitrary power maps are homomorphisms.

Apply the retained input to the powerful group L=K_i. For odd p,
L/L^(p²) has class at most two: [L,L]≤L^p and
[L^p,L]≤L^(p²). Its commutator group has exponent dividing p.
The class-two formula (uv)^p=u^p v^p[v,u]^(p(p−1)/2) therefore gives
(uv)^p≡u^p v^p modulo L^(p²). For p=2, L/L⁴ is abelian by the
definition of powerful, and the same square congruence is immediate.
Also every v∈L^p has v^p∈L^(p²), so the map factors through L/L^p.
Input 2, or the definition of the power subgroup, gives surjectivity.

Compose (3) to obtain a linear map

    τ: V=K/K^p → K_(r−1),  uK^p ↦ u^(p^(r−1)).        (4)

For r=1 this is the identity on K, which is elementary abelian. All maps
are equivariant under automorphisms of K. The target in (4) has exponent
p and is abelian. At least one of τ(x),τ(y),τ(z) is nonidentity, and
τ(w) is trivial exactly when the indicated cyclic generator w has order
less than p^r.

## 6. The same action obstruction now lifts through τ

Conjugation by a,b on V gives commuting maps S,T with S^p=T^p=I:
their p-th powers and commutator act by inner automorphisms of K,
which are trivial on V. The subgroup K^p is characteristic in K and
normal in P. Thus calculations can be made in P/K^p, where the image
of K is abelian. The norm identities from phase11_nilpotent_frattini.md,
§3, hold there exactly. For p=2 the additional square compatibility from
phase11_abelian_frattini.md, §4, holds there too, with c=abf for f∈K.

For odd p the dimension-three norm argument proves

    Sx=Tx=x,  Sy=Ty=y,  Sz−z,Tz−z∈⟨x,y⟩.

If τ(x) or τ(y) is nontrivial, it is a nonidentity fixed element of both
actions. If both are trivial, then τ(z) is nontrivial and is fixed as
well, because all correction terms lie in their kernel.

For p=2 the binary square-action lemma says either S=T=I, or their
common image under subtraction of I is a coordinate line L, fixed by
both maps. If τ(L) is nontrivial, its coordinate gives a nontrivial
fixed image. If τ(L) is trivial, all corrections lie in ker τ, so every
image under τ is fixed; choose a coordinate with nontrivial image.
The case S=T=I is immediate.

In each case the resulting element is a nonidentity element of one of
X,Y,Z and is fixed by conjugation by a,b. It is therefore central in
P=⟨a,b⟩, contradicting (1). This proves Theorems A and B. ∎

## 7. What this does and does not settle

Theorem A rules out a powerful Frattini subgroup in a minimum counterexample
to the unrestricted p-group problem. It strengthens the abelian obstruction
without classifying all surviving groups. Theorem B is a uniform inequality
for the explicitly defined hereditary condition on Frattini subgroups.
Its direct Sylow-product version holds for finite nilpotent groups whose
Sylow subgroups satisfy that condition.

To obtain the superficially stronger statement for every P with powerful
Φ(P), one must still justify induction on proper pair-generated subgroups
or replace that reduction. This note does not assume that every subgroup
of a powerful group is powerful, and does not promote that stronger
statement. The full cyclic-TPP inequality and Hou–Feng–Leemans conjecture
remain unresolved. The new proof needs internal review before export;
no formal verification or publication-priority determination is asserted.
