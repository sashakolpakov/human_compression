# Independent review of the finite toroidal-pyramid covers

## Verdict and exact scope

I accept the written theorem in frozen phase239, together with its retained phase224 special case, under the explicitly declared classical polytope and surface inputs. For every integer $n\ge3$ and prime $\ell\nmid2n$, the pyramid over $\{4,4\}_{(n,0)}$ has a finite regular polytopal cover whose marked group maps onto its monodromy group with elementary abelian kernel of dimension 642. For each odd $n\ge3$, the construction yields infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic finite minimal regular polytopal covers, with nontrivial degrees $\ell^e$ over that monodromy group and $1\le e\le642$.

The conditional transfer to a base polyhedron with the stated marked smallest reflexible cover is also accepted. This is a uniform family theorem. It does not settle the finite minimal-cover question for arbitrary polytopes, classify the minimal descendants, determine their exponents, or replace either historical source paper. Mere finite-cover existence was already known. No comprehensive priority claim follows from this review.

## Primary sources and the actual polytope

I read the exact source interfaces in Cunningham–Pellicer–Williams, *Stratified operations on maniplexes* (2022), and visually inspected physical PDF pages 8, 9 and 17, corresponding to printed pages 273, 274 and 282. Definition 3.1 has exactly the four cases used for the layer action. Proposition 3.3 makes each local word independent of the base maniplex. Theorem 3.4 preserves the marked monodromy group when replacing the base by its smallest reflexible cover. Theorem 4.3 retains the prior Pellicer–Williams classification identifying the odd square-torus family as the nonpolytopal exception. I did not independently reproduce that 2018 classification or claim to read all its original proof.

I also read Monson–Schulte, *Finite Polytopes have Finite Regular Covers*, the retained seven-page author edition, at Lemma 3.3, Theorem 3.4 and Example 3.7, physical pages 5–7. The source already proves finite regular-cover existence and explicitly studies the same $n=3$ pyramid, including its monodromy order and the index-two top intersection defect. Its typeset date is not used to redetermine the arXiv edition. The additional content being accepted is the controlled prime-power construction and multiplicity, with the uniform quotient argument for all parameters.

The affine description is literal. A torus flag is determined by a vertex, one of four incident directed edge directions and one of two incident squares. For $n\ge3$, these give $8n^2$ different flags and identify the flags with the affine signed-permutation group. The three displayed reflections send the base flag to its three adjacent flags. An arbitrary affine image of the base flag is therefore adjacent by right multiplication by the same reflection. Modulo $n$, the eight signed matrices remain distinct; this is where the lower bound on $n$ enters the faithful description.

Inserting the apex at the five possible positions produces exactly $40n^2$ pyramid flags. Reversing the insertion-position index gives the author's layer number and all four branches of the source formula. In particular layer 0 is the base facet and layer 4 is the apex layer. The literal face program independently defines adjacency by the two flags with all other faces fixed; the affine-to-face bijections at $n=3,5$ test this convention. They are not the proof of its validity for every $n$.

## The uniform kernel argument

Every generator has a layer permutation and a right affine multiplier on each input layer. Composition has the corresponding permuted-coordinate rule, so the action embeds faithfully into the wreath product. Forgetting translations is a marked homomorphism onto the same fixed orientation group $E$, independently of $n$. Its kernel embeds into ten translation coordinates over $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, yielding the divisibility bound $|K_n|\mid n^{10}$. No full monodromy order formula is required.

Here the two support restrictions are essential. A word in $s_0,s_1,s_2$ fixes layer 0. A path beginning in another layer cannot visit layer 0, and every nontrivial local factor on that path belongs to $\langle r_0,r_1\rangle$. This is the eight-element integral group fixing $(1/2,1/2)$; the multiplier with matrix $M$ has translation $(I-M)(1/2,1/2)$. Its linear part determines it uniquely. Thus a word from this end subgroup in the orientation kernel can translate only layer 0. Reversing the argument, a word from $\langle s_1,s_2,s_3\rangle$ in the kernel can translate only layer 4, since the other local words fix the origin. Both restrictions hold for an element in the common end intersection. All translations vanish, and its layer permutation was already trivial. This proves actual injectivity on that intersection.

An element in the intersection fixes layers 0 and 4 and permutes the middle three. On each middle input layer its affine multiplier is both a face-center stabilizer and a vertex stabilizer. The former has translation entries 0 or 1 and the latter has translation zero. Since $n\ge3$, congruence forces both entries to be zero integrally. Hence $M(1,1)=(1,1)$; exactly the identity and the coordinate swap satisfy this among the signed matrices. The argument compares the same input layer, so a nontrivial permutation of the three middle layers creates no omitted case.

This proves an upper bound on the image of the actual intersection by the constrained subgroup $J$. It does not assert equality with $J$, and does not assume that all $T'_n$ have order 48. The latter would be an unjustified extrapolation from an odd parameter. The middle parabolic maps onto the fixed middle parabolic because the maps preserve its generators.

I independently rebuilt $E$ in native GAP using $D_8$ as permutations of four directions, without importing the author's forty-point generator lists. The resulting orders are

\[
 (|E|,|A|,|B|,|C|,|A\cap B|,|J|,|D|,|\ker\pi|)
 =(61440,12288,12288,24,3072,48,3840,16).
\]

The assignments to the literal ten-point quotient define a checked epimorphism. Actual subgroup equality gives $\ker\pi\cap J=\ker\pi\cap C$, of order two. Omitting the middle-center constraint instead admits a kernel intersection of order 16, not contained in $C$. Thus the constraint is not cosmetic.

For $x$ in the original kernel and end intersection, its orientation image lies in this order-two subgroup of $C$. Lift that image inside the original middle parabolic. The lift and $x$ lie in the end intersection, on which the orientation map is injective; consequently they are equal. This proves the desired original kernel intersection equality, without assuming injectivity on either whole end subgroup. The full kernel has order $16|K_n|$, so every prime not dividing $2n$ is coprime to it.

## Surface, pullback and minimality

The fixed quotient satisfies the surface theorem's hypotheses: four distinct nonidentity involutions, proper nonadjacent four-groups and middle product of order six. My separate Python implementation enumerates its group, constructs the four kinds of polygonal cells, checks every edge has two sides and every vertex link is the required four-cycle, and propagates orientations across actual boundaries. It finds 3840 vertices, 7680 edges and 3200 faces, Euler characteristic $-640$, and a connected orientable surface. It independently reconstructs each of the three marked subcomplex unions and gets connected complementary regions of 2704, 3199 and 3199 faces. Removing a disk fails the closed-surface edge-incidence condition.

The retained, previously independently reviewed phase211 surface theorem therefore applies for every prime. Its homology dimension is $2-\chi=642$, and its three lifted intersections give the full string C-property. This use of topology is a written theorem application, not a finite-field extrapolation or a new Lean proof of homology. Fresh replay of phase224 reproduces its literal 360-flag model, exact group orders, quotient and complete surface witness byte for byte.

The author's general rank-three observation is sound. A word in the first two colors acts on each polygon in dihedral normal form. If its global permutation also fixes the vertex at every flag, its rotation exponent vanishes on every such polygon. Its reflection parity is determined by the common word, so the action is everywhere either the identity or the middle generator. This proves the adjacent-parabolic intersection uniformly over different faces. The dual argument handles the other end. The end subgroups of the rank-four monodromy thus satisfy the required intersection property.

The coprime pullback argument works on every marked parabolic. Take a root inside the cyclic subgroup of a kernel element, lift it to the subgroup generated by paired marks, and raise to the exponent of the other kernel. This isolates the desired kernel factor; surjectivity then gives the full fiber product. It requires no splitting of the extension. Subtracting a lift of the common image in the C-group coordinate gives the displayed intersection formula. The end C-properties and the newly proved top kernel equality supply all recursive intersections. For all marks together the fiber-product equality identifies the kernel over the original monodromy as the complete 642-dimensional homology group.

Finite descent gives minimal regular quotients of each constructed finite cover. Minimality here is among all regular covers, since every quotient of this finite cover is itself finite. For odd $n$ the retained non-C result rules out degree one, so the remaining degree is a positive power of the selected prime. Different primes give different group orders, hence nonisomorphic regular polytopes. No minimal descendant is explicitly selected or classified.

The transfer corollary uses the source's marked group isomorphism. More precisely, if a polytope has universal flag stabilizer $H\le W$ and monodromy kernel $K=\operatorname{core}_W(H)$, a regular cover with normal universal kernel $N$ covers it exactly when $N\le K$. Normality turns containment in a conjugate of $H$ into containment in its core. The two pyramids therefore have the same admissible regular-cover kernels and the same minimal kernels. This also explains why merely knowing an unmarked group isomorphism would be insufficient; the retained theorem supplies the marking.

## Formal verification and limitations

I read all three actual Lean files. The four new statements prove disjoint support triviality, the 0-or-1 congruence step, injectivity on a subgroup from a trivial restricted kernel, and transport of the fixed intersection test. They have the stated hypotheses and do not assume the conclusion under another name. The three retained coprime pullback statements and six retained word-kernel statements retain their earlier scope. Fresh phase239 replay compiled all thirteen declarations and reported only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

The runner rejects an incorrect affine reflection, the omission of the center constraint, and three false weakenings of the new Lean hypotheses. Their mathematical necessity can also be seen without the compiler: equal support points admit a nontrivial supported function; modulus one admits the coordinate one; and a trivial map from $C_2$ is not injective on its whole group. The independent orientation calculation and independent cell calculation are separate implementations, while the author replay checks the frozen complete path.

The affine action, all-parameter geometric bridge, finite GAP algorithms, topology, retained toroidal classification and complete application are not Lean formalized. Cached imported Lean objects remain trusted dependencies. This acceptance adds no complete historical-source replacement and licenses no compression ratio or general finite-conjecture solution. Phase224 is a specialization of the accepted phase239 theorem, not another independent family or a second copy of the same question result.
