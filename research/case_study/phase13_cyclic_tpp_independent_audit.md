# Independent written review of the smallest-factor-eight theorem

Review date: 10 October 2026. Reviewer: the `recent_conjectures` research agent, distinct from the proof author. This is an internal mathematical review, not external peer review or a priority determination.

**Result: no gap found in the reviewed written argument.** The reviewed proof is [phase13_cyclic_tpp.md](phase13_cyclic_tpp.md), SHA256 `ef5df3b894fa87bab382a290be3075859612081686ed1bf91fcce0cc81412835`. The companion JSON binds the exact proof, retained power-layer note, checker and saved report. No independent execution of the checker or of the SmallGroups catalogue is claimed by this review.

The principal new step is Section 2. In the normal powerful subgroup, the highest nontrivial power subgroup is characteristic and elementary abelian. The published powerful-group generation/factorization input, together with the assumed bijective marked coordinates, makes its dimension exactly the number of marked factors attaining the largest order. Restricting an injective multiplication map is legitimate here; the proof does not infer that injectivity from TPP alone. A one-dimensional layer gives a marked central involution. For dimension two, the image of the ambient 2-group in GL₂(F₂) has order at most two. The images of the two pair-generators fix different basis vectors. If both images were nontrivial, they would coincide and fix the whole basis; if one is trivial, one of the marked basis vectors is fixed by both generators. Each case contradicts the retained center-disjointness hypothesis. The resulting equality of all three nontrivial marked orders follows.

I checked Sections 3–4 against the retained minimum-counterexample and power-layer argument. A central quotient absorbs into its distinguished factor; intersections with proper pair-generated subgroups and maximal subgroups, and uniform powering, do not increase any factor order. Thus every appeal to smaller-order minimality remains inside the class with a factor of order at most eight. The smallest-factor-four exclusion is retained as its structural theorem, rather than inferred by an unjustified unrestricted-minimum assumption. The use of the powerful eighth-power subgroup is consequently legitimate in both minima. If it is trivial the order is 256 and the retained finite theorem applies; otherwise Section 2 excludes an order-eight factor. For an unrestricted minimum, the same argument forces all orders to equal 2ᵉ with e≥4.

The finite consequences follow with the stated cutoffs: a minimum counterexample would have order 2^(3e−1)≥2048, so all orders at most 1024 are covered. The hypertope deduction uses the previously proved index-eight derived-group reduction and halves the distinguished pair orders. It therefore covers a pair order at most sixteen and hypertope group order at most 2¹³. Flag transitivity/typed Helly remains a retained input to that reduction; it is not replaced by the intersection property alone.

The review boundary is explicit. The phase-twelve powerful-subgroup theorem, the published Lubotzky–Mann power results, the marked isobicyclic input in the smallest-factor-four theorem, and the existing SmallGroups-through-256 certificate are retained dependencies. This review rechecks their use and the restricted-minimality interface; it does not independently re-prove all those sources or rerun the catalogue. The unrestricted cyclic-TPP inequality for 2-groups and the full hypertope lower-bound conjecture remain unresolved by this note.
