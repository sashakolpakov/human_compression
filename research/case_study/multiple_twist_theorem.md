# Prescribing intersections by several elementary twists

This theorem extends the single-transvection calculation to simultaneous
centralizer conditions. It supplies a concrete way to remove the excess
intersection left by one transvection in dimension greater than two.
The proof is elementary and has been independently checked within this
session. Publication priority is unverified.

## Theorem

Let E,F be subfields of K, let L extend K, and put k=E∩F. Let
N_1,...,N_r be n-by-n matrices over k satisfying N_i²=0. Suppose z∈L
has degree at least 3^r over K. Set

    t_i=z^(3^(i-1)),
    U=(I+t_1 N_1)...(I+t_r N_r).

Then, taking all groups inside GL_n(L),

    GL_n(E) ∩ U GL_n(F) U^(-1)
      = C_(GL_n(k))(N_1,...,N_r),

and likewise

    SL_n(E) ∩ U SL_n(F) U^(-1)
      = C_(SL_n(k))(N_1,...,N_r).

Neither commutativity of the N_i nor linear disjointness of E and F is
assumed. Each I+t_i N_i has determinant one.

## Proof

First treat T_1,...,T_r as independent indeterminates. Since N_i²=0,
(I+T_i N_i)^(-1)=I-T_i N_i. Thus every entry of U(T)^(-1) M U(T)
is a polynomial over K having degree at most two in each T_i, whenever
M has entries in E. Its constant term is M; its coefficient of T_i is
M N_i-N_i M.

Under T_i=z^(3^(i-1)), distinct monomials with exponents in {0,1,2}
become distinct powers z^j with 0≤j<3^r, by uniqueness of base-three
expansions. These powers are linearly independent over K by the degree
assumption.

If B=U^(-1)MU has entries in F, apply this independence to every entry
of U(T)^(-1) M U(T)-B. The constant coefficient gives M=B, so M has
entries in E∩F. The coefficients of T_i give M N_i=N_i M for all i.
Conversely any matrix over k commuting with every N_i commutes with U
and belongs to both subfield groups. Restricting to invertible matrices
or determinant-one matrices proves both identities. ∎

For finite fields, take K=F_Q and any L=F_(Q^d) with d≥3^r; a
multiplicatively primitive z has the required field degree. This is an
explicit finite construction, not a transcendental genericity assertion.

## A cyclic intersection in every dimension

Choose r=n-1 and N_i=E_(i,i+1), the adjacent upper matrix units. Comparing
entries in M N_i=N_i M shows that every row numbered 2 through n is
zero off its diagonal, every column numbered 1 through n-1 is zero
off its diagonal, and all diagonal entries agree. Hence the common
centralizer in the full matrix algebra is exactly

    { aI+bE_(1,n) : a,b∈k }.

For n≥2 its determinant is a^n. The SL intersection is therefore

    { aI+bE_(1,n) : a,b∈k, a^n=1 }.

When all scalar n-th roots of unity in L belong to k, passage to projective
images is legitimate and the intersection is exactly the root group

    { [I+cE_(1,n)] : c∈k }.

For k=F_p this is cyclic of order p. The condition on the projective
center is essential to this stated deduction; no unrestricted projective
claim is made when extra roots of unity enter L.

In particular, two explicit copies of SL_3(3) in SL_3(3^9), conjugated
by

    U=(I+zE_12)(I+z³E_23),

intersect exactly in {I+cE_13:c∈F_3}, of order three. Here the
projective center is trivial, so the same holds for PSL. The
[GAP verifier](multiple_twist_check.g) enumerates all 5,616 matrices
in the first SL_3(3) and tests membership in the other conjugated copy.

## Matrix-algebra centralizers and unit groups

The square-zero hypothesis still permits arbitrary matrix-algebra centralizer
conditions after doubling the dimension. Let A be the unital k-subalgebra of
Mat_d(k) generated by A_1,...,A_s. In dimension 2d use the s+2 square-zero
matrices

    N_0 = [[0,I],[0,0]],   N_1 = [[0,0],[I,0]],
    N_(j+1) = [[0,A_j],[0,0]]   (1<=j<=s).

Their common centralizer in Mat_(2d)(k) is exactly

    { diag(P,P) : P in C_(Mat_d(k))(A) }.

Indeed, writing a matrix in four d-by-d blocks, commutation with N_0 forces
the lower-left block to vanish and the two diagonal blocks to agree.
Commutation with N_1 then kills the upper-right block. The remaining
commutators are precisely P A_j=A_j P. Thus, with the theorem's extension
degree at least 3^(s+2),

    GL_(2d)(E) intersect U GL_(2d)(F) U^(-1)
      = { diag(P,P) : P in C_(GL_d(k))(A) }.

This supplies arbitrary algebra-centralizer conditions, not only root groups.
The SL version retains exactly those P with det(P)^2=1; this determinant
condition cannot be silently dropped.

In particular, let B be any finite-dimensional unital k-algebra and represent
it on its underlying vector space by left multiplication. Its commuting
linear maps are exactly right multiplications: a commuting map f satisfies
f(b)=b f(1). Consequently its invertible commutant is the group of right
multiplications by B's units, isomorphic to B^times by b |-> R_(b^(-1)).
Taking E=F=k above realizes a copy of B^times as the exact intersection of
two conjugate copies of GL_(2 dim_k B)(k) over a sufficiently large extension.
For finite k this is a finite-field construction. For arbitrary k the same
argument works over k(z) with z transcendental, using linear independence of
its powers. This corollary does not assert that every finite group is the unit
group of a finite-dimensional algebra.

An independent [GAP verifier](algebra_intersection_check.g) takes k=F_2,
A_1=[[0,1],[1,1]], and L=F_(2^27). It enumerates all 20,160 elements of
GL_4(2) and checks that the conjugate intersection is exactly the three
matrices diag(P,P) for P in {I,A_1,A_1^2}, realizing F_4^times.
The [saved output](algebra_intersection_check.log) records a successful run.

## Scope

This computes the prescribed local intersection. It does not establish
that the two copies generate the entire ambient SL group, or that
specific generators satisfy polytope or hypertope relations. Those remain
separate mathematical requirements. The parameter-degree cost 3^r is
sufficient and is not claimed optimal. No Lean formalization of the
multivariate substitution argument is claimed here; the related
single-transvection theorem is formalized separately.
