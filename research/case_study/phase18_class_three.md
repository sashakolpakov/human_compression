# All class-three path markings: an exact transfer formula

10 October 2026. Phase18 author proof; internal review and diagnostic
records are separate from the uniform mathematical argument.
All phase17 artifacts remain frozen. The marking is ordered, consists
of generating involutions, has the path commutations and actual IP,
and has no adjacent product of order two. Throughout d>=3.

**Theorem.** The number c_k(d) of these finite two-groups of class at
most three and order 2^(2d−1+k) is the coefficient of x^k in

    F_d(x) = sum_(a in {0,1}^(d−1))
               x^(sum a_i) product_(i=1)^(d−2) (x+2^(a_i+a_(i+1))).   (1)

Equivalently,

    F_d(x) = [1,x] M(x)^(d−2) [1;1],
    M(x) = [[x+1, x(x+2)], [x+2, x(x+4)]].                            (2)

This is a complete uniform parameter classification, not a count
interpolated from finite computations. In particular

    c_0(d)=1,  c_1(d)=5d−10,
    c_2(d)=(25d^2−125d+162)/2.                                       (3)

The unrestricted excess-two census remains separate: class-four
groups are not counted by (1).

## 1. The central kernel and its local generators

We retain the [forest canonical-quotient theorem](phase15_graded_support.md)
and the elementary seed/central-sum construction proved in
[phase17](phase17_k1_classification.md). Put K=gamma3(G). Since the
group has class at most three, K is central, and G/K is the standard
class-two path group C_d of order 2^(2d−1).

Write c_i=[r_i,r_(i+1)], alpha_i=c_i^2 and
beta_i=[c_i,r_(i+2)]. These lie in K. Endpoint involutions invert c_i,
so centrality of c_i^2 forces c_i^4=1. Also a central commutator with
an involution has square one. The class-three Hall–Witt calculation
gives the same table as phase17: the commutators of c_i with its two
endpoints are alpha_i; with its neighboring outside markers they
are beta_(i−1),beta_i; all other values are trivial. Thus K is
elementary abelian, generated by all alpha_i and beta_i. We write it
additively as an F2-vector space of dimension k.

Let E_i=<alpha_i>, and let N_i=<alpha_i,alpha_(i+1),beta_i> for
1<=i<=d−2. These are respectively gamma3 of the edge and triple
parabolics. The canonical-quotient restriction gives

    K intersect G_S = gamma3(G_S)                                   (4)

for every marked subset S: both global and local forest quotients
are the same induced class-two forest group. In particular (4)
applies to every edge and triple below. Adjacent E_i,E_(i+1) have
trivial intersection by actual IP and the fact that no nontrivial
element of K belongs to a one-marker parabolic.

Put a_i=dim(E_i), either zero or one, and define b_i to be zero if
beta_i lies in E_i+E_(i+1), and one otherwise. Then

    dim(N_i)=a_i+a_(i+1)+b_i.                                       (5)

## 2. Actual IP forces a global basis

For i>=2, let Q_(i−1)=N_1+...+N_(i−1). This is an actual subgroup,
because K is central. It lies in the parabolic on vertices 1,...,i+1;
N_i lies in the parabolic on i,i+1,i+2. By actual IP and (4),

    Q_(i−1) intersect N_i <= K intersect G_{i,i+1}=E_i.

Conversely E_i is contained in N_(i−1) and N_i. Therefore equality
holds. Since K=N_1+...+N_(d−2), repeated dimension addition yields

    k = sum_(i=1)^(d−1) a_i + sum_(i=1)^(d−2) b_i.                  (6)

Let A={i:a_i=1} and B={i:b_i=1}. The following marked vectors span K:

    alpha_i for i in A,  and beta_i for i in B.                      (7)

Indeed every omitted beta belongs to its two adjacent E spaces.
There are exactly k vectors in (7), by (6); hence they form a basis.
This is where actual IP prevents hidden global dependencies. Merely
declaring locally new beta values independent would not suffice.

Consequently the complete candidate data are:

* a subset A of the d−1 edges;
* a subset B of the d−2 triples;
* for every i not in B, an arbitrary vector beta_i in the span of
  the active adjacent basis vectors alpha_i,alpha_(i+1).

The basis vectors beta_i for i in B require no further coefficient
choices: they are *defined* to be the corresponding marked beta
values in (7). We do not choose an external basis and then count its
changes as different markings. For i not in B there are exactly
2^(a_i+a_(i+1)) choices.

## 3. Every candidate exists and has actual IP

Take all elementary edge and triple covers E_T of the same marked
C_d from phase17, and form their fiber product F. Its kernel has
the independent basis z_T indexed by every edge and triple. The
diagonal marker lifts generate F, since each parameter word isolates
exactly one z_T. Every parabolic satisfies

    F_S intersect ker(F -> C_d) = span{z_T:T subset S}.              (8)

For candidate data (A,B), create an abstract vector space V with
basis e_i for i in A and f_i for i in B. Send the edge coordinate
z_{edge i} to e_i or zero according as i belongs to A. Send the
triple coordinate to f_i if i belongs to B, and otherwise to its
chosen linear combination of the adjacent e coordinates. This
defines a surjective linear map L from the fiber kernel to V.
Quotient F by ker(L), obtaining G. The markers still generate,
|G|=|C_d|*2^k, the kernel V is central and equals gamma3(G), and
the prescribed alpha/beta values hold.

By (8), the kernel of a parabolic G_S is the image under L of the
indicated coordinate span. A dependent beta belonging to S only
uses edge coordinates whose endpoints are also in S. Thus this
kernel is exactly

    V_S = span{e_i:edge i subset S}
            + span{f_i:triple i subset S}.                          (9)

These are coordinate subspaces of a genuine basis, so
V_S intersect V_T=V_(S intersect T). This proves actual IP in G.
Explicitly, for g in G_S intersect G_T, quotient IP in C_d gives
h in G_(S intersect T) with the same image. Then g*h^(-1) lies in
V_S intersect V_T=V_(S intersect T), which is contained in
G_(S intersect T); hence g belongs to that parabolic. The reverse
inclusion is immediate. This argument is valid for higher-dimensional
kernels and makes no false interchange of intersection and quotient.

Finally the marked data determine the group uniquely. Present the
markers and the k central involutory basis vectors, impose the
string and involution relations, the assigned c_i^2 values and the
class-three commutator table. Modulo the k central generators,
collection gives at most |C_d| elements. The presented group thus
has order at most |C_d|*2^k, while the constructed quotient has that
order. Equality proves the complete presentation. Any ordered marked
isomorphism preserves the actual alpha/beta words, so recovers A,B
and every dependent coefficient. Different candidate data cannot
represent the same ordered marking.

## 4. Count and the excess-two coefficient

The cost of each active edge is one dimension. For each triple i,
the intrinsic option b_i=1 has cost one and exactly one coefficient
choice; the option b_i=0 has cost zero and 2^(a_i+a_(i+1)) choices.
This proves (1). Recording the last edge bit gives the transition
matrix (2), with initial weight [1,x] and terminal summation [1;1].

For k=2 there are three disjoint cases. Put n=d−2. With no active
edge and two intrinsic triples, the count is binomial(d−2,2).
With one active edge e and one intrinsic triple, let m_e be the
number of triples containing e. It is one for either end edge and
two for each interior edge. Summing over the chosen intrinsic triple
gives m_e*2^(m_e−1)+(n−m_e)*2^m_e. Thus this case contributes

    2(1+2(d−3)) + (d−3)*4(d−3) = 4d^2−20d+26.

With two active edges and no intrinsic triple, the weight of the
pair e,f is 2^(m_e+m_f), including when the edges are adjacent.
The edge weights are 2,4,...,4,2, so their second elementary
symmetric sum is

    ((4d−8)^2 − (8+16(d−3)))/2 = 8d^2−40d+52.

Adding the three expressions proves (3) for every d>=3. These
coefficients are derived from the complete parameter classification,
not fitted to finite data.

There is also a closed count across *all* excesses. Let F_n denote
the Fibonacci numbers with F_0=0 and F_1=1. Then the number of all
ordered marked class-at-most-three groups of rank d in this category
is F_(4d−5). To see this, put x=1 in (2). The matrix M(1) is conjugate
by the coordinate swap to Q^4, where Q=[[1,1],[1,0]]. The row and
column [1,1] are fixed by that swap and equal e_1^t Q and Q e_1.
Thus F_d(1)=e_1^t Q^(4d−6)e_1=F_(4d−5). Here F_d(x) is the
generating polynomial, while F_n without an argument denotes the
Fibonacci sequence. This is again a proved identity, not a fit.

For a rational generating function, extend the polynomial definition
to d=2 by F_2(x)=1+x. Since tr(M)=1+5x+x^2 and det(M)=x^2,
Cayley–Hamilton gives

    F_(d+2)(x)=(1+5x+x^2)F_(d+1)(x)−x^2 F_d(x)  (d>=2),
    sum_(d>=2) F_d(x)t^(d−2)
      = (1+x−xt)/(1−(1+5x+x^2)t+x^2 t^2).                         (10)

Here the d=2 term is an algebraic boundary value; the theorem was
stated for d>=3. The initial next polynomial is
F_3(x)=1+5x+6x^2+x^3, so the numerator follows directly.

## 5. Scope and remaining excess-two work

The theorem classifies every class-at-most-three group in the stated
marked category, for every rank and every excess. It does not assert
that all groups of excess two have class at most three: their
gamma3 can be noncentral. Such class-four groups require a separate
analysis. No general fixed-excess coefficients beyond this subclass,
unmarked census, publication priority or complete Lean proof is
asserted here.

The only retained inputs are the proved forest quotient/restriction
and the explicit central-join/seed fiber constructions. The global
basis argument and all coefficient counts are proved above. The
bounded diagnostics below are not inputs to the uniform argument.

## 6. Finite diagnostics and reproduction

The [GAP/NQ checker](phase18_class_three_check.g) tests rank-two
central parameter quotients. It builds every such quotient at d=3,4
(7 and 155 respectively), and all 81 predicted IP quotients at d=5.
It does not build the 2,586 predicted rejected rank-five groups.
Across these 243 actual groups, it verifies 5,128 parabolic orders
and 123,072 actual subgroup intersections. There are 118 IP groups
and 125 rejected groups. The accepted counts are 6,31,81, agreeing
with c_2(d). See the [report](phase18_class_three_check.json) and
[transcript](phase18_class_three_check.log).

A separate [author linear checker](phase18_class_three_linear_check.py)
tests all 2,829 surjective rank-two kernel maps modulo GL(2,2) in
these ranks, using 2,771,136 subspace intersections. It includes the
rank-five rejected candidates omitted by the GAP calculation.

The [independent checker](phase18_class_three_independent_linear_check.py)
instead enumerates **every** subspace R of the universal kernel,
at every excess, and directly tests
(R+W_S) intersect (R+W_T)=R+W_(S intersect T). It does not use the
candidate-classification filter. Its [report](phase18_class_three_independent_linear_check.json)
records all 16,374,29,212 subspaces for ranks 3,4,5 and 4,320,898
intersection tests, stopping early when a candidate fails. The
accepted coefficient vectors are respectively

    [1,5,6,1],
    [1,10,31,35,11,1],
    [1,15,81,195,211,90,16,1].

These two linear calculations are diagnostics of the central-cover
vector-space model; they are not separate group enumerations.
The GAP run uses GAP 4.15.1 and NQ 2.5.11. From this directory:

    gap -q -T phase18_class_three_check.g
    python3 phase18_class_three_linear_check.py --output /tmp/phase18_rank_two.json
    python3 phase18_class_three_independent_linear_check.py --output /tmp/phase18_all_subspaces.json

The saved GAP transcript removes its leading carriage return only.
The scripts contain no group-catalogue or finite-census input.
