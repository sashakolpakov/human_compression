# Bounded cores for string C-groups of fixed excess

10 October 2026. New private proposed uniform results. Earlier frozen
files are unchanged. Independent review and diagnostic computation are
separate records; publication priority is not determined here.

Let G be a finite two-group with a nondegenerate string C-group marking
r_1,...,r_d, d>=3. Write

    |G|=2^(2d-1+k),    K=gamma3(G).

The retained [forest theorem](phase15_graded_support.md) gives
|K|=2^k and identifies G/K with the marked class-two path-cocycle group
C_d. The results below go beyond that order and class bound.

**Theorem.** There is a uniquely determined smallest marked support J
of K. It has at most 3k vertices and at most k connected path intervals.
If I is the closed path neighborhood of J and H=G_I, then |I|<=5k and

    K=gamma3(H),    |H|<=2^(11k-1)  if k>0.

The complete marked group G is determined by I and the marked group H:
use its full presentation on the I-generators, the path involution and
distant-commutation relations, product order dividing four on every
edge not wholly in I, and class-two relations on every consecutive
triple not wholly in I. This presentation is isomorphic to G.

For fixed k, the number of ordered marked isomorphism classes of such
groups of rank d is Theta_k(d^k), retaining the explicit construction
of Cunningham--Feng--Hou--Schulte for the lower bound. This is a necessary finite-core encoding,
not an assertion that arbitrary bounded cores have valid attachments.

## 1. Marked support and the general support-cost lemma

For any subgroup U of a group with marked IP, define

    supp(U)=intersection of all A with U<=G_A.

There are finitely many marked generators. Repeated actual IP gives
U<=G_supp(U); hence this is the unique smallest support. Supports are
monotone under subgroup inclusion, and the support of a generated
subgroup is the union of the supports of its generators.

**Support-cost lemma.** Let P be a finite p-group generated by a finite
marking having actual IP. Suppose a normal subgroup N is normally generated by
elements each supported on at most w marked generators. Then

    |supp(N)| <= w log_p |N|.                         (1)

More precisely, if U_0 is the ordinary subgroup generated by all those
seed elements and l=log_p|U_0|, then

    |supp(N)| <= log_p|N|+(w-1)l.                    (2)

To prove this, choose a subset of the seed elements generating U_0 by
successively adding a seed outside the subgroup already generated.
Each addition increases the order by at least p. There are therefore
at most l chosen seeds, so |supp(U_0)|<=wl.

Given U<=N, put J=supp(U) and

    U^+=<U,[u,r_v] : u in U, all marked v>.

Every displayed commutator is supported on J union {v}. Call v fresh
if v is outside J and at least one such commutator lies outside G_J.
Then supp(U^+) is exactly J together with the fresh vertices. For each
fresh v choose x_v in G_(J union {v}) outside G_J. Set H=U^+ and order
the fresh vertices arbitrarily as v_1,...,v_s. The subgroups

    H intersect G_(J union {v_1,...,v_j}),   0<=j<=s,

form a chain with every inclusion strict. Indeed x_(v_j) belongs to
the jth subgroup, and actual IP shows that its membership in the
previous one would place it in G_J, a contradiction. Hence

    p^s <= |H : H intersect G_J| <= |H:U|.            (3)

Iterate U -> U^+. Every strict step increases order; a stationary step
is invariant under conjugation by the marked generators and therefore
normal. (For general markings, conjugation is injective on the finite
stationary subgroup, and hence surjective.) The process terminates
at the normal closure N. Summing (3) bounds all new support vertices by
log_p|N:U_0|, proving (2) and (1). No quotient IP, commuting product or
unproved multiplication injectivity is used.

## 2. Applying the lemma to gamma3

In any generated group, gamma3 is normally generated by the basic
weight-three commutators [[r_i,r_j],r_l]. One direct verification is
to kill all of these normally: every basic generator commutator then
commutes with every generator, so the resulting group is class two.

For a string marking, every nontrivial seed has a raw support that is
an edge or a consecutive triple: the first commutator vanishes on
nonedges, and a generator farther than one vertex from that edge
commutes with both its endpoints. The support of a nontrivial seed is
also connected. In fact a disconnected subset of an edge or triple
consists of at most two nonadjacent vertices. Its parabolic is elementary
abelian and meets G' trivially, since the marked generators have
independent images in G/G'. Thus no nontrivial seed in gamma3 can have
such support.

Use w=3 in (1). It gives |J|<=3k. In the proof there are initially at
most l<=k connected seed supports. A fresh vertex in (3) must be adjacent
to J; otherwise its marked generator commutes with every generator of
G_J and hence with U. Adding adjacent vertices cannot increase the
number of components. Consequently J has at most k intervals.

For k=0, J is empty and G is exactly C_d. In what follows suppose k>0.
Its closed neighborhood I has at most |J|+2k<=5k vertices.

## 3. Restriction of the canonical kernel

For every marked subset A, not necessarily an interval,

    K intersect G_A = gamma3(G_A).                  (4)

Indeed, the image of G_A in the canonical cocycle quotient has exactly
2^(|A|+e(A)) elements, where e(A) is the number of path edges with both
ends in A. The forest theorem applied to the marked subgroup G_A gives
the same order for G_A/gamma3(G_A). The natural quotient map and the
containment gamma3(G_A)<=K then force equality of the kernels.

Since K<=G_J<=H=G_I, equation (4) gives K=gamma3(H). If I has h vertices
and c components, e(I)=h-c. Thus

    |H|=2^(2h-c+k)<=2^(11k-1).                      (5)

Every marked subgroup on a set disjoint from J has trivial gamma3 and
is exactly its corresponding class-two forest-cocycle group. In
particular, the complementary path intervals are completely specified
class-two segments. This statement identifies their marked parabolics;
it does not assert that G is their direct product with the core.

There is a further decomposition of the kernel itself. If the intervals
of J are J_1,...,J_t, then their parabolics commute and have trivial
mutual intersection, so G_J is their internal direct product. Hence (4)
gives

    K = product_j gamma3(G_(J_j)).                  (6)

Each factor is normal in G: conjugation by a marked generator in J_j
preserves it; a generator in another component commutes with it; and
for a generator outside J the commutator lies both in K<=G_J and in
G_(J_j union {v}), hence in G_(J_j), by actual IP. If its order is
2^k_j, then sum k_j=k. Minimality of J and (6) show that the support of
gamma3(G_(J_j)) is all of J_j. Applying the support-cost lemma to that
marked parabolic therefore gives |J_j|<=3k_j. Thus even the bounded kernel splits
along the exceptional intervals.

## 4. Exact reconstruction from a bounded core

Take J and I as above, and retain the complete marked multiplication
data of H=G_I. A finite presentation on its marked generators exists;
no efficient bound on the number of its relators is presumed.

Define W on r_1,...,r_d by the following relations:

1. Each r_i is an involution, and distant generators commute.
2. Impose a full marked presentation of H on the generators indexed by I.
3. If an edge is not wholly in I, impose (r_i r_(i+1))^4=1.
4. If a consecutive triple T is not wholly in I, impose that its subgroup
   is class at most two; equivalently impose every basic weight-three
   commutator on those three generators.

All these relations hold in G. For item 3, an edge not wholly in I
contains no vertex of J, so its fourth-power kernel is trivial by (4).
For item 4, a triple not wholly in I contains at most one vertex of J:
any triple with two J-vertices lies in the closed neighborhood of J.
Equation (4), actual IP and K intersect <r_j>=1 then make its gamma3
trivial. Thus there is a marked surjection W->G.

The natural map H->W is injective because its composite H->W->G is
the original inclusion. We may identify H and its subgroup K_H=gamma3(H)
inside W. In H, K_H is generated by words on J, since it equals K in G.
Every generator outside I commutes with every J-generator. Therefore
it centralizes K_H; H normalizes K_H by definition. It follows that
K_H is normal in W.

In W/K_H, every consecutive triple is class two, by item 4 or by the
class-two core quotient. Every edge product has order dividing four,
by item 3 or the same core quotient. Each edge commutator consequently
commutes with its two endpoints and both neighboring vertices; it
commutes with all farther generators by the string relations. Thus
W/K_H is class two and is a quotient of C_d. On the other hand W->G
induces W/K_H->G/K=C_d. Hence both maps are isomorphisms. We conclude

    |W|=|K_H| |C_d|=|G|,

and the surjection W->G is an isomorphism. This proves the exact
presentation assertion, including finiteness of W. No assumption that
an arbitrary presented candidate has IP occurs in the proof.

## 5. Polynomially many marked groups at fixed excess

For k>0 the possible sets J have at most k runs and at most 3k vertices.
The number having s vertices and t runs is

    binomial(s-1,t-1) binomial(d-s+1,t),

with impossible choices interpreted as zero. Summing over 1<=t<=k
and t<=s<=3k is O_k(d^k). Once J is specified, I and its ordering are
known. There are finitely many marked groups H with at most 5k marked
generators and order at most 2^(11k-1), a number depending only on k.
Section 4 shows that each such core yields at most one global marked
group in the intended class. Multiplication gives the claimed bound.

This is not an efficient enumeration algorithm for the bounded cores,
nor a sufficiency criterion for them. Inconsistent cores or attachments
can collapse the core, lower the excess or fail IP; the reconstruction
theorem asserts uniqueness for the cores extracted from actual groups.
The ranks and lengths of the intervening class-two segments remain
unbounded, but do not create additional presentation parameters.

### An effective finite acceptance procedure

For each fixed k>0, one can precompute a finite library of candidate
cores by enumerating two-group multiplication tables of order at most
2^(11k-1), together with at most 5k ordered generating involutions. Retain
only markings with the required induced-forest IP and noncommuting
edges, whose gamma3 has order 2^k and is supported on the designated
subset J. For a given d, enumerate the finitely many positions of J
allowed by section 2, take I to be its closed neighborhood, and form
the presentation W of section 4 from every corresponding core.

This presentation is finite even before any acceptance decision.
The image L of gamma3(H) in W is normal: it is represented by J-words,
the outside-I generators centralize it, and H normalizes it. Hence
|L|<=2^k. The same local class-two argument gives W/L as a quotient
of C_d. Conversely the canonical forest quotient H->C_I satisfies
every core relator inside C_d, so all the defining relations of W
hold in C_d and W/L maps onto C_d. Thus

    W/L=C_d,    |W|<=2^(2d-1+k).

Use exact finite-group arithmetic to accept precisely those W having
the full displayed order and every marked intersection equality.
These tests are finite. For example, enumerate all group tables up to
the proved bound, with generating marked tuples satisfying the finite
presentation. Every resulting group is a quotient of W, and W itself
occurs because it is finite within the bound. A solution of maximum
order is therefore isomorphic to W. This deliberately elementary
procedure proves effectiveness; no computational efficiency is claimed. Full order
also forces H to embed, since any kernel of H->W lies in gamma3(H)
and its image already has order 2^k. Nondegeneracy follows from the
quotient C_d. Completeness is section 4, and every accepted group has
exactly the required order and IP by the acceptance tests themselves.

This supplies an exhaustive fixed-library construction and verification
framework. It does not remove the final order/IP checks or assert that
all candidate cores attach. For k=0 the library and acceptance are
unnecessary: the unique group is C_d.

## 6. A sharper local excess budget

Write D_j=<(r_j r_(j+1))^4> and a_j=log2|D_j|. For each consecutive
triple put N_i=gamma3(G_[i,i+2]) and e_i=log2|N_i|. By (4) inside that
triple, its intersection with either edge subgroup is exactly the
corresponding D. The edge kernels D_i,D_(i+1) intersect trivially, so

    b_i=e_i-a_i-a_(i+1)>=0.

The following product cardinality is exact, without assuming the
product is a subgroup:

    |N_1 ... N_(d-2)|
       = product_i |N_i| / product_(j=2)^(d-2) |D_j|.

Induct on the number of factors. The earlier product is contained in
G_[1,i+1] and is invariant under right multiplication by D_i, since
its last factor N_(i-1) contains D_i. Its multiplication with N_i has
fibers of size exactly |D_i|: equality of two products puts the
intermediate difference in N_i intersect G_[1,i+1]=D_i. This proves
the formula. Since the product is contained in K,

    sum_j a_j + sum_i b_i <= k.                     (7)

In particular at most 2k consecutive triples have nontrivial gamma3;
an edge with a_j>0 affects at most its two incident triples, and a
triple with b_i>0 accounts for at least one further budget unit. If
all edge orders are four, there are at most k exceptional triples.

This bound includes a familiar order lower bound from overlapping
rank-three sections. Its use here is as a local defect budget, not a
priority claim for the numerical product inequality.

## 7. Sharpness and source connection

The primary source Cunningham--Feng--Hou--Schulte,
*Symmetric polytopes whose automorphism groups are 2-groups*,
[arXiv:2512.15511v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15511v1), Theorem 5.1,
constructs regular polytopes with prescribed toroidal triple sections
of orders 2^(n_3),...,2^(n_d), and total order
2^(sum n_j-3(d-3)). Here every edge order is four and e_i=n_(i+2)-5,
so these constructions attain equality in (7): k=sum(n_j-5).
The construction also supplies a matching count. Set exactly k of the
d-2 values n_3,...,n_d equal to six and all the others equal to five.
Its groups have excess k. Distinct choices give different sequences of
marked triple subgroup orders, and therefore distinct ordered marked
isomorphism classes. There are binomial(d-2,k) choices. Together with
section 5, this proves Theta_k(d^k) for fixed positive k and d tending
to infinity. For k=0 there is exactly one class, C_d. The convention is
that a marked isomorphism sends each r_i to the corresponding r_i;
only the upper/lower growth order, not an exact census polynomial, is
asserted.

All four bounds in the displayed theorem are sharp for every k>=1.
Take d=5k, and choose the k order-64 sections to start at vertices
2,7,...,5k-3; give every other triple order32. Each order-64 triple has
a kernel of order two. Since its two edge products have order four,
that nontrivial kernel meets either edge parabolic trivially, and its
marked support is the entire triple. Consequently J contains the 3k
selected vertices. The upper bound |J|<=3k forces equality. They form
k disjoint triples, and their closed neighborhoods cover all 5k
vertices. Thus |I|=5k and |H|=|G|=2^(11k-1). The kernel-support and
component bounds, the neighborhood bound and the core-order bound
cannot be decreased in general.

The source's Theorem 5.3 describes additional disjoint-edge commutator
relators for its construction; that theorem or its order calculation
is not needed for the lower bound or sharpness above. Our theorem does
not replace the retained Theorem 5.1 construction, its flatness
assertions or its sufficiency proof. It instead applies to every finite
two-group string C-group, including groups outside that family.

## 8. Dependencies and limits

The retained forest theorem and elementary finite-group support/IP facts
are the mathematical inputs. Neither a finite-group catalogue nor
finite diagnostics are used to prove these uniform statements. No full
Lean formalization, complete classification of bounded cores or verified
publication-priority claim is made.

## 9. Separate finite diagnostics and a failed shortcut

The original GAP/NQ script [phase16_fixed_excess_check.g](phase16_fixed_excess_check.g)
constructs six class-three nilpotent quotients with explicit path
relations. Their ranks are 3,5,6,7,5,5 and their excesses are 1,1,2,2,1,0.
It verifies all 23616 ordered subset intersections, 296 canonical-kernel
restriction equalities, the actual minimal kernel supports, component
and neighborhood bounds, and the exact local product formula. The
rank-five example with its only triple defect in the middle attains
|J|=3, |I|=5 and |H|=1024 at k=1. The examples are diagnostics, not
evidence of exhaustion at any order or excess.

The separate [phase16_hidden_kernel_check.g](phase16_hidden_kernel_check.g)
constructs one rank-four class-four group of order 1024 and verifies
all 256 marked subset intersections. Its two local triple kernels
have order two, but their ordinary generated subgroup U has order
four, whereas |gamma3(G)|=8. Their normal closure is gamma3(G), and

    [[r_1,r_2],r_3,r_4] in gamma3(G) \ U

is an explicit involution. Thus one cannot replace the normal-closure
steps by ordinary generation by the local kernels, nor assume equality
in (7). The checker defines the group by the displayed finite
presentation and a class-five nilpotent quotient, which it finds has
class four. No identification with a universal polytope or a source
group is needed for this finite warning.

The scripts retain GAP 4.15.1 and NQ 2.5.11 as exact arithmetic inputs.
Their saved transcripts and hash-bound reports describe only these
finite checks. Independent proof reviews are separate records.
