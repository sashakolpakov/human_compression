# Independent written review of the rank-three form alternative

10 October 2026. **PASS**, under the retained ambient-group facts and the cited rank-three intersection theorem. The accompanying JSON binds the reviewed proof and matrix sources. This review does not include a new execution of the exploratory probes, a formal verification, or a publication-priority finding.

## Linear-algebra argument

The algebraically closed, odd-characteristic setting permits separate rescaling of each involution lift to square one. If endpoint lifts satisfy AC=λCA, their squares imply λ²=1. Thus the commuting and anticommuting cases exhaust the possibilities.

For commuting endpoints, simultaneous diagonalization is valid. Invariant symmetric forms have dimension Σ d_j(d_j+1)/2, which is four only for four one-dimensional simultaneous weight spaces and at least five otherwise. The invariant symmetric space for the middle lift has dimension six for multiplicities (2,2), and seven for (3,1). Intersection dimensions inside the ten-dimensional symmetric space therefore prove every claimed form-producing case. The sole configuration left by this calculation is four separate endpoint weights and middle multiplicities (2,2).

For anticommuting endpoints, the normal form with A=diag(I₂,−I₂) and C swapping the blocks is valid: C identifies the equal-dimensional eigenspaces of A and C²=I removes the second identification matrix. A skew form anti-invariant under A has blocks [0,S;−Sᵀ,0]; anti-invariance under C is precisely S=Sᵀ. This gives dimension three. For a balanced middle involution, anti-invariant skew forms have dimension four. Their intersection in dimension six is nonzero. This leaves only middle multiplicities (3,1) as the alternative not excluded by the form calculation.

The radical of a form transformed by a nonzero multiplier is invariant under every generator. Irreducibility makes a nonzero such form nondegenerate. This step is valid for both symmetric and alternating forms and does not require a finite group. Rescaling lifts affects multipliers only. The argument gives necessary configurations for a group having no ordinary bilinear similarity form, not sufficient generation criteria.

## Actual frozen matrices and the two folds

I checked the displayed matrices in phase sixteen and both phase-seventeen notes, not only the abstract string relations. In each family r₀ and r₂ commute as matrices. The linear r₁r₃ is diag(−1,1,−1,−1), while the unitary product is the transposition of coordinates 1 and 3. They are projective reflections. For the first fold, the middle symmetric-invariant space therefore has dimension seven; even the smallest endpoint space has dimension four, forcing a nonzero symmetric form if the fold is irreducible.

For the second fold, r₁ and r₃ commute as matrices and r₁ has an exceptional one-dimensional eigenspace. That eigenspace supplies one endpoint character, while the complementary three-space supplies at most two. Hence there are at most three simultaneous characters and the endpoint symmetric-form space has dimension at least five. The middle lift r₀r₂ is a nonidentity projective involution, so its invariant symmetric space has dimension at least six. Again the intersection is nonzero.

The twisted unitary lift can be a Hermitian anti-isometry; this causes no problem. This proof concerns ordinary bilinear forms over the algebraic closure, and the required rescalings exist there. The retained phase-nine proof excludes every ordinary bilinear similarity form for the full ambient groups and establishes their absolute irreducibility, including q=3. Thus both reducible folds and the irreducible form-preserving folds are proper subgroups. The conclusion is uniform in all parameters covered by the frozen constructions.

## The rank-reduction and rank-three intersection inputs

I reread Brooksbank–Leemans, arXiv:1812.01055v2, pp.1–2: Theorem 1.1 assumes an irreducible rank-at-least-four string C-group and the explicit membership of r₀ in ⟨r₀r₂,r₃⟩; Corollary 1.2 gives the odd-order sufficient case. The source expressly allows success outside that sufficient condition. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01055).

The note's even-dihedral obstruction to that membership is correct. Actual IP gives the direct product ⟨r₀⟩×⟨r₂,r₃⟩. The character sending r₂ to one and r₃ to zero is well defined when |r₂r₃| is even. The reduced generators lie in its graph, which excludes the first-factor element r₀. The reversed marking gives the other endpoint obstruction. This alone would not prove properness of arbitrary folds; the independent form calculation supplies that conclusion here.

For the Conder–Oliveros input, I directly read Conder's primary slides, PDF pp.21–22. The stated sufficient theorem assumes a finite group generated by three involutions, endpoint product of order two, and both adjacent products of order at least three. Its proof sketch explicitly concerns the given generators' intersection condition, so the conclusion is about the same marking. The published theorem is also identified as Theorem 4.1 in the 2025 coauthored restatement, Theorem A. [Primary slides](https://www.fields.utoronto.ca/programs/scientific/13-14/discretegeom/Slides/Conder.pdf), [2025 primary record](https://elib.sfu-kras.ru/handle/2311/156158).

I did not retrieve and read the complete original 2013 article; its publisher full-page fetch returned HTTP 403. That access limitation is distinct from reading the author slides and the indexed 2025 theorem. The exact sufficient scope used in the note is supported by those primary materials.

A nontrivial cyclic normal subgroup of one of the almost-simple ambient groups intersects its simple socle trivially. The commutator of these two normal subgroups lies in that intersection, so the cyclic subgroup centralizes the socle. The retained trivial-centralizer fact excludes it. The cited intersection theorem therefore applies to any proposed generating triple satisfying the stated order conditions. Finiteness is required for this final input, although not for the linear-algebra alternative.

No part of the argument establishes uniform rank-three existence or nonexistence of the diagonal extensions. It excludes the two specific folds and restricts the linear-algebra configurations of future generating triples. No group spectrum or new paper-level classification follows solely from these restrictions.
