The theorem's connectedness condition explicitly requires connected transformed rank-two graph residues. Its later theorem6.1 treats the inverse flag-geometry construction. These are distinct source targets, not one automatic corollary. The printed application tables require their own input and diagram checks.

**Independent mathematical adapter, not a source summary.** In a finite-rank residually connected geometry satisfying the graph-shadow hypotheses, assume every residue of cotype{0,1} has a nonbipartite graph. Take its canonical parity cover. Any higher flag extends to one of those maximal higher flags, so its common graph shadow contains an odd cycle. Its monodromy is consequently all ofC2. Thus H and M in the reviewed cover theorem are automatic for every higher flag; each individual higher shadow also has one lifted component. The component construction therefore coincides with the unpartitioned neighborhood construction that leaves higher objects unsplit. It is residually connected, and every type-preserving original automorphism has its canonical color-preserving lift. Chamber transitivity transfers when present. This gives a direct route to the forward finite-rank RC component of the older theorem and explains why its explicit local connectedness condition avoids the later failed blanket assertion.

2. Prove the reverse connectedness statement and every asserted diagram identification, not merely the forward RC implication above.
3. Match the disjoint-endpoint iteration in4.1(iii), and prove the precise automorphism/transitivity statement.
4. Independently treat6.1 and its hypotheses; the graph-incidence expansion from our cover theorem is a plausible adapter but does not by itself identify every source assertion.

## 2. Remove a retained input: Danzer's power-complex construction

The [publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304020808728159) identifies L. Danzer, *Regular Incidence-Complexes and Dimensionally Unbounded Sequences of Such,I*, North-Holland Mathematics Studies87(1984),115–127, DOI10.1016/S0304-0208(08)72815-9. Its principal scope is broader than the binary generalized-cube construction retained by N013. The original chapter was not retrieved: publisher/PDF paths returned failures or HTML.

A coordinate proof of the binary construction would remove a concrete retained input from the existing N013 reduction. Its faces can be described as coordinate subcubes indexed by faces of the base complex; coordinate agreement gives intersections, and coordinate flips plus base automorphisms give the group action. This is an extension of the current coordinate-incidence machinery, but **not literally an application of the nested-shadow graph-cover theorem**: it changes rank and graph rather than covering a fixed graph. Before granting any old-paper credit, the original exact theorem set, lattice hypotheses, general complex cases and claimed infinite sequences would have to be read. The accessible later Duke–Schulte papers are primary sources for their own results, not substitutes for reading1984.

## 3. Further candidate: Schulte's facet-extension theorem

The [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0195669883800353) for E. Schulte, *On Arranging Regular Incidence-Complexes as Faces of Higher-Dimensional Ones*, European Journal of Combinatorics4(1983),375–384, DOI10.1016/S0195-6698(83)80035-3, states a regular-complex extension theorem preserving finite/nondegenerate inputs. The complete original body was not retrieved. Schulte's [2017 primary treatment](https://arxiv.org/html/1711.02297v1), section6, discusses the older group-extension mechanism, but that is a later source.

This target is less immediate. A graph cover preserves the existing rank; creating a new facet type requires a group extension and an independent incidence-poset/lattice argument. The current theorem may discharge a local connectedness step after a construction is identified, but it does not imply the full extension theorem merely by nesting. Pursue only after the original principal statements and the precise finite/nondegenerate conclusion are available.
