**Algebra and geometry.** For arbitrary subgroups A,B of a group H and normal M, the exact equivalence is

`q(A) ∩ q(B) = q(A ∩ B)  ⇔  M ∩ AB = (M ∩ A)(M ∩ B)`.

Both directions of the factorization proof are valid without centrality or finiteness. In the reverse direction, writing `ab⁻¹=m_A m_B` puts `m_A⁻¹a=m_Bb` in both subgroups. AB is a subset product throughout; nothing assumes it is a subgroup. Under the separate local-injectivity assumptions `L∩A=L∩B=1`, every normal `M≤L` consequently passes the quotient intersection test exactly when it avoids the defect set `D=L∩AB` except at the identity.

The finite size formula is also valid: the unique lifts of an element of `π(A)∩π(B)` give `a_x b_x⁻¹`, and equal products have precisely the same left coset modulo `π(A∩B)`. Normality of that image subgroup is not needed to count these cosets. This proof does not assert that D has any group structure.

For regular polytopes, the two end subgroups in every intermediate quotient are unchanged marked groups because of local injectivity. Thus the retained recursive string-C recognition theorem applies with its local hypotheses established. In the rotation version, the same reasoning uses string-C-plus recognition, and kernels must also be invariant under the global mirror. The mirror descends through exactly these invariant kernels. The correspondence and recognition theorems remain retained background; Lemma 1 alone does not prove abstract polytopality.

Global minimality has the required additional premise: R is the smallest regular maniplex cover of P. Any regular quotient of H/M that still covers P factors through R, so its kernel in H lies between M and L. Maximal admissibility within that interval therefore gives minimality even among covers outside the original interval. The converse classifies only the minimal covers actually covered by H. It does not show that every minimal cover is in the interval. For the chiral case, orientability pulls back along the cover, so the rotation-plus-mirror argument includes every regular cover in question. A finite H also removes any ambiguity about infinite proper quotients.

Distinct invariant kernels give distinct regular polytopes under rank-preserving isomorphism: compose an isomorphism with a target automorphism to align the base flags, after which the universal marked relations must coincide. This does not identify objects under arbitrary permutations of ranks or claim nonduality.

**Support and central-kernel theorems.** In the explicitly assumed multiplicity-free semisimple decomposition `L=⊕L_i`, every invariant subspace is a sum of constituents. The supplied proof establishes this directly. A simple submodule has a nonzero coordinate projection, which is an isomorphism onto one simple constituent; two nonzero projections would contradict pairwise nonisomorphism. The submodule therefore equals that coordinate constituent, and induction splits the rest. Normality and mirror stability are exactly invariance under the stated action group Ω.

The defect-support hypergraph then classifies all admissible coordinate sums. A constituent is present in the join precisely when D meets it trivially. If every constituent is safe and R fails polytopality, all singleton subsets are admissible but their total set is not. A unique maximal admissible subset would contain every singleton, a contradiction. Lifting these normal subgroups through the universal quotient preserves joins; the resulting finite admissible kernels already generate K, so the join of all finite admissible kernels is K. No downward-cofinality or finite-cover-existence theorem is incorrectly substituted for this join argument. A failed constituent test obstructs only this chosen finite witness, as the text states.

The central theorem does not use the multiplicity-free hypothesis. Centrality makes the unique-lift map a homomorphism, with image D, so D is a vector subspace. All subspaces of L are invariant under conjugation; the additional scalar-mirror assumption ensures invariance in the oriented case. Maximal subspaces disjoint from D are exactly complements. Choosing one complement F identifies all complements with linear maps `F→D`, giving exactly `p^{e(d−e)}` possibilities and quotient degree `p^e`. For `0<e<d`, the graphs of maps sending one fixed nonzero vector of F to each vector of D generate L together. This supplies global join-density and the claimed finite minimal covers under the smallest-cover premise. The e=0 and e=d endpoint statements are correctly limited. In particular, the theorem is not applied to the noncentral source example.
