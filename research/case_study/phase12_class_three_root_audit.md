## Marked Frattini lemma

Factoring by K⁴ is legitimate: K=Φ(P) is characteristic, and K⁴ is
characteristic in K. The quotient preserves the marked generation and
the class bound. Its Frattini subgroup is the image of K, now of exponent
at most four. The collected identities with d=[b,a], e=[d,a], f=[d,b]
give K′=<e²,f²>; their signs were checked against the stated commutator
convention. Class-three centrality puts K′ in the center. In a class-two
exponent-four group the fourth-power word formula makes commutators have
order at most two, hence squares form an elementary abelian central
subgroup W. Thus V=K/W has dimension at most three.

For dimension two a nonzero alternating commutator map has zero radical,
even when its target is not initially assumed one-dimensional. Its values
are generated by the value on a basis pair. The central elements e,f
therefore map to zero, so their squares are trivial and K′=1. Dimensions
zero and one give a cyclic group by the Burnside basis theorem.

For dimension three the marked square vectors are a basis. The frozen
binary lemma applies in P/W; no abelianness of K itself is presumed.
If the images of e,f both vanish, their squares again vanish. Otherwise
one is a central representative of the common coordinate line. The
corresponding marked square differs by a central element of W and is
itself central in K. Relabeling the three marked generators is valid:
their images are precisely the three nonzero vectors of P/Φ(P), so any
pair generates P. With this coordinate named a², the two collection
identities force e²=f²=1. This proves K powerful before quotienting.

The class-at-most-three family is closed under subgroups and quotients.
Consequently the proper-pair restriction and central-quotient absorption
are valid inside this family. Pair generation puts the cyclic factors
on the three nonzero Frattini lines. Minimality in Φ(P) gives ratio two;
equality there forces generation by the three squared factors, without
assuming their ordered multiplication is injective. The nontriviality
of those factors follows from the disjoint-conjugate bound as written.

The retained phase-eleven contradiction uses exactly these reductions
and powerful K. Its factorization theorem supplies surjectivity before
equality is used for injectivity. The resulting power-layer map is
linear and equivariant, so the binary action lemma produces a prohibited
central element of a cyclic factor. No unstated hereditary-powerful
hypothesis is needed: the induction here is in the class-three family.
The earlier all-odd-order theorem and Sylow decomposition then give the
stated wider nilpotent family.
