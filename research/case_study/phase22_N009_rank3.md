# Completing the rank-three order-1024 census by invariant kernels

10 October 2026. Private proposed source-bound rederivation of N009's rank-three census. The new computation is separate from the frozen phase21 rank-four package. Its candidate list uses neither a group catalogue nor the source presentations or expected counts.

## 1. Exact theorem and source target

**Computed classification theorem, conditional on the stated exact algorithms.** Nondegenerate rank-three string C-groups of order 1024 have 235 ordered marked isomorphism classes. Exactly 15 classes are fixed by reversing the marking, so there are 125 classes under the generator-set isomorphism convention of Gomi–Loyola–De Las Peñas. Every canonical type count agrees with their Table 5, printed p. 11, and every class is specified by a normal-kernel coordinate list in the retained universal marked group.

The source is *String C-groups of order 1024*, Contributions to Discrete Mathematics 13(1) (2018), [DOI 10.55016/ojs/cdm.v13i1.62475](https://doi.org/10.55016/ojs/cdm.v13i1.62475). The original primary PDF and extraction hashes are carried in the certificate; the third-party full text is not part of this original-artifact package. This is a rederivation of a published finite classification, not a novelty claim about the count.

## 2. Why the universal group suffices

Let G=<a,b,c> be any target. The reviewed all-class canonical forest quotient theorem gives |G/gamma3(G)|=2^5. Hence |gamma3(G)|=32. In a finite nilpotent group, successive nontrivial lower-central subgroups strictly decrease. Starting at gamma3 of order 2^5 permits at most five nontrivial factors, so G has class at most seven.

Define

    U = <a,b,c | a^2=b^2=c^2=1, [a,c]=1> / gamma8.

This universal class-at-most-seven quotient is finite: its graded lower-central factors are generated by finitely many multilinear commutators in degree-one elements of order two, and therefore are finite elementary abelian groups. There are only seven such factors. Thus U is a finite 2-group. Deleting any subset of the original generators respects all defining relations and the nilpotency law. The resulting commuting deletion maps show directly that the universal marking has actual IP: an element fixed by the S and T deletions is fixed by their composition, the S-intersect-T deletion.

The NQ calculation retains the epimorphism of the original marking. Its exact values are

    |U|=2^38,   |gamma3(U)|=2^33,
    lower-central orders:
    2^38, 2^35, 2^33, 2^30, 2^26, 2^20, 2^12, 1.

K=gamma3(U) is **not abelian**: its derived subgroup has order 512. No abelian-kernel assumption is made in this phase. The invariant-factor lists displayed by the run concern abelianizations, not a claimed classification of K itself.

Every target G is U/N for a unique normal subgroup N contained in K with [K:N]=32. The universal map exists by the class bound. On class-two quotients it induces the marked isomorphism of the canonical rank-three path groups, so its kernel lies in K; group order then gives the index. Conversely, any such N gives an order-1024 generated marking whose nonadjacent commutations and adjacent noncommutations survive the canonical quotient. Actual IP is still tested; it is never inferred from a general quotient operation.

Distinct kernels give distinct ordered marked isomorphism classes. A marked isomorphism must commute with the universal maps and hence have the same kernel. Reversal of a,b,c is an actual automorphism of U and acts on kernels. Its orbits are exactly the source's generator-set equivalence classes: a connected three-vertex path has only identity and reversal as diagram automorphisms.

## 3. Exhaustive invariant index-two ladder

For an ambient-normal subgroup M of U, put

    C_M = Phi(M)[M,U].

The quotient M/C_M is elementary abelian with trivial U-action. Its hyperplane preimages are exactly the U-normal subgroups of index two in M. Necessity follows because an index-two subgroup contains Phi(M) and the quotient C2 has no nontrivial automorphism. Sufficiency follows from containment of [M,U]. This works for nonabelian M.

Every U-normal index-32 subgroup N of K occurs in five such steps. If M/N is nontrivial, the 2-group U/N acts on the nonzero characters of (M/N)/Phi(M/N). Their number is odd, whereas every nontrivial orbit has even size; hence a nonzero fixed character exists. Its kernel supplies a U-normal index-two step between M and N. Iterate.

The implementation enumerates all hyperplanes, explicitly checks their number, normality and index, and deduplicates exact subgroups by canonical induced pc generating sequences in one ambient pc sequence. All 38 relative pc orders are explicitly asserted to be two before encoding exponent vectors as binary integers. The completed ladder levels contain

    index 2: 7 kernels;
    index 4: 23 kernels;
    index 8: 61 kernels;
    index 16: 147 kernels;
    index 32: 401 kernels.

Compressed full coordinate checkpoints are retained at every level. This is an exhausted normal-kernel search, not a sample or a fitted family parameterization.

## 4. Actual IP, including rejected candidates

For each subset S of the three positions precompute K_S=K intersect U_S. In U/N, with T=K/N, the intersection of T with the marked S-parabolic is exactly (N K_S)/N. The quotient by T is the canonical path group and has actual IP. Therefore the image-plus-kernel argument proves that the full marking has IP precisely when these eight kernel intersections have exact setwise meets.

The final script computes this profile in a group of order 32. It then constructs **every one of the 401 full candidate quotients**, including the profile rejections, constructs all eight marked subgroups, and tests all 64 actual intersections. Profile acceptance must agree with actual IP for each candidate or the script fails. Thus the final executed run contains 25,664 full-group intersection tests, 9,402 profile comparisons, 166 rejected candidates and 235 accepted markings. No failed candidate is omitted from the direct intersection cross-check.

The code is an explicit excess-five/class-seven adaptation of the reviewed phase21 invariant-kernel algorithm. It is not advertised as an independent implementation of that algorithm. The nonabelian K is handled by the same proved general ladder and profile lemmas. GAP 4.15.1 and NQ 2.5.11, including NQ's universal quotient guarantee and GAP's exact subgroup/pc operations, are retained computational inputs. No proof-assistant formalization of the finite census is claimed.

## 7. Exact scope within N009

Together with the reviewed phase21 rank-four census and the earlier first-excess rank-five result, this completes the three nondegenerate rank-at-least-three census components: 125,105,8 classes under the source convention. The elementary rank bound excludes rank at least six; rank two is the single dihedral marking of order 1024. The phase21 short proof separately recovers Theorems 3.6 and 3.8, and its raw Todd–Coxeter check plus rank-four census recovers the two presentations and completeness of Theorem 5.5.

A literal whole-document reconstruction is a stronger claim. The source's Table 6 also labels individual facet and vertex-figure groups and records the orders of their particular universal amalgams. Its Tables 2–4 and Examples 3.9 and 4.3–4.5 contain particular presentation labels. Those labels and intermediate universal orders are not all rebound by the type totals above. Source Lemma 5.2 gives explicit centers of finite toroidal {4,4} groups; Lemma 5.4 gives a kernel-location statement conditional on the quoted infinite decomposition. Those are additional lemma-level obligations if every source statement is desired. The quoted Theorem 4.2, Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.3 remain retained or bypassed background, not newly credited results.

The source's remaining mathematical sections, examples, exact theorem statements and computational conventions were read in the phase21/22 source audit. This note claims the exhaustive classification component and the exact source-type match, with these presentation/lemma boundaries explicit. It does not infer a matched proof-length ratio, publication novelty, or a new paper count from three agreeing totals alone.
