# Infinitely many regular affine halving counterexamples from map covers

Private research theorem, 10 October 2026. This note keeps the frozen phase30 example unchanged. It proves a uniform lifting criterion for its binary construction, and then obtains infinitely many finite examples with the same rank and diagram. No large cover or resulting affine group is enumerated.

## 1. The retained finite seed

Let M be the regular map of type {5,5} constructed in [phase30_affine_map.md](phase30_affine_map.md), with regular group K of order 120. It has 12 vertices, 30 edges and 12 faces. Put E=F₂^Ω for its edge set Ω, and let C be generated by the incidence vectors of all vertex stars and face boundaries. The quotient Q=E/C has dimension 13.

The following exact properties are retained from the frozen finite certificate, not assumed for arbitrary regular maps:

- The 30 coordinate images q_e are nonzero and distinct.
- Each of the 24 five-edge blocks B spans a four-dimensional space W_B.
- q_e∈W_B holds exactly for e∈B.
- At each incident vertex-face pair, the two block spaces intersect in the span of their two common edge columns; that span has dimension two.

The sole relation among the five columns of any block is their total sum. Since its weight is odd, the corresponding local Cayley graph is nonbipartite.

The map is a cellulation of a closed orientable surface of genus four. Here is a direct verification of this topological input. Its flags give a triangulated surface: each barycentric edge has exactly two incident triangles by thinness, and each barycentric vertex has a circular link because the corresponding rank-two residue is a polygon. The triangulation is connected by the verified residue connectivity. The seed generators c,b,d all exchange 6 and 7 in their seven-point permutation realization. Thus this exchange bit is a homomorphism K→C₂ taking every flag-adjacency generator to the nonidentity element. It supplies the bipartite coloring of the flag-adjacency graph, and hence a consistent orientation of the surface triangles. Finally χ=12−30+12=−6=2−2g, so g=4.

## 2. What passes to a surface cover automatically

Let π:M′→M be a finite connected unbranched surface cover of degree t, with the lifted cellulation. Suppose its deck group D has order t and that every element of K lifts. Let K′ be the group of all lifts. Then

    1 → D → K′ → K → 1,
    |K′|=120t.

This extension need not split. The group K′ acts regularly on lifted flags: lift the unique element carrying the projected source flag to the projected target flag, then adjust by the unique deck transformation carrying the lifted flag to the target flag. A flag stabilizer projects trivially to K and is a deck transformation fixing an interior point, so it is trivial.

The lifted cellulation is an actual regular map. An unbranched cover is locally a homeomorphism, so every lifted vertex still has a five-edge star with cyclic link, every face boundary is still a pentagon, and each edge has two distinct endpoints and two distinct faces. A face is a disk and its closed pentagonal cell lifts homeomorphically. Distinct base vertices and edges on that boundary remain distinct in every lift. Each incident lifted vertex-face pair therefore has exactly two common edges. Its incidence geometry is thin and residually connected, as is also immediate from the polygonal links and connected surface cellulation. Its counts are

    V′=12t,  E′=30t,  F′=12t.

Define C′ in F₂^(Ω′) by **all** lifted vertex-star and face-boundary rows, and put Q′=F₂^(Ω′)/C′. Write q′_e for its coordinate images. Summing coordinates over edge fibers gives a linear map F₂^(Ω′)→F₂^Ω. Each lifted block maps bijectively onto its corresponding base block, so its row maps to the corresponding row of C. Consequently there is a surjective map

    f: Q′ → Q,          f(q′_e)=q_(π(e)).               (1)

For any lifted block B′ over B, its five columns sum to zero. Their span W′_(B′) has dimension at most four, while (1) maps it onto the base space W_B of dimension four. Therefore

    f|W′_(B′): W′_(B′) → W_B is an isomorphism.         (2)

This proves, without any covering-code rank computation, that every local block still has precisely its one odd five-term dependence.

For an incident pair B′,D′, both spaces in (2) map onto the corresponding base spaces. Their intersection maps injectively into W_B∩W_D, which has dimension two. The two common lifted edge columns are independent by their images under f and lie in the intersection. Hence

    W′_(B′)∩W′_(D′)
      =span{q′_e:e∈B′∩D′},       dim=2.               (3)

Thus the exact common-shadow condition is inherited automatically. It is not inferred from subgroup IP of a quotient.

Nonzero columns and distinctness of columns above different base edges also follow from (1). The only remaining obstruction is equality of two columns above the **same** base edge. If all columns of Q′ are distinct, condition B2 is inherited too: suppose q′_e∈W′_(B′). Its image belongs to W_B, so π(e)∈B by the base certificate. Let e_B be the unique edge of B′ over π(e). Both q′_e and q′_(e_B) lie in W′_(B′) and have the same image under the isomorphism (2); hence they are equal. Column distinctness implies e=e_B∈B′. The converse is tautological.

We have proved a compact sufficient criterion: **every such regular map cover with no equal same-fiber columns satisfies every hypothesis of the affine construction theorem.** The next section rules out that last obstruction for a broad class of covers.

## 3. A dimension argument rules out equal columns

Assume D is a finite p-group for a prime p≥5. The code C′ is K′-invariant because K′ permutes its two families of defining rows. There are two independent upper bounds on the ranks of those row families: the sum of all vertex-star rows is zero, since every edge has two endpoints; the sum of all face-boundary rows is zero, since every edge has two incident faces. Thus

    dim C′ ≤ (12t−1)+(12t−1)=24t−2,
    dim Q′ ≥ 30t−(24t−2)=6t+2.                        (4)

These are relations within different lists of rows; the bound follows by summing their two individual rank bounds, regardless of further intersections between their row spaces.

Suppose two distinct lifted edges e,e′ above the same base edge have equal columns. Deck transformations act regularly on that fiber, so e′=δe for some nonidentity δ∈D. The stabilizer

    L_e={h∈D:h·q′_e=q′_e}

is a subgroup of D containing δ, so |L_e|≥p. The distinct columns obtained from this edge fiber form its D-orbit, of size t/|L_e|≤t/p. This is an orbit-stabilizer argument on column vectors, not a claim that δ fixes every column or that D is abelian.

The lifted K′-action is transitive on edges and normalizes D. Transporting e to any other lifted edge conjugates the corresponding column stabilizer. Therefore **every** one of the 30 base edge fibers yields at most t/p distinct columns. In total there are at most 30t/p distinct vectors among the coordinate images. Since these vectors span Q′,

    dim Q′ ≤ 30t/p ≤ 6t,

contradicting (4). Hence no equal same-fiber columns exist. Combining this with §2 proves:

### Cover-stability theorem

Every finite connected regular p-group surface cover, p≥5, of the certified genus-four seed, along which K lifts, again satisfies all the binary hypotheses of the phase30 affine construction. No additional code intersections or subgroup-IP assumptions are needed.

The argument admits the following more general numerical form. Retain the seed hypotheses of the phase30 affine theorem, including its simple closed face boundaries and exactly two common edges at each incident vertex-face pair. If this certified seed map has V vertices, E edges and F faces, and each block span has dimension one less than its block size, let a normal deck group of order t have no nonidentity element of order below ℓ. If all seed symmetries lift and

    ℓ(E−V−F) ≥ E,

then the same proof rules out column equality: the lower bound is t(E−V−F)+2, whereas a collision gives the upper bound Et/ℓ. Local isomorphisms and intersections lift exactly as above. This statement is conditional on the explicit seed code properties; it does not assert that an arbitrary odd self-dual regular map has them.

## 4. Infinitely many qualifying covers

Let S be the genus-four surface underlying M, and Π=π₁(S). For every prime p≥5 define

    Π_p=[Π,Π] Π^p.

This subgroup is characteristic. The standard surface presentation

    Π=〈x₁,y₁,…,x₄,y₄ | [x₁,y₁]···[x₄,y₄]=1〉

has abelianization Z⁸, so Π/Π_p≅(C_p)^8. The connected covering associated with Π_p is unbranched and regular, of degree

    t=p⁸.                                             (5)

Every seed automorphism acts simplicially on the barycentric triangulation of S and hence is a surface homeomorphism. This includes the orientation-reversing seed generators. Its induced outer automorphism of Π preserves Π_p: characteristicity gives preservation under every representative. The lifting criterion therefore gives a lift. The inverse automorphism lifts too, and uniqueness of lifts makes the lift a homeomorphism. Each lift preserves the lifted cellulation. Thus every K-element lifts and the cover-stability theorem applies. No conformal-only lifting statement or splitting of K′ is assumed.

For completeness, the standard covering inputs are the classification of connected covers by fundamental-group subgroups, the lifting criterion, and the description of the deck group for a normal subgroup. They apply to closed surfaces. See Hatcher, [Algebraic Topology, §1.3](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf), Proposition 1.33, Theorem 1.38 and Proposition 1.39. The same characteristic homology-cover construction is described in Hidalgo, [Homology Covers of Riemann Surfaces and Automorphisms: Examples, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05442v1). Our explicit characteristic-subgroup argument supplies the orientation-reversing case needed here.

## 5. The resulting infinite family

For each p≥5, let m_p=dim Q′ for the homology cover in §4, and apply the phase30 affine construction with translation group Q′ and complement K′. The result Γ_p is an actual finite regular rank-four hypertope satisfying B1 and B2. Its distinguished involutions have the same star diagram, with labels 4,5,5. Its full type-preserving automorphism group and chamber number are

    G_p=Q′⋊K′,       |G_p|=120 p⁸ 2^(m_p),
    m_p≥6p⁸+2.                                       (6)

Here the outer affine semidirect product is part of the construction. It does not imply that the covering extension 1→(C_p)^8→K′→K→1 splits.

Every individual higher shadow still has five columns of rank four whose only relation has odd weight, so it is nonbipartite. Every incident pair of higher objects still has two independent common directions and hence has a square shadow. Geometric halving leaves the individual higher objects unsplit, but replaces their common square by two disjoint squares. Thus **every Γ_p is a counterexample to residual-connectivity preservation under the inspected halving hypotheses, with actual regular input.** The local subgroup argument in phase30 §5 also applies unchanged: each changed higher parabolic equals the old group of order 160, their intersection is D₈ of order eight, and the required changed intersection is a Klein four group.

The object counts are

    type 0: 2^(m_p),
    type 1: 15p⁸ 2^(m_p),
    each higher type: 12p⁸ 2^(m_p−4),
    disconnected higher-pair residues after halving: 15p⁸ 2^(m_p).

The groups, and therefore the regular hypertopes, are pairwise nonisomorphic as p varies: the odd part of (6) is 15p⁸. These odd parts are different for different primes p≥5, irrespective of the unknown m_p. In particular this is an infinite family at fixed rank and fixed diagram, rather than a disconnected-diagram product construction.

## 6. Evidence, retained inputs and limits

The uniform theorem is proved by the local-isomorphism and orbit-dimension arguments, not by extrapolation from finite covers. The sole nonstandard finite seed input is the already frozen phase30 certificate. The surface-cover facts are explicitly retained standard topology.

The completed [seed-surface checker](phase31_seed_surface_check.py) and [report](phase31_seed_surface_check.json) verify all 12 embedded pentagonal faces, 54 circular barycentric links and 180 two-triangle panels. They independently orient the faces so every edge cancels over the integers, construct the two cellular boundary matrices, and check their composite is zero. The exchange-bit character is checked on all 14400 products in K and on every adjacent flag pair. The selected mod-2,3,5,7,11 boundary ranks are 11 and 11, giving first-homology dimension eight in these finite diagnostics; the all-prime dimension used in §4 follows from the standard genus-four presentation, not from these samples.
