## 1. Exact target and retained background

The target is the relative-generating-set result in its finite-group scope (Theorem 2.1), Frattini containment (Theorem 3.1), the quotient reduction (Corollary 3.3 and Theorem 3.4), the complete finite-p-group geometry conclusion (Proposition 3.7 and Theorem 3.8), and the residue Frattini test in §4.2. Propositions 3.2 and 3.5 and Lemma 3.6 are also proved below, rather than treated as additional classification inputs.

Retained background consists of subgroup correspondence, the definition of the Frattini subgroup as the intersection of maximal subgroups, and the elementary finite-p-group fact that G/Φ(G) is a vector space over F_p. No classification of finite groups, tables of marks, or coset-geometry census is used. For the infinite version of Theorem 3.1 we use Zorn's lemma to extend a proper subgroup to a maximal subgroup when the ambient group is finitely generated relative to that subgroup.

Here the subgroup family is finite and indexed, H_i≤G, and H_J=∩_{j∈J}H_j, with H_∅=G and B=H_I. The source assumes flag transitivity when translating geometry axioms into subgroup conditions. In those terms firmness says the H_J are all distinct, and RWPRI says that for each J⊊I some H_{J∪{i}} is maximal in H_J. We do not infer flag transitivity from these subgroup conditions. It remains a separate coset Helly condition, and is preserved by the quotient argument below.

## 2. The normal-subgroup ladder

**Master lemma.** Suppose the intersection family is irredundant: all H_J are distinct. Suppose that for some ordering 1,…,r the chain

G=K_0>K_1>⋯>K_r=B,  K_j=H_{\{1,…,j\}},

has every inclusion maximal. If N◁G is contained in every maximal subgroup of G, then N≤B. The group G need not be finite.

First G is generated by B and finitely many elements: choose t_j∈K_{j−1}\K_j and use K_{j−1}=⟨K_j,t_j⟩ backwards along the chain. Consequently each proper subgroup containing B is contained in a maximal subgroup of G. Indeed a chain of proper overgroups of such a subgroup cannot have union G, since the finite list t_1,…,t_r would then be contained in one member. In particular NH_i=G is impossible for a proper H_i.

If N⊈B, choose the first k with N⊈K_k. Then N≤K_{k−1} and maximality gives

K_{k−1}=N(K_{k−1}∩H_k).

Suppose for 1≤j≤k−1 that K_j=N(K_j∩H_k). Since N≤K_{j−1},

K_j ≤ N(K_{j−1}∩H_k) ≤ K_{j−1}.

Maximality leaves only the two endpoints. The lower endpoint would force K_{j−1}∩H_k≤K_j, hence K_{j−1}∩H_k=K_j∩H_k, contradicting irredundancy because j<k. Thus the upper endpoint occurs. Iterating gives G=NH_k, the contradiction established above. Therefore N≤B.

The only lattice input is one maximal intersection chain, not maximality in every residue. RWPRI supplies that chain by choosing a maximal next step repeatedly. Taking N=Φ(G) proves source Theorem 3.1, including its explicitly stated infinite-group scope.

## 3. Kernel transport and the faithful action

If N◁G and N≤B, send the typed coset H_i g to (H_i/N)(gN). This is a bijection on every type. A point g belongs to all cosets in a given family precisely when gN belongs to all their quotient cosets: the forward implication is immediate; for the converse any lift of the quotient point belongs to every original coset because each contains N. In particular pairwise incidence, flags, chambers, residues and common-point witnesses are preserved. This proves Proposition 3.2, including pregeometries that are not flag transitive.

Subgroup correspondence preserves maximality in every interval above N. Therefore firmness, flag transitivity, residual connectivity, RWPRI and RPRI are all preserved in both directions. Taking N=Φ(G) gives Corollary 3.3 and Theorem 3.4. More precisely there is a bijection between indexed subgroup families for G with Φ(G)≤B and indexed subgroup families for G/Φ(G), with the displayed incidence isomorphism. This is not an additional claim about orbits under Aut(G) versus Aut(G/Φ(G)), since quotient automorphisms need not all lift.

The kernel of the action on all typed cosets is core_G(B)=∩_i core_G(H_i). Thus the faithful action is obtained by replacing G by G/core_G(B). The master lemma says that Φ(G) is already in this kernel for a firm RWPRI geometry. This is the same common torsor/coset-kernel transport used elsewhere in the project: deleting a kernel preserves the actual coset partitions and all their common-point relations, rather than only an ordinary linear representation.

Maximal subgroups of G/Φ(G) correspond to maximal subgroups of G, so their intersection is trivial. This justifies the term Frattini free here. No structural classification of Frattini-free groups is needed.

## 4. Relative generators, and a necessary scope distinction

For finite G, any proper subgroup is contained in a maximal subgroup. Hence Φ(G)H=G implies H=G. Given B≤G and a finite indexed list s_1,…,s_r, generation by B and all s_i is therefore equivalent to generation by their images in G/Φ(G). Under generation, independence is equivalent to each deletion subgroup ⟨B,s_j:j≠i⟩ being proper. Applying the same equivalence to each deletion proves Theorem 2.1 in the finite-group setting, with no geometry hypothesis.

The unqualified infinite-group version needs an extra hypothesis. The source writes Theorem 2.1 for a “group G”, and its preliminary assertion ⟨Φ(G),S⟩=G iff ⟨S⟩=G is not valid for arbitrary infinite groups and arbitrary S. For example let D=C_{p^∞} be the Prüfer p-group and G=D×C_2. Every maximal subgroup of this abelian group has quotient of prime order. The divisible group D has no nontrivial homomorphism to a finite group, so D×1 is the unique maximal subgroup. Thus Φ(G)=D×1. The single element generating the C_2 factor gives an independent generating set of G/Φ(G), but does not generate G. This is a scope diagnostic, not a claim to a newly discovered erratum. It does not affect the infinite geometric master lemma, whose finite maximal chain supplies exactly the relative finite-generation condition needed in its proof.

For finitely generated G, or more generally whenever Φ(G) is superfluous in G, the same proof of Theorem 2.1 works. If one already knows G=⟨B,s_1,…,s_r⟩, its independence equivalence also works without G finite: each deletion subgroup has a finitely generated relative extension and hence lies in a maximal subgroup when proper.

## 5. The p-group classification is a coordinate theorem

**Coordinate lemma.** Let V be a finite-dimensional vector space over F_p and let (W_i)_{i=1}^r be a family of subspaces with distinct intersections W_J. Suppose there is an ordering whose successive intersections form a maximal-subspace chain from V to B=∩W_i. Then dim(V/B)=r and, in a suitable basis of V/B, W_i/B is the coordinate hyperplane omitting the i-th basis vector.

Each maximal step has codimension one, so dim(V/B)=r. Irredundancy supplies v_i∈∩_{j≠i}W_j\W_i. Their classes modulo B are independent: in any relation, reducing modulo W_i kills every term except the i-th. Hence they form a basis of V/B. Now W_i/B contains the other r−1 basis vectors and is proper, so it is exactly their span.

Apply the master lemma and kernel transport to a finite p-group P. Its geometry descends to V=P/Φ(P), where the coordinate lemma applies. Every subgroup interval in a residue is now a coordinate-hyperplane inclusion. Thus RWPRI implies RPRI, proving Proposition 3.7. Each type has p vertices, and every choice of one vertex of each type is a chamber: the map

V/B → ∏_i V/W_i

is the coordinate bijection. The geometry is the direct sum of r rank-one primitive geometries, proving Theorem 3.8. It is automatically firm, flag transitive and residually connected; the induced faithful group P/B≅F_p^r acts regularly on its chambers.

This proves more explicitly what the source's Grassmann argument encodes: all finite-p-group examples are coordinate coset geometries. In rank two, complete incidence is equivalent to H_1H_2=G, because H_1x meets H_2y exactly when yx^{-1}∈H_2H_1. This proves source Proposition 3.5. The source's Lemma 3.6 follows from dim(W_1∩W_2)=dim V−2 and W_2⊈W_1: necessarily dim W_2=dim V−1. Neither subsidiary result needs to be retained as an external theorem.

Conversely, for every 1≤r≤d=dim P/Φ(P), choose r independent linear functionals modulo nonzero scalar. Their kernels pull back to a firm FT RC RPRI family in P. The number of indexed subgroup families of rank r is

∏_{j=0}^{r−1}(p^d−p^j)/(p−1)^r.

This counts the subgroup families themselves, not their Aut(P)-orbits. All their underlying type-preserving incidence structures are isomorphic to the same complete r-partite geometry with p vertices per type. A faithful action by P occurs only when B=1; then P is elementary abelian and r=d.

## 6. Every residue supplies a Frattini test

Apply the master lemma inside H_J to its remaining subgroups H_J∩H_i. Every residue of a firm flag-transitive RWPRI geometry is firm and RWPRI, so

Φ(H_J)≤∩_{i∉J}(H_J∩H_i)=B

for every J⊊I. Equivalently Φ(H_J)≤H_{J∪{i}} for all remaining i. This is exactly the source's §4.2 test, stated as a common Borel containment. It is necessary, not sufficient. The inclusion Φ(H_J)≤Φ(G) is not assumed and is generally false.

In a computation on the marked coset lattice, these are tests on the groups already assigned to nodes. They can reject candidate residue systems before coset Helly enumeration. The argument makes no claim that this ordering is faster on a particular corpus; no benchmark of the original implementation is available here.

## 8. Focused executable evidence

`phase16_frattini_coordinate_check.py` is a standalone standard-library check. It enumerates all subspaces by actual vector addition, then all unordered proper-subspace families in the specified ranks. It tests distinct intersections and the existence of a maximal intersection chain directly. For every passing family it verifies all intersection orders and explicitly partitions V into the coordinate cosets of B supplied by panel witnesses. It also compares the complete count with the displayed independent-functional formula, divided by r! for unordered families.

Run `python3 phase16_frattini_coordinate_check.py` without the third-party PDF. The optional `--source-pdf PATH` verifies an independently acquired source against the recorded digest; the report explicitly distinguishes `verified` from `not_supplied`. The PDF is used only for optional identity verification, not by the mathematical checks.

The checked spaces are F₂^d for 1≤d≤4, F₃^d for 1≤d≤3, and F₅^d for 1≤d≤2, through rank d and also rank d+1 in the smaller cases. The saved report `phase16_frattini_coordinate_check.json` records **774,039** families examined and **1,801** verified coordinate bijections, all passing. This finite diagnostic does not prove the normal-subgroup ladder for arbitrary groups, does not audit infinite-group subgroup structure computationally, and does not rerun the paper's historical SmallGroups census.
