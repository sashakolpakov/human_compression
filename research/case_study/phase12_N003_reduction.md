# A forest-and-parity reduction of Cameron–Cara (2002)

10 October 2026. Private written derivation; complete internal root review
found no gap. Internal review is distinct from external peer review.
This recovers established results; it makes no novelty claim. The mathematical
claim is all eight numbered results in the finite-group setting of the paper,
including both maximum-generator and maximum-rank geometry classifications
for n≥7. Whiston's bound and one structural consequence of his proof remain
explicit inputs. The CFSG-free question and the unnumbered small-degree census
are not solved here. No description-length ratio is assigned by this note.

For a finite group G, write μ(G) for the largest size of an irredundant
generating family, and μ′(G) for the largest irredundant family, generating or
not. Relative to B≤G, irredundancy means
`s_i ∉ <B,s_j : j≠i>`; use μ(G,B), μ′(G,B). For a group A acting on N,
an A-independent family has no member in the subgroup generated by all
A-images of its other members; its maximum size is μ′_A(N).

We retain the following inputs, on both sides of any eventual measurement.

* **W1 (Whiston).** μ(S_m)=μ′(S_m)=m−1, and every independent family of
  m−1 permutations of m points generates S_m. This is cited on source p.3.
* **W2 (Whiston's omission-subgroup consequence).** If n≥7 and S is an
  independent generating family of n−1 elements of S_n, every subgroup
  generated by all but one element is intransitive, transitive imprimitive
  with blocks of size two, or A_n. This is exactly the input invoked on
  source p.4. W2 is not inferred from W1 alone and is not reproved here.
  The source derives it from Whiston's work and explicitly retains CFSG.
* **Cayley's formula.** There are m^(m−2) labelled trees on m≥2 vertices.
* Elementary permutation and coset-action facts, and the standard coset
  criterion: distinct intersections, residual generation and typed Helly
  give a firm, residually connected, flag-transitive geometry. The source
  states this criterion as (G1)–(G3) on pp.7–8. We use the same criterion.

In particular no classification of maximal subgroups of S_n beyond W2 is
retained: the only needed two-block overgroup assertion is proved below.
The 1997 classification of inductively minimal geometries, retained in the
earlier 1998/2005 reduction, is unnecessary for this 2002 target.

## 2. The elementary quotient bound

Let N be normal in G, and S an independent generating family of G. Choose
T⊆S whose images form an independent generating family of G/N. For s∈S\T,
choose a word w_s in T with the same image, and put v_s=s w_s^−1∈N.
The v_s are independent: an expression for v_s in the others would express
s in S\{s}, because every correction w_u uses T. Therefore

    μ(G) ≤ μ(G/N)+μ′(N).                                    (1)

If N is abelian, every conjugation action on N can be represented by a word
in T, since G=N<T> and N acts trivially on itself. Consequently the same
contradiction works even for an expression using all G-conjugates of the
other v_u. This gives μ(G)≤μ(G/N)+μ′_G(N), the second part of Theorem 1.1.

Taking N=G×1 gives μ(G×H)=μ(G)+μ(H) when μ(G)=μ′(G): (1) is the upper
bound, and the union of embedded independent generating families gives the
lower bound. This recovers the following unnumbered source consequence too,
without addressing its unrestricted direct-product question.

## 3. The common forest core and the classification adapter

For a tree T on Ω, let τ_e be the transposition on edge e. For F⊆E(T),

    K_F=<τ_e:e∈F>=∏_{C component of (Ω,F)} Sym(C),
    K_F∩K_D=K_(F∩D).                                      (2)

Path conjugation generates every transposition inside a component. For the
intersection formula, preserving both component partitions is equivalent
to preserving their common refinement. Two points are in one refined block
exactly when their unique tree path uses only edges in F∩D. Equivalently,
intersecting stabilizers of selected tree cuts leaves the symmetric groups
on the forest obtained by deleting those cuts. This is the same core used
in the earlier [tree-cut](phase5_tree_cuts.md) and
[weighted-tree](phase6_weighted_tree.md) derivations.

First remove the imprimitive case in W2, spelling out a module argument
that the source abbreviates as “easy to see.” Suppose n=2m, m≥4, and an
omission subgroup H is transitive inside C₂ wr S_m. Its m block kernel N
is a subspace of F₂^m. The n−2 remaining generators are independent, so
μ(H)≥2m−2. Equation (1) with its action refinement gives

    2m−2 ≤ μ(H/N)+μ′_H(N) ≤ (m−1)+(m−1).                  (3)

Here the quotient bound follows from W1. For the second bound, if dim N<m
it is automatic. If N=F₂^m and an H-independent family had m members,
they would be a vector-space basis. The H-span of all but each basis vector
would then be that coordinate hyperplane; all such hyperplanes invariant
forces every basis line invariant. Over F₂ that makes H act trivially,
contrary to its transitive, nontrivial action on m coordinates.

Equality in (3) and W1 give H/N=S_m. Any nonconstant vector in F₂^m has
two unequal coordinates; subtracting its transposition image gives e_i+e_j.
Its S_m-span therefore contains the entire even-weight subspace W. Since
W has codimension one, an S_m-independent family in any invariant N has
at most two members: a nonconstant vector spans W, and at most one further
vector is needed; a family of constants has at most one member. Thus
2m−2≤(m−1)+2=m+1, impossible for m≥4. This excludes the imprimitive case.

There can be at most one omission subgroup A_n: such an omission makes
its omitted generator the unique odd generator. Relabel so that the
omission groups G_i=<s_j:j≠i> are intransitive for i≥2.

For i≥2 choose an edge e_i={x_i,s_i(x_i)} crossing two G_i-orbits; it exists
because <G_i,s_i>=S_n is transitive. Choose e_1 from any point moved by s_1.
For each i≥2 every other edge has both endpoints in one G_i-orbit. A cycle
containing e_i would give an alternative path inside a G_i-orbit, a
contradiction. A cycle cannot consist of just e_1. Thus the n−1 edges form
a tree (parallel edges would be a two-edge cycle).

More is true: the two components of T−e_i are exactly the two G_i-orbits.
Along every edge e_j, j≠i, its endpoints are in a common G_i-orbit; hence
each component is contained in an orbit. The chosen e_i separates orbits,
so the two components cannot merge. Therefore s_j preserves cut e_i
whenever j≠i. Formula (2) now immediately gives

    s_1 ∈ K_{e_1},       s_i ∈ K_{e_1,e_i}  (i≥2).          (4)

Independence makes s_1 the transposition τ_{e_1}. If all G_i are intransitive,
choose e_1 crossing G_1-orbits as well. Then every s_i∈K_{e_i}, so all
generators are the edge transpositions of a tree.

Otherwise G_1=A_n and all s_i, i≥2, are even. If e_i meets e_1, the second
group in (4) is S₃ and its nontrivial even elements are the two 3-cycles.
If they are disjoint it is C₂×C₂, whose sole nontrivial even element is the
double transposition. Crossing cut e_i excludes the identity. Hence

    S={s} ∪ {(s τ_e)^ε_e:e∈E(T)\{e_1}},  s=τ_{e_1},
    ε_e∈{1,−1}.                                           (5)

This proves the forward part of Theorem 2.1 from W1/W2 and the forest core.
It replaces the source's individual cycle-support analysis with one
intersection of cut stabilizers. The converse follows from §4.

## 4. Parity kernel: both families and every subgroup intersection

If a group K is generated by odd involutions including s, the kernel of
its sign map is generated by the elements sτ for all the other generators
τ. Indeed every even-length word can be paired, and
τυ=(sτ)^−1(sυ). This elementary kernel argument applies to every forest
containing the distinguished edge d.

For a set I of retained marked generators in (5), let
F(I)={d}∪{e≠d:the generator on e is retained}. Then its generated subgroup is

    L_I = K_F(I)             if s is retained,
          K_F(I) ∩ A_n       if s is not retained.          (6)

Inverting a generator leaves its cyclic subgroup unchanged. Since
F(I)∩F(J)=F(I∩J), equations (2) and (6) imply
L_I∩L_J=L_(I∩J) for all I,J. For the empty I the right-hand side is
K_{d}∩A_n=1. This proves Corollary 2.3 for both families.

The full forest generates S_n. Omitting an ordinary tree generator splits
the tree. In (5), omitting a non-distinguished generator also splits its
edge cut, while omitting s leaves only even permutations. Thus both
families are independent and generate S_n, completing Theorem 2.1.

The maximal parabolics of the twisted family have the especially simple form

    G_1=A_n,    G_e=Sym(A_e)×Sym(Ω\A_e)  (e≠d),            (7)

where A_e is either side of cut e. These explicit parabolics will control
the geometry without constructing all its flags by group enumeration.

## 5. All three counts

Ordinary tree sets correspond bijectively to labelled trees, so their
number is n^(n−2).

In a twisted set its unique odd member identifies s=(a b). For every other
actual generator h, the product sh is a transposition. The edges sh,
together with (a b), form a unique tree: relative to any representation
(5), changing ε replaces an edge incident with a or b by the edge with the
other endpoint. Contracting (a b) is unchanged, so these independent
endpoint changes preserve the tree. Conversely a labelled tree with one
distinguished edge gives the set {s}∪{sτ_e:e≠d}. These are inverse maps.
Thus the number of actual twisted sets is

    (n−1)n^(n−2).                                         (8)

If inverse 3-cycles are identified, contraction of the distinguished edge
removes exactly the endpoint ambiguity. The data are the unordered pair
{a,b} and a labelled tree on the n−1 blocks {a,b},{c}:c∉{a,b}. Edges at
{a,b} determine the cyclic subgroup on {a,b,c}; other edges determine a
unique double transposition. Consequently the count is

    binomial(n,2)(n−1)^(n−3).                              (9)

This proves all of Corollary 2.2, with precisely Cayley's formula retained.
No signed presentations are incorrectly counted as distinct marked sets.

## 6. Two-block overgroups and the relative-B result

Let H=Sym(A)×Sym(Aᶜ) in S_n, with both blocks nonempty. If an overgroup
contains a permutation not preserving the unordered two-block partition,
the transpositions within its conjugate blocks, together with those in H,
connect the two original components; they generate S_n. Thus the sole
possible proper overgroup of H is its unordered block stabilizer. This
exists strictly above H only for |A|=|Aᶜ|≥2, and then is the wreath product
Sym(|A|) wr C₂, with index two over H. In all other cases H is maximal
(including the separate 1+1 case H=1 in S₂).

There is at most one balanced edge cut in a positively weighted tree:
relative to a balanced cut, the side of any different cut away from it is
a proper, positive-mass subset of one half. In particular among the tree
parabolics at most one has a proper overgroup. Every other one is maximal;
A_n in (7) is also maximal.

Suppose S has size n−1 and is independent relative to B≤S_n. It is ordinarily
independent, hence generates S_n by W1 and belongs to the two families.
For every maximal omission group G_i, independence forces B≤G_i, since
otherwise <B,G_i>=S_n. A pendant edge other than the distinguished edge
exists, and its parabolic is S_(n−1); therefore B fixes a point. At a
balanced cut the only alternative is the unordered two-block stabilizer,
whose elements outside G_i swap the halves and fix no point. Consequently
B≤G_i there too. Equation (2) or (6) then gives B≤∩G_i=1.

This proves Corollary 2.4, and slightly more: μ′(S_n,B)=n−1 also forces B=1.
The extra formulation is useful for Corollary 3.3; no generation assumption
was used before W1 supplied it.

## 7. The general geometry-to-generator adapter

Let H_J=∩_{j∈J}H_j, H_∅=G, and B=H_I. Under source (G1), choose
s_i∈H_(I\{i})\H_i. Such a choice exists because this panel stabilizer
strictly contains B. Every other s_j and B lie in H_i, so the s_i are
independent relative to B. This proves rank≤μ′(G,B).

Under RWPRI, for every proper J there is k∉J such that H_(J∪{k}) is maximal
in H_J. Induction on |I\J| gives

    H_J=<B,s_i:i∉J>:                                       (10)

the equality holds at J=I, and adjoining s_k outside the specified maximal
subgroup gives the induction step. Thus the family generates G relative
to B, proving rank≤μ(G,B). The same equalities also give strong relative
subgroup intersection. This is Theorem 3.1 and its stated strengthening.

For an RWPRI S_n geometry of rank n−1, §6 gives B=1; W1 and §3 classify
the resulting marked family. Equation (10) recovers every parabolic.
Every panel group is cyclic of order two or three by (6). Its generator
is therefore unique up to inversion; changing the base chamber conjugates
the subgroups and marking. This proves the forward and uniqueness directions
of the source Theorem 3.2, under the source convention that types may be
renamed together with the unordered generator family.

## 8. The converse geometry: laminar cuts and one parity type

Root a tree. Its edge cuts A_e away from the root form a laminar family.
The cosets of H_e=Sym(A_e)×Sym(A_eᶜ) identify with |A_e|-subsets X_e;
two types are incident exactly when |X_e∩X_f|=|A_e∩A_f|. Those cardinalities
force the same containment and disjointness relations as in the base
family. Subtract the maximal selected children from each selected container,
including Ω. The remaining atoms have matching cardinalities, so bijections
on atoms combine to one permutation carrying every selected base cut to
its prescribed X_e. This proves typed Helly, for partial flags too.

Forest formula (2) gives distinct parabolic intersections and their residual
generation. Thus the ordinary family is firm, residually connected and
flag-transitive. In each nonempty forest residue, a leaf edge of a
nontrivial component has a maximal point stabilizer in its symmetric factor.
It is RWPRI. These are exactly the earlier common tree-core arguments.

For the twisted family, contract the distinguished edge d into a vertex of
weight two, leaving all other weights one. The remaining cut parabolics in
(7) are exactly its weighted-tree parabolics. Adjoin the type G_1=A_n:
this type has two vertices, and each is incident with every other vertex,
since every other parabolic contains the odd transposition s. More strongly,
every intersection of selected non-parity parabolics still contains s.
Thus a common coset representative for any cut flag can be changed in parity
without changing that flag. Typed Helly follows from the weighted laminar
argument. The abstract geometry is the join of a two-point rank-one geometry
and the weighted-tree geometry. Its specified acting group remains S_n;
we have not replaced it by the full direct-product automorphism group.

Equations (6) give distinct intersections and residual generation. For
RWPRI, if the parity type has not been fixed, fixing it gives an index-two
maximal subgroup. If it has been fixed, the residue group is the even part
of the product of symmetric groups on a forest which still contains d.
If a nontrivial component other than the d-component exists, take one of
its leaf edges. The even product projects onto its full symmetric factor,
because the d-component supplies an odd permutation to adjust parity.
The corresponding point stabilizer is maximal. If the d-component is the
only nontrivial component and an edge type remains, it has at least three
vertices and a leaf edge other than d. The group is its alternating group;
the natural point stabilizer is maximal (A₃ acts regularly on three points,
and A_m is naturally primitive for m≥4). Thus every residue is weakly
primitive. This completes Theorem 3.2 for both families.

The ordinary basic diagram is the line graph of T. In the twisted geometry
the parity node is isolated. These recover the source's following diagram
remarks; the weighted-tree argument also explicitly describes the other
rank-two residues.

## 9. Why no extra maximum-rank geometries survive

For completeness, Corollary 3.3 needs a bridge beyond the existence of
an RWPRI geometry with the same generators. Start with any source coset
geometry of rank n−1: its designated S_n action is firm, residually
connected and flag-transitive, but RWPRI is not assumed. By §7 choose
an independent family S relative to B. W1 and §6 give <S>=S_n and B=1.
Let G_i=<s_j:j≠i>; then G_i≤H_i<S_n. The strict upper inclusion follows
from s_i∉H_i. Sections 4 and 6 show H_i=G_i except possibly for one balanced
cut k, where H_k could be the unordered two-block stabilizer W_k.

For every other tree cut j, one of its sides is a nonempty subset of one
of the two k-halves. A permutation preserving that j-side cannot swap
the k-halves. Therefore W_k∩G_j≤G_k. Given any panel intersection
P_i=∩_{j≠i}H_j, either k=i and no enlarged group occurs, or among its
intersected groups there is some tree-cut G_j with j≠i,k. There are n−1≥6
types and at most one parity type, so such j exists. Hence replacing W_k
by G_k does not change that panel intersection. We obtain

    P_i=∩_{j≠i}G_j=<s_i>   for every i.                    (11)

Source residual generation (G2), inducting upwards from the panels, now
forces H_J=<s_i:i∉J> for every proper J. In particular H_i=G_i, so the
geometry is exactly one of §8 and is RWPRI. This proves Corollary 3.3
without presuming that a family of independent generators alone identifies
arbitrary larger chosen parabolics.

## 10. Whole-result coverage and measurement boundary

The two abstract principal classifications, every numbered corollary for
n≥7, the finite-group normal-subgroup and rank bounds, and the two diagram
remarks have now been derived. The common core consists of forest partitions,
laminar Helly and an index-two sign kernel. The short added adapters are
the F₂ permutation-module argument, W2-to-tree reconstruction, elementary
two-block overgroups, Cayley counting, and the chamber/panel induction.
The classifying Whiston inputs W1/W2 remain substantial retained background.
This is a full principal-result reduction conditional on those inputs, not
a CFSG-free proof, a full automorphism-group classification, or a proof of
every unnumbered small-n computation in the source.

For a matched measurement the complete source is available, so all target
spans can be selected and hashed reproducibly. They must include the source
arguments for both branches and all these adapters, not merely the shorter
tree branch. The replacement must include the adapters too, with W1/W2,
Cayley and the general coset criterion declared symmetrically as common
background. The descriptive scaffolding and audit ledger here are not yet
a frozen matched proof. No numerical ratio follows from these coverage gains.
The missing N001 (1998) source spans remain missing: this derivation does
not repair that separate cross-paper measurement limitation.

and its [report](phase12_N003_check.json) passed. It constructs actual

the even-weight subspace. None of these tests verifies or replaces W1/W2.
