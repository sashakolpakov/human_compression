# The exact third-excess count from a finite transition graph

10 October 2026. Research draft for independent review. All phase21 and
earlier frozen artifacts are unchanged.

Let a3(d) count ordered marked isomorphism classes of finite two-groups
of order 2^(2d+2), generated by d distinguished involutions satisfying
path commutations and the actual intersection property, with every
adjacent product of order at least four. The marking is ordered; no
identification by reversal is made.

**Theorem.** For d>=4,

    a3(d)=(125d^3-855d^2+2380d-2646)/6.                 (T)

At rank three, a3(3)=37. This is a computer-assisted exact counting
result. The finite computations retain the specified GAP/NQ algorithms;
the passage from finitely many transfer values to all ranks uses a
previously proved polynomiality theorem, not a conjectural fit. The
proof and its exact evidence boundary follow below.

## 1. The deletion defect lies in the small kernel

Let P be a finite two-group with a generating involutory forest marking,
actual IP, and every marked edge product of order at least four. Let B
be a standard parabolic on a subset of vertices, let O be its complement,
and let L be the normal closure in P of the O-markers. Then

    R=L intersect B <= gamma3(B).                         (1)

Indeed put K=gamma3(P). By the forest canonical-quotient theorem,
P/K is the standard class-two forest cocycle group: its unique coordinates
are the vertex bits and one central commutator bit for every edge. The
normal closure of the O-markers in this group has exactly the vertex
coordinates in O and the edge coordinates incident with O. Containment
in that coordinate subgroup follows because it is normal; the reverse
containment follows by taking commutators of O-markers with each adjacent
marker. The image of B has exactly its own vertex coordinates and the
edge coordinates with both endpoints in B. These two coordinate sets
are disjoint. Thus every element of L intersect B maps trivially into
P/K. The exact parabolic restriction K intersect B=gamma3(B), also given
by the forest theorem, proves (1).

No deletion homomorphism on P is asserted. The normal-closure description
is made only after passing to its known class-two canonical quotient.
In particular, for a path marking of excess at most three, every boundary
deletion defect in the phase20 finite-state theorem lies in a group of
order at most eight.

## 2. Complete finite input and retained transition theorem

For excess at most three take the class bound c=5. The completed phase21
rank-five, excess-three library contains 584 ordered marked groups.
Together with the previously classified counts 1, 15 and 106 at excesses
zero, one and two, the required rank-five window alphabet has 706 members.
The actual groups and markings, rather than only their counts, are
reconstructed and recognized again before use.

Each finite state records a window H and the actual deletion kernel R
on its final rank-four parabolic. The phase20 transition theorem requires
both its action-killing and centralization tests. Bound (1) permits
representing R inside gamma3 of that parabolic; it does not justify
discarding R or either test. A marked boundary isomorphism is unique
when it exists because the marking generates the boundary group.

The completed graph gives exact weighted counts by transfer. The
previously proved fixed-excess polynomiality gives degree at most three
for all ranks d>=17. Four certified transfer values in that range
therefore determine its polynomial as a corollary of that theorem.
Only the degree upper bound and explicit threshold are retained;
the source construction used elsewhere to prove the matching growth
lower bound is not needed here. Values from a few small-rank censuses
alone would not establish a polynomial formula.

## 3. Exact state construction and budget pruning

The window reconstruction uses the immutable phase21 rank-five universal
group of class at most five. Its saved index-two and index-four kernels
are all candidates for excesses one and two. The saved accepted index-eight
kernels are the complete excess-three library. The index-one kernel is
gamma3 of the universal group itself. The program restores each subgroup
from its canonical pc keys, verifies its normality and index, checks the
small-kernel profile, and checks every actual marked meet on each survivor.
Thus the lower-excess windows are reconstructed by the same exact method;
their previously known counts are checks, not candidate filters.

Let U4 be the universal rank-four, class-five path group. For either
rank-four boundary B of a window, the homomorphism U4 -> B taking the
four original generators to the boundary marking is uniquely determined
and surjective. Two such marked boundaries are isomorphic if and only
if these kernels are equal. The implementation identifies this kernel
by the canonical induced pc sequence in one fixed U4, after checking all
relative pc orders are two. Whenever two keys agree, the actual unique
marked boundary isomorphism is also constructed and checked bijective.
This changes only the cost of finding overlaps; it does not weaken the
isomorphism condition.

For each state (H,R), its smallest reachable total excess is maintained.
Initial states have cost equal to the excess of H. Every transition has
weight log2|Hnew:B| - 2, which is nonnegative by the phase20 theorem.
Dijkstra's least-cost-first processing therefore finalizes the true
minimum cost when it processes a state. An edge is discarded for budget
reasons only if this minimum plus its weight exceeds three. Every path
of total excess at most three remains; conversely every retained path
of that total excess is a valid walk of the full phase20 graph.

Each state still stores its actual subgroup R, not merely its order or
isomorphism type. By (1) it is a subgroup of the boundary gamma3, an
abelian group of order at most eight. Such a group has at most sixteen
subgroups. Therefore the search has at most 706 times 16 states, independently
of rank, and terminates. The bound is not a claim that every such subgroup
is reachable. Both compatibility tests are retained before computing
the exact next deletion kernel.

The resulting finite matrix has a monomial x^w for each accepted
transition of weight w. Its initial vector records the actual window
excesses. Iterating this matrix modulo x^4 counts exactly the ordered
marked groups of each rank and each excess at most three. The coefficient
calculation performs no further quotient by automorphisms or reversal.
The previously proved formulas at excess zero, one and two are checked
separately against every computed transfer row.


## 4. Complete finite computation and the exceptional states

The frozen clean run reconstructs the window counts (1,15,106,584)
at excesses zero through three. It performs 615,037 small-kernel meet
comparisons and all 722,944 actual parabolic meets on the resulting
706 groups. The complete exact boundary matching finds 3,689 pairs.
The graph reachable within total excess three has 708 states and
1,522 transitions. Of the remaining transition attempts, 2,185 are
excluded solely by the excess budget. No candidate in this bounded run
fails either extra compatibility test; both tests are executed and
remain part of the general theorem and implementation.

The two additional states have the same windows as certain initial
states but different deletion kernels. Both have an actual kernel of
order two and are reached by one transition from an initial window.
The code independently constructs the two corresponding rank-six groups
as semidirect products with the specified diagonal quotient. It checks
the actual group orders 16,384, both factor embeddings, all 8,192
combined actual marked meets, nilpotency class at most five, and the
actual deletion kernels. In these two examples the gluing intersection
J is trivial; they do not test a nontrivial diagonal quotient or a
failed compatibility condition.

The saved graph has the JSON schema

    [initial_rows, state_rows, edge_rows, count_rows],

where an initial row is [state, excess], a state row is
[window, canonical_pc_keys_of_R, minimum_excess, order_of_R], an edge
row is [source, target, weight], and a count row is
[rank, a0, a1, a2, a3]. States and windows use one-based indices. Each
window record contains its canonical universal kernel, its excess and
the two exact boundary kernels. Thus the result retains the complete
finite graph, not only an aggregate answer.

A separate Python checker recomputes shortest costs with a heap and
recounts walks with a sparse dictionary indexed by (state,excess).
Both differ from the GAP implementation. It agrees on every transfer
row through rank 24 and checks the already proved lower-excess formulas
at every row. This is independent arithmetic on the saved graph, not
an independent regeneration of all GAP groups or transitions.

By section 5 of [the central-join theorem](phase16_central_join.md),
a3(d) agrees with a polynomial P of degree at most three for every
d>=6*3-1=17. Its values at 17,18,19,20 are those just computed. The
successive initial forward differences are 67474,14555,1965,125, so
uniqueness of a polynomial of degree at most three gives

    P(d)=67474+14555(d-17)+1965 binomial(d-17,2)
         +125 binomial(d-17,3)
        =(125d^3-855d^2+2380d-2646)/6.                 (2)

This proves (T) for all d>=17. The separately computed transfer rows
for 5<=d<=16 agree with (2), proving the same formula for those ranks.
The class-five automaton starts at rank five and makes no assertion
about rank four. That remaining rank comes from the complete
[rank-four excess-three census](phase21_rank4_census.json), which gives
199 ordered markings; direct substitution in (2) also gives 199.
This closes the entire range d>=4 without extending the automaton
below its initial window rank.

Finally the complete [rank-three census](phase21_rank3_census.json)
gives 37. Formula (2) would give 29 there, so rank three is an explicit
exception rather than an extrapolation. The computed rows at ranks
21 through 24 provide additional consistency checks but are not needed
for the interpolation argument. Reversal classes are not derived by
dividing (T) by two; their self-dual contribution would require a
separate argument.

## 6. Reproduction, dependencies and limits

From a directory containing these originals and the frozen phase21
inputs, use a fresh output prefix:

```sh
python3 phase22_excess3_run.py --output-prefix scratch/third_excess
python3 phase22_excess3_check.py --prefix scratch/third_excess --output scratch/recount.json
```

The runner finds GAP through the GAP environment variable or PATH;
`--gap` selects an explicit executable. It verifies hashes of the saved
phase21 kernel inputs, writes temporary integer-only GAP data, records
the exact executed source and runner hashes, and requires a terminal
PASS. The final recorded environment is GAP 4.15.1 with NQ 2.5.11.
The final clean run takes about 104 seconds in the recorded environment;
this is an observation, not a general complexity claim. All finite
output files are original mathematical data; no third-party paper or
external presentation list is bundled.

The retained mathematical inputs are:

* [The forest canonical quotient](phase15_graded_support.md), for
  the deletion-defect containment and excess grading.
* [The invariant-kernel census proof](phase21_kernel_ladder.md) and
  its exact complete rank-three through rank-five outputs.
* [The finite-state theorem](phase20_finite_state.md), including its
  two compatibility conditions, exact deletion update, marked walk
  bijection and nonnegative excess weights.
* [The central-join counting theorem](phase16_central_join.md), only
  its polynomial degree upper bound and stabilization threshold.

The lower-excess formulas from phases seventeen and nineteen are
consistency checks; the window set is reconstructed by the exhaustive
kernel method rather than by filtering for their parameter patterns.
The exact universal nilpotent quotient and finite-group operations in
GAP/NQ remain computational inputs. This is neither a full Lean proof
nor an assertion of external peer review or publication priority.
A bounded source-overlap audit is separate from this mathematical and
computational package. Earlier frozen artifacts and their historical
scope statements have not been changed.
