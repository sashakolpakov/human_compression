# Independent audit: smallest cyclic factor eight

10 October 2026. Private internal review of
[phase11_cyclic_tpp.md](phase11_cyclic_tpp.md). **PASS: no mathematical gap
found in the stated structural consequences.** The unrestricted cyclic-TPP
inequality and the full smallest-factor-eight case are not proved here.

I read the entire note, the required phase10 proof, the exact retained
nonmetacyclic multiplication rules in the private primary manuscript,
and both diagnostic scripts. The classification of marked isobicyclic
groups remains a retained input; it was not independently re-proved.

The strengthened outside-element claim is sound. In the nonmetacyclic
case an outside element with odd sum of exponents has a square with two
odd exponents, whose square lies in the central order-two subgroup.
Any resulting element of order eight therefore has central involution.
In the metacyclic case, for i≠0 with w=v₂(i), the exponent K in the square
has valuation w. The subsequent geometric sum contributes e−w−1,
leaving exactly valuation e−1. This includes i=n/2 and the ratio R=1.

The local-centrality argument uses the unique supporting Frattini class
correctly: conjugation by b preserves that class in M_A, while the index-two
Schreier generators force b² to occupy an independent class. If its
involution were central in M_A, it would also centralize b and hence P.
Thus B² is center-free there. Its order n/2>4 is the precise use of n≥16.

The central quotient argument is valid. A central order-two subgroup
different from both commutator subgroups leaves the two cyclic orders
unchanged, and their involutions remain noncentral in the quotient.
The quotient Frattini classes remain distinct. A noncyclic center would
supply such a central subgroup: there are at least three order-two
subgroups in total, so at least one remains after excluding up to two.
The author corrected the original wording to make this count precise.

The normal E₈ subgroup is genuinely elementary abelian. Its three
involutions commute, and commutation with a and b proves their independence.
Both top factor involutions centralize Φ(P); the central commutator lies
in Φ(P). The two displayed independent transvections therefore have
common kernel exactly Φ(P), proving C_P(E)=Φ(P).

Under the temporary assumption that Φ(P) is abelian, d=[a²,b] satisfies
d^(n/4)=z and has order n/2. The three cyclic subgroups generated by a²,
b²,d form a direct product: any nontrivial kernel in their abelian product
map would contain an involution, contradicting independence of u,v,z.
The order inequality (n/2)³≤|Φ(P)|=n² gives n≤8, as claimed.

The coset-action count in §6 is also correct: there are 4n vertices,
two parts of size 2n, and two free oriented cross-part orbitals, each of
size |P|. This gives neither connectedness nor self-pairing of an individual
orbital. The note does not assume either property or an involutory swap.

A fresh normal-form run matched the author's report exactly: 46 models,
96,640 outside elements and 7,280 center-free outside elements. These are
bounded diagnostics, separate from the uniform argument. I also freshly
ran the standalone SmallGroup(32,5) witness with GAP 4.15.1 and SmallGrp
1.5.4: the cyclic orders are 2,8,2, TPP holds, their product equals 32,
and the set ABC has only 24 elements. This refutes the tempting global
injectivity shortcut, not the cyclic-TPP inequality.
