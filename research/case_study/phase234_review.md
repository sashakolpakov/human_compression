## 2. The uniform mathematical reduction

The directly-indecomposable argument correctly excludes adjacent labels two.
For a direct product decomposition, a nonabelian simple normal PSL2 subgroup
must meet one factor nontrivially: otherwise its commutators with both factors
would be trivial. The factor containing it then forces the other to centralize
it. The explicitly retained centralizer fact makes the other factor trivial.
The elementary normal-subgroup lists handle S3,A4,S4 separately. There is no
small-q appeal to simplicity where it fails.

Both projective centralizer calculations are sound. In characteristic two,
a nonidentity involution is unipotent; any commuting projectivity fixes its
unique fixed point and must be a translation. Thus its centralizer is abelian.
In odd characteristic, extending scalars lets a four-group be generated by
z↦−z and z↦1/z. Their common centralizer is exactly that four-group, proving
the obstruction to three independent commuting involutions. These arguments
exclude rank at least five, and all rank-four cases in characteristic two.

The new shared descent lemma is stronger than the special finite tests and
is proved uniformly. If u=ab is semisimple of order greater than two, a
centralizing projectivity fixes its two eigenlines individually. The reflection
a swaps them, so the only possible nonidentity centralizer is the eigenvalue
ratio−1 projectivity. For any matrix A representing u, the trace-zero matrix
A−tr(A)I/2 is invertible, represents this projectivity, and lies over the
original field. This handles nonsplit eigenlines as well as split ones.
If u is unipotent, its commuting translations are inverted by the reflection;
in odd characteristic only the identity translation commutes with both.
The dihedral order 2p case is therefore included, as required by the source's
own corrective remark.

An even endpoint label would already place the unique nonidentity dihedral
centralizer inside that endpoint parabolic. The independent fourth generator
cannot equal it. Thus both end labels are odd. This conclusion is not assumed
from the historical incidence-counting lemmas.

The remaining facet reduction uses only the retained **types** in Dickson's
classification, together with their standard subfield embeddings. Cyclic,
elementary abelian and A4 cases are excluded directly. In a one-dimensional
affine group in odd characteristic, two distinct involutions cannot commute,
excluding the two endpoint markers. In a dihedral group the square of an
adjacent rotation belongs to both adjacent parabolics and is nonidentity,
contradicting IP. These exclusions are complete.

For a standard PGL2(k) facet, centralizer descent immediately puts the fourth
generator inside the facet. For a PSL2(k) facet it puts that generator inside
PGL2(k), which normalizes the facet. In the resulting quotient, the fourth
generator has order dividing two and also dividing the odd last adjacent
order, so it is trivial. This again contradicts independence. The argument
works for every permitted extension degree and the small subfields. An
exceptional S4 or A5 lying in a different characteristic is correctly left
as an exceptional subgroup rather than falsely treated as a subfield group.
No hidden subgroup-conjugacy count is used.

## 3. The exact finite part and completeness

The finite code enumerates all involutions and all ordered commuting endpoint
pairs in S4 and A5. It then checks generation and every marked intersection.
Full automorphism orbits, rather than only conjugacy orbits, give exactly
three local marking classes in each group:72 and360 ordered markings.
Every one of the six Petrie presentations is independently formed as a

presentation, not merely a relation list valid in a quotient.

The fourteen compatible ordered local pairs form ten duality orbits, with
six fixed pairs. The actual ten presentations include every involution,

exact finitely-presented-group orders and transports the literal generators
through an isomorphism to a permutation group. It checks all 256 subset-pair
intersections where the marking is tested. A successful abstract group
identification alone would not suffice; the code supplies the marked tests.
The trivial groups are handled explicitly.

All rows, including the order 96 semidirect product and the collapsed A5 and
S4 rows, are accounted for. The successful principal rows are identified by
actual group isomorphisms with PSL2(19), PSL2(11) and S5. The reversal maps
are checked as bijective homomorphisms. Face counts use actual parabolics;
the K11 claim uses actual vertex/edge cosets and 55 distinct endpoint pairs.
The order 96 structure includes its elementary abelian normal 16-subgroup,
quotient S3 and a complement.

The written quotient argument closes the exhaustion step. The collapsed
rows retain a dependent generator in every quotient. No PSL2(q), q≥4, has
order dividing 96; the small cases cannot have the required proper facets.
The possible PGL2(3) quotient of the order 96 row is likewise excluded by its
facet. Nontrivial quotients of the two simple rows are the rows themselves.
S5 has only C2 as a proper nontrivial quotient. Finally the projective group
orders and odd-characteristic restriction identify q uniquely. Consequently
there are no overlooked quotients with new admissible markings.

Uniqueness follows because every possible local marked pair factors through
its specific universal presentation, and every surviving quotient map is an
isomorphism. The source's local map names are the retained classical
identifications with antipodal Platonic quotients. The claimed covering
universality uses the explicitly retained marked-epimorphism/polytope-cover
interface, after the universal markings have passed IP. It does not assume
that arbitrary group amalgams are polytopal. The adjacent-transposition
marking identifies the final S5 polytope with the simplex.

I found no mathematical gap requiring an amendment to the frozen author proof.

## 4. Independent execution and formal limits

A fresh author replay passed in 6.2 seconds: all six local presentations, ten
universal groups, the complete marked tests, both deliberate bad-input
controls and five standard-only Lean reports. I read each Lean statement and
proof. They formalize the odd-power involution argument, its homomorphic and

normalizer condition, and contradiction with an independent generator. The
strongest statement does not assume facet normality or the desired membership.
It does assume the normalizer and odd-order hypotheses that the written
matrix application supplies.

The matrix argument, Dickson classification, group-to-polytope correspondence
and all finite-presentation order certificates remain outside Lean. GAP's
exact algorithms are trusted mathematical computation here. This is more
than checking a finite image, but it is not a Lean proof of the ten universal
orders. Cached Mathlib dependency objects are also part of the declared
formal trust boundary. The review grants no complete formalization claim.

Separately I built native projective actions over the field extensions
F3⊂F9 and F5⊂F25, without importing the author's finite code. Every ordered
base-field involution pair with product order greater than two satisfies
descent, the exact centralizer size and, in the semisimple case, the explicit
trace-zero representative formula. The two cases cover 24+360 semisimple
pairs and 24+120 unipotent pairs; their product labels are 3,4 and 3,4,5,6.
Every involution in PGL2(4) and PGL2(8) also has abelian centralizer. These
are bounded diagnostics for the written general lemma.

Two separate controls illustrate essential hypotheses: a normal subgroup of
C2×C2 allows an independent involution with an **even** adjacent order, while
the adjacent transpositions of S3 have an odd product but do **not** normalize
the singleton facet. Thus deleting either oddness or normalizing from the
abstract obstruction would give a false theorem.
