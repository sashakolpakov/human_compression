In the replacement notation the permutation degree is N ≥ 4 with N ≠ 6, and n = floor(N/2). The counted objects are unordered pairs of distinct nonidentity commuting involutions, modulo the source's group-automorphism equivalence.

I read the two theorem statements, the definition of equivalence in §2, all selected source proof spans, and the adjacent omitted passages. I also rendered the cached primary PDF locally and visually checked both displayed theorem formulas on pages 1–2. The replacement's floor and ceiling functions, parity cases and numerical coefficients match those formulas. The degree-six exceptional clauses and the polyhedron theorems are explicitly excluded. In particular, degree seven is included: its ordinary permutation conjugacy classes are not fused by the exceptional automorphisms of degree six.

## All-parameter mathematical check

The replacement classifies actions of the marked group V = C₂² generated by the ordered pair. Because V is abelian, its five subgroups are precisely the possible orbit stabilizers, with no conjugacy ambiguity. Orbit multiplicities therefore classify the action under permutation conjugacy. The kernel is the intersection of the occurring stabilizers, so the stated faithfulness condition is exact. Swapping the generators interchanges exactly the multiplicities x and y.

I checked the parameter conversion in both directions:

    k = y+z+2t,   l = z+2t,   z = l−2t,   y = k−l,
    f = N−2(x+k),   0 ≤ x ≤ n−k,   0 ≤ t ≤ floor(l/2).

On a regular four-point orbit each generator contributes two transpositions. On a two-point orbit precisely the generators outside its stabilizer contribute one. This proves the conversion and the degree equation. The inverse parameters are nonnegative in exactly the stated ranges, and f has the parity of N. No orbit types are omitted.

For a nonidentity first involution, the only nonfaithful choices are the second element being the identity or the first involution itself, giving the subtraction of two. Swap-fixed classes have x = y, equivalently equal transposition counts. Their lower bound on l is max(0,2k−n), and only the equality of the two involutions must then be removed. Thus the fixed-class count is exactly D(k,n).

The sum defining λ(k) follows by pairing consecutive even and odd summands. For h = 2k−n > 0, the omitted initial sum is h(h+2)/4 when h is even and (h+1)²/4 when h is odd. Since h and n have the same parity, the two displayed ψ expressions agree with the source, including the odd expression (h−1)²/4+h. Combining ordered and swap-fixed classes gives the exact symmetric formula, not merely a sequence with matching first coefficients.

For the alternating formula, a is even exactly when k is even, and b is even exactly when l+x is even. Summing the even and odd l cases gives the displayed γ(k) and δ(k); counting x of matching parity gives μ(n). Both removed choices have even parity. Restricting the swap-fixed count to even k gives the source's φ(k,n). This proves the exact alternating formula in every allowed degree.

Finally, the retained automorphism identifications turn permutation conjugacy into the required equivalence for both S_N and A_N when N ≥ 4, N ≠ 6. No assertion about conjugacy of pairs inside A_N is substituted for that automorphism equivalence. The proof is elementary and does not depend on a computational table, a finite coefficient comparison or a hidden counting lemma from the source.
