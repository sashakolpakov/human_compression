## A shorter sufficient theorem

Let I and the family F of distinct subsets S⊆I be finite. Let B be generated by marked elements b_i and have the intersection property. For each S∈F require an actual **deletion homomorphism** p_S:B→B_S, with p_S(b_i)=b_i for i∈S and p_S(b_i)=1 otherwise. Let E_S be generated by e_(S,i), i∈S, and let q_S:E_S→B_S be the marked epimorphism. Put N_S=ker q_S. The one additional local hypothesis is:

> For every proper U⊊S, q_S is injective on the subgroup (E_S)_U.

Form the full simultaneous fiber product H with base B and coordinates E_S, and let h_i have base b_i and coordinate e_(S,i) for i∈S, identity otherwise. Then the h_i generate the entire fiber product, and for every L⊆I,

`π(H_L)=B_L`,

`ker π = ∏_(S∈F) N_S`,

`ker π ∩ H_L = ∏_(S⊆L) N_S`.

Consequently H has IP. If finite, `|H_L|=|B_L| ∏_(S⊆L)|N_S|`. These conclusions do not require a separate list of isolated normal generators and do not assert that an arbitrary amalgam of the local groups is this fiber product.

**Proof of generation and isolation.** Write H' for the subgroup generated by the h_i. Prove by inclusion induction that the entire pure coordinate N_S lies in H'_S. For n∈N_S, choose a word w on S evaluating to n in E_S, possible because those markers generate E_S. Its base value is 1: its base evaluation lies in B_S and equals q_S(n).

For another coordinate T, its value has q_T-image 1. If T is not contained in S, the value lies in (E_T)_(S∩T), a proper-support parabolic, so is 1 by injectivity. Thus only coordinates T⊆S can be nonidentity. For every proper T⊊S, its value belongs to N_T. By induction the corresponding pure element belongs to H'_T⊆H'_S. Removing those pure elements isolates exactly the prescribed n in coordinate S. There are finitely many supports, so inclusion induction applies also when supports are nested. No commutativity inside an individual N_S is assumed; different pure coordinate groups commute in the ambient product.

All N_S therefore lie in H'. Since H' maps onto B, it is the full fiber product. For H_L, a kernel element's S-coordinate lies in (E_S)_(L∩S), which injects under q_S unless S⊆L. Conversely every pure N_S with S⊆L lies in H_S⊆H_L by the induction. This proves the profile. Surjectivity onto B_L is immediate on marked words.

If x∈H_L∩H_M, its base lies in B_(L∩M) by IP. Choose y∈H_(L∩M) with the same base. Then xy^-1 belongs to both parabolic kernels. Their coordinate profiles intersect exactly in the profile for L∩M, so xy^-1, and hence x, lies in H_(L∩M). This proves all parabolic intersections, not only the two maximal ends. The order formula follows from the resulting exact sequence.

## Further consequences with no repeated isolation proof

**Antichain local recognition.** If F is an antichain, projection H_S→E_S is a marked isomorphism for each selected S. It is onto on generators. An element in its kernel has trivial base, and the profile for S contains only N_S. The S-coordinate detects that factor. No finiteness or order comparison is required.

**Boolean deletions.** If B has all Boolean deletions, every E_S has them already. For U⊊S the restricted q_S:(E_S)_U→B_U is a marked isomorphism. Define the deletion on E_S to be its inverse after the composite E_S→B_S→B_U. This fixes the U-markers and kills the others; for U=S use the identity. Coordinatewise deletion on H preserves the compatibility equations because the maps commute on all marked generators. Thus the draft's extra assumption that every E_S separately has Boolean deletions is redundant under the local injectivity hypothesis. Geometric FAP conclusions still use their expressly retained interpretation.

**Lower central series.** Suppose c≥1, B has nilpotency class less than c, and N_S=γ_c(E_S) for every S. Then ker π=γ_c(H). The easy inclusion is γ_c(H)≤ker π. Repeat the inclusion induction: an element of N_S is the image of an element of γ_c(H_S), since H_S→E_S is onto and lower central series commute with epimorphic images. Its other nonzero kernel coordinates can occur only at proper subsets T⊊S; those pure kernels already lie in γ_c(H) by induction and can be removed. Hence every pure N_S lies in γ_c(H). No explicit isolated γ_c-word list is needed. This consequence does not require antichain supports.

**Separator factorization.** Suppose I=L∪M, B=B_L B_M as a product of sets, and every support is contained in L or M. Then H=H_L H_M. Lift a base factorization and remove it from h, leaving a pure kernel element. Assign each coordinate kernel to one of the containing sets to write it n_L n_M. Each pure N_S is normal in H, so n_M can be moved across the chosen H_L lift and remains in the same product of M-supported kernels. The resulting factors lie in H_L and H_M. This argument assumes neither commutation nor normality of the whole parabolics.

The separate normal-generator hypothesis in the original SK draft is sufficient but unnecessary. The simpler interface uses all proper-support injectivity as the actual boundary. This is a reviewer-suggested strengthening accepted provisionally by the author; final frozen wording still needs review.

## Small counterexamples and safeguards

The accompanying standard-library program gives exact finite arithmetic for two examples and records the definitional third check. It runs with explicit exceptions under optimized Python.

1. **Singleton injectivity is insufficient.** Let E=D8×C2, with r of order four, reflections s and rs, and central involution u. Mark a=s, b=rs, c=su. The quotient by <r²> is B=C2³, and each marked cyclic subgroup maps injectively. Yet <a,b> and <b,c> each have order eight and intersect in <b,r²> of order four, not <b> of order two. The end markers a,c commute, so even the string relation holds. With the one support {a,b,c}, any normal-generator isolation requirement is automatic; IP still fails if the full proper-parabolic hypothesis is weakened to singletons. The program enumerates all sixteen elements and checks the quotient, singletons and exact intersection.

2. **Full fiber product is not a universal presentation.** Take B=C2³, supports {a,b} and {b,c}, with each local extension D8→C2². The generated full simultaneous fiber product has order 32 and IP; the program verifies all 36 unordered parabolic pairs. The universal Coxeter group [4,4] has the same marked two-generator subgroups and distant commutation but is infinite. Explicit affine reflections a(x,y)=(-x,y), b(x,y)=(y,x), c(x,y)=(x,1-y) satisfy those relations, while applying a,b,c,b successively gives the unit translation (x,y)→(x+1,y). Its powers are distinct. Thus local data alone cannot identify the finite supported-kernel realization with a universal amalgam or its particular presentation quotient.

3. **Retraction must mean deletion.** In B=C2²=<a,b>, p(a)=a,p(b)=a is a retraction onto <a>. With E_<a>=<a> and q the identity, the proposed tuple for b is (b,1), which is not in the fiber product. Explicitly requiring omitted marks to map to 1 prevents this ambiguity. The author's intended maps were deletions; the statement should say so literally.

These are counterexamples to weakened or conflated claims, not to the original fully interpreted sufficient SK theorem.
