## The theorem being compared

The reviewed phase216 theorem says that every abstract polytope of finite rank has a minimal regular polytopal cover, and that this cover is unique up to rank-preserving isomorphism if and only if its monodromy group, with the distinguished adjacency generators, is a string C-group. Neither the base polytope nor the covers must be finite. The geometric argument gives admissible kernels from all sufficiently large powers; the normal-kernel join and maximal-kernel argument supplies the uniqueness conclusion. This is the exact target used throughout the audit.

The proof is bound by `phase216_export.json`, SHA256 `daba2e40dc03b5c7f6067e9ea938ff4e408d82d45e412b4ff9b72e70b0269f13`, and its independent review by `phase222_manifest.json`, SHA256 `819bfc4835ada4653094f33e5620399fce06d5a26acde747d2d28a91bc0270b8`. The general geometric theorem is written and independently reviewed; its geometric inputs and complete application are not a full Lean formalization. Nothing in this priority audit enlarges that formal scope.

## The original problem and the later statements

**Cunningham–Pellicer, 2018.** In §3.1, Problem 5, the authors ask whether failure of the monodromy intersection property can coexist with a unique minimal regular polytopal cover. The bound author PDF is dated 1 July 2018, with the question on page 8; its historical local filename is not an edition description. The publisher's accessible §3.1 repeats the same question without a finiteness restriction. The surrounding discussion separately mentions the theorem that finite polytopes have finite regular covers. That adjacent fact does not change the question's quantifiers. Problem 4, recognizing when monodromy is a C-group, and Problem 6, classifying minimal C-group covers of an arbitrary sggi, are different tasks. [Publisher article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2018.03.004); [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07993).

**Cunningham, 2023 author edition / 2024 publication.** The complete eight-page author text was read in this audit. The published JCTA preview retains the general expectation and the minimal-cover definition. **Cunningham–Mixer–Williams, 2024.** The acquired journal PDF defines minimality in the covering order in §2.2, printed page 197. Theorem 4.10, page 207, proves that prisms over polyhedra have C-group monodromy. This is a family for which the existing sufficient condition gives a unique minimal cover; it is not the general converse. Its Question 5.1 and Conjecture 5.2 concern higher-rank prisms and a mix with a cube. The abstract's phrase equating C-connectedness with uniqueness must be read with this established family result, rather than promoted to a proof of the unrestricted converse. ## Four distinctions that must survive any public summary

| Question | What the frozen general theorem supplies |
|---|---|
| Smallest regular maniplex | Its existence is prior theory. It may fail polytopality; it is not automatically a minimal regular polytope. |
| Unique minimal regular **polytope** | The complete equivalence with distinguished monodromy being a string C-group, in arbitrary finite rank. |
| Finite base, finite covers only | The theorem allows infinite competing covers. It does not prove two finite minimal covers whenever a finite base has non-C monodromy. |
| Number of minimal covers | In the negative case it guarantees at least two. It does not prove infinitely many in every case, a prescribed covering degree, or effective construction bounds. |

Minimality here is the absence of a strictly smaller regular polytopal cover below a given cover. Being covered by every regular cover is the stronger minimum property. The kernel argument in phase216 proves when these notions coincide through uniqueness; their equivalence cannot be assumed as vocabulary before that argument. Similarly, the general result concerns kernels of actual polytope flag actions. It does not solve the entire arbitrary-sggi classification problem.

The known implication from C-group monodromy to a unique smallest polytopal cover remains credited to the earlier monodromy theory. The new result claimed by phase216 is the missing converse together with unrestricted existence, through its specified retained geometric inputs and new application. This audit does not establish historical novelty of either component separately.
