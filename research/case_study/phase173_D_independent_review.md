## Independent mathematical assessment

I read the full final application and all four source text bodies, and checked the ledger against their actual statements. The source editions, exact hashes and limits of visual inspection are recorded in `phase173_D_reading.json`. I independently inspected all 18 rendered application pages. The notation, displayed arguments and tables are readable; long program names on page 17 wrap awkwardly but are legible. No blocking rendering defect was observed. The author render log has one underfull-box warning and no overfull boxes or missing-glyph diagnostics.

For L066, the five transitive Klein-four actions give precisely the multiplicities with stabilizers the whole group, its three order-two subgroups, and the identity. Faithfulness and parity impose the stated restrictions, and reversal exchanges the two relevant multiplicities. The centralizer and sign-kernel arguments are sufficient for the symmetric and alternating branches. The floor formulas follow by summing the multiplicities in every nonexceptional degree; they are not inferred from a finite fit. The degree-six branch first enumerates all raw pairs and computes their full automorphism orbits. It then uses the resulting representatives for the middle-involution fibers. This order matters: a borrowed representative list would not establish completeness. The resulting five symmetric pair classes and one alternating pair class, with two symmetric regular-polyhedron completions and no alternating completion, satisfy generation and the complete subgroup intersection tests. The source's endpoint reversal remark is appropriately separated from self-duality of a completed triple.

For L074, the two-coordinate normalization uses explicitly retained Suzuki structure. Normalized triples are parametrized by nonzero coordinates with the stated exclusions, and the Frobenius and endpoint-reversal actions are derived on those coordinates. The generation proof treats the positioning of a subfield subgroup: after fixing the two ovoid points, the remaining torus conjugation is forced into the subfield by the normalized first involution. Consequently the relevant condition is the joint field of definition of both coordinates, rather than either coordinate alone. The other maximal-subgroup possibilities are excluded by their Sylow-two or fixed-point structure. The intersection-property proof uses the unique torus containing an odd-order intersection to force a forbidden proper normalizer. The rank restriction and the obstruction to a proper almost-simple extension follow with the retained involution-centralizer and odd-order outer-automorphism facts.

Exact-degree Frobenius orbits are free. Endpoint reversal has no fixed Frobenius orbit for odd extension degree; the order-two versus odd-order argument forces the excluded coordinate value. Möbius inversion therefore yields the displayed formula for every admissible field, and the fiber argument proves equality with the source's nested formula. Small-field computations are diagnostics for these written arguments, not evidence sufficient to establish their uniform conclusion. The application now identifies the broader arbitrary-field Bruhat and ovoid background that it does not reprove. Suzuki, Lüneburg and other classical structural results remain assumptions with attribution.

For the geometry sections, the panel-recognition theorem distinguishes the subgroup intersection property from the source's rank-two geometric axiom. Typed coset Helly gives flag transitivity, the join conditions give residual connectedness, and the panel indices give firmness. The converse reconstructs the panel joins using residual connectedness. The local double-coset and common-neighbor tests express the stated two-transitivity and rank-two conditions. The maximal-overgroup transport step accounts for every conjugate inclusion through the relevant normalizer orbits. Residual weak primitivity supplies a maximal deletion, so complete augmentation is justified inductively. The strict subgroup chain gives termination; checking an empty next level supplies an executable completeness witness for each Borel, rather than relying only on the abstract rank bound.

The J2 implementation covers all 146 Borel conjugacy classes and their maximal inclusions, reaches terminal empty levels, and performs full group-automorphism and type fusion. Its separate final witness pass checks the geometric conditions on all 30 representatives. These arguments support the declared complete all-ranks census, conditional on the named-group catalogue and exact group algorithms.
