Equivariant incidence reconstruction for two Livingstone-graph polytopes
1. Scope and source boundary
Hartley, Hubard and Leemans [HHL12] construct two regular rank-four polytopes from the Livingstone
graph of the first Janko group. Their main contribution is geometric: the faces are explicit graph subsets,
and incidence is specified by intersections and distances. The existence of abstract polytopes with the
same automorphism group does not establish this construction. The additional task is to identify the
literal graph incidence relations with the already certified coset incidence relations.
We give a common verification argument and carry out that identification. All six relations defining the
second structure, of type {5, 6, 5}, agree with its coset geometry. For the first structure, of type {5, 3, 5},
five of the six displayed relations agree. The remaining printed relation omits a compatibility condition:
it creates twelve incident facets at each 2-face, whereas the intended construction has two. We exhibit a
pairwise incident flag that cannot extend to a chamber. Adding the compatibility supplied by the source’s
Proposition 3.5 recovers the intended regular polytope.
Consequently this work does not prove both main theorems for the literal structures printed in [HHL12].
It recovers Theorem 5.1 and its stabilizer lattice, proves a corrected form of Theorem 4.1, and records a
defect in the printed definition. The correction must remain visible in any source ledger or compression
count. It is not an open-conjecture result. No priority for the correction is asserted: a targeted title/DOI
search on 10 October 2026 did not locate an erratum, but that is not a proof that none exists.
The reusable mathematical step is an elementary equivariant reconstruction. The finite source-specific
part is a complete examination of the graph objects and their neighbour sets. The abstract marked-group
certificates of Module C are retained unchanged; they supply generation, the full intersection property,
typed Helly and the two Schläfli types. Named-group data, exact permutation-group algorithms and the
classical identification of the degree-266 action remain explicit inputs.

3. The graph and its finite object families
Let 𝐺 = 𝐽 1 in its standard transitive action on 266 points. We retain the classical identification of this
action, with vertex stabilizer PSL2(11), and of its unique orbital graph of valency 11 with the Livingstone
graph 𝐿. This is the construction used in [HHL12, §3], ultimately from Livingstone and the classical
distance-regular-graph literature. The finite checker reads the named permutation group from TomLib
and takes the unique 11-point orbit of a point stabilizer as the neighbourhood of that point. Transport
defines the other neighbourhoods.
The checker verifies graph invariance under every supplied group generator, symmetry, absence of loops,
and the complete intersection array
{11, 10, 6, 1; 1, 1, 5, 11}.
It checks the intersection numbers for every ordered pair of vertices. In particular the distance distribution
is 1, 11, 110, 132, 12, and the graph has girth five. There are 1463 edges. No source geometry list or
source pentagon list is used as input.
Every pentagon is enumerated by taking its least vertex first and its smaller adjacent vertex second, ex-
tending four edges, and requiring the last edge to close. Girth five excludes chords; the ordering removes
the two orientations and the five starting points. The enumeration produces 11,704 pentagons, and their
actual 𝐺-orbits have lengths 2926 and 8778. Following [HHL12], call the smaller orbit white, 𝒲, and
the larger orange, 𝒪.

sets 𝑃𝑒. Each contains six white pentagons; their complements in 𝑃𝑒 are orange pentagons. Each white

Finally, a type-preserving automorphism fixing a chamber fixes its adjacent chamber across every panel,
because each panel belongs to exactly two chambers. Chamber connectedness propagates this to every
chamber, and every face belongs to a chamber. Thus such an automorphism is the identity. The full type-

namely |𝐽1|. The already faithful, regular action of 𝐽1 reaches this bound. □
This last argument uses the geometry’s thinness and connectedness, rather than assuming that every
automorphism of the geometry comes from the graph. It therefore supplies the full-group conclusion
without an unproved converse between graph and geometry automorphisms.
6. Attribution, verification and cost
The constructions, their intended polytope types and their geometric ingredients belong to [HHL12]. Its
Propositions 3.1–3.3 explicitly cite the classical distance-regular-graph literature. The 𝐽1 action, vertex
subgroup structure, local design and previous abstract-polytope classification are retained historical in-
puts. Aschbacher’s theorem appears as source Theorem 2.1; our argument instead reuses the existing U/C
recognition proof. The orbit–stabilizer reconstruction in §2 is standard. The present work contributes its
common verification interface, the exact source-to-coset reconstruction, and the explicit audit and repair
of the missing condition. None of these statements asserts historical priority.
The computational proof obligations are finite and fully specified: construct every relevant family; prove
its completeness and transitivity by the arguments above; calculate the exact stabilizers; compare every
local source neighbour set with the appropriate stabilizer orbit. The GAP checker performs those tasks
5
from the named group, not from the source geometry lists. It also retains the literal failure as an expected
counterexample, so a successful repaired run cannot erase it.
The Lean theorem certifies the reusable logical implication after those hypotheses have been supplied. It
is not a Lean certificate of the GAP objects or the two full polytopes. The trusted base includes the pinned
compiler and cached Mathlib dependency objects for the component proof, and GAP/TomLib and their
named-group data for the finite work. The classical graph identification and the retained C proof remain
mathematical inputs.
This application must be charged for the new manuscript, GAP program, exact drivers and Lean source,
together with the retained C/U dependencies under the project’s common allocation rule. Source PDFs,
generated renderings, logs, manifests and review reports are support artifacts. The source has 22 pages;
that number alone does not determine a compression ratio. In particular the literal first principal theorem
cannot be admitted as an unchanged corollary when its displayed definition is false. The source ledger
records one recovered principal construction and one explicitly repaired construction, without inflating
the unchanged principal-source count.
