# Source scope for the M22 and M23 panel censuses

The classification problems are primary-source verified. The original two-page M22 announcement and four-page M23 paper were not obtained in readable full text in this phase. The author abstracts, the M22 publisher abstract, and two later primary summary tables were read. Consequently these are rederivations of their stated principal census components, conditional on the subgroup catalogue; we do not claim that every statement, construction or diagram in either inaccessible article was reproduced.

## Direct classification targets

**L101.** Dimitri Leemans, *The residually weakly primitive geometries of M22*, Designs, Codes and Cryptography **29** (2003), 177–178. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1024160626710), [author abstract](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~dleemans/abstracts/m22.html).

The author and publisher abstracts specify firmness, residual connectedness, `(IP)2`, `(2T)1`, flag-transitivity and RWPRI. They announce the complete classification, referring to a separate supplement for the list. The publisher landing page returned subscription-preview HTML, not a PDF. The author page's PostScript, DVI and archive links returned unavailable pages from the ULB/Auckland mirrors tried. The historical local file named `catalogue_L101.pdf` is a different J2 paper and is not evidence for reading L101.

**L098.** Dimitri Leemans, *The residually weakly primitive geometries of M23*, Atti Sem. Mat. Fis. Univ. Modena e Reggio Emilia **52** (2004), 313–316. [Author abstract](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~dleemans/abstracts/m23.html).

The author abstract specifies the identical axioms for M23 and points to the complete supplement. Its linked supplement was not retrieved. The historical `catalogue_L098.pdf` is also a wrong J2 paper. The abstract's publication year is retained here; later bibliographic records sometimes distinguish the volume's 2004 date from publication in 2005.

Neither abstract establishes the detailed equivalence convention of its inaccessible supplement. Our exact equivalence relation, Aut(G) together with arbitrary type permutation, is therefore always stated explicitly. Agreement with later tables is a consistency check, not a replacement for missing source text.

## Read primary numerical comparisons

**L096.** P. Jacobs and D. Leemans, *An algorithmic analysis of the intersection property*, LMS Journal of Computation and Mathematics **7** (2004), 284–299. [Publisher DOI and article](https://doi.org/10.1112/S1461157000001133).

Section 5 explicitly introduces the firm, residually connected, `(IP)2`, flag-transitive, RWPRI, locally two-transitive class. Table 1, printed p. 288, reports M22 ranks 3–5 as `(25,16,9)` and M23 as `(13,21,10)`. Truncations are included. The table also studies the additional shadow intersection properties `(IP)3` and higher; the present census does not claim to recompute those columns. Its `(IP)2` condition and a C-group's subgroup intersection property must not be conflated.

The Cambridge supplementary archive was recovered in prior phases and contains M22/M23 permutation data. It is not an enumeration input, is not redistributed, and was used only for a post-hoc comparison after independent enumeration completed. An archived geometry list would provide witnesses but would not establish independent exhaustion.

**L091.** Dimitri Leemans, *The residually weakly primitive geometries of the Higman–Sims group*, Australasian Journal of Combinatorics **33** (2005), 231–236. [Primary PDF](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/33/ajc_v33_p231.pdf).

The introductory class and Table 1 were read visually on pp. 231–233. Its M22 nontruncation row, ranks 2–6, is `(1,20,12,9,0)`; M23 is `(0,3,16,10,0)`. The natural reading restricts higher geometries to the same locally two-transitive class, which our post-processing computes. This table is not the source for claiming any HS computation in this phase.

## Access and integrity boundary

The bibliography and local filenames have been checked against article identity rather than trusted by name. An indexed ResearchGate record titled *The Residually Weakly Primitive Geometries of J3* carries the M22 DOI and abstract; it is a metadata collision, not evidence that either body was read. ResearchGate returned access errors when investigated. No inaccessible body is included in the reading count.

Private source hashes and the response types for this phase are recorded in `phase7_sporadic_source_assets.json`. Source full texts and retrieved HTML are not copied into the public result package. The public certificate contains only our original programs, written proofs, numerical certificates and generated mathematical witnesses.
