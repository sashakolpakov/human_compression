# A path cocycle and the intersection of rotation Frattini subgroups

10 October 2026. Original calculation, with a separate finite coordinate
checker and an independent presentation-based check at rank four. The
counterexample concerns the specified auxiliary subgroup in a versioned
preprint. It does not disprove its main common-quotient existence claim.

## 1. One construction at every rank

Let d≥2, V=F₂^d, and Z=F₂^(d−1), with bases e₀,…,e_(d−1) and
c₁,…,c_(d−1), respectively. On V×Z put

    (v,z)(w,t) = (v+w, z+t+β(v,w)),
    β(v,w) = Σ_(i=1)^(d−1) v_i w_(i−1) c_i.

Bilinearity gives β(u,v)+β(u+v,w)=β(v,w)+β(u,v+w), so the law
is associative. Its identity is (0,0), and (v,z) has inverse
(v,z+β(v,v)). Write r_i=(e_i,0). These are involutions, with

    [r_(i−1),r_i]=c_i,   [r_i,r_j]=1 if |i−j|≥2,

where c_i also denotes (0,c_i). The c_i are independent central
involutions. The r_i generate the whole group G_d: their products
provide every V-coordinate, and their commutators provide every
Z-coordinate. Thus |G_d|=2^(2d−1) and its class is two.

For I⊆{0,…,d−1}, let E(I)={i: i−1,i∈I}. Directly from the law,

    <r_i: i∈I> = { (v,z): supp(v)⊆I, supp(z)⊆E(I) }.       (1)

Both containments hold: the right side is a subgroup containing the
generators, and its independent central coordinates are their
commutators. Since E(I∩J)=E(I)∩E(J), formula (1) proves the complete
intersection property. Every adjacent product has order four.
Consequently this is a nondegenerate string C-group of rank d.

It is also the particular group G(C_d) described in Section 3 of
[Hou–Schulte, arXiv:2605.20691v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20691v1).
Indeed their presentation has the same involution and distant
commutation relations, with central adjacent squares. Collection puts
every word in the form r₀^a₀⋯r_(d−1)^a_(d−1)c₁^b₁⋯c_(d−1)^b_(d−1),
with binary exponents. Its order is therefore at most 2^(2d−1), while
the coordinate model realizes that many distinct elements. This
identification is proved here and does not require the paper's later
Theorem 4.2.

## 2. Compute the source's subgroup C

Use exactly the source's equations (7)–(9):

    σ_i=r_(i−1)r_i,       A=<σ₁,…,σ_(d−1)>,
    A_i=<σ₁,…,σ_(i−1),σ_i²,σ_(i+1),…,σ_(d−1)>,
    C=intersection_i Φ(A_i).

Here σ_i²=c_i. The V-images of the σ_i are independent and span
the even-weight subspace. Each A_i contains all of Z, using the
squares of its generators and its explicitly retained c_i, and its
image has dimension d−2. Hence |A_i|=2^(2d−3).

In any class-two group generated by g₁,…,g_s, whose squares and
commutators have order at most two, its square subgroup is generated
by the g_j² and [g_j,g_k]. This follows by squaring a collected word.
For a finite 2-group that subgroup is Φ. Apply this to A_i.

For d≥3 the endpoint calculations give

    Φ(A₁)=<c₂,…,c_(d−1)>,
    Φ(A_(d−1))=<c₁,…,c_(d−2)>.

The included unsquared σ_j supply the displayed coordinates by
squaring. No missing endpoint coordinate can arise from a commutator
of the remaining σ_j: at that endpoint both vectors have the missing
vertex coordinate zero. The additional c_i is central of order two
and contributes no square or commutator.

For 2≤i≤d−2, all coordinates other than c_i again arise as squares,
and the missing one arises from

    [σ_(i−1),σ_(i+1)]=c_i.

Thus Φ(A_i)=Z for internal indices. Intersecting gives the uniform
answer

    C=<c₂,…,c_(d−2)>,  |C|=2^(d−3)             (d≥3).     (2)

At d=2 there is one A_i=<c₁>, and C=1 separately. In particular,
at d=4 the three Frattini orders are 4,8,4. The middle A_i needs
only two generators: its squared middle rotation is the commutator
of the other two rotations. This is the failure of the asserted
minimality of the defining generator list.

## 3. Exact source consequence and its limit

Theorem 4.2(c) asserts |C|=2^(n−2d+1). Here n=2d−1, so it predicts
|C|=1. Formula (2) contradicts that assertion for every d≥4.
Part (d)'s asserted quotient order and identification also fail:
the actual quotient has order 2^(d+2), rather than 2^(2d−1).
Part (e) fails because γ₃(G_d)=1 and C≠1.

The counterexample does not contradict the mere string C-group
property of this quotient. Killing the interior central coordinates
gives the same coordinate construction with only the two endpoint
edges retained, so (1) still proves intersection. At rank four its
type is {4,2,4}; more generally the interior adjacent orders become
two. Nor does the example refute Theorems 1.1–1.2 asserting existence
of a common quotient: G_d already maps to G(C_d) by the identity.
A different proof or corrected kernel for that general existence
statement is a separate problem. No claim about its truth is inferred
from this failed auxiliary construction.

## 4. Verification and reusable content

[The coordinate checker](phase12_path_cocycle_check.py) checks ranks
2–6, including the cocycle identity on every triple of V-vectors,
every standard parabolic, every pairwise intersection, and Φ(A_i)
generated from the squares of every element of A_i. It uses no group
catalogue or external algebra package. Its finite range is a check
of the proof, not the basis for extending the theorem to every d.

The independent [GAP/NQ check](phase12_hou_schulte_independent_audit.md)
starts from a free presentation rather than these coordinates. At
rank four it independently obtains all 256 intersection comparisons,
the orders 4,8,4, and C of order two. The construction above also works
with any simple graph in place of the path: a coordinate for each edge,
the same bilinear cocycle, and the same support proof give a marked
C-group. A path supplies the additional string condition. For general
graphs this alone makes no assertion of coset flag transitivity.

This is an internally checked correction to specified claims, not an
open-conjecture resolution, a whole-paper reduction, or a priority
determination. It receives no infinity marker or measured length ratio.
