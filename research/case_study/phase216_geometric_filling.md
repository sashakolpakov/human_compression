# Large powers and proper parabolic subgroups

**Private, unfrozen proof awaiting independent review.** This supplies the
geometric argument left open in Section 4 of `phase216_general_covers.md`.
The final consequence for minimal covers is proposed for review, not admitted.
All named geometric theorems are retained inputs, with their primary sources
listed below. No computational experiment is used in this proof.

## 1. Collapsing proper parabolic cosets

Let \(V\) be a right-angled Coxeter group on a finite set \(S\). Suppose its
noncommutation graph is connected and \(|S|\geq3\). Put
\[
 \mathcal Q=\bigcup_{I\subsetneq S}V_I,
 \qquad Y=\operatorname{Cay}(V,\mathcal Q),
 \qquad V_I=\langle I\rangle.
\]
We retain the identity in \(\mathcal Q\), including during filling; its loops
do not affect distances. If a Cayley-graph convention initially omits the
identity, finite-change invariance [DGO, Corollary 4.27] permits adding it
before applying the filling theorem. Thus injection will also distinguish
every nonidentity generator from the identity.

Let \(X\) be the Davis CAT(0) cube complex. Its vertices are \(V\), edges
are the distinguished involutions, and cubes are the cosets of subgroups
generated by commuting subsets. We use its cubical metric \(d_2\) and edge
metric \(d_1\). If \(d=\dim X\), then on vertices
\[
 d_2\leq d_1\leq\sqrt d\,d_2.                                    \tag{1}
\]
For the second inequality, use the cubical \(\ell^1\) metric along a CAT(0)
geodesic, cube by cube. Standard parabolic coset subcomplexes are convex:
the graph-product normal form makes them gated in the edge graph, and their
full cubical subcomplexes are intersections of cubical halfspaces.

For nonempty \(I\subseteq S\), let \(X_I\) be the subcomplex with vertex
set \(V_I\), and put
\[
 \mathcal F=\{gX_I:g\in V,\ \varnothing\ne I\subseteq S\}.
\]
We exclude single vertices and use the threshold \(\xi=1\) in the definition
of a factor system.

**Lemma 1.1.** The collection \(\mathcal F\) is a \(V\)-invariant factor
system in the sense of [BHS, Definition 8.1].

**Proof.** It contains \(X\), every member is nonempty and convex, and at most
\(2^{|S|}-1\) members contain a given vertex. A combinatorial hyperplane dual
to an edge labelled \(s\) is a coset subcomplex of
\(V_{\operatorname{link}(s)}\). Any parallel copy has the same edge labels
and contains all the translated cubes of that subcomplex; its vertex set is
again a coset of \(V_{\operatorname{link}(s)}\). Thus all the nontrivial
parallel copies required by Definition 8.1 belong to \(\mathcal F\).

We verify the gate condition by the cubical argument of [BHS, Proposition
8.3], rather than applying that proposition for Artin groups without an
adaptation. For two members of \(\mathcal F\), translate a closest pair of
vertices to \(1,g\), so the subcomplexes have vertex sets \(V_I,gV_J\).
Their convex bridge is a product: its end factors are the mutual gate images,
and its other factor contains a shortest edge path \(\gamma\) from \(1\)
to \(g\). This is [BHS, Lemmas 2.4 and 2.6], for arbitrary CAT(0) cube
complexes. Let \(T\) be the labels of \(\gamma\) and set
\[
 L=I\cap J\cap\operatorname{link}(T).
\]
An edge of the gate image in \(X_I\) has its label in \(I\cap J\). The
product bridge supplies a square with it and every edge of \(\gamma\), so
its label is distinct from, and commutes with, every member of \(T\).
Every vertex of that gate image is consequently in \(V_L\). Conversely,
take a path labelled in \(L\) from \(1\). Commutation with every label of
\(\gamma\) supplies its product with \(\gamma\); its first end is in
\(X_I\), its other end in \(gX_J\). It is therefore in the gate image.
That image is exactly \(X_L\). If \(L\ne\varnothing\), it belongs to
\(\mathcal F\); otherwise its diameter is zero, less than \(\xi\).
This verifies the last factor-system condition. \(\square\)

Let \(\widehat{\mathcal C}X\) be the associated factored contact graph.
Ordinary vertices are hyperplanes, adjacent when their carriers meet. A cone
vertex for each parallelism class of proper members of \(\mathcal F\) is
joined to the hyperplanes crossing that class. All nontrivial members have
diameter at least \(\xi=1\).

**Lemma 1.2.** There is a \(V\)-equivariant quasi-isometry
\(Y\to\widehat{\mathcal C}X\).

**Proof.** Fix \(s\in S\), let \(H_s\) be the hyperplane of the edge
\(1,s\), and set \(f(g)=gH_s\). Suppose \(g^{-1}h\in V_I\), where
\(\varnothing\ne I\subsetneq S\). At each of \(g,h\), take an edge with
a label in \(I\). Its hyperplane contacts \(f(g)\), respectively \(f(h)\),
and the two chosen hyperplanes are adjacent to the cone vertex for \(gX_I\).
It follows that
\[
 d_{\widehat{\mathcal C}X}(f(g),f(h))\leq4d_Y(g,h).                 \tag{2}
\]

The reverse estimate must take account of parallel copies. The union of all
vertex sets of copies parallel to \(gX_I\) is
\[
 gV_{I\cup\operatorname{link}(I)}.                                \tag{3}
\]
Parallel copies have the same hyperplane labels, hence the same set \(I\).
After translating a closest pair to \(1,u\), their full gate image is
\(X_I\). The formula in Lemma 1.1 implies that every label of \(u\)
commutes with, and is distinct from, every member of \(I\). Thus
\(u\in V_{\operatorname{link}(I)}\). The converse follows from the product
subcomplex on the two commuting sets \(I\) and \(\operatorname{link}(I)\).
This proves (3). Since the noncommutation graph is connected,
\(I\cup\operatorname{link}(I)\) is proper whenever \(I\) is nonempty and
proper. Otherwise there would be no noncommutation edge between \(I\) and
its complement.

Associate to an ordinary vertex \(H\) its carrier vertex set, a coset of
\(V_{\{t\}\cup\operatorname{link}(t)}\), where \(t\) labels \(H\).
Associate to a cone vertex the coset in (3). Each associated set is a proper
parabolic coset, hence has \(Y\)-diameter at most one. Associated sets for
adjacent vertices intersect: this is contact for two ordinary vertices, and
for an ordinary/cone pair it follows because a hyperplane crossing a
parabolic coset has a carrier meeting that coset. Thus a path of length
\(k\) joins any selected vertices in its first and last associated sets
by at most \(k+1\) steps in \(Y\). As \(g\) belongs to the set associated
to \(f(g)\),
\[
 d_Y(g,h)\leq d_{\widehat{\mathcal C}X}(f(g),f(h))+1.               \tag{4}
\]
Every ordinary vertex lies within one step of some \(f(g)\), and every cone
vertex within two. This proves quasi-isometry. Equivariance is exact from
\(f(g)=gH_s\). \(\square\)

By [BHS, Proposition 8.25], the factored contact graph is a quasi-tree. By
[BHS, Corollary 14.5], its action is acylindrical, since \(V\) acts properly
and cocompactly on \(X\) and preserves \(\mathcal F\). Consequently \(Y\)
is hyperbolic and its action is acylindrical. For the latter transfer, an
element moving two vertices of \(Y\) by at most \(\varepsilon\) moves their
images by at most \(4\varepsilon\), by (2); points sufficiently far apart
in \(Y\) have images sufficiently far apart by (4). The bound given by
acylindricity on the factored graph therefore applies directly.

## 2. Elements with full support

Let \(w\in V\) be cyclically reduced with support \(S\). Because the
noncommutation graph is connected and has at least two vertices, every
nonzero power has the same support, and positive powers are represented by
concatenating a reduced word for \(w\). A cancellation across a period would
either contradict cyclic reduction or require a support letter to commute
with every other support letter. In particular, \(w\) has infinite order.

The pure-factor decomposition and centralizer theorem [CH, Theorem 3.7] give
a primitive element \(u\) and an integer \(e>0\) such that \(w=u^e\) and
\[
 C_V(w^k)=\langle u\rangle\cong\mathbb Z\quad(k\ne0).              \tag{5}
\]
There is only one pure factor, and the common-link factor is empty. Thus
\(w\) is a rank-one isometry of the Davis complex by [CF, Corollary 4.6(ii)].
It has a \(B\)-contracting CAT(0) axis \(L\) by [BF, Theorem 5.4]. Let
\(\tau>0\) be its translation length on \(L\) and \(\pi_L\) nearest-point
projection.

**Lemma 2.1.** For every \(R\geq0\), a bound \(b(R)\), independent of the
proper parabolic coset subcomplex \(C\), satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(L\cap N_R(C))\leq b(R).
\]

**Proof.** Put \(p=\pi_L(1)\), and let \(A=d_2(1,p)+\tau\). Every
\(x\in L\) is within \(A\) of a vertex \(w^k\), chosen so the coordinate
of \(x\) along \(L\) differs from that of \(w^kp\) by less than \(\tau\).
For \(x,y\in L\cap N_R(C)\), choose such integers \(k,l\), and vertices
\(c,d\in C\) within \(R+\sqrt d\) of \(x,y\), respectively. Equation (1)
gives
\[
 d_1(w^k,c),d_1(w^l,d)\leq M_R:=\sqrt d\,(A+R+\sqrt d).
\]
Write \(C=gX_I\), and choose \(j\in S\setminus I\). No hyperplane labelled
\(j\) crosses \(C\). At least \(|k-l|\) distinct hyperplanes labelled
\(j\) separate \(w^k,w^l\): the reduced periodic edge path has a \(j\) in
each period and crosses each hyperplane at most once. Each of these walls
separates either \(w^k,c\) or \(w^l,d\), because the connected subcomplex
\(C\) lies entirely on one side. Therefore \(|k-l|\leq2M_R\). Coordinates
along \(L\) now give
\[
 d_2(x,y)\leq(|k-l|+2)\tau\leq(2M_R+2)\tau,
\]
which is the required uniform bound. \(\square\)

**Lemma 2.2.** A constant \(M>0\), independent of the proper parabolic coset
\(C\), bounds \(\operatorname{diam}\pi_L(C)\).

**Proof.** By [BF, Lemma 3.2], the subintervals of \(L\) are uniformly
contracting. Its Corollary 3.4 supplies \(T\geq1\) with the following
property: if \(a,b\) project to the two endpoints of an interval
\(J\subset L\) whose length exceeds \(T\), then there are points
\(x\in[a,b]\) and \(y\in J\) with \(d_2(x,y)<T\).

Take \(a,b\in C\), let \(v,z\) be their projections to \(L\), and put
\(D=d_2(v,z)\). If \(D>2(T+1)\), take intervals of length \(T+1\) at
the two ends of \([v,z]\). The projections of \(a,b\) to either interval
are its endpoints: restricting projection to an interval of a CAT(0)
geodesic line truncates its coordinate at the endpoints. Apply the quoted
corollary to both intervals. Since \([a,b]\subset C\), each interval
contains a point of \(N_T(C)\). The two points are separated by at least
\(D-2(T+1)\), which Lemma 2.1 bounds by \(b(T)\). Thus in all cases
\(D\leq b(T)+2(T+1)\). This choice of \(M\) proves the claim. \(\square\)

**Corollary 2.3.** The element \(w\) acts loxodromically on \(Y\).

**Proof.** Each \(\mathcal Q\)-edge lies in one proper parabolic coset, so
its endpoints have projections at distance at most \(M\). Projection
commutes with \(w\), and a path from \(1\) to \(w^k\) has projection
displacement \(|k|\tau\). Consequently
\[
 |w^k|_{\mathcal Q}\geq |k|\tau/M.
\]
The opposite linear upper bound follows by concatenation. Thus the orbit
map of \(\langle w\rangle\) is a quasi-isometric embedding. \(\square\)

## 3. Filling and the conclusion for covers

**Theorem 3.1.** Let \(V\) be a right-angled Coxeter group whose finite
noncommutation graph is connected and has at least two vertices. If \(w\)
is cyclically reduced with full support, then for every sufficiently large
integer \(m\), the map
\[
 V\longrightarrow V/\langle\!\langle w^m\rangle\!\rangle
\]
is injective on the union of all proper standard parabolic subgroups.

**Proof.** In rank two, this is the infinite dihedral group. Its finite
dihedral quotients preserve the two distinct generating involutions as soon
as the order of their product is at least two, giving the claim.

In larger rank, Sections 1 and 2 give a hyperbolic, acylindrical action on
\(Y\), with \(w\) loxodromic. Acylindricity implies WPD, so [H, Corollary
3.17] gives
\[
 E_V(w)\hookrightarrow_h(V,\mathcal Q)
\]
with this same, possibly infinite, generating set. Every element of
\(E_V(w)\) conjugates some nonzero power of \(w\) to itself or its inverse
[DGO, Lemma 6.5 and Corollary 6.6(b)]. Equation (5) therefore shows that it normalizes the common
cyclic centralizer \(\langle u\rangle\), hence sends \(u\) to \(u\) or
\(u^{-1}\). In particular \(\langle w^m\rangle\) is normal in \(E_V(w)\)
for every \(m\). No divisibility restriction on \(m\) is needed.

The relative metric on \(E_V(w)\) is proper, so every bounded relative ball
is finite. Nonidentity elements of \(\langle w^m\rangle\) have the form
\(u^{emk}\), \(k\ne0\). They avoid any fixed finite set for every sufficiently
large \(m\). Thus the depth condition in [DGO, Theorem 7.15] holds for all
large integers \(m\), and part (c) gives injection on the entire set
\(\mathcal Q\). It does not merely give injection on a finite subset.
\(\square\)

**Corollary 3.2.** If the noncommutation graph is a path, the quotients in
Theorem 3.1 are string C-groups for every sufficiently large \(m\).

**Proof.** If \(a\in V_I\), \(b\in V_J\) have equal images and both
\(I,J\) are proper, injection on their union gives \(a=b\), so their common
value lies in \(V_{I\cap J}\). When one index set is all of \(S\), the
intersection assertion is automatic. The distinguished generators remain
distinct nonidentity involutions, and the string commutations remain valid.
\(\square\)

Combining this corollary with the independently written elementary reductions
in `phase216_general_covers.md` gives the following candidate theorem.

**Proposed general theorem, awaiting independent review.** An abstract
polytope of finite rank has a unique minimal regular polytopal cover if and
only if its monodromy group, with its distinguished generators, is a string
C-group. Every abstract polytope of finite rank has at least one minimal
regular polytopal cover. No finiteness assumption is imposed on its flag set
or on the covers.

**Proof.** Ranks zero and one are immediate. In rank two the flag graph is
a polygonal cycle or a doubly infinite path, and the monodromy group is the
corresponding dihedral string C-group. In every rank the trivial subgroup
of \(W_n\) is admissible. Chain unions are admissible, so Zorn's lemma
gives a maximal admissible subgroup and hence a minimal regular cover.

For the converse about uniqueness, write \(K=\ker(W_n\to\operatorname{Mon}(P))\).
The disjoint-support lemma for actual flag actions in the companion paper
shows that \(K\) is normally generated by its cyclically reduced relations
whose supports are consecutive intervals with at least two ranks. Apply
Corollary 3.2 in each such interval and use the free extension lemma there
to restore the other generators. For each resulting relation \(w\), the
normal closures of \(w^m,w^{m+1}\), for sufficiently large \(m\), are
admissible subgroups of \(K\). Their join contains \(w\). Hence the join
of all admissible subgroups is \(K\). If there were a unique maximal
admissible subgroup, it would contain all admissible subgroups, and thus
equal \(K\). The monodromy group would then be a string C-group.
Conversely, if it is a string C-group, \(K\) is itself the greatest
admissible subgroup and gives the unique minimal cover. \(\square\)

This proof does not say that the resulting minimal covers are finite when
the original polytope is finite. It also does not show that the number of
minimal covers is infinite whenever it exceeds one. The surface theorem
in frozen phase 211 has that stronger conclusion for its stated families.

## 4. Retained inputs and verification scope

The new argument is the application to polytope flag kernels and the explicit
comparison and projection lemmas. The following substantial theorems are
borrowed, not reproved or Lean-certified here.

* [CH] L. Chen and N. Heuer, *Stable commutator length in right-angled Artin and
  Coxeter groups*, `sources/phase216_ChenHeuer.pdf`: graph-product normal forms
  and pure factors in Section 3; the centralizer formula in Theorem 3.7.
* [CF] P.-E. Caprace and K. Fujiwara, *Rank one isometries of buildings and
  quasi-morphisms of Kac--Moody groups*, `sources/phase216_CapraceFujiwara.pdf`:
  Corollary 4.6(ii), equivalence of rank one and virtually cyclic centralizer.
* [BF] M. Bestvina and K. Fujiwara, *A characterization of higher rank symmetric
  spaces via bounded cohomology*, `sources/phase216_BestvinaFujiwara.pdf`:
  Theorem 5.4, contraction of periodic rank-one axes; Lemma 3.2 and Corollary
  3.4, the uniform contraction and geodesic-proximity estimates.
* [BHS] J. Behrstock, M. Hagen and A. Sisto, *Hierarchically hyperbolic spaces,
  I: curve complexes for cubical groups*, Geometry & Topology 21 (2017),
  1731--1804, `sources/phase216_BHS2017.pdf`: convex bridges (Lemmas 2.4 and
  2.6), Definition 8.1, Proposition 8.25, and Corollary 14.5.
* [H] M. Hull, *Small cancellation in acylindrically hyperbolic groups*, Groups
  Geom. Dyn. 10 (2016), 1077--1119, `sources/phase216_Hull.pdf`: Corollary 3.17
  retains the original generating set when embedding the elementary subgroup.
* [DGO] F. Dahmani, V. Guirardel and D. Osin, *Hyperbolically embedded subgroups
  and rotating families in groups acting on hyperbolic spaces*,
  `sources/phase216_DGO.pdf`: Lemma 6.5, Corollary 6.6(b), and Theorem 7.15(c).
