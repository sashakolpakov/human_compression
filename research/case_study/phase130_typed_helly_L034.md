# Supported kernels and regular three-generator hypertopes

Author expansion candidate for **Module A: Construction and classification of two-group polytopes**. This note uses the frozen U v2 and A author checkpoints, whose exact bindings are in the companion ledger. It does not change either manuscript. Independent review and integration are pending.

The purpose is to derive the complete construction theorem of Hou–Feng–Leemans [HFL19] from the supported-kernel construction already used in A. The extra geometric fact is a coset intersection theorem. It applies to nonabelian kernel factors and to infinite groups as well. The construction's displayed presentation and its extension structure require additional checks; existence of a group of the correct order alone would not recover the source result.

## 1. A Helly theorem for coordinate kernel profiles

Write right cosets as \(P g\), with the subgroup on the left. Let \(q:H\to B\) be an epimorphism of groups generated by corresponding marked families \((h_i)_{i\in I}\) and \((b_i)_{i\in I}\), where \(I\) is finite. For \(J\subseteq I\), write \(H_J=\langle h_j:j\in J\rangle\) and \(B_J=\langle b_j:j\in J\rangle\). In particular, \(q(H_J)=B_J\) for every J.

A family of subgroups has **typed coset Helly** if every finite pairwise intersecting family of their right cosets, at most one of each type, has a common element. Fix any set \(\mathcal J\) of subsets of I as the permitted types. The maximal-parabolic case is \(\mathcal J=\{I\setminus\{i\}:i\in I\}\).

**Theorem U.SK.Helly (coordinate-kernel transfer).** Suppose B has IP. Suppose \(N=\ker q\) is an internal direct product \(\prod_{\alpha\in A}N_\alpha\), with a finite factor set A, and for every \(J\in\mathcal J\),
\[
 N\cap H_J=\prod_{\alpha\in A_J}N_\alpha
 \qquad\text{for some }A_J\subseteq A.
 \tag{H1}
\]
If the family \((B_J)_{J\in\mathcal J}\) has typed coset Helly, then so does \((H_J)_{J\in\mathcal J}\).

No commutativity within the factors, finiteness of H or B, or faithfulness of an action of B on N is assumed. The required intersection-image fact is
\(q(H_{J\cap K})=B_J\cap B_K\); it follows here from the marked map and IP of B. IP of H is not an additional assumption for this transfer theorem.

*Proof.* Consider pairwise intersecting cosets \(H_{J_i}g_i\). Their images \(B_{J_i}q(g_i)\) intersect pairwise. Choose a common base element b by the assumed Helly property, and choose u in H with \(q(u)=b\). Each set
\[
 F_i=H_{J_i}g_i\cap q^{-1}(b)
\]
is nonempty: because \(bq(g_i)^{-1}\in B_{J_i}\), a preimage of that element in \(H_{J_i}\), multiplied on the right by \(g_i\), belongs to \(F_i\).

We first show that \(F_i\cap F_j\ne\varnothing\). Choose \(x\in H_{J_i}g_i\cap H_{J_j}g_j\) and put z=q(x). Both b and z belong to each of the two base cosets, so
\[
 bz^{-1}\in B_{J_i}\cap B_{J_j}=B_{J_i\cap J_j}.
\]
The marked map is surjective on this intersection parabolic. Choose \(h\in H_{J_i\cap J_j}\) with \(q(h)=bz^{-1}\). Since h belongs to both \(H_{J_i}\) and \(H_{J_j}\), left multiplication by h preserves both right cosets. Thus hx belongs to their intersection and has image b. This proves the assertion without assuming that q carries an arbitrary intersection onto the intersection of its images.

Choose \(x_i\in F_i\) and put \(k_i=x_i u^{-1}\in N\). If \(y\in F_i\), then \(yx_i^{-1}\in N\cap H_{J_i}\); the converse also holds. Hence
\[
 F_i u^{-1}=(N\cap H_{J_i})k_i.
 \tag{H2}
\]
Under the direct product coordinates of N, (H1) makes (H2) unrestricted in coordinates \(\alpha\in A_{J_i}\), and fixes coordinate \(\alpha\notin A_{J_i}\) to \((k_i)_\alpha\). Pairwise intersections imply that any two constraints fixing the same coordinate fix it to the same value. Choose that value in every constrained coordinate and the identity in every unconstrained coordinate. The resulting element belongs to all sets in (H2). Multiplication on the right by u supplies a common element of the original cosets. This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

**Corollary U.SK.Geometry.** In the supported-kernel realization U.SK, assume the base marking gives a regular hypertope. Then the resulting marking also gives a regular hypertope, and its full type-preserving automorphism group is the constructed group.

*Proof.* U.SK gives IP and the exact profile
\[
 N\cap H_J=\prod_{S\subseteq J}N_S.
\]
The base maximal-parabolic cosets have typed Helly. Theorem U.SK.Helly applies with factors indexed by the supports S. The generators remain nonidentity involutions because their base images are such. U.Geometry now gives thinness, residual connectedness, chamber regularity and equality with the full type-preserving automorphism group. In particular, this conclusion uses typed Helly in addition to IP. \(\square\)

## 2. The square quotient and its two decompositions

Retain A's affine generators
\[
 a(x,y)=(-x,y),\quad b(x,y)=(y,x),\quad c(x,y)=(x,1-y)
\]
of \(W=\mathbb Z^2\rtimes D_8\), with presentation
\[
 a^2=b^2=c^2=(ab)^4=(bc)^4=(ac)^2=1.
 \tag{H3}
\]
A §3 proves this complete presentation, the lattice quotients and their deletion maps. For \(\delta\ge0\), set
\[
 Q_\delta=
 \begin{cases}
 W/q\mathbb Z^2,&\delta\text{ even},\quad q=2^{(\delta+2)/2},\\
 W/q\langle(1,1),(1,-1)\rangle,&\delta\text{ odd},\quad q=2^{(\delta+1)/2}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{H4}
\]
Thus \(Q_\delta=H_{\delta+5}\) in A, and its order is \(2^{\delta+5}\). It is a string C-group of type \(\{4,4\}\), has all Boolean deletions, and its maximal-parabolic cosets have typed Helly by the retained regular-polytope correspondence.

The two words needed in the source are, in these affine coordinates,
\[
 [(ab)^2,c]=(0,-2;1),\qquad
 [(ab)^2,(bc)^2]=(-2,-2;1),
 \tag{H5}
\]
where \((v;1)\) denotes translation by v. Indeed \((ab)^2\) is the half-turn \(x\mapsto-x\), while \((bc)^2\) is the half-turn \(x\mapsto(1,1)-x\); composing these with c gives the first formula, and composing the two half-turns twice gives the second. The normal closure in W of a translation consists of the lattice generated by its signed-permutation orbit. Therefore adjoining respectively
\[
 [(ab)^2,c]^{2^{\delta/2}}=1
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 [(ab)^2,(bc)^2]^{2^{(\delta-1)/2}}=1
 \tag{H6}
\]
to (H3) gives exactly (H4), not just a quotient of the stated order.

For completeness the two square decompositions used by the source can be proved in the same affine model for every integer \(q\ge2\), not only the powers of two in (H4). We use \(D_{2q}\) for the dihedral group of order \(2q\), including \(D_4=C_2^2\). The source's presentations \(M_2\) and \(M_1\) adjoin respectively \((bcba)^q=1\) and \((cba)^{2q}=1\) to (H3). Here bcba is translation by \((1,0)\), and \((cba)^2\) is translation by \((1,1)\). Their signed-permutation orbits therefore generate exactly the square and diagonal lattices in (H7)–(H8). This identifies those complete presentations with the indicated lattice quotients.

In the square case, \(\langle a,c^b\rangle\) acts on the first coordinate and \(\langle a^b,c\rangle\) on the second. Their translations have order q; each is \(D_{2q}\). They commute and intersect trivially. The reflection b interchanges the two factors and lies outside their product. They and b generate all three original generators. Hence
\[
 W/q\mathbb Z^2\cong(D_{2q}\times D_{2q})\rtimes C_2.
 \tag{H7}
\]

In the diagonal case, put \(A_0=\langle b^a,b^c\rangle\) and \(B_0=\langle b,b^{ac}\rangle\). Their reflecting axes are respectively \(x+y=0,1\) and \(x-y=0,-1\). The first factor has translations along \((1,1)\), the second along \((1,-1)\), of order q in each case. All cross-generators commute. Their linear parts show that an element of their intersection must be a translation; the independence of the two diagonal translations modulo the defining lattice then makes that intersection trivial. Thus \(A_0B_0\cong D_{2q}\times D_{2q}\).

Both a and c exchange the two factors by conjugation, so normalize their product. The two individual reflections a,c do not belong to the product by their linear parts. The only remaining possible nontrivial intersection with \(\langle a,c\rangle\) is ac. Every translation part of an element of \(A_0B_0\) with linear part \(-1\) has coordinates of the same parity, whereas ac has translation part \((0,1)\). The diagonal lattice preserves this parity condition, so ac is also outside the product. Finally b already lies in \(B_0\), so the two factors and \(\langle a,c\rangle\) generate the full group. Consequently
\[
 W/q\langle(1,1),(1,-1)\rangle
 \cong(D_{2q}\times D_{2q})\rtimes(C_2\times C_2).
 \tag{H8}
\]
These arguments specify the actions by the original affine generators. They prove the needed decompositions rather than identifying groups from equal orders alone.

## 3. The complete three-generator construction

Fix integers \(n\ge10\), \(s,t\ge2\), \(\ell\ge1\), with \(\delta=n-s-t-\ell\ge0\). Let \(P=P(n,s,t,\ell)\) have generators a,b,c and relations
\[
 a^2=b^2=c^2=(ab)^{2^s}=(bc)^{2^t}=(ac)^{2^\ell}=1,
\]
\[
 [(ab)^4,c]=[a,(bc)^4]=[(ac)^2,b]=1,
 \tag{H9}
\]
together with the appropriate parity relation (H6). This is the exact defining presentation in [HFL19, Theorem 3.2], with the source markers renamed a,b,c.

**Theorem A.H (the source construction as an attachment).** P has order \(2^n\), its three pair-product orders are exactly \(2^s,2^t,2^\ell\), and its maximal-parabolic coset system is a regular rank-three hypertope with full type-preserving automorphism group P. Moreover the three normal subgroups
\[
 K_{01}=\langle(ab)^4\rangle,\quad
 K_{12}=\langle(bc)^4\rangle,\quad
 K_{02}=\langle(ac)^2\rangle
\]
form an internal direct product
\[
 K\cong C_{2^{s-2}}\times C_{2^{t-2}}\times C_{2^{\ell-1}},
 \qquad P/K\cong Q_\delta.
 \tag{H10}
\]
The factors of order one are permitted. With the extension dot making no splitting assertion, the corrected structural conclusion is
\[
 P\cong K\mathbin{.}
 \begin{cases}
 (D_{2^{(\delta+4)/2}}\times D_{2^{(\delta+4)/2}})\rtimes C_2,
     &\delta\text{ even},\\
 (D_{2^{(\delta+3)/2}}\times D_{2^{(\delta+3)/2}})\rtimes(C_2\times C_2),
     &\delta\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{H11}
\]

*Proof.* Start with \(Q_\delta\), whose three two-generator subgroups have orders 8,8,4. On supports \(\{0,1\},\{1,2\},\{0,2\}\), attach the marked dihedral epimorphisms
\[
 D_{2^{s+1}}\to D_8,\qquad
 D_{2^{t+1}}\to D_8,\qquad
 D_{2^{\ell+1}}\to D_4.
\]
Each is injective on every proper marked parabolic: such a parabolic is trivial or generated by one reflection. The base has exact deletion maps. U.SK therefore constructs a group H with the full coordinate kernel, Boolean deletions, IP and order
\[
 |H|=2^{\delta+5}\,2^{s-2}\,2^{t-2}\,2^{\ell-1}=2^n.
\]
All supports have the same size, so U.SK.Local identifies each attached dihedral group with the corresponding two-generator subgroup of H. The three pair orders are consequently exact. Corollary U.SK.Geometry proves that H gives a regular hypertope; it supplies the flag-transitivity assertion missing from an IP argument alone.

The marked tuple in H satisfies every relator of P. The pair-power relations have just been checked. For each commutator in (H9), the relevant power is trivial in the base and in every local factor other than its own support, while in that support the third marker is one. For either word in (H6), the base satisfies it by (H5)–(H6). In the \(\{0,1\}\) coordinate c is one; in the \(\{1,2\}\) coordinate a is one; in the \(\{0,2\}\) coordinate b is one. Thus each commutator word is trivial in every local coordinate. There is a marked epimorphism \(P\to H\).

It remains to prove that this map has no kernel. In P each \(K_{ij}\) is normal: its two endpoints invert the pair-product power, and the third marker centralizes it by (H9). Their cyclic orders are at most \(2^{s-2}\), \(2^{t-2}\) and \(2^{\ell-1}\). Their normal product K is therefore finite of order at most \(2^{s+t+\ell-5}\); neither commutation nor independence of the factors is being assumed for this bound. After quotienting by K, all remaining pair-power and commutator relations reduce exactly to (H3) and (H6). Section 2 identifies that quotient with \(Q_\delta\), of order \(2^{\delta+5}\). Hence P is finite and
\[
 |P|\le2^{s+t+\ell-5}2^{\delta+5}=2^n=|H|.
\]
The epimorphism is an isomorphism, by U.Order. This recognizes the bare source presentation and transfers the hypertope conclusion.

In H, the word \((ab)^4\) is trivial in the base and all coordinates except support \(\{0,1\}\), where it generates exactly the kernel of \(D_{2^{s+1}}\to D_8\). The corresponding statements hold for \((bc)^4\) and \((ac)^2\). Thus the displayed three subgroups are precisely the pure coordinate factors in U.SK. This proves the direct product and the exact quotient in (H10). Equations (H7)–(H8) now give (H11), including its action and parity. \(\square\)

The bound \(n\ge10\) was not needed in the proof, but it is retained in the statement to match the declared source contract. No nonexistence assertion for \(n<s+t+\ell\) follows from this construction.

## 4. Source contract and correction boundary

[HFL19] is D.-D. Hou, Y.-Q. Feng and D. Leemans, *Existence of regular 3-hypertopes with 2^n chambers*, arXiv:1901.09034v1, 23 January 2019. The bound primary author PDF has eleven pages. Its complete extracted text was read for this attachment; pages 7–8 were additionally inspected as rendered primary pages. The current [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09034) was checked on 10 October 2026 and lists that one version. No journal-version comparison or global priority search is claimed here.

The main theorem's literal parity assignment in its final extension display needs correction. On page 7, and again on page 8, its even case uses a dihedral subscript \(2^{(\delta+3)/2}\) and a \(C_2^2\) complement; its odd case uses \(2^{(\delta+4)/2}\) and a \(C_2\) complement. In those respective parities the exponent is not integral. The source's preceding quotient calculation, as well as (H5)–(H8), gives the reverse assignment shown in (H11). The literal faulty rows are not counted as proved. A second local transcription error defines B by repeating \((\rho_0\rho_1)^4\); its required value is \(\langle(\rho_1\rho_2)^4\rangle\), as used consistently by the later argument and by (H10).

The declared recovered target is therefore all of Theorem 3.2 with this explicit structural correction: the exact original presentation, exact order and pair orders, the regular coset geometry, and the corrected complete extension description. The elementary square decompositions of Lemma 3.1 are also derived in Section 2. Earlier standard background propositions are not counted as additional source papers or new results.

Theorem U.SK.Helly and this attachment are repository work; the supported-kernel isolation argument already credited in U retains that attribution. The original construction and its original conclusions belong to the source authors. No historical novelty claim is made for the transfer theorem, and no open conjecture is claimed solved. In particular [HFL19, Conjecture 3.3] is outside this result. Neither this note nor the new Helly proof has a complete Lean formalization at this checkpoint. All additional proof cost must be charged when this extension is incorporated into U+A.
