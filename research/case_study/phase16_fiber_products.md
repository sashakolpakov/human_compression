# Boolean retractions, fiber products and the 2025 two-group constructions

10 October 2026. Proposed replacement proof; reviews are recorded separately.
This note recovers the six principal theorems 5.1, 5.3, 6.1 and 7.1–7.3 of
Cunningham–Feng–Hou–Schulte, *Symmetric polytopes whose automorphism groups
are 2-groups*, [arXiv:2512.15511v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15511v1).
All 19 pages of that version, including references, were read. Its title
page says 18 December 2025; its arXiv stamp says 17 December 2025.
We also recover its Lemma 4.1 relative to the standard square-torus
classification. These are rederivations of stated results, not open-problem
resolutions. No matched proof-length ratio or publication priority is claimed.

The finite construction uses one operation: a fiber product of marked
groups with compatible deletion retractions. The infinite construction
uses a different operation: a free product with amalgamation. Keeping
these two operations distinct is essential.

## 1. Deletion retractions make intersections automatic

A group G marked by r_v, v in a finite set V, has Boolean deletion maps
if, for every S contained in V, the assignment

    e_S(r_v) = r_v if v is in S, and 1 otherwise

extends to an endomorphism. Necessarily e_S e_T=e_(S intersect T),
and the image and fixed subgroup of e_S are both G_S=<r_v:v in S>.
For x in G_S intersect G_T, therefore,

    x=e_S(x) is in e_S(G_T)=G_(S intersect T).

The opposite inclusion is automatic. Thus the full marked intersection
property follows. Also G=ker(e_S) semidirect G_S, and the kernel is the
normal closure of the deleted markers: the quotient by those markers
is generated by the retained ones and the retraction is the identity
on them. This proves every required flat amalgamation property (FAP)
once the corresponding subset of markers is specified.

Suppose A and B have such maps, their marker sets overlap in C, and
their C-parabolics are identified by the marking. Write p_A and p_B
for the retractions to that common group, also denoted C. Then

    A x_C B = {(a,b):p_A(a)=p_B(b)}
              = (ker p_A x ker p_B) semidirect C.             (1)

Here the last C is embedded diagonally. For a marker in A only use
(r,1), for one in B only use (1,r), and for a common marker use (r,r).
These markers generate the whole fiber product: the A-markers generate
the section a -> (a,p_A(a)), and conjugating the exclusive B-markers
by the B-markers generates (1,ker p_B). The latter assertion is the
normal-kernel description just proved. The deletion maps on both
coordinates preserve compatibility and provide all Boolean maps on
the union of the marker sets. In particular the fiber product has IP,
contains both A and B as the indicated marked subgroups, and, if finite,

    |A x_C B| = |A||B|/|C|.                                  (2)

Any pair of generators occurring together in neither factor commutes.
These statements can be iterated along a tree of overlapping bags with
the running-intersection property and identified separator groups.
Only the path and two-branch cases are needed below.

## 2. The rank-three seeds, without an intersection shortcut

Use the square reflection group W=Z^2 semidirect D8, where D8 has
order eight. The distinguished affine transformations are

    a(x,y)=(1-x,y),   b(x,y)=(y,x),   c(x,y)=(x,-y).

They are involutions, a and c commute, and ab and bc have order four.
Put T=abcb, U=cbab; these are translations by (1,0) and (0,-1).
For n>=5 define the invariant translation lattice L_n by

    n=2e+3: L_n=< (2^e,0),(0,2^e) >;
    n=2e+4: L_n=< (2^e,2^e),(-2^e,2^e) >,   e>=1.

Let H_n=W/L_n. This is an abstract affine group, retaining the linear
part: its action on the vertices modulo L_n need not be faithful.
Its order is 8[Z^2:L_n]=2^n. The standard Coxeter presentation [4,4]
and the two translation-lattice relators present it.

Every deletion assignment preserves that presentation. The Coxeter
relations have even labels. If any marker is deleted, both T and U
have order dividing two; since 2^e is even, each lattice relator
vanishes. Thus H_n has Boolean maps. Its adjacent two-generator
subgroups retain order eight (their D8 linear parts already have
order eight); its nonadjacent pair has order four. Its IP follows
from Section 1. It is the marked toroidal group of type {4,4} with
parameters (2^e,0) or (2^e,2^e).

The square-torus classification retained in the source identifies all
finite regular {4,4} maps with parameters (s,0) or (s,s), s>=2,
and orders 8s^2 or 16s^2. The two-power condition forces s=2^e.
This gives exactly source Lemma 4.1, including uniqueness at each n.
Equivalently, the relevant square-invariant lattices are the ideals
of Z[i] of two-power index; powers of (1+i) alternate between the
two displayed lattice shapes. The classification of regular maps
by these lattices is the retained geometric input.

There is also an exact local kernel calculation:

    gamma3(H_n) = 2Z^2/L_n,  of order 2^(n-5).                (3)

Indeed W/2Z^2 is class two: the squares of the adjacent products
are central there. Conversely [[a,b],c] and [[b,c],a] are translations
by (0,2) and (-2,0), using commutator x^-1 y^-1 x y. They generate
2Z^2. Consequently gamma3(W)=2Z^2 and the quotient calculation follows.
In particular H_5 is the class-two path-cocycle group of order 32.

For a diagonal lattice the two squared translations can intersect
nontrivially. At n=6 their images coincide and have order two.
The kernel order in (3) uses the lattice index, not a product of their
individual orders. This avoids the unnecessary disjointness assertion
in the source proof on page 11; the resulting order formula is the same.

## 3. The entire finite regular family: source Theorem 5.1

Given d>=3 and n_3,...,n_d>=5, place H_(n_(i+3)) on the consecutive
bag {i,i+1,i+2}, 0<=i<=d-3. Glue successive bags by their shared
D8 edge. Sections 1 and 2 define a group G with its d markers.
It has Boolean maps and the string relations. Each local triple
is exactly its prescribed H_n: each gluing embeds the previous
group and the new seed. The standard string-C-group/polytope
correspondence now gives the required regular d-polytope. Its order is

    |G| = product_j 2^(n_j) / 8^(d-3)
        = 2^(sum_j n_j - 3(d-3)).                            (4)

For d>=4 the final gluing has A on {0,...,d-2}, B on
{d-3,d-2,d-1}, and C on {d-3,d-2}. Formula (1) gives G=B A.
The subgroup A is the facet stabilizer. B is contained in the
stabilizer of the base (d-4)-face. Thus the product of these two
stabilizers is G. By the elementary coset-incidence criterion every
(d-4)-face is incident with every facet. This gives the specified
(d-4,d-1)-flatness, and hence flatness. All the source FAP assertions
follow from the Boolean maps, in fact for every subset of markers.
This proves every part (a)–(d) of source Theorem 5.1.

The construction has an additional exact kernel description. Realize
G as the compatible tuples in the product of all its seed groups.
Every element of gamma3 of a local triple has trivial projection to
every other bag: its projection is a third commutator in a subgroup
on at most two markers, all such subgroups having class at most two.
Projection to its own bag is injective and gives its complete kernel.
Therefore these local kernels are independent direct factors, and

    gamma3(G) = product_j gamma3(H_(n_j)),
    |gamma3(G)| = 2^(sum_j (n_j-5)).                          (5)

To see equality rather than just inclusion, quotient by that product.
All consecutive triples are then class two; each edge commutator
commutes with its own endpoints, their neighbors, and all more distant
markers. Thus the entire quotient is class two. Formula (4) shows
that the quotient has order 2^(2d-1), and collection identifies it
with the same path-cocycle group as in the forest theorem.

This directly supplies the sharp examples for the
[fixed-excess core theorem](phase16_fixed_excess.md). Choosing k
of the d-2 seeds to have order 64 and every other seed to have order
32 produces binomial(d-2,k) distinct ordered markings at excess k.
Their different local triple orders distinguish them.

## 4. A four-marker identity gives the entire presentation: Theorem 5.3

Here is the only additional local calculation. Let a,b,c,d be
involutions with ac=ca, ad=da, bd=db, (ab)^4=(cd)^4=1, and

    [(ab)^2,(cd)^2]=1.                                      (6)

Set x=bab, E=(ab)^2=ax, F=(cd)^2. The element a commutes with F,
so (6) implies x commutes with F. Also x commutes with d. Hence
x commutes with Fd=cdc, and conjugating by c shows cxc commutes
with d. Both factors of

    (cbab)^2 = (cxc)x

therefore commute with d. Since abcb=b(cbab)^-1 b and b commutes
with d, the square (abcb)^2 also commutes with d. Reversing the
four-marker string proves the corresponding left-neighbor assertion.

Now let Q be the group with the string [4,...,4] presentation,
each prescribed H_n triple presentation, and the extra relations
(6) at each four-marker window. The constructed G satisfies all
these relations: in the tuple model no bag contains both disjoint
edges in (6), and whichever edge is only partly present contributes
the identity square. Thus Q maps onto G. Each local H_n embeds
in Q, because its map onward to the already embedded triple in G
is injective.

Let K_i be its subgroup gamma3(H_n), generated by T^2,U^2.
It is normalized by the three local markers. The four-marker
calculation shows both nearest outside markers centralize K_i;
more distant markers do so by the string relations. Hence every
K_i is normal in Q, and their product K is finite, with

    |K| <= product_j 2^(n_j-5).

The quotient Q/K is class two by the same local-to-global argument
as above. Collecting involutory markers and central involutory edge
commutators gives |Q/K|<=2^(2d-1). Consequently |Q| is at most
the value (4). Its surjection onto G, of exactly that order, is
therefore an isomorphism. This proves source Theorem 5.3 for every
rank and every parameter, without assuming IP for Q or its quotients.

## 5. One branched fiber product: source Theorem 6.1

Let P and Q be the rank-(d-1) groups already constructed, having
the same first d-4 seeds and final seeds H_(n_(d-1)) and
H_(m_(d-1)), respectively. Their common facet group C is the
rank-(d-2) group obtained by deleting the final marker. For d=4
it is simply D8. Glue P and Q over C, with distinct names for
their final markers. The result is the marked group

    D = P x_C Q = (ker(P->C) x ker(Q->C)) semidirect C.

It has IP by Section 1 and the tail-triangle diagram with the
two new markers commuting. The standard tail-triangle construction
gives the alternating semiregular d-polytope. Retain the construction
and full-automorphism criterion of Monson–Schulte, *The assembly
problem for alternating semiregular polytopes* (2020), Theorem 5.3,
as the geometric bridge; its hypotheses are the two facet FAP
retractions, compatible common facet, and marked groups just checked.
The full group is D when the two facets differ, and D semidirect C2
when they coincide, with C2 swapping the branches. They coincide
exactly when their last parameters agree: equality gives identical
marked constructions, and inequality gives different facet orders.

The kernel orders are 2^(n_(d-1)-3), 2^(m_(d-1)-3), and
|C|=2^(sum_(j=3)^(d-2) n_j - 3(d-5)). The last formula also gives
8 when d=4, with the sum empty. Formula (2) gives

    |D|=2^(sum_(j=3)^(d-2) n_j+n_(d-1)+m_(d-1)-3(d-3)).

Doubling exactly in the equal-parameter case proves both parts of
source Theorem 6.1. The tail-triangle geometric theorem is retained,
not silently derived from the group order alone.

## 6. One torsion lemma: source Theorems 7.1–7.3

Say that a group has two-primary torsion if each of its elements
of finite order has two-power order. This is the source's definition
of an infinite two-group (together with finite generation and infinitude).
It does not require all elements to have finite order.

Subgroups inherit this property. An extension of such a group by
a finite two-group also inherits it: a finite cyclic subgroup meets
the kernel in a two-group and has a two-group image, so its order is
a power of two. Finally A *_C B inherits the property from A and B.
For completeness, the amalgam normal-form theorem conjugates any
element into a factor or a cyclically reduced word of length at least
two; powers of the latter stay reduced with growing length, so have
infinite order. Thus every torsion element is conjugate into a factor.
If C is proper in both factors, choosing a outside C and b outside C
also makes (ab)^n reduced of length 2n, proving infinitude.
Finite generation follows from finite generation of the two factors.

Apply this single lemma to the following standard geometric free-
extension identifications, retained as stated in the source:

* Regular extension: A=Gamma(K), C=Gamma(F), B=C x C2.
* Oriented chiral extension with directly regular facets:
  A=Gamma(K), C=Gamma+(F), B=Gamma(F)=C semidirect C2.
* Alternating semiregular extension: A=Gamma(P), B=Gamma(Q),
  C=Gamma(K), with the full automorphism group additionally extended
  by the branch-swapping C2 when P and Q are isomorphic.

In every case C is a proper face stabilizer in the relevant factor,
and is proper of index two in B in the first two cases. The marking
or flag action verifies properness. All factors have two-primary torsion by subgroup inheritance
and the finite-two-group extension observation. The amalgams are
infinite and finitely generated, and the optional C2 extension
preserves the torsion condition. This proves all three stated
Theorems 7.1, 7.2 and 7.3, including infinite input groups.

In the chiral case only the rotation group of the facet needs to
embed in Gamma(K). No embedding of its full automorphism group is
assumed; its index-two extension is sufficient for the torsion argument.

## 7. Scope and retained inputs

The retained geometry consists of the regular-polytope/string-C-group
correspondence, classification of regular square maps by invariant
lattices, the stated tail-triangle construction and automorphism
criterion, and the three universal-extension/amalgam identifications.
The group inputs are the elementary affine square reflection model
and the amalgam normal-form theorem. Boolean IP, split kernels,
finite orders, local kernel independence and the finite presentation
are proved above. The source's flat-amalgamation theorem 3.3 is an
explicit restatement of an older theorem; its group construction is
covered here, and it is not counted as a new principal result.

Section 8 of the source recalls known power-polytope constructions.
Conditional on their displayed semidirect-product groups, their
orders follow immediately from |C2^v semidirect H|=2^v|H| and
|(D_(2^m))^(v/2) semidirect H|=2^((m+1)v/2)|H|, where the source
dihedral notation has |D_(2^m)|=2^(m+1). We do not claim to reconstruct
those previously published constructions or every informal remark.

This is a six-principal-theorem reduction, with explicit geometric
dependencies, rather than a declaration that all referenced theory
has been replaced. Exact finite diagnostics and a formal certificate
for the Boolean-IP and four-marker components are recorded separately;
neither constitutes an end-to-end formalization of this note.

## 8. Exact diagnostic and formal scope

The independent finite-presentation construction in
`phase16_fiber_products_check.g` checks the eight parameter lists
[5], [6], [7], [5,6], [6,6], [5,7], [6,5,6], [5,6,5]. GAP obtains
the presented finite groups before testing their claimed orders, all
Boolean maps, all 3,008 subgroup meets, the exact local triples and
the independent local-kernel product. This diagnostic is not the
all-parameter proof in Sections 1–4. The Python runner binds its
source and output and checks the complete expected case list.

`formal/BooleanRetractions.lean` compiles against the repository's
pinned Lean/mathlib and uses no incomplete-proof axiom. Its two
theorems establish (i) intersection of the images from Boolean
composition of arbitrary group endomorphisms and (ii) the four-marker
centralization implication in arbitrary groups. The latter actually
does not need the two adjacent-product order assumptions in Section 4.
Construction of the Boolean maps, the lattice calculation, finiteness,
order comparison and geometric correspondences remain written proofs.
