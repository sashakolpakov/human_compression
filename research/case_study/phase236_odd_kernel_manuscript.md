## 1. The source contract and the common argument

This paper gives a common proof of the three principal results of Hou, Feng,
Leemans and Qu, *String C-groups of order $4p^m*$ [HFLQ]. The primary edition
used here is arXiv:2407.10388v1, comprising twelve pages. Its Theorems 1.1,
1.2 and 1.3, with the construction in Theorem 5.1, concern nondegenerate
regular polyhedra of order $4p^m$, where $p$ is an odd prime. We recover the
structural theorem, classify the abelian case, and realize every member of
the nonabelian family. A displayed marking in Theorem 1.2 of that edition
requires a correction, given in §4. We do not assert that the later journal
edition contains the same error.

The proof has two parts. First, the odd Sylow subgroup is generated by one
orbit under a Klein four group. If this subgroup is abelian, precisely two
characters can occur; the intersection property then determines their
orders. Second, a truncated cyclotomic module realizes the nonabelian
construction. Complex conjugation supplies one reflection and additive
negation supplies the other. This also identifies, generator by generator,
the presentation used in [HFLQ]. The character decomposition is a reworking
of [HFLQ, Lemma 2.4 and §4]. The cyclotomic organization and its direct
presentation bridge are the replacement proof supplied here; no historical
priority claim for this model is made.

We retain the odd/even part of Cunningham–Pellicer's classification of tight
regular polyhedra [CP, Theorem 1.1] in one converse. That theorem is already
an explicit input to [HFLQ]. Sylow's theorem, Lagrange's theorem, elementary
finite abelian group theory, and Eisenstein's irreducibility criterion are
standard background. The proof below does not require a classification of
groups of maximal class or of Hughes subgroups.

A **marked string C-group of rank three** is a finite group generated by
three distinct involutions $r_0,r_1,r_2$, with $r_0r_2=r_2r_0$, such that

$$G_I\cap G_J=G_{I\cap J},\qquad
G_I=\langle r_i:i\in I\rangle,\quad I,J\subseteq\{0,1,2\}.$$

Its type is $\{k_1,k_2\}$, where $k_1=|r_0r_1|$ and $k_2=|r_1r_2|$.
Nondegeneracy means $k_1,k_2\geq3$. Duality reverses the marking. We call
the marking tight if $|G|=2k_1k_2$. Isomorphisms of marked groups preserve
the three generators. The standard correspondence with abstract regular
polyhedra is retained when translating these statements into geometric
language.

**Theorem A (the odd kernel).** Suppose that $p$ is odd, $m\geq2$, and
$(G;r_0,r_1,r_2)$ is a nondegenerate string C-group of order $4p^m$. Its
Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ is normal, and

$$G=P\rtimes K,\qquad K=\langle r_0,r_2\rangle\cong C_2\times C_2.$$

Both type parameters are divisible by $p$, at least one is divisible by
$2p$, and the minimum number of generators of $P$ is two.

**Theorem B (the abelian kernel).** Under the hypotheses of Theorem A,
$P$ is abelian if and only if the marking is tight. Up to duality, all such
markings are the following, one for each $1\leq a\leq b$ with $a+b=m$:

$$P=\langle x\rangle\times\langle y\rangle,
\quad |x|=p^b,\quad |y|=p^a,$$
$$x^{r_0}=x,\quad y^{r_0}=y^{-1},\quad
x^{r_2}=x^{-1},\quad y^{r_2}=y^{-1},\quad
r_1=xy r_2.$$

The type is $\{2p^a,p^b\}$, and for this type the marked group is unique.
Here and below $x^g=g^{-1}xg$.

**Theorem C (the nonabelian family).** For every odd prime $p$ and every
$m\geq3$, there is a string C-group of order $4p^m$ and type $\{p,2p\}$
whose Sylow $p$-subgroup is nonabelian. Put

$$O=\mathbb Z[\zeta],\quad \zeta=\zeta_p,\quad \pi=\zeta-1,
\quad A=O/\pi^{m-1}O.$$

Let $P=A\rtimes\langle\beta\rangle$, where $|\beta|=p$ and
$v^\beta=\zeta v$. Define

$$\sigma(v)=\overline v,\quad\sigma(\beta)=\beta^{-1},
\qquad \tau(v)=-v,\quad\tau(\beta)=\beta.$$

Then $G=P\rtimes\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle$ with marking

$$r_0=1_A\tau\sigma,\qquad r_1=\beta\tau\sigma,\qquad r_2=\sigma$$

has the asserted properties. The symbol $1_A$ means the additive class of
the ring element $1$, not the identity of the additive group. This is
exactly the marked presentation of [HFLQ, Theorem 5.1], under
$s_i\mapsto\pi^{i-1}$.

## 2. Intersection and orbit lemmas

**Lemma 2.1 (the rank-three test).** Let three distinct involutions generate
$G$, let $r_0$ commute with $r_2$, and suppose both adjacent products have
order at least three. The full intersection property is equivalent to

$$\langle r_0,r_1\rangle\cap\langle r_1,r_2\rangle
=\langle r_1\rangle.\tag{2.1}$$

*Proof.* Necessity is immediate. For sufficiency, put $H_{ij}=\langle
r_i,r_j\rangle$. The subgroup $H_{02}$ consists of
$1,r_0,r_2,r_0r_2$. If its intersection with $H_{01}$ contains either
$r_2$ or $r_0r_2$, then $r_2\in H_{01}\cap H_{12}=\langle r_1\rangle$,
contrary to distinctness. Thus $H_{02}\cap H_{01}=\langle r_0\rangle$;
dually $H_{02}\cap H_{12}=\langle r_2\rangle$. These three equalities
also show that no generator belongs to the subgroup on the other two.
Intersections involving singletons now follow, while those involving the
empty or full index set are immediate. This covers every pair of subsets.
$\square$

In particular, for a string C-group,

$$2k_1k_2=|H_{01}H_{12}|\leq |G|.\tag{2.2}$$

The equality uses $|H_{01}|=2k_1$, $|H_{12}|=2k_2$ and (2.1).

**Lemma 2.2 (prime-index intersection).** Let $C\leq H\cap J$ be
subgroups, with $[H:C]$ prime. If $H\not\leq J$, then $H\cap J=C$.

*Proof.* The tower formula gives
$[H:C]=[H:H\cap J][H\cap J:C]$. If the first factor were one, then
$H\leq J$. Therefore the second is one. $\square$

**Lemma 2.3 (one inverted orbit).** Suppose $G=P\rtimes K$, where $P$
has odd order and $K=\langle a,b\rangle$ is a Klein four group. Suppose
$G=\langle K,r\rangle$ with $r$ an involution. Write $r=ut$, with
$u\in P$ and $t\in K$. Then $t\ne1$, $u^t=u^{-1}$, and for either
$k\in K\setminus\langle t\rangle$,

$$P=\langle u,u^k\rangle.\tag{2.3}$$

*Proof.* An odd-order group contains no involution, so $t\ne1$.
The equation $r^2=1$ gives $u^t=u^{-1}$. The subgroup
$L=\langle u,u^k\rangle$ is stable under $t$, which inverts these two
generators, and under $k$, which exchanges them. Consequently $LK$ is a
subgroup containing $K$ and $r$. It is $G$, and intersection with $P$
gives $L=P$. $\square$

The last statement concerns the two specified generators; it does not
assert that inversion is an automorphism of an arbitrary nonabelian $P$.

## 3. Proof of the structural theorem

Let $n_p$ be the number of Sylow $p$-subgroups. Sylow's theorem gives
$n_p\mid4$ and $n_p\equiv1\pmod p$. The only possibility other than
$n_p=1$ is $p=3$, $n_p=4$. In that case conjugation on the four Sylow
subgroups gives a homomorphism $G\longrightarrow S_4$. Its kernel $N$
lies in $N_G(P)=P$, since the normalizer has index four. Thus the image
has order $4\cdot3^j$ and divides $24$, so $j=0$ or $1$. If $j=0$,
then $N=P$, making $P$ normal, a contradiction. If $j=1$, the image is
the subgroup $A_4$ of $S_4$. But the involutions of $A_4$ all lie in its
normal Klein four subgroup, so they cannot generate $A_4$. The image of
an involution-generated group is generated by involutions or identities.
This again is a contradiction. Hence $P$ is normal.

The endpoint subgroup $K=\langle r_0,r_2\rangle$ has order four by the
intersection property. Its intersection with $P$ is trivial. Since
$|PK|=4p^m$, we have $G=P\rtimes K$.

Each adjacent dihedral subgroup has order $2k_i$, so $k_i\mid2p^m$.
Nondegeneracy therefore implies $p\mid k_i$. If both $k_i$ were odd,
their images in $G/P=K$ would force
$r_0P=r_1P=r_2P$, contradicting the distinct images of the endpoints.
At least one $k_i$ is even. Lemma 2.3 shows that $P$ has at most two
generators. If $P$ were cyclic, the nontrivial $p$-parts of both adjacent
rotation subgroups would contain its unique subgroup of order $p$.
Their intersection would then be nontrivial of odd order, contradicting
(2.1). Thus $P$ is not cyclic and needs exactly two generators. This
proves Theorem A.

## 4. The two-character classification

Assume that $P$ is abelian, and temporarily write its operation additively.
For commuting involutions $a,b$ on an abelian group of odd order, division
by four is a well-defined automorphism. The four operators

$$e_{\epsilon,\delta}
=\tfrac14(1+\epsilon a)(1+\delta b),\qquad
\epsilon,\delta\in\{1,-1\},\tag{4.1}$$

are orthogonal idempotents whose sum is the identity. This follows by
expanding the products and using $a^2=b^2=1$, $ab=ba$. Thus $P$ is the
direct sum of their images, on which $a,b$ act respectively by
$\epsilon,\delta$.

Write $r_1=ut$ as in Lemma 2.3. Since $t$ inverts $u$ and commutes with
$K$, it inverts every generator in the $K$-orbit of $u$, and hence all of
$P$. Only the two characters $\chi$ of $K$ with $\chi(t)=-1$ can occur.
For either of them, the projection of every orbit element is plus or minus
$e_\chi u$. Therefore its component is cyclic, generated by this
projection. Both components are nonzero: otherwise $P$ would be cyclic.
This argument justifies the two-component conclusion without deducing it
merely from the number of generators of $P$.

Return to multiplicative notation. If $t=r_0r_2$,
the two components have characters $(+,-)$ and $(-,+)$. Choose their
generators $x,y$ so that $u=xy$. Direct multiplication gives adjacent
orders $2|y|$ and $2|x|$. Formula (2.2) would imply

$$8|x||y|\leq |G|=4|x||y|,$$

which is impossible. Hence $t$ is an endpoint generator. By duality take
$t=r_2$. The two components now have characters $(+,-)$ and $(-,-)$,
so their actions and $r_1=xy r_2$ are precisely those in Theorem B.
Writing $|x|=p^b$, $|y|=p^a$, we obtain

$$|r_0r_1|=2p^a,\qquad |r_1r_2|=p^{\max(a,b)}.$$

Applying (2.2) once more gives $a+\max(a,b)\leq a+b$, and hence $a\leq b$.
Equality holds in (2.2); the marking is tight.

Conversely, take the group and marking displayed in Theorem B. Both
$r_0$ and $r_2$ invert $y$, while only $r_2$ inverts $x$, so the three
marked elements are involutions and the endpoints commute. The elements
$u=xy$ and $u^{r_0}=xy^{-1}$ generate $x,y$: their product and quotient
give $x^2,y^2$, and squaring is invertible on either cyclic odd-order
group. Thus the marking generates $G$.

The adjacent orders are $2p^a,p^b$. The intersection of $H_{01}$ with
$P$ is $\langle y\rangle$: its image in $K$ has order four and its
rotation square generates $\langle y\rangle$. Similarly
$H_{12}\cap P=\langle xy\rangle$, and its image in $K$ is
$\langle r_2\rangle$. The groups $\langle y\rangle$ and
$\langle xy\rangle$ meet trivially. Indeed, $x^j=1$ forces $p^b\mid j$,
and then $y^j=1$ because $a\leq b$. It follows that the homomorphism
$H_{01}\cap H_{12}\to K$ is injective with image contained in
$\langle r_2\rangle$. The intersection contains $r_1$, so it is exactly
$\langle r_1\rangle$. Lemma 2.1 proves the full intersection property.
The two cyclic component orders and their marked generators determine
the marked group, proving uniqueness in the abelian case.

It remains to prove that a tight marking has an abelian kernel. By
Theorem A its type parameters have the form $p^b$ or $2p^b$. Tightness
and $|G|=4p^m$ force exactly one parameter to be even, and the two
exponents sum to $m$. The retained theorem [CP, Theorem 1.1(b),(c)] says
that in this odd/even situation, excluding the parameter $4$, tightness
requires that the even parameter divide twice the odd one, and that the
marked polyhedron is unique. The even parameter here is $2p^a$, never
$4$. Thus $a\leq b$. The model already constructed is tight of that
type, so uniqueness identifies every tight marking with it. This proves
the converse and completes Theorem B.

**The literal marking in the primary edition.** The displayed presentation
in [HFLQ, Theorem 1.2, physical p.2] has $x^{r_0}=x$,
$y^{r_0}=y^{-1}$ but writes $r_1=xy r_0$. Its square is $x^2$, not the
identity, since $|x|$ is a positive power of the odd prime $p$. The body
of [HFLQ, §4, physical p.6] uses $xy r_2$ in the corresponding character
case. Our model uses that marking and assigns the larger exponent to $x$.
The corrected statement, rather than the impossible literal formula, is
what Theorem B recovers. No open conjecture is proved by this correction.

## 5. The cyclotomic lattice

Let $p$ be an odd prime, $O=\mathbb Z[\zeta_p]$ and $\pi=\zeta_p-1$.
The polynomial

$$\Phi_p(1+X)=p+\binom p2X+\cdots+\binom p{p-1}X^{p-2}+X^{p-1}$$

is Eisenstein at $p$. In particular $O$ is an integral domain with
$\mathbb Z$-basis $1,\pi,\ldots,\pi^{p-2}$. Also
$O/\pi O\cong\mathbb F_p$, by setting $\zeta=1$ in $\Phi_p$.
Multiplication by $\pi^j$ identifies $O/\pi O$ with
$\pi^jO/\pi^{j+1}O$: injectivity follows by cancellation in the domain.
It follows that

$$|O/\pi^nO|=p^n\qquad(n\geq1).\tag{5.1}$$

For use in the precise presentation bridge, we record the entire additive
lattice, not just its order. The identity

$$p=\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}(1-\zeta^j)$$

implies $pO=\pi^{p-1}O$. To justify equality of these ideals, each ratio
$(1-\zeta^j)/(1-\zeta)$ lies in $O$ and is a unit. Indeed, the
automorphism $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^j$ shows that numerator and denominator
both have norm $p$; the ratio has norm one. Multiplication by it is an
integer matrix of determinant one, so its inverse is an integer matrix
and the ratio is a unit.

For $1\leq r\leq p-1$, the lattice $\pi^rO$ is

$$p\mathbb Z\,1+\cdots+p\mathbb Z\,\pi^{r-1}
+\mathbb Z\,\pi^r+\cdots+\mathbb Z\,\pi^{p-2}.\tag{5.2}$$

Every displayed generator belongs to $\pi^rO$; the displayed lattice has
index $p^r$, equal to (5.1), so inclusion is equality. Write uniquely

$$n=(e-1)(p-1)+r,\qquad e\geq1,\quad1\leq r\leq p-1.$$

Since $\pi^nO=p^{e-1}\pi^rO$, (5.2) gives the additive decomposition

$$O/\pi^nO\cong
(\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z)^r\oplus
(\mathbb Z/p^{e-1}\mathbb Z)^{p-1-r},\tag{5.3}$$

with the indicated basis classes $1,\pi,\ldots,\pi^{p-2}$. A factor
with modulus one is interpreted as trivial. This supplies both an exact
finite coordinate interface and the individual orders of every $s_i$ in
the source presentation.

Conjugation preserves the ideal $\pi^nO$, since
$\overline\pi=-\zeta^{-1}\pi$. Multiplication by $\zeta$ has order $p$
on $O/\pi^nO$ when $n\geq2$: it is not the identity, because
$\pi\notin\pi^nO$, while its $p$-th power is the identity. Finally,

$$1+\zeta+\cdots+\zeta^{p-1}=0.\tag{5.4}$$

For each $j$ not divisible by $p$, replacing $\zeta$ by $\zeta^j$ gives
the same zero sum.

## 6. Proof of the nonabelian construction

Put $n=m-1\geq2$ and $A=O/\pi^nO$. To specify the conjugation convention
without ambiguity, realize $P$ as pairs $(v,k)\in A\times\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$
with multiplication

$$(v,k)(w,l)=(v+\zeta^{-k}w,k+l).\tag{6.1}$$

Then $\beta=(0,1)$ satisfies $v^\beta=\zeta v$. Equation (5.1) gives
$|P|=p^m$. It is nonabelian because multiplication by $\zeta$ is nontrivial.
Every element outside $A$ has order exactly $p$: for $k\ne0$, its $p$-th
power has first coordinate $\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\zeta^{-ik}v=0$ by (5.4),
and its image in $P/A$ has order $p$.

The maps

$$\sigma(v,k)=(\overline v,-k),\qquad
\tau(v,k)=(-v,k)$$

preserve (6.1), because conjugation sends $\zeta^{-k}$ to $\zeta^k$.
They are commuting involutory automorphisms. They are distinct and
independent: $\sigma$ inverts the nontrivial element $\beta$, whereas
$\tau$ fixes it; $\tau$ is nontrivial on the nonzero odd-order group $A$.
Thus they generate a Klein four group and $|G|=4p^m$.

Set $s=1_A$ and $h=\tau\sigma$. We have $s^h=s^{-1}$ and
$\beta^h=\beta^{-1}$, in multiplicative notation inside $P$. Hence
$r_0=sh$, $r_1=\beta h$ and $r_2=\sigma$ are involutions. Also

$$r_0r_2=s\tau=r_2r_0,\qquad
r_0r_1=s\beta^{-1},\qquad r_1r_2=\beta\tau.$$

The second product is outside $A$ and has order $p$. The last has order
$2p$, because $\beta$ and $\tau$ commute and have coprime orders $p,2$.
These computations also show that the three involutions are distinct.

They generate $G$. Indeed, $r_1r_2=\beta\tau$ has $p$-th power $\tau$;
it then yields $\beta$. Together with $r_2$ this gives $\sigma$, and
$r_0$ gives $s$. The $\beta$-conjugates of $s$ are the classes of
$1,\zeta,\ldots,\zeta^{p-1}$, which additively generate $A$.

Finally let $H=\langle r_0,r_1\rangle$ and
$J=\langle r_1,r_2\rangle$. Their orders are $2p$ and $4p$.
The subgroup $C=\langle r_1\rangle$ has prime index $p$ in $H$.
If $H\leq J$, then all three generators lie in $J$, forcing
$G=J$, contrary to $4p^m>4p$. Lemma 2.2 gives $H\cap J=C$,
and Lemma 2.1 proves the intersection property. This proves Theorem C.

## 7. The exact source-presentation bridge

Use the integers $e,r$ of (5.3) with $n=m-1$, and let
$s_i=\pi^{i-1}$ for $1\leq i\leq p-1$. Their orders are $p^e$ for
$i\leq r$ and $p^{e-1}$ for $i>r$. All $s_i$ commute. Our convention
$[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ gives

$$[s_i,\beta]=\pi s_i=s_{i+1}\quad(1\leq i<p-1).$$

The cyclotomic equation $\Phi_p(1+\pi)=0$ gives the final relation

$$[s_{p-1},\beta]
=\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}s_i^{-\binom pi}.\tag{7.1}$$

Thus every relation in [HFLQ, §5, physical p.6] holds in our group.
Conversely, in the abstract group defined by that presentation, the
subgroup on the commuting $s_i$ has order at most $p^{m-1}$. The
conjugation relations make it invariant under conjugation by $\beta$:
inclusion is equality because this subgroup is finite and conjugation
is injective. The quotient is generated by $\beta$ of order dividing
$p$. Therefore the presented group has order at most $p^m$.
The surjection to our group of order $p^m$ is consequently an isomorphism.
It sends every named generator to the stated ring class, not merely to
some group with the same order.

Under this isomorphism, $\sigma$ fixes $s_1$ and inverts $\beta$,
and $\tau$ inverts $s_1$ and fixes $\beta$. These are exactly the
maps and markings of [HFLQ, Theorem 5.1]. More generally,

$$s_i^\sigma=(-1)^{i-1}\zeta^{1-i}\pi^{i-1}
=\begin{cases}s_i^{\beta^{1-i}}&i\text{ odd},\\
(s_i^{\beta^{1-i}})^{-1}&i\text{ even}.
\end{cases}\tag{7.2}$$

In (7.2) the middle expression is additive ring notation and the right
side is group notation. It follows from
$\overline\pi=-\zeta^{-1}\pi$ and also holds for the additional
commutators $s_p,s_{p+1}$ used by the source. Expanding
$\zeta^{p+1-i}=(1+\pi)^{p+1-i}$ gives the binomial formula of
[HFLQ, Lemma 5.2(1)]. Formula (7.2) gives Lemma 5.2(2), while the
automorphism proof in §6 gives the content of Lemma 5.3. Thus the
presentation and its involutory automorphisms are recovered directly,
including parameters for which some listed $s_i$ are trivial.

For completeness, $[A,\beta]=\pi A$, and induction gives
$\gamma_j(P)=\pi^{j-1}A$ for $j\geq2$. These groups are nontrivial
through $j=n$ and trivial at $j=n+1$. Hence $P$ has nilpotency class
$n=m-1$, as in the source's maximal-class description. The motivating
condition $A=H_p(P)$ on a Hughes subgroup is not needed: when
$n\leq p-1$, (5.3) makes $A$ elementary abelian, all elements of $P$
have order dividing $p$, and $H_p(P)=1$. This explains why that condition
should not be imposed on every parameter of the construction itself.

## 8. Attribution, verification, and limits

Theorem A is [HFLQ, Theorem 1.1], with its Sylow argument and inverted
orbit made explicit. The abelian classification is [HFLQ, Theorem 1.2]
with the literal marking corrected; the two-character proof retains the
classical idempotent decomposition underlying its Lemma 2.4. The
tightness converse retains precisely [CP, Theorem 1.1(b),(c)] and the
exclusion of its exceptional parameter $4$. Theorem C and the marked
presentation are [HFLQ, Theorems 1.3 and 5.1]. Sections 5–7 replace its
binomial verification and presentation-existence input by one cyclotomic
lattice argument. These attributions concern the mathematical results;
the replacement proof does not claim their discovery.

The accompanying finite checker uses the mixed-modulus coordinates
(5.3), the companion matrix of $\Phi_p(1+X)$, and literal permutation
actions. It verifies the displayed matrices, source relators, generator
orders and all $64$ subset-intersection equalities for each selected
finite model. Its checks are examples of the uniform written proof,
not a substitute for the arbitrary-$p,m$ argument. It also checks the
abelian marking and rejects the literal faulty formula.

The accompanying Lean file verifies the prime-index intersection
argument and elementary reflection-product identities used in §6.
Cyclotomic arithmetic, Sylow normality, the character classification,
and the retained tight-polyhedron classification remain written proof
or retained input; they are not represented as established Lean facts.
The replay report states the exact tested parameters and formal theorem
names. All required mathematical programs and dependencies must be
