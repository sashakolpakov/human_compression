**(d) Exact intersections after a quotient.** Let q:H→Q be a homomorphism with kernel N, and let A,B≤H. Then

\[
q(A)\cap q(B)=q(A\cap B)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N\cap AB=(N\cap A)(N\cap B).
\tag{Kd1}
\]

Here AB is a set product; it need not be a subgroup. If q is injective on both A and B, the right side is simply N∩AB=1. If N≤A, equality always holds. Thus, when A∩B=C, these are exact criteria for their quotient images to meet in q(C), rather than an assumption that arbitrary quotients preserve marked IP.

*Proof.* Suppose first that the image equality holds and ab∈N, where a∈A and b∈B. Choose c∈A∩B with q(c)=q(a)=q(b)⁻¹. Then a c⁻¹∈N∩A and cb∈N∩B, so ab=(a c⁻¹)(cb). The reverse set inclusion is immediate. Conversely, if q(a)=q(b), then ab⁻¹∈N∩AB. Write ab⁻¹=uv with u∈N∩A and v∈N∩B. The element u⁻¹a=vb lies in A∩B and has image q(a), proving the image equality. If N≤A, both sides of the right-hand equality are N. ∎

### A single finite-kernel classification for every rank and excess

**Theorem Q5.5 (universal kernel classification).** Let T be a forest on the specified d vertices, with m edges, and let k≥0. Define F_T by involutory generators x_i, imposing [x_i,x_j]=1 for every pair not joined by an edge, and put

\[
U=F_T/\gamma_{k+3}(F_T),\qquad K=\gamma_3(U).
\tag{Q5.2}
\]

Then U is a finite two-group, its original marking has Boolean deletion maps and IP, and its class-two quotient is the coordinate group Q_T of (Q5.1). The following objects are in bijection:

1. Ordered marked finite two-groups G with IP, noncommutation graph exactly T, and order 2^(d+m+k).
2. Normal subgroups N of U contained in K, with [K:N]=2^k, for which the subgroups

\[
L_S=(N K_S)/N\le K/N,
\qquad K_S=K\cap U_S=\gamma_3(U_S)
\tag{Q5.3}
\]

satisfy L_S∩L_R=L_(S∩R) for every pair of vertex subsets S,R.

The bijection sends N to the marking on U/N. It does not divide out any further automorphisms for ordered markings. If generator-set equivalence is desired, it is exactly the orbit relation from Aut(T) on these kernels. For a nondegenerate path this group consists of identity and reversal.

*Proof.* The lower-central graded group of F_T is generated as a Lie ring by the degree-one involutions. Multilinearity shows that every homogeneous component is generated by finitely many elements of exponent two. Consequently each lower-central factor through degree k+2 is a finite elementary abelian group. More explicitly, the left-normed commutators of weight j on the d letters give at most d^j generators, so |U|≤2^(∑_(j=1)^(k+2) d^j). This written finite bound suffices for termination independently of the smaller computed orders; the quotient U is a finite two-group. The involution/commutation relations are respected when any selected subset of the x_i is sent to 1; functoriality of the lower central series makes this descend to U. The deletion maps e_S commute, with e_S e_R=e_(S∩R), fix U_S pointwise and have image U_S. An element of U_S∩U_R is therefore fixed by e_(S∩R), proving IP.

Collection in the class-two involution/commutation presentation bounds its order by 2^(d+m). The coordinate group (Q5.1) realizes all d vertex coordinates and all m edge coordinates independently, attaining this bound. It is a quotient of U, since it has class at most two. Thus U/K is exactly that marked coordinate group. In particular every edge of T remains noncommuting in U. Also, for x∈K∩U_S, applying e_S to an expression of x in γ₃(U) places x in γ₃(U_S). The opposite inclusion is immediate, proving the restriction equality in (Q5.3).

For a target G, Theorem Q5.3 gives |γ₃(G)|=2^k. A nontrivial lower-central term of a finite nilpotent group strictly decreases at the next step: equality would persist forever, contradicting nilpotence. There can consequently be at most k nontrivial successive factors starting at γ₃(G), so γ_(k+3)(G)=1. The marked presentation map F_T→G factors through U. Its induced map on class-two quotients is the marked isomorphism Q_T→Q_T, hence its kernel N lies in K. The orders give [K:N]=2^k.

Conversely, such a kernel gives a quotient G=U/N of the required order. Its specified noncommutations survive in the quotient G/(K/N)=Q_T, and its nonedge commutations are inherited. If q:U→G and D=K/N, then

\[
D\cap G_S=q(K\cap U_S)=q(K_S)=L_S.
\tag{Q5.4}
\]

Indeed q⁻¹(q(K))=K because N≤K, so an element of U_S mapping into D must lie in K. The marking on G/D has IP by the coordinate description of Q_T. Theorem K(a), applied to G→Q_T, now gives exactly the displayed criterion on the L_S. This proves necessity and sufficiency without assuming IP for a quotient.

An ordered marked isomorphism between two quotients of U commutes with their maps from U because those maps agree on every original generator; thus their kernels are equal. Conversely equal kernels give the same ordered marked quotient. A permutation of the generator set in an isomorphism preserves commutation and is an automorphism of T. Each such permutation is an automorphism of F_T and U, and acts on their kernels. Its orbits are therefore precisely the claimed generator-set classes. ∎

**Theorem Q5.6 (complete invariant index-two ladder).** Let U be a finite two-group and M◁U. The U-normal subgroups L≤M with [M:L]=2 are exactly the preimages of the hyperplanes in

\[
M/(\Phi(M)[M,U]).
\tag{Q5.5}
\]

For any K◁U, every U-normal N≤K of index 2^k is obtained by k successive such steps. This includes nonabelian K and M.

*Proof.* An index-two subgroup L of M is normal in M, contains Φ(M), and has quotient C₂. If L is U-normal, conjugation by U on M/L is trivial because Aut(C₂)=1, so [M,U]≤L. Conversely, the preimage of a hyperplane contains both Φ(M) and [M,U]; the latter containment makes it U-normal, and its index in M is two. The quotient in (Q5.5) is elementary abelian by the Burnside basis theorem.

Suppose N◁U and N<M. The finite two-group U/N acts on the nonzero characters of (M/N)/Φ(M/N). This nonzero vector space exists because M/N is a nontrivial finite two-group. Its nonzero characters number 2^a−1, which is odd. Since orbit sizes are powers of two, at least one orbit has size one. The kernel of that fixed nonzero character lifts to a U-normal subgroup between N and M, of index two in M. Repeating constructs the required chain from K down to N. Breadth-first generation of every hyperplane preimage at each step, removing only exactly equal subgroups, is therefore exhaustive. ∎

The algorithmic consequence is concrete. For a path on d vertices and a target order 2^n, set k=n−(2d−1). Construct U by the universal nilpotent-quotient map of class k+2, preserve the original marked generators, enumerate the ladder in K and test (Q5.3) in its quotient of order 2^k. Full marked-subgroup intersection tests can independently check this profile criterion. Neither an anticipated group count, an Atlas name nor an abstract-group catalogue enters candidate generation. A canonical pc subgroup key must encode actual subgroup elements injectively; a binary exponent encoding is valid only after checking that all ambient relative pc orders are two.

The cases (d,k)=(3,3),(3,5),(4,3),(5,1) respectively give the order-256 seed and the three nondegenerate order-1024 censuses. Their terminal counts and the order-256 word-pattern implications require the complete executed programs printed with the new manuscript; they are not conclusions of this proof alone.
