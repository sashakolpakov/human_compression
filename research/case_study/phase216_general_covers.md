# Minimal regular covers in arbitrary rank: working proof

**Private, unfrozen proof awaiting independent review.** Phase 216 continues the
general conjecture after the separate, frozen surface theorem in phase 211.
This paper gives the elementary reduction. Its companion
`phase216_geometric_filling.md` supplies the complete proposed geometric
argument and conclusion. Neither is admitted yet. No finite-cover conclusion
is intended when the resulting groups are infinite.

## 1. The precise question and the reduction

Let \(P\) be an abstract polytope of finite rank \(n\), with no restriction on the
number of its flags. Write
\[
 W_n=\langle r_0,\ldots,r_{n-1}\mid r_i^2=1,
       [r_i,r_j]=1\ (|i-j|>1)\rangle,
 \qquad K=\ker(W_n\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mon}(P)).
\]
For \(I\subseteq\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), put \(W_I=\langle r_i:i\in I\rangle\).
A normal subgroup \(N\leq K\) is *admissible* if the images of these standard
subgroups in \(W_n/N\) have the intersection property. The correspondence
between string C-groups and regular polytopes identifies admissible subgroups
with regular polytopal covers of \(P\), reversing the covering order. A maximal
admissible subgroup therefore gives a minimal regular cover.

The exact general question in Cunningham--Pellicer's survey, Problem 5, is
whether \(\operatorname{Mon}(P)\) can fail the intersection property while \(P\)
has a unique minimal regular cover. That question does not require either
\(P\) or its covers to be finite. Cunningham's 2023 author manuscript N016
states the same expected conclusion in its final section. Finite examples and finite families do not settle
these quantifiers.

The following reduction is elementary. Let \(\mathcal A_K\) be the family of
admissible subgroups. The union of a chain in \(\mathcal A_K\) is admissible:
an equality of two parabolic cosets modulo the union is already an equality
modulo one member of the chain, where its intersection witness exists.
Normality and containment in \(K\) also pass to the union. Consequently every
admissible subgroup is contained in a maximal one. This is the Zorn argument
formalized, in greater group-theoretic generality, in frozen
`formal/CoverKernelChains.lean`.

It follows that
\[
 \bigvee_{N\in\mathcal A_K}N=K                                      \tag{1}
\]
would settle the question. Indeed, if there were just one maximal member \(M\),
every member would lie in \(M\); (1) would force \(K=M\), making the monodromy
group a string C-group. Conversely, when the monodromy group is a string
C-group, \(K\) itself is admissible and is the greatest admissible subgroup.

## 2. Relations supported on disjoint sets of ranks

**Lemma 2.1.** Suppose \(I_1,\ldots,I_t\) are pairwise disjoint and
\(g_j\in W_{I_j}\). If \(g_1\cdots g_t\in K\), then every \(g_j\in K\).

**Proof.** First consider two factors \(a\in W_I\), \(b\in W_J\) with
\(I\cap J=\varnothing\). Their actions on flags are inverse permutations.
The action of \(a\) preserves every face of a rank outside \(I\); its inverse,
being the action of \(b\), preserves every face of a rank outside \(J\).
Thus the action of \(a\) preserves the face of every rank. A flag is determined
by these faces, so \(a\) fixes every flag. The same holds for \(b\).
Apply this argument first to the first factor and the product of the others,
then induct on the remaining product. The two rank sets used at each step
are disjoint. This proof uses actual flags; it is false for an arbitrary
quotient generated by string involutions. \(\square\)

The usual normal form for a graph product gives a useful consequence. Conjugate
any \(w\in K\) to a cyclically reduced word. Its support decomposes into the
connected components of the graph in which two letters are joined when they do
not commute. Here that graph is a subgraph of the path
\(0-1-\cdots-(n-1)\). Its components are intervals \(J_1,\ldots,J_t\).
The word is a product of commuting, cyclically reduced words \(w_j\in W_{J_j}\),
each with support exactly \(J_j\). Lemma 2.1 puts each \(w_j\) in \(K\).
A component consisting of one letter cannot occur, since a distinguished
involution changes a face in every flag and is therefore not in \(K\).
Each remaining component word has infinite order: a cyclically reduced graph
product word supported on a connected noncommutation graph with at least two
vertices is a nontrivial pure factor, and all its positive powers remain reduced.

Hence \(K\) is normally generated by its cyclically reduced, infinite-order
elements whose supports are consecutive intervals of length at least two. We
call this set \(\mathcal R\); there is no finiteness assertion about it.

To prove (1), it is enough to prove that for each \(w\in\mathcal R\) there is
an integer \(m_0(w)\) such that
\[
 W_n/\langle\!\langle w^m\rangle\!\rangle
 \quad\hbox{is a string C-group for every }m\geq m_0(w).            \tag{2}
\]
Both normal closures for consecutive integers \(m,m+1\) then lie in
\(\mathcal A_K\), and their join contains \(w\), since
\(w=w^{m+1}(w^m)^{-1}\). Normal generation gives (1).

## 3. Extending an interval of generators

**Lemma 3.1.** Let \(H=\langle s_0,\ldots,s_{d-1}\rangle\) be a string
C-group. Adjoin an involution \(t\) commuting with \(s_0,\ldots,s_{d-2}\),
with no further relations. The resulting marked group is a string C-group.

**Proof.** Put \(A=H_{\{0,\ldots,d-2\}}\). The presented group is the
amalgamated free product
\[
 T=H *_A(A\times\langle t\rangle),\qquad \langle t\rangle\cong C_2.
\]
Both factors inject by the amalgam normal form. We induct on \(d\). For
\(d=1\), this is \(C_2*C_2\), the infinite dihedral string C-group.

For \(d>1\), let \(B=H_{\{1,\ldots,d-1\}}\) and
\(C=A\cap B=H_{\{1,\ldots,d-2\}}\). Normal forms in the displayed amalgam
show that the subgroup \(\langle B,t\rangle\) is naturally
\(B *_C(C\times C_2)\): a reduced word in this smaller amalgam remains
reduced in the larger one, because an element of \(B\) belongs to \(A\)
exactly when it belongs to \(C\). The same normal form shows
\(H\cap\langle B,t\rangle=B\). By induction \(\langle B,t\rangle\) is a
string C-group. The two end parabolics are therefore string C-groups and
intersect in their common middle parabolic. The usual recursive intersection
criterion proves the intersection property for \(T\). \(\square\)

The left-hand extension is identical after reversing the indices. Thus, once
(2) is known inside \(W_J\), where \(J\) is the support of \(w\), repeated
left and right extensions establish (2) in \(W_n\). There are no hidden
relations in this step: both presentations contain precisely the original
string commutations, the involution relations, and \(w^m=1\).

If \(|J|=2\), write \(w=(r_i r_{i+1})^e\), up to inversion, with \(e>0\).
The quotient is the dihedral group with product order \(em\), so (2) holds
as soon as \(em\geq2\).

## 4. The geometric theorem and conclusion

The complete proof of the large-power statement is in
`phase216_geometric_filling.md`, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2. Its precise
inputs and conclusions are:

1. In an irreducible right-angled Coxeter group, the Cayley graph whose
   generators are the union of all proper standard parabolics is hyperbolic
   and its action is acylindrical. The proof verifies a factor system and
   constructs an explicit equivariant comparison with its factored contact
   graph, including the treatment of parallel copies.
2. A cyclically reduced word with full support is loxodromic on this graph.
   The proof bounds the projections of all proper parabolic cosets uniformly,
   using missing hyperplane labels and a contracting periodic axis.
3. Hull's theorem then embeds the elementary subgroup hyperbolically relative
   to the same generating set. Dahmani--Guirardel--Osin's filling theorem is
   injective on that entire set after imposing a sufficiently large power.
   No restriction to multiples of a fixed integer is imposed.
4. This injection preserves all standard parabolic intersections. The free
   extension lemma above deals with a relation supported on a proper interval.

Thus the join identity (1) follows, and the proposed general conclusion is:
an abstract polytope of finite rank has a unique minimal regular polytopal
cover if and only if its monodromy group is a string C-group. Covers and flag
sets are allowed to be infinite. Existence of maximal admissible kernels
also gives at least one minimal regular cover in every case.

The proof does not establish finite minimal covers for finite polytopes, nor
does it prove infinitely many minimal covers whenever there is more than one.
Those are stronger assertions. Frozen phase 211 proves an infinite-family
conclusion for its stated examples by a different, more explicit construction.

## 5. Covering order and uniqueness

We make the correspondence used in Section 1 explicit. Fix a flag of the
polytope P. The action of W_n on all flags is transitive; let L be the
stabilizer of that flag and K its normal core. A regular marked polytope
with group W_n/N covers the rooted P exactly when N is contained in L.
Since N is normal, that condition is equivalent to N being contained in K.
The canonical map on flags sends the coset of a word to the image of the
chosen flag under that word. It commutes with every distinguished adjacency.
The standard covering/flag-graph equivalence turns it into a polytope cover.
This construction uses no finite-index hypothesis.

Between two regular polytopes, a covering can be aligned at chosen flags:
compose with an automorphism of its regular domain to send the selected
flag to the selected flag. Equivariance under all flag adjacencies then
says that a cover from W_n/N to W_n/M exists exactly when N is contained
in M. A rank-preserving isomorphism of regular polytopes gives equality
of these normal kernels, since its effect on a chosen flag can again be
removed by an automorphism. Hence the unrooted covering order and unrooted
isomorphism classes introduce no extra identifications between different
normal kernels. This justifies the meaning of uniqueness used above.

## 6. Attribution and formal scope

The correspondence between regular polytopes and string C-groups, the
recursive intersection criterion, graph-product normal forms, and the
normal form for an amalgamated free product are standard inputs. The
geometric companion lists the six primary works providing its substantial
retained theorems. None of those general geometric theorems is claimed as
new or as formally verified here.

Frozen `formal/CoverKernelChains.lean` proves the chain-union and Zorn
statements for the explicit word form of the intersection property.
New `formal/CoverKernelGeneration.lean` uses that same definition. It proves
that inverse permutations supported on disjoint coordinate sets are trivial,
then verifies that an explicitly stated supply of consecutive powers of
normal generators makes the join of admissible kernels equal the target
kernel. Its final implication says that a unique maximal admissible kernel
then forces the target intersection property. The normal-generation and
power-supply hypotheses remain explicit; their application to actual
polytopes is proved in the written argument, not asserted by a Lean axiom.

The proposed general result still requires independent mathematical review,
with exact primary-version and locator bindings. No new compression ratio
or complete replacement of an entire source paper is claimed by this proof.
