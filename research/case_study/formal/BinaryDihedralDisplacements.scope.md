# Scope of BinaryDihedralDisplacements.lean

For an arbitrary group G, elements x,y and integer k, define tₖ=yᵏxᵏ.
The five printed declarations prove:

1. If tₖ²=1 for every integer k, then all tᵢ,tⱼ commute.
2. The identity x⁻¹tₖx=t₁⁻¹tₖ₊₁, without the square hypotheses.
3. Under the first hypothesis, the subgroup generated by all tₖ is commutative.
4. For any positive natural n, xⁿ=yⁿ=1 and the relations tₖ²=1 for 1≤k<n imply tₖ²=1 for every integer k, including negative indices and zero.
5. The commutativity conclusion for the generated subgroup follows from those exact finite-period hypotheses.

These statements verify the central displacement argument of the universal
presentation proof. They do not yet formalize the subgroup's normality or
cardinality, the semidirect model, its parabolic intersections, the matrix
basis bridge or the regular-polytope correspondence. No axiom stands in for
those missing formal proofs; they are separately reviewed written arguments.

reports only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. Pinned package
