# Equal cyclic orders and the smallest-factor-eight case

10 October 2026. Private continuation for review. All earlier proof and
certificate files are unchanged. The unrestricted cyclic-TPP inequality
for finite 2-groups remains unresolved.

**Uniform theorem.** If A, B, C are cyclic subgroups of a finite 2-group
P satisfying the triple product property and

    min(|A|,|B|,|C|) <= 8,

then |A||B||C| <= |P|.

**Structural theorem.** In a minimum-order counterexample to the
unrestricted cyclic-TPP inequality for finite 2-groups, the three cyclic
orders are equal to 2^e for some e>=4. Consequently

    |P| = 2^(3e-1),
    P^8 is powerful of index 256,
    |<a^8>| = |<b^8>| = |<c^8>| = 2^(e-3),

and the three displayed cyclic subgroups give bijective ordered
coordinates on P^8. The existence of such a counterexample is not proved.

**Finite and geometric consequences.** The cyclic-TPP inequality holds
for every finite 2-group of order at most 1024. In the setting of
Hou--Feng--Leemans Conjecture 3.3, the hypertope order bound holds if
one of the three pair orders is at most 16, and also for every hypertope
group of order at most 2^13. These conclusions use the same exact
finite input through order 256; no order-1024 catalogue is searched.

## 1. Definitions and retained inputs

For subgroups A, B, C, the triple product property (TPP) means that
uvw=1, with u in A, v in B, w in C, forces u=v=w=1. This condition is
invariant under permuting the three subgroups and passes to subgroups
of the three factors. It does not assert injectivity of the ordered
multiplication map A x B x C -> P.

We retain the following previously reviewed results explicitly:

1. The minimum-counterexample reductions and the powerful eighth-power
   subgroup theorem in [phase12_power_layers.md](phase12_power_layers.md).
   They give center-disjoint factors, generation by each pair, ratio
   |A||B||C|/|P|=2, and a normal powerful subgroup

       N=P^8=<a^8,b^8,c^8>,  |P:N|=256.

   The ordered product of the three marked cyclic subgroups of N is
   bijective, as are all its restrictions obtained by powering all three
   factors equally. The proof retains the binary square-action lemma,
   the powerful-Frattini obstruction, and the marked-isobicyclic input
   to the smallest-factor-four theorem. They are not re-proved here.
2. The exact cyclic-TPP bound for all finite 2-groups of order at most
   256, proved by the minimum-counterexample reduction and the exhaustive
   calculation in [phase3_cyclic_tpp_exploration.md](phase3_cyclic_tpp_exploration.md),
   with [certificate](phase3_cyclic_tpp_certificate.json). Its catalogue
   input is GAP 4.15.1 / SmallGroups 1.5.4. This is an explicit retained
   finite theorem, not a conclusion extrapolated to larger catalogues.
3. The powerful-group power and coordinate results listed with primary
   locators in [phase11_powerful_frattini.md](phase11_powerful_frattini.md),
   section 2: Lubotzky--Mann, *Powerful p-groups. I. Finite groups*,
   Journal of Algebra 105 (1987), 484--505,
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(87)90211-0), in particular
   the 2-group statements 4.1.1, 4.1.3, 4.1.9 and 4.1.11 on printed
   pages 499--501. These imply that powerful power subgroups are
   powerful and are generated by the corresponding powers of a given
   generating list. The source reading is inherited from the frozen
   phase11 audit; no new primary full-text reading is asserted here.

## 2. A direct action lemma on the largest power layer

**Lemma.** Suppose a finite 2-group P is generated by each pair from
a,b,c, and each of <a>, <b>, <c> meets Z(P) trivially. Let N be a normal
powerful subgroup of P. Suppose, for a positive integer m,

    N=<a^m,b^m,c^m>,

and these three cyclic subgroups give a bijective ordered product on N.
If N is nontrivial, the three orders |a^m|, |b^m|, |c^m| are equal and
nontrivial.

**Proof.** Put x=a^m, y=b^m, z=c^m and let 2^r, r>=1, be the largest
of their orders. Powerful power generation gives N^(2^r)=1, so this is
also the exponent of N. Put U=N^(2^(r-1)). This subgroup is normal in
P because it is characteristic in N. It is powerful of exponent two,
hence elementary abelian.

The retained power-generation theorem gives

    U=<x^(2^(r-1)), y^(2^(r-1)), z^(2^(r-1))>.

The ordered multiplication map for these three subgroups is the
restriction of the assumed bijective map on N. It remains injective;
powerful ordered factorization makes it surjective onto U. Thus the
nonidentity displayed elements form a basis of U. Their number is
exactly the number of marked cyclic factors attaining order 2^r.

If exactly one factor is largest, U has order two. Every automorphism
of U is trivial, so its displayed nonidentity element belongs to Z(P)
and to one of <a>, <b>, <c>, a contradiction.

If exactly two factors are largest, relabel them as x and y. Their top
powers u and v form a basis of U. Since P=<a,b>, the conjugation image
R of P in GL(U)=GL_2(F2) is generated by the images S,T of a,b. The
group R is a 2-group, so |R|<=2 because |GL_2(F2)|=6. Also S fixes u
and T fixes v. If both S and T are nonidentity, they are the same map
and fix both basis vectors, which would make them the identity. Hence
at least one is the identity. If T is the identity, u is fixed by both
generators; if S is the identity, v is fixed by both. Either way a
nonidentity element of a distinguished cyclic factor lies in Z(P),
again a contradiction.

Only the case of three largest factors remains. QED.

The action is on the actual characteristic subgroup U. No assertion
that an arbitrary power map is a homomorphism is needed. The argument
also does not assume that a 2-subgroup of GL_3(F2) fixes a marked
coordinate; that stronger statement is false, as recorded in phase12.

## 3. Unrestricted minimum counterexamples have equal orders

Choose a counterexample of minimum group order among all violating
cyclic triples in finite 2-groups. Retained input 1 applies. If N=P^8
were trivial, |P|=256, contrary to retained input 2. Thus N is nontrivial.
Apply the lemma with m=8. All three eighth-power cyclic orders are
equal and nontrivial. Hence the original cyclic orders are equal to
2^e with e>=4. The ratio-two reduction gives |P|=2^(3e-1).
The other structural assertions are precisely the retained power-layer
conclusions. This proves the structural theorem.

This statement concerns a minimum counterexample. It does not assert
the inequality for every individual triple whose three orders differ:
the class of unequal-order triples need not be preserved by all of the
inductive reductions.

## 4. Why the bounded-factor induction is legitimate

To prove the uniform theorem, instead choose P of minimum order among
violating triples with at least one cyclic factor of order at most eight.
We explain why the reductions behind retained input 1 remain valid in
this restricted class, rather than simply applying an unrestricted
minimum-counterexample statement.

Every use of smaller-order minimality in that proof does one of the
following:

* A central subgroup contained in one cyclic factor is factored out.
  The new factors are the images of the old ones, so no factor order
  increases. The quotient triple has TPP by absorption into that factor.
* For a proper pair-generated subgroup H, the two generating factors
  are kept and the third is replaced by its intersection with H. The
  small factor is either kept or replaced by a subgroup of itself.
* The triple is restricted to a maximal subgroup to obtain the ratio
  two, or each factor is replaced by a square/power subgroup. These
  operations reduce each factor order. Passing to the subgroup
  generated by those smaller factors leaves their orders unchanged.

Thus every smaller violating triple considered still has a factor of
order at most eight. All minimum-order arguments go through. The
powerful-Frattini obstruction and the linear and weak-embedding
arguments use only the resulting relations and the same center-free
condition, so their proofs require no additional class closure.

For completeness, the lower bound of eight used before the second
power-layer step also holds in this restricted minimum. The elementary
center-free bound and the ratio two give a smallest factor at least
four. The actual structural theorem of
[phase10_cyclic_tpp.md](phase10_cyclic_tpp.md), section 1, rules out
equality four using only the three distinct Frattini classes and the
center-free cyclic factors. Its application is independent of how the
minimum counterexample was selected. Consequently the smallest factor
in the present restricted minimum is exactly eight.

We now have all conclusions of retained input 1. If N=P^8=1, then
|P|=256 and the retained finite theorem is a contradiction. If N is
nontrivial, the lemma in section 2 forces all three eighth-power cyclic
factors to be nontrivial, whereas the original order-eight factor has
trivial eighth power. This is again a contradiction. The uniform
smallest-factor-eight theorem follows.

## 5. Consequences and precise remaining scope

If the cyclic-TPP inequality failed at any order at most 1024, an
unrestricted minimum counterexample would have order at most 1024.
Section 3 instead gives order at least 2^(3*4-1)=2048. This proves the
claimed finite-order consequence. The new conclusion uses the fixed
order-256 certificate; it is not an enumeration of order-512 or
order-1024 groups.

For a regular rank-three hypertope in the setting of
[Hou--Feng--Leemans Conjecture 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09034),
the reviewed reduction in [phase3_order_bound.md](phase3_order_bound.md)
gives P=G' of order |G|/8 and a cyclic TPP triple whose cyclic orders
are half the three distinguished pair orders. Thus a pair order at
most sixteen is covered by the uniform theorem. If |G|<=2^13, then
|P|<=2^10, so the finite-order consequence applies. The reduction
retains flag transitivity and its typed Helly identity; the subgroup
intersection property alone is not silently substituted for them.

The unrestricted cyclic-TPP inequality, and the full hypertope order
conjecture, remain unresolved here. The new statements are internally
reviewable proofs retaining the published powerful/isobicyclic inputs
and the exact SmallGroups calculation specified above. They are not a
full Lean formalization or a determination of publication priority.
