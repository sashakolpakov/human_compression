# Coset upper bounds from checked deductions

Phase219 author manuscript, 10 October 2026. This note formalizes the deduction principle used by the frozen phase214 certificates. It does not change their presentation or the reviewed label-eleven obstruction. The rank-four and rank-five computations remain outside the formal claims made here.

## 1. The required invariant

Let G be generated by involutions indexed by an alphabet A, and let H be a subgroup. A finite permutation action satisfying the relators and having an H-fixed point gives a quotient of the coset action. It need not account for every H-coset. In particular, the one-point action satisfies every relator and cannot generally bound the index by one.

For an upper certificate, assign a word w_v to each name v in a finite set V. Interpret v as the actual right coset Hw_v in the presented group. Every installed transition v --s--> z must prove

\[
 H w_v r_s=H w_z.
\]

The names need not represent distinct cosets. We do not assume they already represent all cosets: that conclusion follows only after the proved transitions are complete.

The inference rules below are classical coset-enumeration deductions. The contribution here is their Lean implementation, a proved executable checker, and its interface to the actual quotient of a free group by the defining relations. The general mathematical method is not claimed as new.

## 2. The inference system

Write E(p) for the value of a word p. Involutivity gives

\[
 E(pq)=E(p)E(q),\qquad E(p^{\mathrm{rev}})=E(p)^{-1}.
\]

Choose a root o whose word is empty, a set B of alphabet letters whose elements belong to H, and a set R of relator words of value one. The judgement v --p--> z is generated by six rules:

1. Every name has an empty path to itself.
2. For s in B, there is an s-loop at the root.
3. A tree edge v --s--> z is permitted when w_z=w_v s as literal words.
4. Consecutive derived paths may be concatenated.
5. A derived path may be reversed, with its word reversed.
6. If p s q is a stated relator, and paths u --p--> a and u --reverse(q)--> b have already been derived, infer a --s--> b.

The starting name u of a relator scan can be any name. The third rule checks a definition of a representative word, rather than importing a coset equality from an oracle. A breadth-first naming tree supplies exactly these definitions. Freshness of the underlying coset is unnecessary. The formal predicate Derives is inductive, so a deduction cannot justify itself through a cycle of unproved transitions.

## 3. Soundness and completeness

**Theorem 1.** A derived path v --p--> z implies Hw_v E(p)=Hw_z.

**Proof.** Empty paths and subgroup loops have the asserted meaning. A tree edge follows from its literal word definition. Concatenation follows from associativity. Reversal follows by right multiplication by E(p) inverse and the reverse-word identity.

For the final rule, the two known paths give

\[
 H w_u E(p)=H w_a,
 \qquad H w_u E(q)^{-1}=H w_b.
\]

The relator gives E(p)r_sE(q)=1, hence E(p)r_s=E(q) inverse. Substitution gives Hw_a r_s=Hw_b. Induction on the finite derivation proves the theorem. ∎

The Lean proof constructs right multiplication on the quotient by the right-coset relation. It proves this operation is well defined without assuming H normal. Inversion gives the standard equivalence with Mathlib's left-coset quotient when measuring the index.

Call the derived transition system complete when every name and every generator have an outgoing derived single-letter path.

**Theorem 2.** If the involutions generate G, the root word is empty and the derived system on finite V is complete, then G/H is finite and

\[
 [G:H]\le |V|.
\]

**Proof.** Completeness and concatenation give a root path for every word. The values of positive words form a subgroup: they contain the identity and generators, multiplication concatenates words, and inversion reverses words. Since the generators generate G, every g in G is E(p) for some word p. Theorem1 shows that the endpoint of the root path for p represents Hg. Thus the map from names to actual cosets is surjective, proving both finiteness and the bound. ∎

If H is finite, the index formula further gives

\[
 |G|=|H|[G:H]\le |H|\,|V|.
\]

The formal theorem proves G finite as well. It does not use the convention that Nat.card of an infinite type is zero to obtain a vacuous numerical bound.

## 4. The executable checker

The checker maintains a list of proved edges. Its instructions are rootLoop, tree, reverse and gap, containing only vertices, letters and finite paths of edge records. There is no instruction that inserts an arbitrary oracle transition.

The walk function verifies that every edge of a supplied path is already in the proved list and starts at the current vertex. A gap instruction also checks that its reconstructed word p s q belongs to the finite relator list. Both the forward prefix and reversed suffix are checked paths starting at the same name.

The theorem step_sound turns each accepted instruction into a Derives proof. The theorem replay_sound preserves this invariant through the instruction list. Only then does accept check that every name has an outgoing proved transition for every letter. The theorem accept_complete converts Boolean acceptance into the hypothesis of Theorem2.

List storage makes this a small inference kernel, not an optimized discovery algorithm. Its soundness theorem does not identify the Lean code with the frozen C++ scanner. The scanner can instead emit a finite trace whose deductions are checked independently of its internal search choices. A second route, used for the larger certificate, emits individual Derives proof terms directly. These terms must still pass Lean's kernel.

## 5. The universal-presentation interface

For an alphabet and unsigned relator list, Universal is the quotient of the free group by the normal closure of every generator square and every listed relator. No finite action or expected group order is added. The marked generators satisfy those relations and generate the quotient. The facet subgroup is the closure of the selected subgroup generators.

Consequently accepted_universal_index_bound concerns the index in this actual presented group. Its hypotheses are the empty root word and Boolean acceptance of the proof trace. It does not assume a coset enumeration, a finite group order or surjectivity of the supplied names. The marked relators and generation statement are obtained through Mathlib's PresentedGroup interface.

## 6. Concrete checks

Two kernel-checked examples use the literal dihedral presentation a²=b²=(ab)^11=1. An eleven-name certificate bounds the index of the subgroup generated by a. A twenty-two-name certificate uses the trivial subgroup and proves the whole presented group has order at most twenty-two. Their acceptance equations use ordinary Lean decision reduction, without native_decide.

Both examples exercise tree definitions, inverse transitions and one-missing-edge deductions; the first also uses a root subgroup loop. Four kernel-checked rejection statements cover a one-name table containing only its justified root loop, removal of all relators from the full trace, an empty trace, and a false tree-word definition.

The rank-three certificate is generated from the exact frozen 1024-name table. It contains 3072 individually checked directed-edge deductions. Every non-tree edge cites only previously proved edges and a stated relator; the generator is outside the proof trust boundary. Representative words are defined along the naming tree and reused as constants. A finite array contains, for each name, proofs of its three outgoing transitions. Lean checks that the array's name field covers exactly the 1024 names. This yields the concrete theorem

\[
 [U_3:\langle r_0,r_1\rangle]\le 1024.
\]

Here U_3 is the actual group on three involutions with the three pair relators and all ten source triple-power relators at label eleven. A separate kernel-checked equation identifies the emitted relator list with that displayed formula. The bound is not inferred from an action of a smaller quotient.

The exact full build and axiom reports are bound by phase219_replay/report.json. The rank-three file is a direct proof-carrying replay through Derives, whereas the two smaller examples also exercise the executable Boolean trace checker. These are distinct verification paths and are not described as one identical program. Rank-four and rank-five bounds, and the phase212 finite-field orders, simplicity and signed memberships, remain outside this formalization. The entire conjecture counterexample is therefore still not an end-to-end Lean theorem.
