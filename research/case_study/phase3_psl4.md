# Further uniform obstructions for PSL(4,q)

9 October 2026. These are partial results toward the odd-characteristic
maximum-rank question. They do not prove the full conjecture. The common-form
obstruction in [psl4_theory.md](psl4_theory.md) is used below: the natural
projective group PSL(4,q), q odd, cannot preserve any nonzero bilinear form,
even up to scalar and even if the form is degenerate. These lemmas have not
been checked for publication priority.

The exact target is Conjecture 2 in Leemans,
[*String C-group representations of almost simple groups: a survey*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08843),
§5: for odd q, the maximum rank for PSL(4,q) should be four. The preceding
Theorem 5.8 cites Brooksbank–Leemans, *Polytopes of large rank for PSL(4,q)*,
J. Algebra 452 (2016), 390–400, for existence of rank four. The present
notes supply uniform restrictions, not a resolution of that conjecture.

Throughout, k is algebraically closed of characteristic different from two.
Lift each projective involution to a matrix T with T²=I. Two such lifts that
commute projectively either commute or anticommute linearly.

## 1. A separated-support case missing from the tensor argument

**Lemma.** Suppose V=U⊕W, with both summands two-dimensional, and

    T₀=L₀⊕I, T₁=L₁⊕I, T₃=I⊕R₃, T₄=I⊕R₄,

where all Lᵢ and Rᵢ are nonscalar involutions. Suppose the projective pairs
(T₀,T₁) and (T₃,T₄) do not commute. If T₂²=I and T₂ commutes projectively
with T₀ and T₄, then all five matrices preserve a common nonzero bilinear
form.

**Proof.** Diagonalize L₀ and R₄ as diag(1,−1), and write

    L₁=[[a,b],[c,−a]],  a²+bc=1,
    R₃=[[d,e],[f,−d]],  d²+ef=1.

The forms QL=diag(c,b) and QR=diag(f,e) are invariant under their respective
two involutions. They are nonzero: b=c=0 would make T₀,T₁ commute, and the
same argument applies to e,f.

The −1 eigenspaces of T₀ and T₄ each have dimension one, while their +1
eigenspaces have dimension three. An invertible matrix cannot anticommute
with either one, because it would interchange eigenspaces of unequal
dimension. Thus T₂ commutes linearly with both. It acts separately on U−
and W−, by signs, and acts on the joint +1 eigenspace U+⊕W+ by an involution S.

If S is scalar, every form xQL⊕yQR is invariant under T₂. Otherwise write

    S=[[u,v],[w,−u]],  u²+vw=1.

The diagonal form diag(xc,yf) on U+⊕W+ is S-invariant whenever

    xc·v = yf·w.                                      (1)

Indeed, multiplying out Sᵀ diag(xc,yf) S and using (1) and u²+vw=1 gives
diag(xc,yf), without dividing by any parameter. Equation (1) is one
homogeneous linear equation in x,y, so it has a nonzero solution. For that
solution B=xQL⊕yQR is nonzero. The sign actions on U− and W− also preserve
B, and the four endpoint matrices preserve it by construction. ∎

This proof includes rank-one QL or QR, zero values of c or f, and triangular
dihedral modules. No semisimplicity assumption is used in this lemma.

## 2. Complete coverage of the untwisted, completely reducible endpoint case

**Proposition.** Suppose the four endpoint lifts T₀,T₁,T₃,T₄ commute
linearly across the partition {0,1}|{3,4}. Suppose their joint action on V is
completely reducible and dim V=4. If neither adjacent projective pair
commutes, then their representation has one of the following two forms,
up to conjugacy and scaling individual generators by signs:

1. The tensor form T₀=L₀⊗I,T₁=L₁⊗I,T₃=I⊗R₃,T₄=I⊗R₄.
2. The separated-support direct-sum form in Lemma 1 above.

Consequently no rank-five string C-group generating PSL(4,q) can satisfy
these endpoint hypotheses.

**Proof.** Every irreducible representation of a group generated by two
involutions has dimension at most two. To see this, put A=T₀T₁ and choose
an eigenvector v of A. The span of v,T₀v is invariant under T₀ and A because
T₀AT₀=A⁻¹, and hence under T₁. Irreducibility gives the bound.

Each irreducible joint module for two commuting groups is a tensor product
of irreducible modules for the factors. An elementary argument avoids a
restriction on the characteristic: choose a simple module U for the first
factor inside the joint module. Its translates by the commuting second
factor are isomorphic simple modules; their sum is the entire joint module
by irreducibility. Thus it is isotypic for the first factor, and is
U⊗Hom(U,V), with the second factor acting irreducibly on the multiplicity
space. Both tensor factors consequently have dimension at most two.

The completely reducible four-dimensional module is therefore a sum of
blocks of dimensions 1, 2, or 4. The first factor must act noncommutatively
on some block, so it needs a two-dimensional factor somewhere. The same
holds for the second factor. Either both occur in one four-dimensional
2⊗2 block, or they occur in separate two-dimensional blocks, of respective
types 2⊗1 and 1⊗2. These exhaust the dimension budget. In the second case,
the scalar actions on the opposite summands are signs and can be normalized
to +1 individually. This gives the asserted forms.

The first form is covered by Lemma 1 of psl4_theory.md. The second is covered
by the new lemma. In both cases any middle involution satisfying the string
commutations yields a common nonzero bilinear form, which is incompatible
with generating PSL(4,q). ∎

In particular, complete reducibility holds by Maschke's theorem when the
characteristic divides neither endpoint-dihedral order. The proposition
does not claim that this condition is necessary.

## 3. An all-rank obstruction for projective reflections

**Theorem.** Let T₀,…,Tᵣ₋₁ be distinct projective involutions acting
irreducibly on a vector space V of dimension n>1. Suppose each has
eigenspace dimensions 1 and n−1, and suppose n>2. If distant generators
commute projectively, then the group preserves a nondegenerate symmetric
bilinear form, up to the scalars used to choose the lifts.

Consequently the natural PSL(4,q), q odd, has no string C-group
representation, of any rank, in which every generator has type 1+3.

**Proof.** Normalize each lift as a reflection

    Tᵢ=I−vᵢ fᵢ,          fᵢ(vᵢ)=2,

where vᵢ is a nonzero vector and fᵢ is a linear functional. Since the two
eigenspace dimensions are unequal, distant projective commutation is
linear commutation. For distinct commuting reflections, their −1
eigenlines are different and each lies in the other's +1 eigenspace. To
justify the first assertion, if two commuting reflections had the same
−1 line, normalizing the same v would give
2v(fᵢ−fⱼ)=0 upon comparing products, so they would be equal. Hence

    fᵢ(vⱼ)=0 whenever |i−j|>1.                       (2)

The span of all vᵢ is a nonzero invariant subspace, hence equals V. The
intersection of all ker fᵢ is fixed pointwise by the group, hence is zero.
Irreducibility also forces both coefficients across every adjacent pair
to be nonzero. For example, if fᵢ(vᵢ₊₁)=0, then

    W=span(vᵢ₊₁,…,vᵣ₋₁)

is invariant: the generators on the right preserve it, and all those on
the left fix it by (2) and the assumed zero. It is nonzero and proper,
because fᵢ vanishes on W but fᵢ(vᵢ)=2. The reverse coefficient is handled
by the left-hand span.

Let E=kʳ, define π:E→V by eᵢ↦vᵢ, and define F:V→E by
F(v)=(f₀(v),…,fᵣ₋₁(v)). The preceding paragraph says that π is surjective
and F is injective. Thus C=Fπ has ker C=ker π. Its matrix is tridiagonal,
with diagonal entries 2 and both off-diagonal entries across every
successive pair nonzero. Choose nonzero weights dᵢ recursively so that

    dᵢ fᵢ(vᵢ₊₁)=dᵢ₊₁ fᵢ₊₁(vᵢ).

Then D=diag(dᵢ) is nonsingular and DC is symmetric. Its radical is
ker C=ker π, so it descends to a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form B
on V. By construction,

    B(vᵢ,v)=dᵢ fᵢ(v),       B(vᵢ,vᵢ)=2dᵢ.

Expanding B(Tᵢx,Tᵢy) with these identities gives B(x,y). Thus every
reflection preserves B. This proves the theorem for every rank r. ∎

This result addresses a genuine all-rank configuration; it does not infer
a higher-rank exclusion from the rank-five calculation. It is also a
standard style of reflection-form argument, so no novelty is asserted.

## 4. Mixed signs when the entire endpoint action is irreducible

**Theorem.** Suppose T₀,…,T₄ are normalized lifts of a rank-five string
C-group generating set, and the joint action of T₀,T₁,T₃,T₄ on k⁴ is
irreducible. Suppose neither endpoint adjacent pair commutes projectively.
Then all five matrices preserve a common nonzero bilinear form up to
individual scalar multipliers. Thus these generators cannot generate the
natural PSL(4,q), for any odd q.

Unlike Proposition 2, this theorem allows every choice of signs in the
four cross commutators. It does not assume characteristic-zero or
semisimplicity of a finite-group algebra.

**Proof of the endpoint normal forms.** Let A be the algebra generated by
T₀,T₁. Each of T₃,T₄ normalizes A, sending its two generators to signs of
themselves. The sum of the simple A-submodules of V is therefore invariant
under all four endpoint matrices. This sum is nonzero (choose a minimal
nonzero A-submodule), so irreducibility of the joint action makes V a
semisimple A-module. Its isotypic components are permuted transitively by
T₃,T₄; otherwise an orbit sum would be a proper joint invariant subspace.

As proved in Proposition 2, simple A-modules have dimension at most two.
They cannot all have dimension one, since then T₀,T₁ would commute
linearly. Thus all simple constituents have dimension two. On any such
constituent, choose

    L₀=Z=[[1,0],[0,−1]],
    L₁=[[a,b],[−b,−a]],       a²−b²=1,
    K=X=[[0,1],[1,0]].

Here b≠0: otherwise the constituent is reducible. This normalization is
possible because, before a diagonal basis change, both off-diagonal
entries of L₁ are nonzero; choose their ratio using a square root in k.
We have KLᵢ=−LᵢK. Also a≠0: if a=0, the two left generators anticommute
on this constituent and on every sign twist of it, hence on V. That
would make them commute projectively, contrary to hypothesis.

The isomorphism class of this two-dimensional simple representation is
determined by tr(L₀L₁)=2a. Twisting both signs preserves it, with
intertwiner K. Twisting exactly one sign changes a to −a, giving a
different class because a≠0. Consequently exactly two normal forms are
possible in dimension four:

    (I)  T₀=L₀⊗I,       T₁=L₁⊗I;
    (II) T₀=L₀⊗I,       T₁=L₁⊗Z.

In (I), V is isotypic for A, of multiplicity two. Each right generator
is

    Tⱼ=K^εⱼ⊗Rⱼ,       εⱼ∈{0,1}, Rⱼ²=I, j=3,4.    (3)

Indeed its two cross signs must be equal, by the nonzero trace 2a; the
intertwiner on the simple factor is respectively I or K. Uniqueness up to
scalar of that intertwiner gives (3) by Schur's lemma.

In (II), the two inequivalent constituents have multiplicity one. An even
number of negative cross signs preserves each constituent; an odd number
interchanges them. The same intertwiner calculation gives (3), where Rⱼ
is respectively diagonal or off-diagonal in the displayed Z basis.
A diagonal involution Rⱼ is scalar or ±Z. It commutes projectively with
every diagonal or off-diagonal matrix. Since the K factors commute, a
diagonal R₃ or R₄ would make T₃,T₄ commute projectively. Therefore both
R₃,R₄ are off-diagonal in (II). In both forms they are nonscalar, because
a scalar Rⱼ would also make the right pair commute projectively.

**Proof of the form obstruction.** Both Lᵢ preserve Q=Z, and K preserves
Q up to the scalar −1. Every two-dimensional matrix preserves the
alternating form J=[[0,1],[−1,0]] up to its determinant.

First suppose ε₄=1. Diagonalize R₄ as Z. The middle generator commutes
projectively with

    T₀=Z⊗I,           T₄=X⊗Z.

The block calculation in Lemma 2 of psl4_theory.md says that T₂ preserves
F=Z⊗J up to scalar, provided [T₀],[T₂],[T₄] are independent; that
independence follows from the intersection property. The four endpoint
matrices preserve F as well. This is immediate in (I). In (II), T₁ has
an additional two-dimensional factor conjugate to Z, which still preserves
J up to its determinant. This treats ε₄=1 for both normal forms and
either value of ε₃.

Now suppose ε₄=0 and diagonalize R₄ as Z. The middle generator commutes
projectively with Z⊗I and I⊗Z, so the monomial argument in Lemma 1 of
psl4_theory.md applies. In form (I), let Q_R be the nonzero diagonal form
preserved by R₃,R₄, as in that lemma. Its four possible forms are

    Z⊗Q_R,   J⊗Q_R,   Z⊗J,   J⊗J.                  (4)

Each is preserved up to scalar by the endpoints, including the K factor
in T₃ when ε₃=1.

For form (II), first rescale the second-factor basis so R₄=X; this keeps
the old Z diagonal. Then diagonalize R₄. In the resulting basis, the old
Z is X, while R₃ anticommutes with X. Hence

    R₄=Z,       R₃=[[d,e],[−e,−d]],       d²−e²=1.

Both R₃,R₄ preserve Q_R=Z, and the additional factor X in T₁ preserves
both Q_R and J up to scalar. Therefore all four forms in (4), with
Q_R=Z, are again endpoint similarities. The monomial argument selects
one which is also a T₂ similarity. Every selected form is nonzero.
This completes the proof. ∎

The normal-form argument also gives a useful restriction: in an
irreducible endpoint action with noncommuting adjacent pairs, the two
columns of the cross-commutator sign matrix have the same parity. Mixed
column parities force the endpoint action to be reducible.

## 5. A finite all-rank bound from projective elementary abelian groups

**Lemma.** Let an elementary abelian group E of rank k embed in PGL(V),
over an algebraically closed field of odd characteristic. The commutator
form of its lifts has even rank 2m. Put r=k−2m. Then

    dim V ≥ 2^m(r+1).                                 (5)

**Proof.** Normalize lifts of a basis of E to square to I. Their
commutators are signs, defining an alternating bilinear form on E over
F₂. A symplectic basis for its nondegenerate quotient produces m pairs
of anticommuting involutions, with distinct pairs commuting. The algebra
of these pairs is a tensor product of m copies of M₂(k). Its irreducible
module therefore has dimension 2^m.

The r-dimensional radical has commuting central lifts. They act by
characters on the multiplicity space; simultaneous diagonalization is
valid because the lift group is a finite 2-group and the characteristic
is odd. Let s be the number of distinct such characters occurring. Their
ratios to one fixed character must separate the radical: otherwise a
nonidentity element of the radical would act by a scalar on all of V,
contradicting faithfulness of the projective representation. At most s−1
characters of an elementary abelian group can span a character space of
dimension s−1, so r≤s−1. Each character requires at least one
2^m-dimensional constituent. This proves (5). ∎

For dim V=4, (5) excludes k≥5. If k=4, it forces m=2 and r=0; thus every
projective E₂⁴ in PGL₄ has a nondegenerate lift commutator form and acts
irreducibly. In a rank-d string C-group, the generators with even indices
form an elementary abelian group of rank ceil(d/2), by the string and
intersection properties. Consequently every string C-group embedded in
PGL₄ in odd characteristic has

    d ≤ 8.

This restricts a remaining all-rank investigation to ranks five through
eight. It does not exclude ranks six, seven, or eight, and it should not
be confused with the conjectured sharper bound four for PSL(4,q).

## 6. Reducible mixed signs: completing the semisimple endpoint case

**Theorem.** In the setting of Theorem 4, replace irreducibility of the
joint endpoint action by complete reducibility. The conclusion remains
valid: all five matrices preserve a common nonzero bilinear form up to
scalar. Therefore a rank-five string C-group generating the natural
PSL(4,q) must have a nonsemisimple joint endpoint module.

**Proof.** The irreducible case is Theorem 4. The case where every cross
commutator is +1 is Proposition 2. Suppose some cross commutator is −1
and the endpoint module is completely reducible but reducible. The same
anticommuting pair acts on every simple summand. Its determinant equation
forces the dimension of each summand to be even. Consequently the module
is U⊕W with both summands two-dimensional.

Record the four cross signs in a two-by-two matrix, with the left
generators labeling rows and the right generators labeling columns. In
dimension two, a nonscalar involution is traceless. Two commuting
nonscalar involutions are proportional, and two anticommuting involutions
can be put in the form Z,X. These observations give the following complete
list. Signs of individual block matrices do not affect any of the form
identities below.

| Negative cross signs | Consequence in the two summands |
|---|---|
| One | The two generators on that negative edge are Z,X on both blocks. The other two generators have complementary scalar/nonscalar patterns: X,I on one block and I,Z on the other, in the corresponding commuting positions. |
| Two in one row or one column | The generator on both negative edges is Z on both blocks. The opposite two generators are off-diagonal. The remaining generator is nonscalar on exactly one block and scalar on the other. On the nonscalar block, the three generators opposite Z are proportional to X. |
| Two in opposite corners | Both adjacent pairs anticommute on both blocks, contradicting their projective noncommutation. |
| Three | Again both adjacent pairs anticommute on both blocks, giving the same contradiction. |
| Four | Every endpoint matrix is nonscalar on both blocks. |

Here are details of the scalar-pattern assertions. With one negative
edge normalized as Z,X, the two remaining commuting conditions force the
other left generator to be scalar or proportional to X, and the other
right generator to be scalar or proportional to Z. They cannot both be
nonscalar on the same block, because their cross sign is positive. For
the left pair not to commute projectively globally, the first choice must
be nonscalar on exactly one block; for the right pair the second choice
must be nonscalar on exactly one block. Thus their nonscalar blocks are
complementary. With two negatives in a row, the remaining left generator
commutes with both right generators. If nonscalar, it is proportional to
both of them and anticommutes with Z. If it is nonscalar on both blocks,
or scalar on both, the adjacent left pair commutes projectively globally.
This forces exactly one of each. A column is the same argument with the
roles exchanged.

For opposite-corner negatives, normalize one negative pair as Z,X.
The other negative pair must then be proportional to X,Z because its
members commute with X,Z respectively and cannot be scalar. For three
negatives, the unique positive cross pair consists of proportional
nonscalar involutions; the remaining two involutions each anticommute
with this pair and with each other. In both cases the stated adjacent
anticommutations follow on every block.

It remains to include T₂ for the three viable rows of the table. Each
row supplies a two-parameter space of endpoint similarity forms:

    one negative:                 diag(λI₂, μI₂);
    two negatives in a row/col:   diag(λX,  μX);
    four negatives:               diag(λJ,  μJ).       (6)

For the middle row, normalize one off-diagonal involution to X on each
block; the other remains off-diagonal and preserves X. The Z generator
has multiplier −1 on X; all other endpoints have multiplier +1. For the
last row every block has determinant −1, so every endpoint has multiplier
−1 on J. In the first row every block matrix is scalar, X, or Z and
preserves I₂. Thus in each row the multiplier for each endpoint agrees
on U and W.

If T₀,T₄ anticommute, put them in the common Pauli form I⊗Z,I⊗X,
where the first factor indexes U,W. A matrix commuting projectively with
both has the form B⊗P, where P is one of I,Z,X,J up to scalar. Indeed,
the four simultaneous sign-eigenspaces for conjugation by Z and X in
M₂(k) are the four Pauli lines. Rescale P and B so B²=I. In the bases
just used, P preserves the corresponding Q in (6) up to scalar; Q can
also become Z if the roles of the two Pauli matrices were interchanged
during normalization. Each of I₂,X,Z,J is a similarity form for every
Pauli matrix. If B is scalar, every diagonal weight matrix works. Otherwise
write B=[[a,b],[c,−a]], a²+bc=1, and choose a nonzero pair λ,μ with

    λb=μc.                                           (7)

Then Bᵀ diag(λ,μ) B=diag(λ,μ), by the same identity as Lemma 1. Hence
the chosen form from (6) is a T₂ similarity as well.

If T₀,T₄ commute linearly, inspection of the viable scalar patterns shows
that at least one has eigenspace dimensions 1+3. This occurs only in the
first two rows. Thus T₂ commutes linearly with that reflection. It cannot
anticommute with the other outer generator: on the preserved
three-dimensional eigenspace, anticommuting invertible involutions would
give a contradiction on taking determinants. So T₂ commutes linearly
with both. Their joint eigenspaces have dimensions 2,1,1; the
two-dimensional space has one line from U and one from W. Diagonalizing
the appropriate X or Z within each block makes the relevant forms from
(6) diagonal, with nonzero fixed entries times λ on U and μ on W.
The two one-dimensional spaces present no constraint, since T₂ acts
there by signs. On the remaining two-dimensional space, the single
equation analogous to (7), with the two fixed nonzero entries included,
always has a nonzero solution. This gives a nonzero common form in every
case. ∎

**Consequence.** If the characteristic p divides neither
ord(ρ₀ρ₁) nor ord(ρ₃ρ₄), this rank-five endpoint module is semisimple
by Maschke's theorem, so the candidate cannot generate PSL(4,q). Indeed
the projective endpoint group is a direct product of the two dihedral
groups by the intersection property. Its lift has only an additional
2-primary scalar kernel, which does not affect divisibility by p.

Thus any still-possible rank-five representation must have a p-singular
endpoint product and a genuinely nonsemisimple endpoint action. Neither
condition by itself is asserted to be sufficient.

### Exact probe of a reducible mixed-sign family

The script [phase3_psl4_reducible_probe.g](phase3_psl4_reducible_probe.g)
constructs a deliberately narrow candidate over F₂₅. Let t have order
three and put

    T₀=Z⊕Z,   T₁=X⊕I,   T₃=X⊕[[0,t],[t⁻¹,0]],   T₄=X⊕X,
    T₂=[[2,1],[2,−2]]⊗I.

The projective generators are involutions in PSL(4,25), and all six
distant commutations hold. Their adjacent product orders are 4,6,6,3.
The exact projective group order is 28,800, whereas |PSL(4,25)| is
232,442,642,250,000,000,000. All three consecutive rank-three tuples
satisfy the intersection property. The left rank-four intersection has
order 36 rather than the required 12, so this tuple fails the intersection
property. The right rank-four intersection has the required order 12.
The checks and explicit common form are recorded in the accompanying log.

This family initially looked like an obstacle to the form argument.
It is not a counterexample: the previously overlooked form

    diag(2X,X)

is a similarity form for all five displayed matrices, with T₀ multiplier
−1 and the other four multipliers +1. Allowing that negative multiplier
is essential. Formula (7) proves the corresponding obstruction for the
whole normal-form family, beyond this finite example.

## 7. Excluding the saturated rank-eight case

This section uses the intersection property in addition to the string
commutations. It gives an all-rank improvement independent of the
rank-five form calculations.

**Lemma (projective centralizer).** The projective centralizer of an
irreducible subgroup of GL(V) is abelian.

**Proof.** If [x] centralizes the subgroup projectively, write
xhx⁻¹=χₓ(h)h for each h in the subgroup. The scalar function χₓ is a
multiplicative character. The map [x]↦χₓ is a homomorphism into an
abelian character group. Its kernel consists of linear centralizers,
which are scalars by Schur's lemma. Hence the map is injective. ∎

**Lemma (alternating center).** In a string C-group with all adjacent
generators noncommuting, no nonidentity product of alternating
generators of a contiguous parabolic of rank at least two centralizes
that entire parabolic.

**Proof.** The local observation is the following. Let r,s,t be three
consecutive generators. If rt commutes with s, then rt is central in
⟨r,s,t⟩, since r,t already commute. Writing t=r(rt) gives

    (sr)²=(st)² ∈ ⟨r,s⟩∩⟨s,t⟩=⟨s⟩.

The value cannot be s: the equation (sr)²=s would imply rsr=1 and hence
s=1. Thus (sr)²=1, so r and s commute, a contradiction. Likewise, a
single neighbor of s cannot centralize s.

Now a product of alternating generators commutes with an intervening
generator s exactly when its product of the selected immediate neighbors
does: all the other factors commute with s. If either immediate neighbor
were selected, the one-neighbor or two-neighbor observation would give a
contradiction. Every alternating generator in the parabolic has such a
neighbor, so none can be selected. ∎

**Theorem.** A string C-group embedded in PGL₄(k), with k algebraically
closed of odd characteristic and with all adjacent generators
noncommuting, has rank at most seven. In particular, any string C-group
representation of PSL(4,q), q odd, has rank at most seven.

**Proof.** Section 5 already gives rank at most eight. Suppose the rank
is eight, with generators ρ₀,…,ρ₇. The elementary abelian subgroup

    E₄=⟨ρ₀,ρ₂,ρ₄,ρ₆⟩

has rank four by the intersection property. By Section 5 its lift
commutator form is nondegenerate and its four-dimensional representation
is irreducible. Set E₃=⟨ρ₀,ρ₂,ρ₄⟩. The restriction of the commutator
form to this three-dimensional subspace has a one-dimensional radical,
generated by a nonidentity element z of E₃. A normalized lift of z has
two eigenspaces U,W of dimension two. Indeed ρ₆ pairs nontrivially with
z under the commutator form, so its lift interchanges the eigenspaces.
The lift algebra of E₃ acts irreducibly on each of U,W, with these two
simple modules inequivalent because the central lift of z has different
eigenvalues on them. Consequently the only nonzero proper E₃-submodules
are U and W.

Let H=⟨ρ₀,…,ρ₄⟩. Both ρ₆ and ρ₇ centralize H projectively. If the
natural action of H were irreducible, the projective-centralizer lemma
would force ρ₆,ρ₇ to commute, contrary to hypothesis. Thus H has a
nonzero proper invariant subspace, which must be U or W because it is
E₃-invariant. The lift of ρ₆ projectively centralizes H and interchanges
U,W, so the other subspace is H-invariant as well. Every lift in H
therefore commutes linearly with the lift of z. Hence z centralizes H.

But z is a nonidentity product of ρ₀,ρ₂,ρ₄, contradicting the
alternating-center lemma for this rank-five parabolic. This excludes
rank eight and proves the theorem. ∎

All adjacent generators in a string C-group generating a nonabelian
simple group do fail to commute: a commuting adjacent pair would split
the string into two commuting subgroups with trivial intersection,
making the whole group a nontrivial direct product. This verifies the
extra hypothesis in the PSL(4,q) consequence.

## Scope still unresolved

Theorem 6 removes every completely reducible endpoint action, including
all mixed signs. Nonsemisimple endpoint extensions remain unresolved.
The all-rank reflection theorem does not cover mixed collections of 1+3
