# Comparing marked coset geometries through a finite set of products

This theorem isolates the general mechanism behind the all-modulus tensor
construction. The group and its exceptional local subgroup may be infinite.
If the other maximal marked subgroups are finite, only a finite set of
products needs a faithful comparison and an exact exceptional-membership
test. Finding that membership test remains a separate mathematical problem.

## 1. The comparison theorem

Let a group G be generated by a finite indexed family (r_i), i in I, where
|I| is at least three. For J contained in I put G_J=<r_j:j in J>, including
G_empty=1. Write P_i=G_(I minus {i}), fix one exceptional type e, put
E=I minus {e}, and set

    B = union of P_i over i != e,
    W = BB = {ab : a,b in B}.

The set B contains 1 and is closed under inverses, though it need not be a
subgroup. In particular B is contained in W. Let q:G -> H be a surjective
homomorphism, and mark H by the images of the r_i. Suppose:

1. q is injective on W.
2. For every w in W,

       q(w) belongs to q(P_e) if and only if w belongs to P_e.       (E)

**Theorem.** The marking of G has the full subgroup intersection property
if and only if the marking of H does. The family of maximal parabolics of G
has typed coset Helly if and only if the corresponding family in H does.
These two equivalences are independent; neither property is assumed to
imply the other.

Here full subgroup IP means G_A intersection G_C=G_(A intersection C)
for all A,C contained in I. Typed coset Helly means that every pairwise
intersecting family of right cosets P_i g_i, with distinct types i, has a
common element. No finiteness, centrality or abelian-kernel assumption is
made on G, H or P_e. The generators need not be involutions for this theorem.

If all P_i with i != e are finite, W is finite and both hypotheses concern
this finite set. Condition (E) nevertheless needs an exact test against the
whole image q(P_e), not membership in an arbitrarily truncated search.

The injectivity hypothesis is sufficient rather than necessary. It must not
be confused with the weaker statement that q is injective on each P_i
separately: two products from different protected subgroups can still become
equal in a quotient.

## 2. Proper subgroup membership and intersections

Every properly indexed marked subgroup G_J with J != E lies inside some
P_i with i != e. Indeed, a proper J either equals E, or omits an index
other than e. Therefore, for every proper J and every b in B,

       q(b) belongs to q(G_J) if and only if b belongs to G_J.       (M)

For J=E this is condition (E), since B is contained in W. In every other case,
an element of q(G_J) has a lift a in G_J contained in B. Equality q(a)=q(b)
forces a=b by injectivity on W. This proves (M) without an IP assumption.

Consider two proper index sets A,C. Unless A=C=E, at least one of A,C
omits a protected index, so its marked subgroup lies in B. Suppose it is
G_A. An element of q(G_A) intersection q(G_C)
has a lift a in G_A; (M) puts a in G_C. Thus q identifies the intersection
G_A intersection G_C bijectively with the intersection of the two images.
It also identifies G_(A intersection C) with its image, since this subgroup
lies in G_A. Equality of these two sets is therefore equivalent upstairs
and downstairs. The indexed comparison A=C=E and every meet with G are
tautological. Distinct index sets may generate the same subgroup in a
redundant marking; those comparisons have already been included above.
These cases exhaust full subgroup IP and prove the first
equivalence.

The same argument gives an additional fact needed below. If a set of
maximal types J includes a protected type j_0 != e, then q induces a
bijection

       intersection_(j in J) P_j  ->  intersection_(j in J) q(P_j). (C)

Lift an element using P_j0 and use (M) for every remaining membership test.
Injectivity holds because P_j0 is contained in B. No prior equality between
this intersection and a smaller marked subgroup is needed.

## 3. Parabolic products seen from a protected subgroup

For g in B and distinct types i,j, one has

       q(g) belongs to q(P_i)q(P_j)
          if and only if g belongs to P_i P_j.                    (P)

If neither type is exceptional, a factorization of q(g) lifts to a product
ab with a in P_i and b in P_j. Both g and ab lie in W; injectivity gives
g=ab. The forward implication in the other direction always follows by
applying q.

If i=e and j is protected, membership downstairs is equivalent to the
existence of b in P_j for which q(g b^-1) belongs to q(P_e). The word
g b^-1 lies in W, so (E) makes this equivalent to g b^-1 in P_e. That is
exactly membership in P_e P_j upstairs. If j=e, multiply on the left by
the inverse of an element of P_i and use the same argument. This proves
(P) for all distinct types. There cannot be two exceptional factors in
these tests.

## 4. A complete anchored criterion for typed Helly

For any family of subgroups (P_i), typed coset Helly is equivalent to the
following extension condition, for all i and all J contained in I minus
{i} with |J| at least two. Choose any j_0 in J. For every g in P_j0,

       [g belongs to P_i P_j for all j in J]
       implies
       [there is k in intersection_(j in J) P_j
        such that P_i g = P_i k].                                (H)

To see necessity, the coset P_i g and all the base cosets P_j are pairwise
incident exactly under the displayed product conditions. A common element
k gives the conclusion.

For sufficiency, induct on the number of types in a pairwise incident
family. Families with at most two types have a common element by definition.
Normalize all but one coset by right multiplication by their common element's
inverse; these become the base cosets P_j. The remaining coset meets P_j0,
so it has a representative g in P_j0. Apply (H) to complete the flag, then
undo the normalization. This proves the criterion without assuming IP.

When there is only one exceptional type and |J| is at least two, a protected
j_0 in J always exists. Use that anchor in both G and H. Every candidate
representative downstairs lifts uniquely to g in P_j0. By (P), its
eligibility against all the base parabolics is the same upstairs and
downstairs. By (C), potential common representatives downstairs lift
uniquely to k in the actual intersection of the source parabolics.

Finally P_i g=P_i k is equivalent to g k^-1 in P_i. Both g,k lie in the
same protected subgroup P_j0, so g k^-1 lies in B and (M) preserves this
last membership test. Thus the conclusion of (H), as well as its premise,
is equivalent in the two groups. The second assertion of the theorem follows.

This proof uses bijections and witnesses. Finiteness is needed for an
exhaustive computational certificate, not for the abstract theorem.

## 5. Comparing many quotients without maps between them

Suppose q_a:G -> H_a is a family of marked quotient maps and each satisfies
the two hypotheses above with the same B and P_e. Then full IP and typed
Helly have exactly the same truth values in G and in every H_a. One verified
base H_a0 therefore determines the result for every member of the family.
There need not be a homomorphism from H_a to H_a0. In particular the theorem
applies to reductions modulo unrelated integers, not only multiples of a
chosen base modulus.

For involutory markings whose local groups and nontrivial panels are separately
identified, IP and typed Helly give the usual regular coset geometry. Its
thinness and residual connectivity must be supplied by that marked-group
adapter; the abstract comparison does not identify local map parameters.

## 6. Matrix certificate specialization

Suppose G is an integral matrix group and q_n its reduction modulo n. If
every entry of every matrix in W has absolute value at most C, then q_n is
injective on W whenever n>2C. For if two reductions coincide, each entry
of their difference is divisible by n and has absolute value at most 2C,
so it is zero.

For (E), suppose there are finitely many explicit tests on each w in W
with integer entries bounded in absolute value by D. Assume:

- membership in q_n(P_e) necessarily makes at least one whole test vanish
  modulo n;
- every integral passing case has an explicit witness placing w in P_e.

For n>D the necessary congruence test forces integral vanishing, hence
membership in P_e. The reverse implication is automatic under q_n. This
proves (E). The bounded tests and all passing witnesses must be complete;
checking only selected words or only necessity is insufficient.

In the frozen tensor construction, C=2 and D=4. The protected union has
172 elements, W has 1928, and the two tests are the three factor commutators
with N before and after multiplication by z. Exactly 52 pairs pass, all
with explicit signed-power witnesses. Thus every n>=5 meets both hypotheses.
The exact modulus-five IP/Helly certificate then proves the corresponding
properties of the integral tensor group and of every such reduction.
Modulus three is a separate finite case.

This recovers the all-modulus geometric transfer from one general theorem.
The prior Fibonacci argument and toroidal presentation/order bounds still
identify the local source parameters. It is not an independent proof of
those retained arithmetic and source-identification steps.

## 7. Scope and retained inputs

The proof above uses only elementary subgroup images, cosets, group
factorization and induction on a finite flag. Its tensor application retains
the complete finite certificate in phase35_L003_tensor_geometry_check.py,
the literal local groups in phase35_L003_tensor_model.md, and the standard
coset interpretation of regular hypertopes. It gives no general algorithm
for recognizing membership in an arbitrary infinite P_e and no polynomial
complexity claim.
