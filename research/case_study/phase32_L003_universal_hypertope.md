# The universal regular hypertope at the fixed parameter \(s=6\)

This is a separate written corollary to the frozen fixed-parameter geometry certificate. It does not alter that certificate's narrower statements.

## 1. Presentation and exact meaning of universality

Let \(W\) be the Coxeter group on \(r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3\) with involutory generators and pair orders dividing
\[
 (m_{01},m_{02},m_{03},m_{12},m_{13},m_{23})=(6,3,2,2,3,6).
\]
In its even subgroup put \(x=r_0r_1,\ y=r_0r_2,\ z=r_0r_3\). Define \(U_6\) by adding the four local relations
\[
 (y^{-1}x^{-2})^3=1,\qquad (zx^3)^2=1,
\]
\[
 (x^{-1}z(y^{-1}z)^2)(zx(zy)^2)=1,\qquad
 (y(zy)^2)^6=1. \tag{1}
\]
All four are even words in the involutions. Here universality means the universal marked regular object for this diagram and the ordered rank-three toroidal residues
\[
 ((3,0),(2,0),(1,1),(6,0)).
\]
It does not mean that a finite quotient constructed elsewhere equals \(U_6\).

**Theorem.** The standard coset geometry of the displayed marked group \(U_6\) is an infinite regular hypertope. Its four local residues are exactly the prescribed toroidal maps. Every marked regular hypertope with this diagram and these ordered marked residues is a quotient of this one by a generator-preserving map, injective on the corresponding maximal parabolics.

Thus the regular universal object at source parameter six is infinite, although it has infinitely many finite regular quotients retaining all four residues.

## 2. Local upper bounds force injectivity

The explicit finite marking \(Q_2\) in phase32_L003_fixed_parameter_hypertopes.md satisfies the Coxeter relations and all four words (1). Hence there is a generator-preserving epimorphism
\[
 U_6\longrightarrow Q_2.
\]
For each omitted type \(i\), the marked parabolic \((U_6)_i\) is a quotient of the appropriate full toroidal-map group. The exact local substitutions into the source's equation (2.3), already displayed in the frozen geometric note, are
\[
 (u,v)=(zy,zx),\quad(y^{-1}z,y^{-1}),\quad
 (x^{-1},x^{-1}z),\quad(x,y)
\]
for \(i=0,1,2,3\), respectively. Their full group orders are
\[
 36,\quad432,\quad48,\quad108.
\]
The images of these four parabolics in \(Q_2\) have exactly these orders, as established by the literal finite certificate. Therefore every upper bound is attained and the map is injective on each maximal parabolic. In particular, no unproved faithfulness of a presented group is being used.

The quotient-transport lemma in §4 of the frozen geometric note now gives the full subgroup intersection property and typed coset Helly in \(U_6\). Its hypertope adapter gives thinness, residual connectivity, and a chamber-regular full type-preserving automorphism group. The local order comparison gives the exact marked residues.

The integer doubled marking also satisfies all defining relations of \(U_6\), and its word \((xyxz)^2\) has infinite order. Consequently \(U_6\) is infinite.

For any other regular hypertope with the specified marked residues, its four canonical involutions satisfy precisely the diagram and local defining relations used above. The defining presentation therefore maps onto its marked group. On each maximal parabolic, both sides are the same marked toroidal-map group, so the map is injective there. The resulting coset map is the stated residue-preserving quotient.

## 3. Relation to the source rotational presentation

The source's group \(P_6\) has the six rotational Coxeter relations and the four words (1). The even subgroup \(W^+\) of \(W\) has presentation
\[
 \langle x,y,z\mid x^6,y^3,z^2,(x^{-1}y)^2,
 (x^{-1}z)^3,(y^{-1}z)^6\rangle.
\]
The last two relators can equally be written \((zx)^3,(zy)^6\), as in the source. Conjugation by \(r_0\) induces
\[
 \tau(x)=x^{-1},\qquad\tau(y)=y^{-1},\qquad\tau(z)=z^{-1}=z.
\]
Let \(N\) be the normal closure in \(W^+\) of the four toroidal relators (1). We justify that it is already invariant under \(\tau\), rather than silently adding the conjugate relators.

For a given local Coxeter triangle, the corresponding torus parameter is of the regular form \((a,0)\) or \((a,a)\). This reflection invariance can be seen directly in triangular-lattice coordinates. The parameter lattice for \((a,b)\) is generated by \((a,b)\) and \((-b,a+b)\). The lattice point group is generated by
\[
 R(u,v)=(-v,u+v),\qquad S(u,v)=(v,u).
\]
For \((a,0)\), the lattice is \(a\mathbb Z^2\), so both maps preserve it. For \((a,a)\), use the basis \(v_1=(a,a)\), \(v_2=(-a,2a)\). Then
\[
 Rv_1=v_2,\quad Rv_2=-v_1+v_2,\qquad
 Sv_1=v_1,\quad Sv_2=v_1-v_2.
\]
Thus this lattice also is reflection-invariant. Equivalently, the kernel of its orientation-preserving toroidal presentation is normalized by every reflection in that local Coxeter triangle. The identification of the translation lattice with source equation (2.3) is the usual toroidal-map input already retained in the geometric note.

Choose any local reflection \(r_j\) in that triangle. Since \(r_0r_j\in W^+\), conjugation by \(r_0\) differs from conjugation by \(r_j\) by an inner automorphism from \(W^+\):
\[
 r_0 f r_0=(r_0r_j)(r_j f r_j)(r_jr_0).
\]
The middle expression preserves the local torus kernel, and the two outside factors preserve its normal closure in \(W^+\). This works also for the triangle omitting type zero. Thus \(\tau(N)=N\).

It follows that
\[
 U_6\cong (W^+/N)\rtimes\langle r_0\rangle
       =P_6\rtimes C_2,
\]
and \(P_6\) is the actual rotation subgroup of the constructed universal regular hypertope. This argument identifies the presented rotation subgroup, not the particular monomial quotient of it.

## 4. Source and status boundary

The primary source is Fernandes–Leemans–Ivić Weiss, *Hexagonal extensions of toroidal maps and hypermaps* (2018), [author repository PDF](https://ria.ua.pt/bitstream/10773/23862/1/2018DFW.pdf). Its equations (2.3)–(2.4) supply the local torus presentations, §8 supplies the eight-parameter rotational presentation, and its definition of universal hypertopes in §3 uses the diagram and full local residue relations.

This corollary answers the finiteness and geometric-existence questions for the single omitted value \(s=6\): its regular universal object is infinite, and finite residue-preserving quotients exist in unbounded orders. It does not prove a family with varying exact parameter \(s\), a complete answer to Problem 9.2, or a current-literature priority claim. Only the already frozen literal certificate is used; no new finite computation or full formalization is claimed here.
