# Root review: dimension-four rank-three form alternative

10 October 2026. **PASS for the written structural alternative and
the two direct-fold obstructions.** The accompanying JSON binds the
final proof bytes and the independent source/matrix review.

Over an algebraically closed field of odd characteristic, lifts of
the projective involutions can be normalized to square one. Projective
commutation of the endpoints then gives ordinary commutation or
anticommutation. In the commuting case, the symmetric endpoint-form
dimension is four only for four distinct one-dimensional characters,
and at least five otherwise. The middle involution fixes a symmetric
space of dimension six or seven. The intersection dimension therefore
gives the asserted form except in the balanced four-character case.

For anticommuting endpoints, their displayed normal form is valid:
one endpoint interchanges the two equal-dimensional eigenspaces of
the other. Alternating forms with both multipliers minus one have
the displayed symmetric cross block, of dimension three. A balanced
middle involution has a four-dimensional alternating minus-eigenspace.
Their intersection in dimension six is nonzero. Every resulting
form has invariant radical, so irreducibility makes it nondegenerate.
Changing the projective lifts only changes the form multipliers.

The frozen linear matrices give r1*r3=diag(-1,1,-1,-1);
the unitary matrices give a coordinate transposition. These are
reflections, and the endpoints of the first fold commute. For the
second fold the commuting endpoints include r1, a reflection, so
they cannot have four distinct joint characters. Both folds therefore
either act reducibly or preserve a bilinear form. The retained
absolute irreducibility and no-form theorem for the full ambient
groups makes either folded subgroup proper, uniformly in the field
parameters. This is a proof of failure of these constructions, not
a proof that rank-three markings do not exist.

The even-dihedral parity argument correctly disproves only the
sufficient local hypothesis of the cited rank-reduction theorem.
The separate Conder–Oliveros criterion is retained as a published
input: a finite group generated by three involutions with endpoint
product of order two, adjacent products of order at least three, and
no nontrivial cyclic normal subgroup has IP for that marking.
The stated almost-simple groups have no such normal subgroup by
their simple socle and trivial socle centralizer. The source review
records direct reading of the author's theorem statement and proof
sketch, and its later primary restatement; it does not claim access
to the full 2013 article.

No new numerical execution or Lean theorem is part of this root
review. Generation for a different triple, complete rank spectra and
publication priority remain unresolved in this note. Exploratory
generation files are excluded from the reviewed export.
