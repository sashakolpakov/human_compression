# Author checks for the general minimal-cover proof

This packet is an author-frozen candidate for independent mathematical review.
A successful replay verifies its elementary Lean portion and, when requested,
the source bytes. It does not certify the complete geometric proof.

Read `phase216_general_covers.md` first for the flag-kernel reduction and the
covering order. Then read `phase216_geometric_filling.md` for the geometric
construction and final theorem. Exact primary editions and theorem locators
are recorded in `phase216_sources.json`; the qualification of each result is
recorded in `phase216_scope.json`.

The following points were checked before the author freeze:

* The input is an actual polytope flag action. A product of elements changing
  disjoint rank sets can act trivially only if each factor does. This is not
  asserted for an arbitrary string group generated by involutions.
* The reduction to interval support uses cyclic reduction and the pure-factor
  decomposition. A singleton factor is excluded by the fixed-point-free action
  of a distinguished adjacency. No finite presentation of the target kernel
  is assumed.
* Adding generators outside the interval uses an explicit amalgam and its
  normal form. The newly asserted high-power theorem is not silently assumed
  again at each extension.
* The factor system excludes single vertices and uses cubical diameter with
  threshold one. Its gate images are either members or single vertices.
* Parallel copies of a proper parabolic coset are accounted for. Their union
  lies in a proper parabolic coset because the noncommutation graph is
  connected. This gives both inequalities in the equivariant comparison.
* Uniform projection bounds use missing hyperplane labels. The contraction
  estimate only asserts the existence of a close pair of points. Applying it
  to two separated intervals gives the required projection bound.
* Hull's Corollary 3.17 retains the original infinite generating set. It is
  not replaced by an assertion of hyperbolic embeddedness relative to an
  unspecified set. DGO Theorem 7.15(c) injects the entire designated set.
* The elementary subgroup normalizes every power subgroup, by the cyclic
  centralizer formula and DGO Corollary 6.6(b). Thus all sufficiently large
  exponents are available, including two consecutive exponents.
* The generating set includes the identity. DGO permits identity loops;
  its finite-change result also permits adding the identity explicitly.
* Normality removes the choice of base flag in the covering order. Distinct
  normal kernels are not identified by an unrooted rank-preserving isomorphism
  of regular polytopes. Cover maps themselves are not claimed to be unique.
* The original question imposes no finite-cover restriction. The stronger
  question asking for two finite minimal covers of every finite example is
  left separate.

Five new Lean theorems compile over the six frozen phase-211 theorems, with
only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. The new declarations
use the actual phase-211 `Admissible` and `IntersectionProperty` definitions.
Normal generation and consecutive-power supply are explicit hypotheses of
the formal deduction. Neither the retained geometric theorems nor the proof
of that supply is represented by a new Lean axiom.

The author replay used `--verify-primary` and verified all eight referenced
PDF hashes. A second replay in a packet containing only the two Lean sources,
the runner, source ledger, and two manuscripts passed without the PDFs or
the phase-211 export manifest. Thus the elementary replay is self-contained
apart from the specified Lean toolchain and pinned mathlib installation.
Use `--verify-primary` only when the separately acquired references are
available at their recorded paths. The packet does not need to redistribute
those PDFs to support its Lean replay.

Three altered proof files were rejected: dropping disjoint coordinate
supports, dropping separation of flags by coordinates, and replacing two
consecutive powers by exponents differing by two. These are controls on the
formal proof's use of its hypotheses, not computational tests of the
geometric theorem.

The source question is Cunningham--Pellicer Problem 5. Cunningham's later
2023 manuscript also states the expected conclusion. A bounded search of
later primary literature on 10 October 2026 did not locate a general answer;
this is not an exhaustive priority assertion. In particular, the retained
geometric theorems carry substantial mathematical content and receive full
attribution. No manuscript-compression ratio is inferred from this candidate.
