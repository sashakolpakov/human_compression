# A canonical common quotient in nilpotency class at most three

10 October 2026. Private theorem with a complete independent root review;
no gap found. Internal review is distinct from external peer review.
This is a partial repair of the common-quotient argument discussed in
[phase12_path_cocycle.md](phase12_path_cocycle.md). It proves the existence
claim uniformly in rank for one structural class. It neither repairs the
source's specified Frattini-intersection subgroup nor proves its unrestricted
claim for all finite 2-groups. Publication priority is not assessed here.

**Theorem.** Let G be a finite 2-group of nilpotency class at most three,
with a nondegenerate string C-group marking r₀,…,r_(d−1), d≥2. Then

    G/γ₃(G) ≅ G(C_d)

by an isomorphism sending the image of each marked generator to the
corresponding marked generator. In particular |G/γ₃(G)|=2^(2d−1), and
the quotient marking is a string C-group of type {4,…,4}.

Here G(C_d) is exactly the path-cocycle group, equivalently the presentation
on d involutions with distant generators commuting and all adjacent squares
central. Involutory generators force those central adjacent squares to have
order at most two. The coordinate construction proves the precise order,
nondegeneracy and intersection property of this target independently.

## 1. Marked supports and an elementary abelian quotient

Write G_I=<r_i:i∈I>. The C-group axiom says G_I∩G_J=G_(I∩J).
It also makes the marked generating family irredundant. Burnside's basis
theorem therefore gives |G:Φ(G)|=2^d. As G is generated by involutions,
G/G′ is elementary abelian; hence Φ(G)=G′. The marked images are a basis
of this elementary abelian quotient. In particular

    G′ ∩ <r_i> = 1.                                       (1)

If an element of G′ belongs to two marked parabolics whose supporting sets
intersect in at most one vertex, it is trivial by (1). This elementary
support test will be used repeatedly. It is a consequence of actual
subgroup intersection in G; no intersection property of a quotient is
presumed.

## 2. Class-three collection along a path

Use [x,y]=x^−1 y^−1 x y and right conjugation. For 1≤i≤d−1 put

    c_i=[r_(i−1),r_i]=(r_(i−1)r_i)²,
    z_i=c_i²,

and for 1≤i≤d−2 put t_i=[c_i,r_(i+1)]. A subscript outside the displayed
range means the identity whenever it occurs in a boundary formula.

The derived subgroup is abelian, because [γ₂,γ₂]≤γ₄=1. The third lower
central subgroup is central. Each endpoint conjugates c_i to its inverse,
so [c_i,r_(i−1)]=[c_i,r_i]=c_i^−2. This commutator is central; conjugating
it once more by an endpoint gives c_i²=c_i^−2, whence c_i⁴=1. Thus
z_i is central of order at most two, and both endpoint commutators equal z_i.
Similarly t_i is central of order at most two: commuting with an involution
twice gives t_i²=1 when the first commutator is central.

Distant commutation gives [c_i,r_j]=1 for j<i−2 or j>i+1. The class-three
Jacobi identity, using [r_(i−2),r_i]=1, identifies
[c_i,r_(i−2)] with t_(i−1)^−1=t_(i−1). Consequently

    [c_i,r_j] = t_(i−1)  if j=i−2,
                z_i      if j=i−1 or i,
                t_i      if j=i+1,
                1        otherwise.                      (2)

The c_i normally generate G′, because every other pair of marked generators
commutes. Equation (2), whose right sides are central, therefore proves

    γ₃(G)=<z₁,…,z_(d−1), t₁,…,t_(d−2)>.                  (3)

In particular γ₃(G) is elementary abelian. These collection facts require
no classification of finite 2-groups. The only general inputs here are
Burnside's basis theorem and the standard weight-three commutator identity.

## 3. A hypothetical relation among the edge commutators

Suppose that, for binary ε_i not all zero,

    c₁^ε₁ ⋯ c_(d−1)^ε_(d−1) ∈ γ₃(G).                    (4)

Squaring, using commutativity of G′ and exponent two of (3), gives
∏z_i^ε_i=1. Let k be its first selected edge. Then z_k belongs both to
G_{k−1,k} and to G_{k,k+1,…,d−1}, since it equals a product of later
selected z_i. Their intersection is <r_k>; equation (1) forces z_k=1.
Repeating removes every selected edge and proves

    ε_i=1 ⇒ z_i=1.                                       (5)

The element in (4) is central. Commuting it with r_j, applying (2), and
using (5) gives

    t_(j−1)^ε_(j−1) · t_(j+1)^ε_(j+2)=1.                 (6)

The first factor is supported on {j−2,j−1,j}; the second on {j,j+1,j+2}.
When a factor is defined, it is in G′. The two supports share only r_j.
The same support test forces both factors to be trivial, including when
one factor is absent at a boundary. For every selected i this yields

    t_(i−1)=t_i=1.                                       (7)

Equations (2), (5), (7) say that every selected c_i is central in G.
This is a conclusion inside G, not only modulo another lower central term.

## 4. Cut the path at the first selected edge

Let k be the first index with ε_k=1. Split the vertex set into
L={0,…,k−1} and R={k,…,d−1}. The only generators in (3) whose supports
cross this cut are z_k, t_(k−1), t_k. They are all trivial by (5), (7).
Because the remaining generators of γ₃ are central, we may group them
by their side of the cut to obtain

    γ₃(G)=Z_L Z_R,    Z_L≤G_L∩G′,  Z_R≤G_R∩G′.           (8)

All later selected c_i lie in G_R. Rearranging (4) with (8) gives
c_k=u v for some u∈Z_L and v∈G_R. Then

    u=c_k v^−1 ∈ G_L ∩ G_({k−1}∪R) = <r_(k−1)>.

Since u∈G′, equation (1) gives u=1. Now c_k∈G_R∩G_{k−1,k}=<r_k>;
again (1) gives c_k=1. This contradicts nondegeneracy, which requires
|r_(k−1)r_k|≥4. Thus the relation (4) cannot exist.

## 5. Identify the quotient and state the boundary

The images of the c_i in G′/γ₃(G) are independent involutions by the
preceding argument. They generate that quotient, so its order is 2^(d−1).
Together with |G:G′|=2^d this gives |G:γ₃(G)|=2^(2d−1).
The quotient has class at most two, retains the involutions and distant
commutations, and has central adjacent squares. Thus the defining path
presentation maps onto it. The path-cocycle model has exactly the same
order, making that marked epimorphism an isomorphism. The target's already
proved intersection property supplies the quotient intersection property;
there is no circular assumption that lower-central quotients of C-groups
automatically remain C-groups.

For the class-two path family, γ₃=1 is the correct kernel even when the
source's C=∩Φ(A_i) is nontrivial. Therefore this theorem is compatible with,
and logically distinct from, the all-rank correction in phase twelve.

The obstacle to extending this proof to arbitrary nilpotency class is exact:
(4) need no longer be central, γ₃ need not have exponent two, and commutator
equations (5)–(7) would hold only modulo deeper subgroups. The intersection
property in G cannot simply be assumed in those quotients. No unrestricted
salvage claim follows from the argument as written.

An exploratory GAP/NQ probe independently examined all 53 normal subgroups
of the universal three-generator class-three involution/string group of
order 256. There were 26 kernels inside its derived subgroup and 21
nondegenerate quotient markings. Five yielded a smaller class-two quotient;
none of those five had the intersection property. This finite diagnostic
motivated the support proof; it does not establish its uniform conclusion.

The final focused checker [phase13_common_quotient_check.g](phase13_common_quotient_check.g)
starts from universal involution/string presentations and computes their
class-three nilpotent quotients using GAP/NQ. For ranks two through six it
checks every instance of (2), central exponent-two generators (3), and the
class-two quotient order: 70 collection comparisons in groups of orders
16, 256, 4096, 65536 and 1048576. It then repeats the complete rank-three
normal-quotient diagnostic, checking all 64 pairs of subset subgroups for
each nondegenerate quotient. Thirteen of the 21 nondegenerate markings pass
IP, and all thirteen have class-two quotient of order 32. The certificate
retains the NQ implementation for these finite checks; the written theorem
uses no finite-group catalogue, NQ enumeration, or classification theorem.
