# A central-quotient adapter for rank-three string 2-groups

Private proposed rederivation, 10 October 2026. This is an alternative proof of existing results, not a novelty claim. The source is Gomi–Loyola–De Las Peñas, *String C-groups of order 1024*, Contributions to Discrete Mathematics 13(1) (2018), 1–22, DOI [10.55016/ojs/cdm.v13i1.62475](https://doi.org/10.55016/ojs/cdm.v13i1.62475). The exact targets are Theorem 3.6 (printed p. 7) and the necessity/completeness of the three relator-lift cases in Theorem 3.8 (pp. 8–9). The argument does not reproduce the order-1024 classification tables by itself.

## 1. Hypotheses and elementary inputs

Let G be a finite 2-group generated by distinct involutions a,b,c, with [a,c]=1, with the actual marked intersection property. Write L=<a,b> and R=<b,c>. Suppose m=|ab| and n=|bc| are at least 4. Thus L and R are dihedral groups of orders 2m and 2n, L intersect R=<b>, and the marking is nondegenerate. Put x=(ab)^(m/2), y=(bc)^(n/2).

Inputs retained explicitly are: finite 2-groups have central involutions; the Burnside basis theorem; elementary dihedral normal forms; and the rank-three intersection criterion (two rank-two string C-group facets with intersection <b> imply the whole rank-three IP). The last criterion can also be checked directly from the eight marked subgroups. None of these inputs presumes a quotient has IP.

## 2. The central elements in the product RL

Every element of RL is uniquely h(ab)^j with h in R and 0<=j<m. Indeed, equality between two such expressions makes a rotation of L lie in L intersect R=<b>, whose intersection with the rotation subgroup is trivial.

Suppose z belongs to Z(G) intersect RL. Commuting z with b changes the rotation exponent j to -j, so uniqueness gives j=0 or m/2. If j=0, z belongs to Z(R)={1,y}. If j=m/2, write z=hx. Since x commutes with b, h commutes with b; in R, the centralizer of b is {1,b,y,yb}. The value h=b is impossible: bx is a reflection of L which does not commute with a when m>=4. Consequently

    Z(G) intersect RL is contained in {1,x,y,yx,ybx}.                 (1)

If yx is central then y commutes with a, since x already does; similarly x commutes with c. Hence both x and y are central in G.

The final candidate ybx is never central. Both x and y lie in the Frattini subgroup Phi(G), because m/2 and n/2 are even and x,y are squares. Thus ybx and b have the same image in G/Phi(G). If ybx were central, Burnside's basis theorem would show that a,ybx,c generate G. These three involutions commute, since a and c commute, and G would be elementary abelian. This contradicts m>=4. Notice that Burnside is used on the full generating set; no assertion about ranks of arbitrary direct factors is needed.

This proves the source's useful central-product obstruction, with a short Frattini argument replacing its final central-candidate exclusion.

## 3. Central quotient with actual IP

**Theorem A.** Under the hypotheses of Section 1, there exists a central subgroup N of order two such that the images of a,b,c form a rank-three string C-group in G/N.

If x is central, choose N=<x>. The quotient L/N remains a rank-two dihedral string C-group (its rotation order is m/2>=2), and R embeds because N intersect R=1. Since N lies in L, the images of L and R meet in the image of L intersect R=<b>. Thus the rank-three criterion applies. The case of central y is symmetric.

Otherwise choose any central involution z of G and N=<z>. Formula (1) and the two subsequent exclusions show z is outside RL. Both facets embed in G/N. If lN=rN with l in L and r in R, then r^-1 l belongs to N intersect RL, which is trivial; hence l=r belongs to <b>. Again the rank-three criterion gives actual IP and all three marked generators remain nontrivial and independent.

This proves precisely the nondegenerate statement of source Theorem 3.6, including the possibility that the resulting quotient becomes degenerate. It does not assert that an arbitrary central involution is safe when x or y is central.

**Boundary for iteration.** A degenerate rank-three string C-group is the direct product C2 times a rank-two dihedral group. Unless it is C2^3, quotienting by the central rotation involution in its nonabelian dihedral factor preserves rank three. Thus the reduction can be iterated down to C2^3. The literal unrestricted wording of source Corollary 3.7 must be read with this bottom boundary: a rank-three group of order 8 cannot have a rank-three quotient of order 4. This note makes no claim about whether the source's contextual convention was meant to exclude that boundary.

## 4. Central relator lifts and the three cases

Let Q=<a0,b0,c0 | Coxeter relations, R_1,...,R_s> be a finite marked rank-three string C-group, and let G=<a,b,c> be a marked central extension with kernel <z> of order two mapping to Q. Assume the lifts are involutions, a and c commute, and G has actual IP. The quotient Q may be degenerate.

Each adjacent product has either the quotient order or twice that order. The two adjacent orders cannot both double: in that event z is the halfway rotation in both L and R, so belongs to their intersection <b>, yet a nontrivial rotation in L cannot equal the reflection b. Therefore there are exactly three possible order patterns: the left order doubles, the right order doubles, or neither doubles.

For every defining relator R_i of Q, its lift in G is either 1 or z. Form a presentation P by adjoining z, imposing z^2=1 and centrality, the actual lifted Coxeter relators, and those choices R_i=z^(epsilon_i). Then P maps onto G, while P/<z> is Q. Therefore |P|<=2|Q|=|G|, so the map P->G is an isomorphism. In the first two patterns, the doubled adjacent product explicitly expresses z as its halfway rotation, permitting elimination of z. In the third pattern, at least one additional relator must lift to z: otherwise all defining relations of Q hold in the generating tuple a,b,c, giving a surjection Q->G, contrary to their orders. Use that relator to eliminate z instead.

These are exactly the three mechanisms in source Theorem 3.8/Table 1. This is a completeness statement for extensions that already have the desired order and IP. It does **not** say every choice of the epsilon_i succeeds. An enumerator must still certify the presented group order, actual IP, and marked-isomorphism deduplication. The order sandwich also depends on retaining a complete presentation of Q, not merely selected relations.
