# Generation of a fiber product with coprime kernels

Let f:A→D and g:B→D be homomorphisms of groups. Their fiber product is

    P = {(a,b) in A×B : f(a)=g(b)}.

It is a subgroup of A×B, since the compatibility equation is preserved
by multiplication and inversion. We do not require either map to be
onto D. Throughout the main statement their kernels are finite.

**Theorem.** Suppose the orders of ker(f) and ker(g) are coprime. If a
subgroup H≤P projects onto both A and B, then H=P.

**Proof.** Put m=|ker(f)| and n=|ker(g)|. We first show that H contains
every element (1,b) with b∈ker(g). Since gcd(m,n)=1, choose integers
r,s with rm+sn=1. The m-th power map on ker(g) is onto: for b in that
kernel, take z=b^r and use b^n=1 to obtain z^m=b. This argument does not
require the kernel to be abelian; it takes place in the cyclic subgroup
generated by b.

Surjectivity of the second projection of H supplies (a,z)∈H. Compatibility
gives f(a)=g(z)=1, so a∈ker(f) and a^m=1. Taking an m-th power in H gives

    (a,z)^m = (1,b) ∈ H.

Now take an arbitrary (a,b)∈P. Surjectivity of the first projection supplies
(a,b')∈H. Both pairs are compatible, whence g(b')=f(a)=g(b). Consequently
b'⁻¹b∈ker(g), and the preceding paragraph puts (1,b'⁻¹b) in H. Multiplying
it by (a,b') gives (a,b). Thus P≤H. The opposite inclusion is a hypothesis,
so H=P. ∎

**Corollary (paired generators).** Let (a_i) and (b_i), indexed by the
same set I, generate A and B respectively. Suppose f(a_i)=g(b_i) for
every i, and that the two kernel orders are coprime. Then the pairs
(a_i,b_i) generate P.

**Proof.** Their generated subgroup lies in P because every generator is
compatible. Its projection to A contains all a_i, so is A; its projection
to B is B by the same argument. The theorem applies. ∎

The theorem also applies separately to any specified pair of parabolic
subgroups, with f and g restricted to those subgroups. Their restricted
kernels are subgroups of the full kernels, so their orders are still
coprime. This statement requires that the chosen local paired generators
generate both local factors. It does not by itself prove an intersection
property or identify a geometric covering.

## Attribution and formal correspondence

The power argument and the resulting coprime subdirect-product fact are
classical finite-group reasoning. No historical priority is asserted.
This file extracts and formalizes a complete reusable step in the
reviewed cover constructions. It replaces the special affine-vector
power calculation by an argument valid for arbitrary coprime kernels.

`formal/CoprimePullback.lean` defines the actual subgroup P and proves:

| Declaration | Written argument verified |
|---|---|
| `snd_kernel_mem` | The complete kernel-isolation argument using a coprime root and an m-th power |
| `eq_fiberProduct_of_coprime` | The whole theorem, including both surjectivity hypotheses and the compatibility inclusion |
| `closure_paired_generators` | The whole paired-generator corollary, including generation of each projection |

The formal theorem uses `Nat.card` and is slightly stronger: it does not
assume finiteness explicitly. Under Lean's convention an infinite type
has `Nat.card` zero. Coprimality then forces the other kernel to have
cardinality one, and the same formal power argument remains valid. The
finite-kernel statement above is an immediate instance, with exactly the
same subgroup, compatibility and surjectivity conditions. The proof is
not a finite test, a bound on the order of a model, or a theorem that
assumes the desired subgroup equality.

The local-parabolic specialization in the paragraph after the corollary
is a written application of the general theorem, not an additional Lean
declaration in this file. Recognition of a specific polytopal cover,
the exact marked quotient and all geometric correspondences remain
separate obligations. Existing cover certificates retain their scopes.

The pinned compiler records the axioms of all three declarations. They
use only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, with no unproved
placeholders or native-evaluation axioms. Independent statement/proof
review and public admission remain separate from an author's compile.
