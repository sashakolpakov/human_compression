# The complement presentation and the universal local completion

10 October 2026. Author theorem package, independent review pending. The group-presentation and graph arguments below are written proofs. There is no new Lean claim, length measurement or historical-priority claim. Exploratory family computations are excluded from this package.

The universal complement presentation has a direct interpretation in the existing prescribed-facet framework. Its nonfree factor is the kernel of the universal group with those local groups. This interpretation removes the coprimality assumption from the finite-kernel existence criterion; the earlier Sylow normalization remains a useful direct construction in its stated scope.

## 1. Exact setting

Let G be a finite group generated by involutions r0,r1,r2,r3 satisfying the string commutations. Put

```text
A=<r0,r1,r2>, B=<r1,r2,r3>, C=<r1,r2>, I=A∩B.
```

Assume A and B are rank-three string C-groups with these markings. No assumption that G itself has the intersection property is made. No assumption that C is normal in I, that I/C has order two, that G is a 2-group, or that a proposed kernel is abelian is made.

The notation Cg denotes a right coset and G acts on these cosets on the right. Define the universal local completion and its natural epimorphism by

```text
J=A *_C B,
W=J / <<[r0,r3]>>_J,       π:W→G,       K=ker π.       (1)
```

The amalgam uses the actual common marked subgroup C and the complete multiplication/defining relations of A and B. Thus (1) includes every local relation, not only local orders. Each local factor embeds in W: its composite map to G is its original injective inclusion. Write i_A and i_B for the two lifts in W of i∈I. They can differ outside C.

The universal complement presentation is

```text
U=< t_(Cg) (Cg∈C\G) |
    t_(Cr3r0g)^(-1) t_(Cr0g) t_(Cg)^(-1) t_(Cr3g)=1
    for every g∈G >.                                    (2)
```

Repeated or oppositely oriented cells may be included; they impose the same normal closure. Formula (2) is exactly the convention in the reviewed phase83 manuscript. Its required Section 5 conjugator correction remains in force when that older certificate is used; nothing here changes it.

## 2. The graph and its path convention

Construct a bipartite graph with vertex sets A\G and B\G and an edge e_(Cg) for each coset Cg. Orient this edge from the B-vertex Bg to the A-vertex Ag. Distinct C-cosets may give parallel edges. The graph is connected because A and B generate G.

For a translate g define a=Cg, b=Cr3g, c=Cr0g and d=Cr3r0g. Since r0∈A, r3∈B and r0r3=r3r0, these four edges have endpoints

| Edge | Initial B-vertex | Terminal A-vertex |
|---|---|---|
| a | Bg | Ag |
| b | Bg | Ar3g |
| c | Br0g | Ag |
| d | Br0g | Ar3g |

Attach a square along the path d^(-1)c a^(-1)b, read **left to right**. It starts and ends at Ar3g. This is exactly a closed path with the relator word in (2). Let X be the resulting finite two-dimensional cell complex. Path products below mean left-to-right traversal.

Choose a spanning tree T in its one-skeleton and root it at the A-vertex A. For a vertex v, let p_v be the unique tree path from A to v; put h_v equal to its edge word. Let s_e denote the based loop p_(initial e) e p_(terminal e)^(-1). For tree edges s_e=1. For other edges these are the usual free generators of the graph's fundamental group. In every case

```text
t_e = h_(initial e)^(-1) s_e h_(terminal e).             (3)
```

This is an invertible free-group change of generators: the edge generators are replaced by the |T| tree-edge generators together with the non-tree s_e. Each h_v is a word in tree-edge generators only. In any closed path the h factors telescope. Hence the square relations become precisely the based square relations in the s_e, conjugated by a tree word; those conjugations do not change their normal closure.

Consequently

```text
U ≅ F_(|G:A|+|G:B|-1) * π1(X,A),                       (4)
```

and, more concretely, imposing t_e=1 for every e∈T in the original presentation (2) gives π1(X,A). Thus a displayed list of original edge generators gives the claimed nonfree factor only after verifying that its edges are a spanning tree. An abstract free-product decomposition with a matching free rank is not substituted for that verification.

## 3. Identification of the nonfree factor

**Theorem 1.** There is an isomorphism π1(X,A)≅K. Under the resulting isomorphism (4), every defect

```text
δ_i=t_C^(-1)t_(Ci),  i∈I,                              (5)
```

belongs to the K factor and maps to

```text
e_i=i_B^(-1)i_A ∈ K∩BA.                                (6)
```

The isomorphism is relative to the chosen rooted tree. The identities (5)–(6) fix its convention, not merely the order of these elements.

**Proof.** Use the standard normal-form theorem for an amalgamated free product: J acts on the tree with vertices the left cosets J/A and J/B and edges J/C, with edge jC joining jB to jA. Let N0 be the kernel of J→G. It acts freely on this tree because it meets every conjugate of either factor trivially. Its quotient is the stated bipartite graph: identify the image of jA with Aπ(j)^(-1), and likewise for B and C. The free deck action is on the left. A lifted loop starting at A ends at nA for a unique n∈N0. With left-to-right path products its deck element is multiplicative: lifting a second loop after the first multiplies its deck element on the right. This identifies the graph's fundamental group with N0.

The relator [r0,r3]=r0r3r0r3 lies in N0. Its conjugates by J give, in this quotient graph, exactly the square loops just attached, with appropriate paths to the basepoint and possibly reversed orientation. To see the underlying loop directly, at g=1 the square traverses the four edges with cosets Cr3r0, Cr0, C and Cr3; its alternating local moves are r0,r3,r0,r3. Translating by g gives the other cells. Choosing one lift in J for each g∈G accounts for all J-conjugates; changing the lift conjugates the based relator inside N0. Van Kampen therefore gives

```text
π1(X,A) ≅ N0 / <<[r0,r3]^j : j∈J>>_(N0)
         = ker(W→G)=K.
```

For the defect convention, the two edges C and Ci have the same endpoints A,B because i∈I. Thus (5) is a loop at A, so its tree words cancel and it lies in the K factor of (4). Lift the first edge C^(-1) from A to B. The lift of the next edge Ci beginning at B is i_B^(-1)C, since i_B∈B, and ends at i_B^(-1)A. This endpoint is e_i A, where e_i=i_B^(-1)i_A∈N0. Hence the loop has deck element e_i. Passing from J to W proves (6). ∎

The graph normal-form theorem and van Kampen are standard retained tools. The proof specifies their exact application, including all stabilizers and cells; no classification of the presented group is imported.

## 4. An exact criterion for arbitrary finite kernel classes

Let F be a nonempty class of finite groups closed under isomorphism, subgroups and finite direct products. Examples are finite odd-order groups, finite p-groups for a fixed prime p, finite solvable groups, finite groups of exponent dividing a fixed integer, and the class of all finite groups.

**Theorem 2.** The following are equivalent.

1. There is a finite regular polytopal marked cover H→G whose kernel belongs to F and whose restrictions on A and B are injective.
2. There is a homomorphism K→L, with L∈F, for which e_i has nonidentity image for every right coset Ci≠C in C\I.
3. For each Ci≠C in C\I, δ_i has nonidentity image in some finite group belonging to F under a homomorphism from U. Different cosets may initially use different groups.

There is no coprimality condition on |G| or on the kernel order.

**Proof.** Any cover in (1) receives the unique marked epimorphism W→H, because its two ends are isomorphic to the exact prescribed local groups and it satisfies the outer commutation. Its covering kernel is an image of K. If e_i dies, the two lifts of i in the ends of H coincide. The intersection property and injectivity on the ends then force i∈C. This proves (2).

Conversely let M be the kernel of the map in (2), replacing L by its image if necessary. M is normal in K, has finite index with quotient in F, and contains none of the e_i for Ci≠C. It need not be normal in W. Form its W-core

```text
M0=∩_(w∈W) M^w.
```

Since M is K-normal and W/K=G is finite, at most |G| conjugates are needed. K/M0 embeds in the finite direct product of the K/M^w and thus belongs to F. Put H=W/M0. It is finite, maps onto G, and is injective on both local factors since K meets them trivially.

An element of the intersection of the two ends of H projects to i∈I. The two uniquely determined lifts coincide exactly when e_i∈M0. That is excluded outside C. The end intersection in H is therefore exactly its marked C. Its ends are the given rank-three string C-groups; the standard rank-four recursive intersection criterion proves the full intersection property. This proves (1).

By Theorem 1 and the free-product splitting, a homomorphism from K to L extends to U by killing the free factor, with δ_i mapping to e_i. Conversely any homomorphism from U restricts to K. To separate all the finitely many e_i simultaneously, take the product of their individual separating homomorphisms. Its target, and the image of K in that target, belong to F. This proves the equivalence with (3). ∎

For I=C⊔Cz, only one defect is needed. Thus, in particular, δ_z^(2^a)=1 in U excludes every finite odd-kernel cover with injective ends, even when G is not a 2-group. More generally, any one defect lying in the intersection of all kernels of homomorphisms from U to groups in F excludes such covers. These conclusions concern only the exact local-cover scope; they do not exclude covers that enlarge either end.

## 5. When a finite completion gives a complete classification

**Corollary 3.** If one locally faithful regular polytopal cover of G exists, W itself is a string C-group. If K is also finite, W is finite, and every locally faithful regular polytopal marked cover of G is a quotient W/N for a W-normal subgroup N≤K satisfying

```text
N ∩ E={1},       E={i_B^(-1)i_A : i∈I}=K∩BA.            (7)
```

This is a classification of every cover in that local scope, not just of quotients of an arbitrarily chosen finite cover.

**Proof.** Map W onto the given cover. The map is injective on each local factor. An element in the intersection of the local factors of W has image in the cover's medial group; injectivity on the first factor identifies it with the corresponding common marked element. Thus the ends of W meet in C and recursive recognition proves its intersection property. This argument also applies when W is infinite.

Every locally faithful marked cover is a quotient of W over G, so its kernel N lies in K. Every such N automatically meets the ends trivially. Any element ba in K∩BA has π(b)=i^(-1), π(a)=i for some i∈I. The local injectivity of π gives ba=i_B^(-1)i_A=e_i. This proves the equality for E. The end-intersection argument in Theorem 2 proves exactly condition (7) for the quotient to be polytopal. If K and G are finite, so is W. ∎

In particular, an exact presentation isomorphism U≅F_T*L in which the displayed original tree-edge generators generate precisely F_T identifies K with L after those generators are killed. One must verify the explicit quotient, including any images of the defect words. This permits the phase92 calculation, once separately frozen and reviewed, to supply a finite K; it is not asserted here as an admitted premise. This manuscript makes no Γ2 completion-order or complete-cover-count claim based on an unfinished calculation.

## 6. Uniform interfaces for the classified 2-power families

There is a parameter theorem available before any further cell elimination. Retain Cunningham–Pellicer, *Tight chiral polytopes*, arXiv:2009.04566v1, Table 2 and Theorems 4.8–4.10 as the exact constructions of Γ2,Γ3,Γ4. Let q=2^β with β≥5 and either sign independently. Their endpoint orders (p,r) are respectively (8,8), (q/2,q/2), (8,q/2); the dual of Γ4 swaps p and r. Retain Proposition 4.2 and its identified chirality subgroup: X(P)=<b^(q/2)> of order two, with the same chirality group in both local polyhedra. These are substantial source inputs, not claims to reconstruct the classification.

Let P=<a,b,c> be one of these rotation groups and x=b^(q/2). It is a central involution, since it generates a normal subgroup of order two. In the mirror mix R≤P×P write

```text
Arot=(a,a^(-1)), Brot=(b,a²b), Crot=(c,c), κ=(1,x).
```

The mirror μ swaps coordinates. By the defining chirality-kernel property, R is the twisted fiber product over P/<x>, has order 2pqr, and its first projection has kernel <κ>. The two rotation ends contain this kernel, since the source's two local chirality groups both have order two. Their intersection is the full preimage of <b> and equals <Brot,κ>, of order 2q. Their regular rank-three interpretations follow from the standard local mirror and rank-three recognition.

Form G=R⋊<μ> with the usual four marked reflections r0=μ, r1=r0 Arot, r2=r1 Brot, r3=r2 Crot. Then |G|=4pqr, |A|=4pq, |B|=4qr and |C|=2q. The full end intersection is I=<C,κ>, of order 4q. The identity a²b=a b^(-1)a^(-1) and centrality of x give Brot^(q/2)=(x,x). Therefore

```text
κ^r1=Brot^(q/2) κ,       Brot^r1=Brot^(-1),
z=Brot^(q/4) κ,          z²=Brot^(q/2)≠1.               (8)
```

Since κ centralizes R, these equations prove that z centralizes r1 and Brot, and hence C. Also z∉C, because κ∉<Brot> in the rotation intersection: equality κ=Brot^j would force b^j=1 in the first projection. Thus I=C⊔Cz. Every one of these families, at every permitted parameter, meets the single-defect hypothesis in Theorem 2. No sample computation is used in this argument.

The exact edge and vertex counts are

| Family | |C\G| | |A\G|+|B\G|−1 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Γ2 | 128 | 15 |
| Γ3 | q²/2 | q−1 |
| Γ4 and its dual | 8q | q/2+7 |

The second column is also the number of generators in (2); the third is exactly the free rank in (4). Quotienting G by a normal subgroup contained in C preserves these indices. Consequently the entire Γ3 or Γ4 coset action cannot become one parameter-independent finite action of bounded degree by that route. A repeated local cell proof could still give a uniform torsion identity, but none for those families is asserted in this package. The existing all-parameter Γ2 torsion certificate remains the reviewed phase83 theorem plus its erratum.

## 7. Attribution, reading and limits

The graph-of-groups normal form, covering-space interpretation, spanning-tree presentation of the fundamental group, van Kampen, free products and recursive string C-group recognition are classical ingredients. The exact identification of this repository's complement presentation with the prescribed-local-group kernel, its defect convention and the resulting unrestricted finite-kernel-class criterion are repository derivations here; their historical priority has not been established. The source family constructions, orders, tightness, intersection property and chirality classification retain Cunningham–Pellicer's attribution.

Neither a twelve-source admission nor a compression factor follows from this standalone theorem.
