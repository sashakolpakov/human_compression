# Endpoint halving as an exact subgroup-intersection criterion

Research draft, 10 October 2026. This note extends the odd-order marking
argument in [the 2005 construction reduction](phase24_L088_theory.md) to an
arbitrary endpoint order. It isolates the additional obstruction as a family
of subsets with a least support. The result is an abstract group theorem;
no finiteness of the group, classification of finite groups, or geometric
flag-transitivity assumption is used. Publication priority is not claimed.

## 1. The operation and the two tests

Let $(G;a,b,(r_i)_{i\in E})$ be a C-group marking of finite rank. Thus the
listed generators are involutions, generate $G$, and satisfy the full
intersection property: the groups generated by any two subsets meet in the
group generated by their common generators. Assume $[a,r_i]=1$ for every
$i\in E$. There is no assumption on $|ab|$, and the rest generators need not
form a string diagram. Put

$$a'=aba=b^a,\qquad R_J=\langle r_j:j\in J\rangle,\qquad R=R_E,$$
$$U_J=\langle a,b,R_J\rangle,\qquad
H_J=\langle a',b,R_J\rangle,\qquad
P=\langle b,R\rangle,\qquad H=H_E.$$

Conjugation is $x^a=axa$, since $a^2=1$. The subgroup $H_J$ is normalized
by $a$: conjugation exchanges $b,a'$ and fixes $R_J$ pointwise. Hence

$$H_J\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}U_J=H_J\langle a\rangle,
\qquad [U_J:H_J]\le2.\tag{1}$$

Record the subsets that recover the removed endpoint:

$$\mathcal F=\{J\subseteq E:a\in H_J\}.\tag{2}$$

This family is upward closed. It may be empty.

**Theorem.** The new marking $(H;a',b,(r_i)_{i\in E})$ is a C-group if and
only if both conditions hold:

1. $P\cap P^a=R$;
2. $\mathcal F$ is closed under binary intersections.

The ambient group here is $H$, which is normal of index at most two in
$G$. It equals $G$ exactly when $\mathcal F$ is nonempty, equivalently
$E\in\mathcal F$. For finite $E$, condition 2 means that either the family
is empty, or there is a unique subset $S\subseteq E$ with

$$\mathcal F=\{J\subseteq E:S\subseteq J\}.\tag{3}$$

Thus the removed endpoint is either never recovered, or is recovered
exactly by marked subgroups containing one least support. The second
condition is stated explicitly whether or not additional hypotheses in a
particular application already imply it.

## 2. Proof: reduce every meet to three kinds of subgroup

Every subgroup generated by a subset of the new marking belongs to exactly
one of three descriptions according to which endpoint labels it uses:

- **A:** it omits $a'$ and is $A_{\epsilon,J}=\langle b^\epsilon,R_J\rangle$,
  where $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}$ means the absence or presence of the generator
  $b$ (so $b^0$ contributes only the identity);
- **B:** it uses both endpoints and is $H_J$;
- **C:** it uses $a'$ and omits $b$, and is $Q_J^a$ with
  $Q_J=\langle b,R_J\rangle$.

Different descriptions of subsets could a priori yield the same subgroup;
the intersection proof will exclude any forbidden coincidence. All the
subgroup equalities below concern the actual subgroups of $G$.

**A with A.** This is the original intersection property.

**A with B.** Since $H_J\le U_J$, the old intersection property gives

$$A_{\epsilon,I}\cap H_J
\ \le\ A_{\epsilon,I}\cap U_J=A_{\epsilon,I\cap J}.$$

Every displayed generator on the right lies in $H_J$, so equality holds.
This is the expected common marked subgroup.

**C with C.** Conjugate the original equality
$Q_I\cap Q_J=Q_{I\cap J}$ by $a$.

**B with C.** Conjugation by $a$ normalizes $H_I$ by (1). Conjugating this
meet therefore turns it into $H_I\cap Q_J$, the A-with-B case just proved.
Conjugating its answer back gives $Q_{I\cap J}^a$, as required.

**A with C.** Both groups lie in $P$ or $P^a$. Condition 1 forces their
intersection into $R$, which $a$ fixes pointwise. The original IP gives

$$A_{\epsilon,I}\cap R=R_I,\qquad Q_J^a\cap R=R_J.$$

Their intersection is therefore $R_{I\cap J}$, the expected subgroup.

**B with B.** Put $K=I\cap J$. The original IP and subgroup containment give

$$H_K\ \le\ H_I\cap H_J\ \le\ U_I\cap U_J=U_K.\tag{4}$$

If $a\in H_K$, then $H_K=U_K$ and (4) is already equality. Otherwise
$H_K$ has index two in $U_K$ by (1), so the middle subgroup is either
$H_K$ or $U_K$. It is the latter precisely when it contains $a$, that is,
when $a\in H_I$ and $a\in H_J$. Condition 2 excludes that possibility
when $a\notin H_K$. This proves all the required meets.

Conversely, the new IP applied to its endpoint-deleted subgroups gives
$P\cap P^a=R$. Applied to $H_I,H_J$, it says $H_I\cap H_J=H_{I\cap J}$,
so whenever $a$ belongs to both it also belongs to their prescribed meet.
This proves condition 2. All new generators are involutions, and the
proved IP gives their independence; they generate $H$ by definition.
Finally, (1) with $J=E$ proves the generation and index assertions. Since
$E$ is finite, a nonempty intersection-closed upward family contains its
total intersection $S$, proving (3). The least support is unique. ∎

## 3. Odd endpoints, genuine halving, and the even full-group case

If $m=|ab|$ is odd, then

$$a'b=(ab)^2,\qquad a=(a'b)^{(m+1)/2}b.$$

Thus $a\in H_\varnothing$, so $H_J=U_J$ for every $J$ and
$\mathcal F=2^E$ with least support $S=\varnothing$. The theorem becomes
the single overlap test used in the 2005 Janko-group construction. No
parity family needs to be checked in this case.

If $H\ne G$, then $[G:H]=2$ and $\mathcal F=\varnothing$. Again only
the overlap test remains. This is the usual situation when the endpoint
operation halves the group. For finite $m$ the new endpoint product has
order $m/\gcd(m,2)$; for infinite $m$ it still has infinite order.

An even endpoint order does not force index two. For example in $S_4$ put

$$a=(12)(34),\qquad b=(23),\qquad c=(34).$$

The original marking has type $\{4,3\}$ and the IP. Its new first
generator is $a'=(14)$; the three transpositions $(14),(23),(34)$ also
generate $S_4$ and have the IP. Here $P\cap P^a=\langle c\rangle$, while
$\mathcal F=\{\{c\}\}$. The least support is nonempty. This case is
covered neither by the odd-order shortcut nor by assuming a proper
index-two group. The explicit finite checker verifies these statements.

For rank at most three, the rest-index set has at most one member.
Every upward family on such a set is intersection closed, so the single
overlap test is exact for every endpoint order in those ranks.

The support condition also cannot be dropped from the general theorem.
In $S_6$ take

$$a=(12),\quad b=(13)(24)(56),\quad c=(45),\quad d=(46).$$

The original rank-four marking is a C-group, and
$P=\operatorname{Sym}(\{1,3\})\times\operatorname{Sym}(\{2,4,5,6\})$
has order 48. Its intersection with $P^a$ is
$R=\operatorname{Sym}(\{4,5,6\})$ of order six, so the overlap test passes.
Nevertheless

$$\mathcal F=\{\{c\},\{d\},\{c,d\}\}$$

is not intersection closed. The two subgroups containing both new
endpoints and respectively $c$ or $d$ both contain $a$, whereas their
prescribed common subgroup $\langle a',b\rangle$ does not. The new tuple
still generates $S_6$ but fails exactly this pair of meets (in its two
ordered orientations). Two independently implemented standard-library permutation checkers
verify the actual subgroups. The
[separate example note](phase26_halving_filter_example.md) supplies their explicit
identifications. Rank four is therefore the least rank where the extra obstruction can occur.
This counterexample separates the two conditions; it
does not refute a published theorem or settle a documented open problem.

## 4. Why this belongs to the common torsor theory

On the regular $G$-torsor, each subgroup gives its orbit partition. The
first condition identifies the common refinement of two endpoint
parabolic partitions. It then determines every mixed meet inside those
partitions. This step is the abstract automorphism/fixed-overlap theorem
in [the Lean companion](formal/EndpointDoublingIntersections.scope.md).
That module checks the mixed meet, not the entire theorem above.

The second condition controls an index-two interval in the subgroup
lattice. Each $H_J\le U_J$ either retains or loses the distinguished
coset represented by $a$. Such local losses are compatible with every
marked intersection exactly when the supports recovering that coset
form a principal filter. This is the same least-support mechanism that
appears in the earlier [kernel criterion](phase17_k1_classification.md),
now obtained without a nilpotence or central-kernel hypothesis.

The operation changes a marking, not just the abstract group. Its overlap
and support tests concern actual distinguished subgroups; ordinary matrix
similarity or an abstract group-isomorphism test would discard that data.
Permutation matrices retain the operation as conjugation, but their
linearity by itself does not establish either required condition.

## 5. Scope and certificates

The conclusion is the full subgroup intersection property. For an arbitrary
diagram it does not by itself establish a thin flag-transitive residually
connected geometry. The separate typed-coset Helly and residue tests in
the 2005 reduction supply that bridge in its six finite applications.
Neither this theorem nor those six constructions prove an exhaustive
classification of all Janko-group geometries.

The [standalone permutation checker](phase26_halving_check.py) computes
groups and all their marked-subset intersections directly. Nine named
examples include the two $S_4$ and $S_6$ cases above, signed-permutation
groups and antipodal cube quotients in ranks three through five, and a
negative control where both conditions fail. The rank-five antipodal
quotient has least support equal to all three rest generators. The checker
also examines all 168 distinct involution triples in $S_4$ whose two
endpoints commute. Exactly 72 original triples satisfy IP, and 48 changed
markings do. The computations check 21,888 actual ordered meets, and the criterion
agrees for every qualifying original marking.
These bounded examples and the exhaustive small marking search corroborate it;
the all-group argument is the proof in §2. No classification catalogue is
used. The [saved report](phase26_halving_check.json) records the exact
executed source hash and its finite scope. These computations, the written
proof, the separate Lean mixed-meet certificate, and any novelty assessment
have distinct meanings. No new-resolution infinity marker or numerical
proof-compression ratio is assigned to this note.

## 6. Prior geometric formulation

The operation itself and its parabolic-overlap test have established
precedents. Piedade and Tranchida, *Constructing new geometries: a generalized
approach to halving for hypertopes*, [arXiv:2405.19050v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19050v1),
Lemma 4.5, identify $P\cap P^a=R$ with the simple vertex-edge graph
condition (B1) for a regular leaf hypertope. Their Theorem 4.4 proves the
geometric halving identification under (B1) and their shadow-incidence
condition (B2). Those statements and their immediate proofs have been read
in the primary manuscript; this citation is not a full-paper reduction.

The theorem above instead assumes only the original marked-group IP and
identifies the exact additional support obstruction in that setting. In
the geometric source's full hypotheses the support condition must hold,
since its theorem gives a regular hypertope and hence a C-group. This
logical implication retains their geometric theorem; it is not a new
proof of its flag-transitivity or shadow conclusions. A mere C-group need
not supply that original geometry. The $S_6$ separator therefore cannot
be used to refute their result. No priority claim for the abstract
criterion follows from this limited literature comparison.
