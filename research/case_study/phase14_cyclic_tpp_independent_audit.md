## What was checked

1. **Minimum order and TPP.** Central quotient absorption preserves the cyclic triple, TPP and excess ratio. The two-factor subgroup reduction uses the orbit bound $|C:C\cap H|\leq|P:H|$, without assuming $H$ normal. Pairwise generation gives Frattini rank two and three distinct nonzero marked images. Restriction to the appropriate maximal subgroup forces excess ratio exactly two. Distinct conjugate cyclic factors have trivial intersection because their involutions differ; this gives the lower bound four for every factor. At every equality-triple step, minimality proves generation of the indicated subgroup. It does not prove product-map injectivity.

2. **Weak embedding.** The induction applies to the image of the normal subgroup after quotienting by its characteristic fourth-power subgroup. In the exponent-four case, the central order-two quotient makes the subgroup class at most two. The class-two power formula then forces its commutators to have exponent at most two and its square subgroup to be central elementary abelian. The subsequent conjugation calculation proves that every ambient square centralizes it. No unjustified identity between iterated and ordinary powers is needed.

3. **Binary action lemma.** I checked the dimension-zero, one and two cases, the cases of equal maps or one identity in dimension three, and the rank-one cases. Distinct image lines would force a common kernel plane containing the spanning marked vectors. With a common image line, the displayed functional pairs give a fixed coordinate in each of the three nonzero choices of $(r,s)$. The commutator convention and identities producing these equations from the group were checked against the retained phase11 derivation.

4. **The marked module lemma.** A 2-subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_3(2)$ lies in a conjugate of the order-eight unitriangular group. An order-four element has a unique fixed line, so an individually fixed marked vector would be globally fixed. A nonabelian image must be dihedral of order eight; its generating involutions have product of order four, and both elements of that coset modulo its Frattini subgroup have order four. The image of $c\in ab\Phi(P)$ therefore excludes this case. An elementary abelian image implies that $\Phi(P)$ acts trivially. The lemma needs no specified GOOD/BAD matrices.

5. **Powerful coordinates and their top layer.** The ordered-coordinate bijection follows only after invoking the retained Lubotzky–Mann ordered factorization and the separately proved equality of orders. Restriction to power subgroups is injective, and the retained power-generation/factorization statements provide surjectivity. Adjacent square maps on a powerful group are linear modulo fourth powers, yielding the equivariant top-layer map. The map is applied only to a group explicitly established to be powerful.

6. **Removal of the older dependencies.** For a nontrivial normal powerful subgroup with the stated marked coordinates, the largest power layer has dimension equal to the number of largest coordinates. Dimension one makes its marked involution central. In dimension two, pairwise generation and the order-at-most-two Sylow subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_2(2)$ again make a marked vector globally fixed. Thus all three coordinate orders are equal and nonzero. This justifies the new $d(S)\leq2$ contradiction, including the separate case $S=1$. It replaces the previous small-factor classification input. The case $N=1$ is elementary abelian and is excluded by one equivariant square map, replacing the order-256 computation.

7. **The decisive two square maps.** For $N\neq1$, the powerful top layer and the marked module lemma give $[N,K]\leq N^2$. Hence $q:S/N\to N/N^2$, $sN\mapsto s^2N^2$, is well defined and equivariant. Its marked images cannot be globally fixed, so the marked module lemma applied to $S/N$ gives $[S,K]\leq N$. This makes $r:K/S\to S/N$, $kS\mapsto k^2N$, well defined and equivariant. The binary action lemma supplies a globally fixed marked source vector whose target was just proved not globally fixed. Neither map is assumed additive or multiplicative. No quadratic-polar calculation is necessary.

8. **Consequences.** I reread the odd-prime argument in [phase11_odd_nilpotent.md](phase11_odd_nilpotent.md). Its minimality reduction forces $\Phi(P)=P^p$; for odd $p$ this makes $P$ powerful, and ordered two-factor factorization contradicts the nontrivial third factor. Sylow decomposition then gives the stated finite-nilpotent corollary. I also reread §§1–3 of [phase3_order_bound.md](phase3_order_bound.md): independence of the three involutions gives $|G:G'|=8$, and flag transitivity supplies the typed coset-intersection identity that gives TPP for the three square-rotation cyclic subgroups. Their order bound is exactly $n\geq s+t+l$. The adapter includes the case of a trivial square-rotation factor; it does not infer flag transitivity from C-group IP alone.
