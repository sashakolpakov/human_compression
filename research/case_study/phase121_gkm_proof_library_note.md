# A proof library, with paid applications

Proposed accounting and intake note, 10 October 2026. This is a methodology proposal and a read-only recount at public commit `5ad49fb0566ce098fe48fcd1d56e3ca0b2c3ec8b`. It adds no mathematical result or approved length measurement.

The useful GKM idea is to retain verified reusable components, try existing interfaces first, and charge both a shared definition and every application of it. For mathematics, the components are proved lemmas with explicit hypotheses; the applications are parameter substitutions together with proofs of every required hypothesis. Correctness is an admission condition. Shortness cannot compensate for an unproved assertion.

This methodology is borrowed from the Gödel–Kolmogorov Machine project, particularly its library-plus-binding accounting and prospective transfer protocol. GKM's own documentation distinguishes measured program growth from semantic reuse and warns that low net growth can hide replacement. Its early cone document also distinguishes categorical design language from a proved universal property. We adopt those qualifications rather than importing its experimental success rates or numerical units. Exact local methodology inputs and reading scopes are bound in `phase121_gkm_method_inputs.json`.

## What an actual reusable proof component must record

A component needs a stable ID, precise statement and parameters, every hypothesis and conclusion, a proof-body span/hash, and its complete list of proof dependencies and retained external inputs. Each application needs source-contract IDs, the exact component version, parameter binding, **a proof span discharging each hypothesis**, the conclusion used, and the remaining source-specific argument. Merely citing the lemma or renaming the objects is insufficient.

The proposed phase117 fields fit this directly: `statement/interface_hash`, `parameters`, `hypotheses`, `conclusions`, `proof_body_span`, `retained_inputs`; and, for an attachment, `callee`, `parameter_binding`, `each_hypothesis_proof_span`, `used_conclusion`, `proof_dependencies`, `source_contract_ids`.

## Five separate cost questions

Fix the source intake, target statements, retained background, representation and counting convention before comparing versions. Charge every reachable shared proof body once, every application and hypothesis proof, and every required table, program, decoder and wrapper under the declared artifact boundary. A union of source-adapter spans is **not** the transitive proof dependency closure and is **not** a source's marginal proof cost.

1. **Full cost:** size of the complete library and all source adapters under that convention. Compare complete old and new versions on the same intake.
2. **Signed marginal cost:** `ΔC = C_after − C_before`. Negative means an actual reduction for that fixed task; zero alone says nothing about reuse.
3. **Gross addition:** all introduced proof, interface, adapter and infrastructure material, even if deletions offset it. Charge a generalized interface's complete new proof; a specialization certificate does not make its acquisition free.
4. **Removed material and removed duplication:** report deleted cost separately, then identify the subset that was an actual duplicated proof replaced by the shared component. Other deletions are not automatically duplication savings. In an additive encoding require `ΔC = added − removed`; otherwise report the counting/boundary residual explicitly.
5. **Prospective transfer:** after freezing a component, test genuinely new source contracts with the same correctness gates and count their complete attachments. This distinguishes a costly generalization from its eventual reuse value.

For a payback comparison, compare successful future attachments against explicit successful independent derivations under the same background and metric. Charge the whole interface acquisition plus every later binding. Failed or absent comparison proofs have no measured cost; they cannot be entered as zero or infinity. Such repayment is relative to those observed proofs, not a theorem of minimal proof complexity.

A no-sharing rendering can measure the cost of repeatedly inlining the chosen proofs. Removing a component and seeing a proof fail establishes dependence of that particular derivation; it does not prove that the result is impossible without the component or that no shorter alternative exists. Acquisition effort, search/model cost, proof length and subsequent transfer cost should remain separate columns.

Use “shared lemma,” “interface” and “dependency graph” for this proposal. A **pushout** claim requires a specified category, objects, arrows and span, followed by the existence and uniqueness of the mediating map. Shared text, a call graph or a suggestive gluing diagram is not that theorem. Existing genuinely universal group constructions may be described categorically within their proved scope; this does not automatically confer a universal property on the whole proof library.

The next concrete comparison should keep the twelve admitted contracts fixed, construct an explicit component/application ledger, replace a measured duplicated argument with a proved reusable component, discharge every application hypothesis, and independently verify the complete replacement before measuring its signed change. Phase117 is developing such a fixed-twelve analysis; its proposed supported-kernel theorem and bindings remain prospective until their proofs and accounting pass review. New intakes and conjecture results should be reported separately so that changing the task cannot manufacture a favorable compression curve.
