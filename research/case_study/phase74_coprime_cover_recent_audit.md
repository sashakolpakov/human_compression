## Proposition 1: all hypotheses and both directions

The general setting is a finite 2-group generated by four involutions with
the string commutations. Its two maximal standard ends are string C-groups;
with `C=⟨r1,r2⟩`, their intersection is `I=C⟨z⟩`, with index two
over `C` and `z` centralizing `C`. These are hypotheses, not conclusions
proved for every four-generator group.

The right-module affine convention is consistent throughout:

\[
(v,g)(w,h)=(v\cdot h+w,gh).
\]

Consequently conjugating `(0,g)` by the translation `(w,1)` gives
`(w(1−g),g)`. The proposed four lifts are involutions. Because `w` is
fixed by `r1,r2`, the vertex is exactly the translated copy of the standard
vertex end and shares the prescribed medial marking with the standard
facet. The `r0,r3` commutation is precisely
`w(1−r3)(1−r0)=0`; the other string commutations follow from the end
models and C-fixity.

Both end projections are isomorphisms. Their intersection consists exactly
of the elements of `I` fixing `w`. Since `w` is C-fixed and `I/C` has
two cosets, it equals `C` if and only if `w(z−1)≠0`. Recursive
string-C recognition then proves polytopality. The generated lift group
surjects onto the marked base and has kernel a subgroup of the finite odd
abelian module. The forward direction therefore constructs exactly the
claimed kind of finite cover.

For the converse, the cover group is a finite extension of the 2-group by
its odd abelian kernel. I checked the explicit averaging proof rather than
assuming a field-semilinear splitting statement. For a normalized left
two-cocycle `f`, summing the cocycle identity gives

\[
f(g,h)=g\cdot a(h)-a(gh)+a(g),
\qquad a(g)=|G|^{-1}\sum_x f(g,x).
\]

Multiplication by the power of two `|G|` is invertible on **any finite odd
abelian group**, including non-elementary ones. Adjusting the section
removes the cocycle. Similarly, summing a one-cocycle over a subgroup gives
`c(g)=b−g·b`; all local complements are translation conjugates because
their subgroup orders are powers of two as well.

After making the facet standard by one global translation, the vertex is
therefore a translated standard complement. The shared marked generators
force C-fixity; the outer commutation forces the displayed y-relation;
the cover's IP forces a nonzero z-displacement. Converting back to the
right convention gives the stated formulas. No faithfulness assumption on
the kernel action, vector-space structure on the kernel, or unmentioned
irreducibility hypothesis is needed.

The converse would not be justified for arbitrary even kernels by this
averaging argument. The theorem correctly keeps its odd-order restriction.

## Proposition 2: the integral and finite-field interfaces

For the right permutation module `P=Z[C\G]`, every C-fixed vector `w`
in a right G-module determines the unique homomorphism
`e_C·g ↦ w·g`. The map is well defined because changing a representative
of `Cg` multiplies on the left by an element of `C`, which fixes `w`.
This verifies the coset/action convention used in both programs.

The homomorphism sends

\[
y=e_C(1-r_3)(1-r_0),\qquad d=e_C(z-1)
\]

to the precise commutation constraint and displacement from Proposition 1.
If y vanishes, its entire G-orbit submodule vanishes. Hence the identity
`2^a d∈⟨y·g:g∈G⟩_Z` forces `2^a w(z−1)=0`. Powers of two act
invertibly on every odd abelian kernel, giving the contradiction. This
supports the full odd-abelian statement, not merely elementary abelian
kernels over the first few primes.

The finite-field converse is also valid for every **odd** prime: when the
class of `d` is nonzero, use the finite module
`F_p[C\G]/(y F_pG)` itself and the class of `e_C`. It satisfies all
the hypotheses of the forward construction. When the class vanishes,
the universal mapping argument excludes every candidate. Thus this is the
stated necessary-and-sufficient criterion, with the geometric hypotheses
of Proposition 1 still in force.

Neither a rational nullspace computation alone nor finitely many modular
rank tests would prove the integral assertion. The actual package provides
the stronger explicit integer equality, with multiplier exactly two.

## Lemma 3: why one finite identity covers every parameter

The required family end defect follows from the phase58 coordinates.
The rotational end intersection is `⟨Brot,Z⟩` of order `2q`.
The full ends share the reflection `r1`, so their intersection has order
`4q`; their medial subgroup has order `2q`. The phase58 actions
`Z^μ=Z⁻¹`, `Z^Arot=Z⁻¹` and `[Z,Brot]=1` imply that `r1`
and `r2` both centralize `Z`. Therefore the general criterion's
index-two central defect hypotheses hold with `z=Z`.

I independently checked the calculation proving normality of
`N=⟨Brot⁴⟩`. With `d₁=ε₁q/4`, reordering gives
`b^(a²)=b^(1+d₁)`. The square relation yields
`a b² a⁻¹=b^(−2−d₁)a⁻⁴`; squaring and using `a⁸=1` gives

\[
a b^4 a^{-1}
 =b^{-4-6d_1-2d_1^2}
 =b^{-4+q/2}.
\]

Here `d₁²` is divisible by `q` and `±q/2` agree modulo `q`, so the
sign disappears. The exponent `k=−1+q/8` is an involutory unit modulo
`q/4`; the reversed parameter presentation gives the same action for the
other end generator. These assertions hold at `q=32` too.

The second coordinate of `Brot⁴` is `b^(4+q/2)`, obtained by summing
the four powers of `1+d₁` in `(a²b)⁴`. Coordinate swapping therefore
acts by `m=1+q/8`. Modulo `q/4`, `k²=m²=1` and `mk=−1`.
Conjugation by the four reflections consequently has exactly the exponent
list `(m,−1,−1,m)`. This proves normality in the full group; the exact
order of `Brot` gives `|N|=q/4`, and `N≤C`.

The full group has order `256q`, so its quotient by `N` has order
**1,024**, while `|C/N|=8`. This order proof precedes the finite model.
Adding `b⁴=1` to the already exact marked presentation makes `b^q=1`
redundant and replaces each `b^(1+εᵢh)` by `b`, since `h` is divisible
by four. All remaining defining words are literally independent of the
parameters. Thus the same marked quotient presentation works for every
`β≥5` and sign pair.

Since `N` is normal and contained in `C`, the coset G-set `C\G` is
canonically the coset G-set of that fixed quotient. The marked word
`Z=[(r2r3)²,r1r2]` is preserved. Consequently the 55-term equality
verified once in the fixed coset module is the same equality needed for
every family member. No unproved assertion that “similar matrices behave
similarly” enters this transport.

## Independent verification of the fixed model and 55 terms

The supplied model gives four one-based permutations on 128 points.
The author's Python checker validates these data separately from GAP. For
this review, I also wrote a **new checker importing none of the author's
helpers**, using one-based tuple permutations, explicit group closure by a
queue, and signed sparse integer vectors. It uses no floating-point or
finite-field arithmetic, and does not use Smith normal form.

That independent calculation verifies:

* all four generators are involutions and all required nonadjacent pairs
  commute;
* their generated group has exactly 1,024 elements and acts transitively;
* `⟨r1,r2⟩` has order eight and equals the full stabilizer of point 1;
* the adjacent rotation products satisfy all the small quotient's defining
  relations, including the equality, centrality in the rotation subgroup,
  and order bound for the two extra relator values;
* the supplied z-word is exactly `[(r2r3)²,r1r2]`, lies outside `C`,
  centralizes it, and generates with `C` the order-sixteen end intersection;
* all 55 coefficients are integers, every word index denotes one of the
  four specified reflections, and the JSON terms equal the literal list
  embedded in the standalone GAP program.

The relation check gives a marked surjection from the parameter-independent
quotient onto this model. Both have order 1,024, so it is an isomorphism.
The exact stabilizer and transitivity checks identify the model with the
required coset action, including its distinguished basis vector. This is
the link from a finite array identity to the abstract module in the proof.

In the supplied point numbering the independent checker obtains

\[
y=e_1-e_2-e_{39}+e_{77},\qquad d=e_{40}-e_1,
\]

and summing all literal translated terms gives exactly

\[
\sum_{(n,u)}n(y\cdot u)=-2e_1+2e_{40}=2d.

Every one of the 128 coordinates is checked. Negative coefficients are
preserved explicitly; the implementation does not use a multiset
subtraction that could discard them. Python integers provide exact
unbounded arithmetic. The independent checker/report are included for
