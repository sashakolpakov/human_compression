## Mathematical statement and proof

For homomorphisms f:A→D and g:B→D, the formal definition is the actual subgroup of A×B given by f(a)=g(b). Multiplication and inversion preserve this equation without any commutativity assumption. Neither f nor g is required to map onto D. The theorem assumes a subgroup H of A×B contained in this fiber product, surjective projections from H onto both A and B, and coprime kernel cardinalities. It concludes equality with the whole fiber product. The desired equality is not hidden in a hypothesis.

The central argument is complete. To obtain a pure element (1,b) with b∈ker(g), choose a root z of b under the m-th power map on ker(g), where m=|ker(f)|. Coprimality makes this power map a bijection of the underlying set. It need not be a group homomorphism, and no abelian-kernel hypothesis is used. Surjectivity onto B gives (a,z)∈H. Compatibility puts a in ker(f), so its m-th power is one. Taking the m-th power of the pair isolates (1,b).

For a general compatible pair (a,b), surjectivity onto A supplies (a,b′)∈H. Equality g(b′)=g(b) puts b′⁻¹b in ker(g), and multiplication by the already isolated (1,b′⁻¹b) gives (a,b). The inverse order and multiplication order are correct for nonabelian groups.

The paired-generator corollary proves its necessary intermediate obligations. The generated subgroup is contained in the fiber product because each literal pair is compatible. Its image under either coordinate homomorphism is the subgroup generated by the corresponding coordinate markers; the two generation hypotheses therefore give the required surjectivities. The main theorem then applies. The index type may be infinite.

## Finite and general formal scope

The written main theorem assumes finite kernels. Its use of Bézout and Lagrange is classical group theory, accurately attributed without a priority claim.

The Lean theorem uses Nat.card and does not assume finiteness. This is a genuine, harmless extension. For an infinite group Nat.card is zero. If one kernel is infinite, coprimality forces the other kernel to have cardinality one, so that other homomorphism is injective. In that case the asserted fiber-product equality also follows directly from surjectivity to the opposite factor and uniqueness of a compatible coordinate. Both kernels cannot be infinite under the coprimality hypothesis.

The reviewer also read the relevant pinned mathlib source, OrderOfElement.lean, including orderOf_dvd_natCard, pow_card_eq_one′ and powCoprime. The power equivalence is explicitly an equivalence of sets, proved by Bézout in the cyclic subgroup of each element. The exponent-zero infinite case is covered by the same declaration. The author's use of its symmetry argument supplies exactly the required root exponent m. Thus the stronger formal scope does not conceal a finite-group typeclass assumption or an invalid nonabelian power homomorphism.

For local parabolics, the restricted kernels are subgroups of the original finite kernels, so their finite orders divide the original orders and remain coprime. A local application still has to prove that its actual paired local markers generate both local factors. That paragraph is a valid written specialization, but there is no separate local-parabolic declaration in this module. No intersection property, geometric cover recognition, source presentation, regularity or chirality conclusion is proved here.
