# Class-three classification: bounded literature and scope audit

10 October 2026. This is a source-status supplement to the
[parameter classification](phase18_class_three.md) and
[leading-coefficient theorem](phase18_leading_coefficient.md).
Their internal mathematical reviews do not establish publication
priority. No infinity marker is assigned.

The category matters: the repository classifies full ordered
involutory path markings with actual IP in every rank. The following
nearby literature concerns maps and must be compared using its
group convention before claiming a new rank-three result.

* Martin Škoviera's [2011 Fields Institute abstract](https://www.fields.utoronto.ca/programs/scientific/11-12/discretegeom/talks/Skoviera.pdf)
  announces class-two and class-three nilpotent-map classifications,
  jointly with Y. F. Ban, S. F. Du, Y. Liu, A. Malnič and R. Nedela.
  The one-page abstract was read; it supplies no proof for comparison.
* A. Malnič, R. Nedela and M. Škoviera,
  [*Regular maps with nilpotent automorphism groups*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2012.06.001),
  European Journal of Combinatorics 33(8) (2012), 1974–1986,
  gives a class-two classification and a decomposition separating an
  odd semistar factor. The indexed publisher abstract, introduction
  and selected definitions were read, not the complete proof. Its
  two-generator description represents orientation-preserving map
  automorphisms, with one distinguished involution.
* W. Xu, S. Du, J. H. Kwak, J. Lei and K. Shi,
  [*On Nilpotent Orientably-Regular Maps of Nilpotency Class 4*](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v29i2p55/pdf/),
  Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 29(2), P2.55 (17 June 2022),
  DOI 10.37236/7786, explicitly attributes class three to unpublished
  work of Ban and collaborators on printed page 2. The title page,
  introduction, group convention, main theorem statement and relevant
  references were inspected; the full classification proof was not
  audited. Nilpotency class refers to the orientation-preserving group.

The distinction between a full reflection group and its orientation
subgroup is substantive: their nilpotency classes need not agree.
Thus these sources neither automatically prove our all-rank theorem
nor justify a claim that the rank-three problem had been open.
An explicit adapter and comparison with the announced class-three
classification remain necessary for a source-level priority decision.

Representative searches included the quoted phrases “regular
polytopes” with “nilpotency class” and “three”; “string C-groups”
with “class 3”; “regular maps” with “class three” and “nilpotent”;
“regular polytopes” with “Fibonacci” and “2-groups”; and “nilpotent”
with “class 3”, “regular maps”, “Ban”, “Du” and “Liu”. A further
search paired “String C-groups” with “Fibonacci”. The inspected
results did not identify the exact all-rank transfer formula or
fixed-excess leading coefficient. This bounded negative result
does not certify novelty, exhaust unpublished work, or establish
that a named conjecture remains open in October 2026.

This intake gives no additional principal-paper reduction, matched
proof-length ratio or whole-corpus credit. The two existing ratios
retain their original target scopes. The new logarithmic class-index
bound is an object-description theorem with its decoder and background
explicitly conditional; it is a different measurement.
