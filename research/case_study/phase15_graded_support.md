# Graded support separation for finite nilpotent groups

10 October 2026. Private proposed proof for independent review. This file
is separate from every frozen class-four and cyclic-TPP artifact. No
publication-priority claim is made.

## 1. A lifting lemma that does not require quotient IP

Let G be a finite nilpotent group, with lower-central series D_j=γ_j(G).
Let A,B be subgroups with A∩B=1. Suppose that, for every j≥m, the abelian
group D_j/D_(j+1) is generated by the images of

    A_j=A∩D_j,   B_j=B∩D_j.

Then

    D_j=A_j B_j  for every j≥m.                     (1)

Here (1) is an equality of sets with a subgroup on the left; no
commutativity or prior permutability of A_j and B_j is presumed.

Proof: descend from a trivial lower-central term. Suppose
D_(j+1)=A_(j+1)B_(j+1). If a∈A_j and b∈B_j have the same image modulo
D_(j+1), write

    a b⁻¹=n_A n_B,
    n_A∈A_(j+1),  n_B∈B_(j+1).

Then n_A⁻¹a=n_B b belongs to A∩B, so both sides are 1. Thus the two
images in D_j/D_(j+1) intersect trivially. They generate that abelian
quotient by hypothesis, whence

    |D_j:D_(j+1)|
       =|A_j:A_(j+1)| |B_j:B_(j+1)|.

Induction gives |D_j|=|A_j||B_j|. Since A_j∩B_j=1, the subset A_jB_j
has this same size and is contained in D_j, proving (1).

## 2. A graded Lie support lemma

Let L be a positively graded Lie algebra generated by degree-one
elements indexed by the disjoint union X∪Y. Write L_X,L_Y for the Lie
subalgebras generated by the respective sets. Assume that

    [x,y]∈L_Y  for every x∈X, y∈Y.                 (2)

Then

    L=L_X+L_Y, degree by degree.                    (3)

Indeed, for each x∈X, the derivation ad(x) preserves L_Y: this holds
on its generators by (2), and the derivation identity extends it to
every bracket word. Every iterated bracket in the X-generators likewise
acts by a derivation preserving L_Y. Hence [L_X,L_Y]⊆L_Y. The vector
space L_X+L_Y is therefore a Lie subalgebra containing every generator
of L, proving (3). This argument is valid in characteristic two as well;
it uses the Lie bracket and Jacobi identity, not division by two.

For a group G generated by involutions, the ordinary lower-central
graded Lie ring

    L(G)=⊕_(j≥1) γ_j(G)/γ_(j+1)(G)

is generated by the images of those generators in degree one. Its
degree-one group has exponent two, so bilinearity makes every graded
component an F₂-vector space. A pure-X bracket of degree j is represented
by an element of G_X∩γ_j(G), and similarly for Y. Consequently (3)
supplies exactly the graded generation hypothesis in section 1, with
A=G_X and B=G_Y.

## 3. The forest theorem

Let G be a finite two-group generated by involutions r_v indexed by
the vertices of a finite forest T. Suppose:

* distinct generators commute whenever their vertices are not joined
  by an edge of T;
* every edge joins a noncommuting pair;
* the marked subgroup intersection property holds:
  G_I∩G_J=G_(I∩J), where G_I=<r_v:v∈I>.

Then the images of the edge commutators are linearly independent in
G′/γ₃(G).

Proof: the marked generators are irredundant by IP. Burnside's basis
theorem, together with Φ(G)=G′ for an involution-generated two-group,
therefore gives

    G/G′≅C₂^|V(T)|,   G′∩<r_v>=1.                 (4)

The edge commutators generate the elementary abelian quotient
G′/γ₃(G). Suppose they have a nonzero linear relation. Root each tree
component arbitrarily and select a participating edge e whose child
endpoint has maximal depth among all participating edges in its
component. Let X be the descendant component obtained by deleting e,
and let Y be all remaining vertices, including other tree components.
No other participating edge lies in X. Exactly one edge joins X to Y,
namely e. Write its endpoints as x∈X and y∈Y.

The relation, solved for the coefficient-one edge e, says in degree two
of L(G) that

    [r_x,r_y] is a sum of commutators of Y-generators.             (5)

All other cross brackets between X- and Y-generators vanish. Thus (2)
holds, and the graded Lie support lemma gives L(G)=L_X+L_Y.

Set A=G_X and B=G_Y. Actual IP gives A∩B=1. Apply section 1 from
degree m=2, using the pure-support representatives of each graded
component. It yields

    G′=(G′∩A)(G′∩B).                               (6)

Let c=[r_x,r_y]. Write c=u v according to (6). Since c is supported
on {x,y} and v∈B,

    u=c v⁻¹ ∈G_X∩G_({x}∪Y)=<r_x>.

Equation (4) gives u=1. Hence

    c∈G_Y∩G_{x,y}=<r_y>,

and (4) gives c=1. This contradicts the noncommuting edge hypothesis.
There was therefore no edge relation.

The root choice in this argument is only a device for finding a cut
with one participating crossing edge and no participating edge on one
side. It makes no assertion about subgroup normality. In particular,
neither G_X nor G_Y is assumed normal in G.

## 4. The canonical quotient and the string case

If T has d vertices and m edges, the theorem gives

    |G:γ₃(G)|=2^(d+m).                              (7)

This quotient has a concrete marked model. Choose an orientation of
each edge and let

    V=F₂^d,  Z=F₂^m,
    (v,z)(w,t)=(v+w,z+t+β(v,w)),

where the e-coordinate of the bilinear map β is v_head(e) w_tail(e).
The marked generator at vertex i is (e_i,0). These are involutions;
their commutators are the independent edge coordinates, with all
nonedge pairs commuting. Bilinearity proves associativity. The marked
subgroup on I consists exactly of vectors with vertex support in I
and central support on edges whose two endpoints lie in I. Thus the
model has the marked intersection property.

The corresponding class-two presentation maps onto G/γ₃(G). Collection
gives at most 2^(d+m) elements in that presentation, and the displayed
model attains that number. Equation (7) therefore identifies the map as
a marked isomorphism.

For a nondegenerate string C-group, T is the path on d vertices and
m=d−1. Hence, with no restriction on nilpotency class,

    G/γ₃(G)≅G(C_d),  |G:γ₃(G)|=2^(2d−1),

with type {4,…,4} and IP. This is the common marked quotient claimed
in the source's main existence statement. It does not identify the
source's specified Frattini-intersection kernel, whose counterexamples
remain unchanged.

## 5. Trust boundary and remaining review

The proof uses the elementary lower-central graded Lie construction,
its generation in degree one, Burnside's basis theorem, finite
nilpotency and the original marked IP. It uses no group catalogue,
finite enumeration, classification by nilpotency class, or assumption
that any intermediate quotient has IP. Section 1 is specifically what
permits the intermediate quotient-IP failure in
[phase15_quotient_ip.md](phase15_quotient_ip.md).

This proposed proof is being independently reviewed. The finite
class-four diagnostics and explicit quotient-IP witness are separate
checks, not inputs to the all-class theorem. No Lean formalization of
this argument or verified publication priority is claimed here.
