# Independent review of the fixed-class boundary-state theorem

Date: 2026-10-10. Internal mathematical review, not external peer review or a priority determination. The companion JSON binds the exact reviewed proof. No frozen earlier artifact was changed.

**Verdict: the written reconstruction and counting argument passes review.** The conclusion concerns ordered nondegenerate involution string C-groups of a fixed nilpotency-class bound. The deletion kernel is essential state data; overlap isomorphism alone is not asserted sufficient.

## Locality and the boundary data

The normal closure of the last marker is generated by the stated left-normed words of weights at most the class bound. Conjugation by an involutory marker appends a commutator, so this list generates a normal subgroup. A nonzero word cannot jump a gap in its path support: at the first such jump the new letter commutes with every preceding letter. This gives both the final-window containment and centralization by markers outside that window. The latter uses class, rather than literal commutation, for words of maximum weight.

In an actual extension, actual IP gives the stated prefix/window overlap. The product and order formula follow from normal-closure locality; neither a retraction nor injectivity after deleting old markers is used. The recorded quotient of the boundary is precisely the quotient of the whole prefix after killing its old markers.

Both transition tests are necessary. For sufficiency, the boundary action on the new normal closure factors through that recorded quotient. The overlap with the normal closure is normal in the prefix: its conjugation under the last old marker is controlled by the second test, and earlier markers commute literally with the boundary. This also proves equivariance of its two inclusions.

## The constructed group and its intersection property

I checked the diagonal normal subgroup in the semidirect product without assuming its overlap is abelian. For elements of the overlap, the prescribed action is inner conjugation. Consequently the displayed diagonal set is a subgroup, is centralized by the normal-closure factor, and is normalized by the prefix factor. Both factors embed in the quotient. The new window embeds with its original relations and has exactly the designated overlap with the prefix.

The mixed-commutator argument controls the class of this finite two-group. Before the first new marker, an old letter makes the commutator's action on the new normal closure trivial. After the first new marker, an old letter centralizes that closure. The two unmixed cases lie in the already bounded-class factors. Vanishing of the elementary commutators gives equality of consecutive lower-central terms, and finite nilpotence then forces the required term to vanish. This does not infer a global class bound merely from arbitrary local class bounds.

The coset-Helly step is valid for string C-groups. I reopened the primary paper [Schulte, *Regular Incidence Complexes, Polytopes, and C-Groups*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.02297), reading Lemma 3.1, the construction and Theorems 4.1–4.2, and the polytope specialization. They provide the subflag stabilizers and regular-polytope correspondence retained in the proof. A coset prescribes a partial flag; pairwise intersecting cosets give a compatible chain of prescribed faces, and a flag containing that chain has a common representative. This argument applies to arbitrary standard parabolics, not only maximal ones. It is not a theorem about every C-group or every incidence geometry.

For each selected set of markers, the constructed subgroup is the product of its selected prefix and window subgroups: old generators centralize the selected new normal closure. Its intersections with each full factor are exactly those selected subgroups. For an element in two selected products, the three prefix cosets used in the proof really do intersect pairwise. Their common representative moves the remaining factor into the intersection of the two window parabolics. This proves actual IP without assuming IP in a quotient.

## State update, uniqueness and counting

The universal presentation in the update argument is exact. The old-marker commutations give a homomorphism from the specified semidirect product; the diagonal overlap dies. Thus the constructed quotient maps onto the presentation, which maps back onto the constructed quotient. Killing old generators therefore leaves exactly the boundary deletion relations already recorded, with no unrecorded past relation. Killing the additional first boundary generator gives the stated next kernel.

The finite alphabet bound follows from multilinearity of the ordinary associated graded commutator: every degree is generated by elementary commutators of exponent at most two. Marked isomorphisms of overlaps are unique. An actual group determines every window and deletion kernel; conversely the extension construction and its exact presentation determine the next group. Thus accepted walks count ordered markings, rather than merely bounding their number. Unreachable formal states cause no overcounting when walks start at the prescribed initial states.

The canonical forest quotient is an explicitly retained earlier theorem. Its exact parabolic restriction makes each transition excess nonnegative and identifies it with the increase of the third lower-central subgroup. Order multiplication then gives the advertised telescoping weights and finite transfer-matrix rationality.

The fixed-excess corollary also passes: an excess-k group has third lower-central subgroup of order 2^k, so strict lower-central descent gives class at most k+2. Its rank-(k+2) windows have order at most 2^(3k+3); shorter initial ranks have order at most 2^(3k+1). Truncation at excess k is legitimate because all increments are nonnegative. This is an effective bounded-window algorithm, not a claim that the whole canonical core has rank k+2.

## Independent consistency check and limits

There is a direct algebraic check at class three using the frozen edge/triple classification. If a,b are the two edge-kernel bits in a rank-three window, a new triple generator contributes one kernel dimension, while a dependent triple has 2^(a+b) choices. The possible overlap kernels are central edge-square subgroups, so both transition tests hold for every matching boundary. The weighted transition from a to b is therefore

    M_ab = x^b (x + 2^(a+b)),

which is exactly the earlier two-state transfer matrix. This calculation is independent of the author's finite diagnostic. This review does not claim to have freshly executed that diagnostic; any such run must be recorded separately.

I also read the final diagnostic and source-scope additions. They correctly distinguish a full class-three window enumeration from a class-four alphabet restricted to excess at most two, whose completeness retains the earlier classifications. The reported positive constructions do not test an actual extra compatibility rejection or a noncentral diagonal overlap. Those cases remain covered by the written proof, rather than by a claimed finite test. The displayed totals of subgroup closures and intersections agree arithmetically with the stated numbers and ranks of tested groups; this is not an independent execution of the group code.

No classification for unbounded nilpotency class with a single fixed alphabet, efficient implementation, minimal automaton, publication novelty, or full Lean formalization has been established by this review. The current proof is a written mathematical argument with explicit retained background.
