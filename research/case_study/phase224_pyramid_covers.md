# Infinitely many finite minimal covers of the pyramid over a square torus

Private author manuscript, phase224, 10 October 2026; not yet frozen or
independently reviewed. This is an additional finite case of the stronger
question, separate from the unrestricted theorem in phase216.

## 1. Result and attribution

Let P be the pyramid over the regular square torus {4,4}_(3,0), whose vertices
are Z_3 squared. Monson and Schulte use this exact polytope in Example 3.7 of
*Finite Polytopes have Finite Regular Covers*, arXiv1209.1167v1, pp. 6--7. They
compute the order of its monodromy group G and the index-two failure of its
top parabolic intersection, and construct a finite regular cover using their
general finite-cover theorem. Those observations and that existence theorem
are earlier work.

The later classification of toroidal pyramids also identifies the odd
{4,4}_(n,0) family as the exceptional non-C family: see Cunningham, Pellicer
and Williams, *Stratified operations on maniplexes* (2022), Section 4.3 and
Theorem 4.3, which cites Pellicer--Williams (2018). The present theorem does
not claim that classification as new.

The additional result established here is:

**Theorem.** For every prime ell>=5 there is a finite regular polytopal cover
H_ell of P with

    |G| = 2^12 3^11 5 = 3,627,970,560,
    ker(H_ell -> G) is elementary abelian of order ell^642.       (1)

The polytope P therefore has infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic finite
minimal regular polytopal covers. For each ell one may choose such a cover
with degree ell^e over G, where 1<=e<=642.

The theorem does not specify a minimal descendant or its degree. It is not
a classification of all covers, does not preserve all original face sizes,
and does not settle the stronger finite question for arbitrary polytopes.
Its new application combines the surface construction of phase211 with the
coprime pullback argument of phase109/126 through a newly constructed quotient
of order 3840. The topological and pullback theorems are reused, rather than
proved anew. No historical-priority claim beyond this exact written source
comparison is made.

## 2. The literal polytope and its flag action

Write v(x,y) for (x,y) in Z_3 squared. There are nine square faces, indexed by
their lower left corners. The face at(x,y) has successive vertices

    v(x,y), v(x+1,y), v(x+1,y+1), v(x,y+1),

and successive edges h(x,y), v_edge(x+1,y), h(x,y+1), v_edge(x,y).
Horizontal and vertical edges have distinct identities even if a different
period were to give them the same endpoints. Here every square has four
distinct vertices and four distinct edges. Each edge belongs to two squares;
the faces around each vertex form a 4-cycle. The grid is connected. These
facts verify the diamond condition and flag connectivity of every section,
so this is the stated abstract toroidal polyhedron. It has 72 flags.

Form its pyramid: retain every base face, add an apex, and add the cone on
each proper base face. The face order is inherited from the base order,
with a base face below a cone exactly when its base is contained in that
cone's base. This is the usual join with a point. For a base flag(v,e,f),
the five pyramid flags are

    (apex, cone(v), cone(e), cone(f)),
    (v, cone(v), cone(e), cone(f)),
    (v, e, cone(e), cone(f)),
    (v, e, f, cone(f)),
    (v, e, f, base facet).

Thus P has 360 flags. The program constructs precisely these flags and defines
s_i by exchanging the two flags with the other three faces fixed. It checks
the unique exchange, the string commutations and the component intersection
property for all color subsets at every flag. The four resulting
permutations generate G. This gives an actual flag action of the specified
polytope, not a marked group guessed from an order or a presentation.

Set A=G012, B=G123 and C=G12. Exact permutation-group calculations give

    |A|=|B|=110592=2^12 3^3,
    |C|=24,           |A intersection B|=48.                       (2)

The two rank-three end intersections are checked as subgroup equalities.
Both ends are therefore string C-groups, while G is not. Equations(1)--(2)
agree with the primary example, but the calculation starts from literal
faces and flags and does not assume those numerical assertions.

## 3. A smaller quotient retaining the obstruction

On ten points let D be generated by the four permutations given as image
lists below; the i-th entry is the image of i.

    d0 = [3,4,1,2,5,6,7,8,9,10],
    d1 = [1,2,5,6,3,4,8,7,10,9],
    d2 = [1,2,3,4,7,8,5,6,10,9],
    d3 = [2,1,4,3,6,5,9,10,7,8].

The assignments s_i -> d_i extend to an epimorphism pi:G->D, verified as a
homomorphism of the exact permutation groups. Its kernel N has

    |D|=3840,     |N|=944784=2^4 3^10,
    N intersection(A intersection B) = N intersection C,
    |N intersection C|=2.                                      (3)

The equality in(3) is checked as containment and equality of actual subgroups,
not inferred merely from orders. The quotient's own top intersection is
larger: its two ends have order768, its middle group order12, and its top
intersection order192. This causes no problem, since the cover constructed
in the next section repairs the quotient's entire intersection. One must
not assume that D inherits the original index-two defect.

## 4. Applying the surface theorem

Construct the closed surface belonging to D as in phase211: vertices are D;
edges have the four marked colors; disks fill all02,03,13 and12 cycles. The
middle product has order6. Literal cell construction gives

    V=3840, E=7680, F=3200, chi=-640.

The Cayley graph is bipartite, so the surface is orientable of genus321.
For the three unions

    X012 union X123,   X01 union X12,   X12 union X23,

the complement has respectively2704,3199,3199 faces, and in each case has
one connected component. The replay records the cell incidence and checks
connectivity; it does not infer it from the genus or from group orders.

The theorem of phase211 now produces, for every prime ell, a finite string
C-group Q_ell mapping onto D with elementary abelian kernel of dimension
2-chi=642. Its proof uses the mod-ell homology cover of this same finite
surface. Thus all primes follow from one checked surface and the uniform
written theorem; there is no extrapolation from a few finite fields.

## 5. Pulling the covers back to the actual monodromy group

For completeness, the elementary group argument is stated in the form needed
here. Suppose Q->D and G->D are marked epimorphisms, ker(Q->D) is a finite
ell-group, and N=ker(G->D) has order prime to ell. Let H be generated by the
paired marks in the fiber product Q times_D G. For each set I of marks,

    H_I = Q_I times_(D_I) G_I.                              (4)

To prove this, the projections of H_I onto both factors are surjective by
generation. Let e be the exponent of ker(Q->D), a power of ell. For n in
N intersection G_I choose an e-th root m of n in its cyclic subgroup. Lift
m to(q,m) in H_I. Its first component lies in ker(Q->D), so(q,m)^e=(1,n).
Thus H_I contains the full standard kernel1 times(N intersection G_I).
Any compatible pair can now be obtained by lifting its first component and
adjusting its second component by this kernel. This proves(4). It is the
same coprime argument used in phase109, and the general finite fiber-product
statement is already verified in formal/CoprimePullback.lean (phase126).
No split-extension assumption is required.

If Q is a string C-group, (4) implies, for J=I intersection I',

    H_I intersection H_I' =
      (1 times(N intersection G_I intersection G_I')) H_J.       (5)

Indeed the image in Q lies in Q_J; subtract a lift from H_J with that image,
and the remaining pair is in the stated standard kernel. Conversely that
kernel and H_J lie in both subgroups, again by(4).

Apply this with Q=Q_ell from Section 4. For ell>=5, (3) gives the required
coprimality. Inside A and B the original intersections are already correct;
(5) therefore gives the corresponding lifted intersections. The only
remaining rank-four equality follows from (3): N intersection A intersection
B is contained in C. Both lifted ends are string C-groups and their
intersection is the lifted middle group. The recursive criterion proves
that H_ell is a string C-group. By(4) its kernel over G is precisely the
kernel of Q_ell over D, giving(1).

This proves all required marked relations as well: H_ell is a subgroup of a
direct product, and the paired involutions have every nonadjacent
commutation in both coordinates. The original marks remain distinct and
nonidentity under projection to G.

## 6. Minimal descendants and their distinction

Every finite regular cover has a minimal regular quotient over P: choose a
maximal admissible normal subgroup in its finite kernel lattice. Let R_ell
be such a quotient of H_ell. Its kernel over G is a quotient of the
ell-group in(1), hence has order ell^e for some0<=e<=642. It cannot be
trivial, since then the marked group would be G, which fails(2). Thus e>=1.
For different primes these nontrivial kernel orders are unequal. A
rank-preserving isomorphism of regular polytopes identifies their marked
universal kernels (normality removes dependence on the chosen flag), so
these minimal covers are pairwise nonisomorphic.

Equivalently, the universal kernels of H_ell and H_m for different primes
have product equal to ker(W_4->G): the quotient by their product is a
quotient of both an ell-group and an m-group. The order and minimal-kernel
lemmas used here are already included in the frozen phase211 Lean packet.
The geometry of this particular pyramid and its surface is not formalized
in Lean; the finite permutation and cellular witnesses are separate exact
computations, and the retained topological proof is written.
