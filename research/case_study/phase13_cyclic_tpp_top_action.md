# The forced action on the three top involutions

10 October 2026. Private structural supplement. This note applies only
to a minimum-order counterexample to the unrestricted cyclic-TPP
inequality in finite 2-groups. It does not prove that no such group
exists, and it does not modify the frozen smallest-factor-eight proof.

## 1. Statement and dependencies

Retain the exact hypotheses and dependencies of
[phase13_cyclic_tpp.md](phase13_cyclic_tpp.md), including the
power-layer theorem in [phase12_power_layers.md](phase12_power_layers.md).
Thus P=<a,b>=<a,c>=<b,c>, the three cyclic subgroups avoid Z(P), and
their common order is n=2^e with e>=4. Put

    u=a^(n/2), v=b^(n/2), w=c^(n/2),
    U=<u,v,w>.

The normal powerful subgroup N=P^8 has three independent cyclic
coordinates of equal order n/8. Its highest nontrivial power subgroup
is U, an elementary abelian group of order eight with basis u,v,w.
This follows from the actual ordered-coordinate bijection, not from
TPP alone. In particular U is characteristic in N and normal in P.

**Theorem.** The conjugation action of P on U has kernel Phi(P) and
image C2^2. In additive coordinates on U with basis u,v,w, its three
marked generators act as follows:

    u^a=u,       v^a=u+w,  w^a=u+v,
    u^b=v+w,     v^b=v,    w^b=u+v,
    u^c=v+w,     v^c=u+w,  w^c=w.

Consequently

    C_P(U)=Phi(P),
    U intersect Z(P)=<uvw>.

The last expression uses multiplicative notation for the product of
the three commuting involutions. It does not assert that Z(P) itself
is cyclic.

## 2. No marked generator can have an order-four action

Write R for the conjugation image in GL(U)=GL_3(F2), and S,T,W for
the images of a,b,c. A 2-group acting on a nonzero F2-space has a
nonzero fixed vector, by counting its vector orbits. Repeating in
successive quotients gives an invariant complete flag, so R is
conjugate to a subgroup of UT_3(F2). This latter group has order eight,
exponent four, and is dihedral: in coordinates

    [[1,s,t],[0,1,r],[0,0,1]],

the square has only the (1,3) entry sr potentially nonzero.

An order-four element g of GL_3(F2) is regular unipotent: writing
g=I+D gives D^3=0 and D^2!=0, so ker D has dimension one. The common
fixed space U^R is nonzero and contained in ker D. Hence its unique
nonzero vector is the unique nonzero vector fixed by g.

If S had order four, it would fix u and therefore u would be fixed by
all of R. This makes u central in P, contrary to <a> intersect Z(P)=1.
The same reasoning applies to T and W. Thus S,T,W all have order at
most two.

## 3. The image is elementary abelian of order four

The image R is generated by S,T. If it were nonabelian, it would be
all of a conjugate UT_3(F2), hence dihedral of order eight. Two
involutions generating this group have product ST of order four,
and its Frattini subgroup is <(ST)^2>.

The minimum-counterexample reductions give c in ab Phi(P), because
a,b,c represent the three nonzero elements of P/Phi(P). A surjection
of finite p-groups maps the Frattini subgroup onto that of its image;
this also follows directly from Phi(H)=H^2[H,H]. Consequently

    W in ST Phi(R).

In the dihedral case both elements of this coset have order four,
contradicting section 2. Therefore R is abelian. Since S,T are
involutions, R is elementary abelian of order at most four.

The possibilities of order one or two make a marked coordinate fixed
by the whole image: if one of S,T is the identity, use the coordinate
fixed by the other; if they coincide, either of their individually
fixed coordinates is globally fixed. Thus |R|=4 and S,T are distinct
nonidentity commuting involutions. Now Phi(R)=1, so W=ST. Because
|P:Phi(P)|=4 and Phi(P) is contained in the action kernel, this also
proves C_P(U)=Phi(P).

## 4. The marked basis determines the action uniquely

Put D=S-I and E=T-I. Each is a rank-one square-zero map: a nonzero
square-zero endomorphism of a three-dimensional space has rank one.
They commute, and

    D(u)=0, E(v)=0, (D+E+DE)(w)=0.                 (1)

None of u,v,w belongs to ker D intersect ker E, since each such
membership would give a central element of one distinguished factor.

Suppose first im D and im E are distinct. The commuting product DE=ED
has image in their intersection, so DE=ED=0. Both image lines lie in
ker D intersect ker E; they are independent, forcing these two
two-dimensional kernels to be the same plane. Equation (1) places u,v
in that plane. Since D(w)=E(w) and the images are distinct, both values
are zero, also placing w in the plane. This contradicts the basis.

Thus im D=im E=L. Choose its nonzero vector l and write

    D=l*phi, E=l*psi

for nonzero linear functionals phi,psi. Square-zero gives
phi(l)=psi(l)=0, hence DE=0. The absence of a globally fixed marked
coordinate and (1) give exactly

    phi(u)=0, phi(v)=1, phi(w)=1,
    psi(u)=1, psi(v)=0, psi(w)=1.

Their common kernel is <u+v+w>, so l=u+v+w. Substitution yields the
three displayed actions in the theorem. The common fixed space is
precisely this line, proving U intersect Z(P)=<uvw>. QED.

## 5. Exact check and scope

[phase13_cyclic_tpp_top_action_check.py](phase13_cyclic_tpp_top_action_check.py)
enumerates GL_3(F2), all two-generator 2-subgroup images, and every
choice W in ST Phi(R) satisfying the three marked fixed-vector
conditions. It retains only cases having no globally fixed marked
coordinate. The [saved result](phase13_cyclic_tpp_top_action_check.json)
contains the unique pair with column encodings

    S=(1,5,3), T=(6,2,3), W=(6,5,4),

and image order four. The checker uses only finite binary matrices and
the Python standard library; it searches no finite-group catalogue.
Run:

    python3 phase13_cyclic_tpp_top_action_check.py

This action is a surviving necessary condition. In particular its
central line is not one of the three marked coordinate lines, so it
does not contradict the center-free cyclic factors. Further extension
or commutator information is still needed for the unrestricted problem.
