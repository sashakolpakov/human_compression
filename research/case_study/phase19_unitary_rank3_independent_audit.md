## Parameters and the projected rank-one group

A square root of a primitive element of F_q has nonzero trace-zero conjugate in F_(q²), and its square generates F_q. The argument applies to every parameter with the stated conditions, not only the primitive-square choice. For t=2s(z−1), the three trace identities check exactly. If E₀=F_p(t²) is not contained in F_q, its degree has the full two-part of [F_(q²):F_p]. Thus [E:E₀] is odd; since t is quadratic over E₀, t belongs to E₀. Conjugation preserves E₀, so recovering s and z from t and its conjugate proves E₀=E. This use of odd extension degree is essential and valid even when e itself is even.

The upper and lower roots x(t),y(t) generate a subgroup D of SL₂(E). Their distinct fixed lines exclude a Borel; their elements of order p exclude torus normalizers. Both elements x(t)y(t) and x(−t)y(t) lie in D. Their projectively invariant trace-square values have difference 8t², so their trace field is all of E in every odd characteristic. This excludes conjugate proper subfield groups, including PGL₂ subgroups. The exceptional cases are correctly separated: orders exclude them for p≥7; their trace invariants lie in F₅ when p=5, and in F₉ when p=3. For the latter, q≥9 makes [E:F₃]≥4. The retained Dickson list therefore forces the projective image of D to be PSL₂(E). The perfect-central-cover argument then yields D=SL₂(E).

I reread the exact PSL₂ maximal-subgroup list in the cached primary 2017 paper, Proposition 2.3, printed p.5. That classification remains an external input. The rank-one surjection and perfectness are applied to the determinant-one root subgroup D, not to the whole projected L, which may also contain matrices of determinant −1.

## Why the radical kernel cannot vanish

The pointwise stabilizer of the maximal isotropic plane is precisely its additive skew-Hermitian radical R: a unitary matrix fixing W has identity dual quotient, and its remaining block is skew-Hermitian. It has no additional semisimple kernel.

Assuming L∩R=1 gives an actual linear copy of SL₂(E) as the inverse image of its projected determinant-one subgroup, containing U. Its torus has order q²−1 prime to p. Averaging supplies an E-linear invariant complement to W. The weights on W are 1 and −1; those on its dual quotient are −q and q. All four are distinct for q≥5. The complementary plane is isotropic because none of its four Hermitian weight-pair exponents is zero modulo q²−1. A dual basis then gives the claimed hyperbolic form matrix.

I independently multiplied the upper-root block matrices. All four displayed off-block functional equations are correct, including the two signs involving w^q. They successively force additivity of d₂₂, then d₁₂ and d₂₁, then d₁₁, once the preceding entries vanish. Every additive map of E is represented by a linearized polynomial with powers p^j, 0≤j<2e. Torus equivariance therefore requires one of the congruences 2p^j≡w modulo q²−1. The proof considers all j, not only F_q-linear maps. For j<e the values are below q−1. For j≥e they lie strictly between q+1 and q²−q−2. The upper estimate holds at the smallest cases (p,q)=(5,5) and (3,9), and increases thereafter. Thus none equals a possible positive weight residue q−1,q+1,q²−q−2,q²−q. All are below the modulus, so no modular wraparound is omitted.

The Weyl lift normalizes the torus and inverts its characters. The two distinct weight pairs are separately closed under inversion, so it preserves the chosen complement. It follows that the entire copy of SL₂(E) is block diagonal in this decomposition. Its root element U can only have Jordan blocks of size at most two. The separately checked identities (U−I)²=2n n̄ᵀH≠0 and (U−I)³=0 contradict this. No semisimplicity of the whole group in defining characteristic was assumed.

## Irreducibility of the additive radical module

The proof correctly establishes irreducibility over F_p, not merely over F_q. A nonzero off-diagonal Hermitian entry produces a nonzero diagonal entry by the nondegenerate trace pairing. An upper-root positive/negative second difference isolates 2N(u)d in a diagonal entry. Since d≠0 and the norm E*→F_q* is surjective, this yields every upper diagonal scalar using only the additive subgroup operations. Applying the lower roots, their difference gives every off-diagonal Hermitian entry, and their sum yields every lower diagonal scalar. Division by two means multiplication by its inverse in F_p and is permitted inside an additive subgroup. No F_q-linearity was silently supplied.

Because R is abelian, its conjugation action depends only on the projected group. The nonzero normal subgroup L∩R is therefore the full radical. Removing the radical part of each lift then supplies the claimed Levi copy in any chosen hyperbolic basis.

## Moving the isotropic plane and completing generation

The exact pairing h(m,Bm)=4s²(1+s) is nonzero: s=−z² and a nonzero trace-zero z cannot have z²=1. The plane BW meets W in En because B fixes n and moves m outside W. After rescaling Bm, its coordinates are x e₁+y e₂+f₂ with y+ȳ=0. A skew-Hermitian radical block can remove x and y simultaneously; its other off-diagonal entry is fixed by skew-Hermiticity and does not affect this vector. This is an actual element already in the generated group.

Conjugating the full center-Bm transvection group from R^B therefore supplies the full center-f₂ group. Together with the center-e₂ group in R, it gives the ordinary rank-one unitary group on their hyperbolic plane, fixing the orthogonal complement. Its Weyl element, and the corresponding one transported by the Levi coordinate swap, exchange the two maximal isotropic planes. Hence both opposite radicals are present. Together with the full extension-field Levi roots they supply the elementary unitary root groups. Their generation of SU₄(q) is the explicit retained elementary elimination fact, not a classification of dimension-four maximal subgroups or a finite recognition oracle.

## Projective and polytope conclusions

The determinant classes are exactly those generated by −1; reducing (q+1)/2 modulo gcd(4,q+1) gives the three groups stated in the note. The kernel of projectivization does not alter this determinant-quotient calculation. For q≡7 mod8 the result is the simple socle, not its index-two extension.

The adjacent characteristic polynomials check symbolically. The second product has a size-three block at eigenvalue +1 and a −1 eigenvalue, giving projective order 2p, including p=3. For the first product, projective commutation would mean ordinary commutation or anticommutation of the involution lifts. Anticommutation is impossible for a reflection's unequal eigenspace dimensions; ordinary commutation fails because A does not preserve the exceptional line Ev. Thus its projective order exceeds two, including the repeated-root parameter s=1. The endpoints are distinct commuting projective involutions.

The final frozen proof now supplies a self-contained same-marking IP adapter. If an endpoint belongs to the opposite adjacent dihedral subgroup, G is dihedral, contradicting absence of cyclic normal subgroups. Otherwise, choose an extra element of the two adjacent dihedral groups' intersection, and multiply by b if necessary to make it a nonidentity rotation of the first. If it is a rotation of both, all three generators invert it, making its cyclic subgroup normal. In the mixed case it is a central involution g of the first dihedral group, and h=gb is a central involution of the second. The two subgroups ⟨a,h⟩ and ⟨g,c⟩ commute elementwise and generate G. A nontrivial rotation subgroup of either is cyclic and normal in the whole product. If neither has one, their product is abelian and still has a cyclic normal subgroup. Every case contradicts the hypothesis. Thus the adjacent meet is exactly ⟨b⟩ and the usual rank-three criterion gives full IP.
