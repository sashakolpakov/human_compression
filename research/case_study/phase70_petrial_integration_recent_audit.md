## The common interfaces and their actual uses

K(c) is correct with its stated two-way interface. Surjectivity of the equivariant forgetting map shows that an element fixing every constructed object fixes every retained object. Injectivity of the equivariant encoding shows the converse: fixing the retained set pointwise fixes each encoded element or subset, its fixed tag, and hence its uniquely encoded object. The kernel is therefore the intersection of retained point stabilizers. Decomposing into orbits gives the intersection of their cores, equal to the core of their intersection. No assumption of a homomorphism between changed marked generating lists enters this argument.

The thin-geometry conclusion needs, and expressly retains, type preservation, residual connectivity, finite rank and chamber transitivity of the faithful image. O0 supplies a free chamber action. The image then equals the full type-preserving automorphism group. The preimage of a panel stabilizer has index two over the action kernel by the orbit–stabilizer bijection; this is not a finite-order division argument. In particular an order-two preimage forces trivial kernel, even if the ambient group is infinite.

O0's gallery proof is sound. Induction connects chambers through each object residue, and an incidence path joins these pieces. Thinness propagates a fixed chamber across its unique panel neighbours. Reading galleries gives the distinguished involutions and generation; reading galleries inside a residue gives the exact missing-type stabilizer. Residual connectivity of finite-rank geometries, rather than finiteness of their object sets, is the hypothesis used.

The three uses of K(c) are actual checked applications. For N008's typed cosets, identity encoding and forgetting give the familiar core of the chamber stabilizer, without changing K(b)'s separate geometry-transport proof. For the endpoint construction, an object is encoded by its old label and the colored vertex subsets of its chosen double-cover component. Forgetting covers all old vertices and higher objects. In the bipartite case the acting group is precisely the color-preserving subgroup, and the selected component contains one lift of every old vertex and a compatible component over each connected higher shadow. Fixing all these retained objects fixes omitted old edges because the graph is simple. Thus faithfulness follows. The replacement gallery argument also justifies the connected truncations used in this application; it does not incorrectly declare a truncation to be a residue.

## Petrial proof, including the infinite case

The operation uses actual pairs of vertex and edge subsets, deduplicating equal pairs. This matters: keeping independently labelled walks would define a different action kernel. The three literal chamber changes, with the stated right-multiplication convention on chamber labels, give the step `cab` and the identities `wv=cv` and `w^-1 e=be`. These justify the conventional-map walk adapter and the distinct adjacent pairs used throughout the proof.

The geometry and residual-connectivity proof covers every required rank-three residue. A defining walk joins every object of a new face residue. In a vertex residue, each old edge–face–edge path can be replaced by the new face through that chamber, because the old panel is thin; the dual argument covers edge residues. Old face passages can be removed through their connected vertex–edge shadows to establish the remaining connectivity. Flags extend by the displayed walk. These arguments allow infinite walks and residues.

With vertices and edges retained, K(c) gives exactly `ker(rho)=core_G(<a>)`, since their selected stabilizers intersect in `<a>`. As `a` is an involution, this kernel is `<a>` if `a` is central and is trivial otherwise. The proof neither assumes unconditional faithfulness nor assumes that the Petrial action is chamber-transitive.

Both obstruction branches are necessary and correct. If `bP != P`, the new vertex residue contains a four-cycle; connectedness and degree two force that cycle to exhaust the residue. The old vertex residue then has exactly two edges and is itself a four-cycle, forcing `|ab|=2`. If `bP=P`, the action of `ca` fixes an edge–face panel. Its square acts trivially by thinness and free chamber action, placing `(ca)^2` in `<a>`. The rotation subgroup of the two-involution group `<a,c>` intersects its reflection subgroup trivially, so `|ac|=2`. The manuscript proves this elementary dihedral fact without a finite-order hypothesis.

After interchanging vertices and edges if needed, the commuting relation `[a,c]=1` gives a dihedral action transitive on all incident pairs within each Petrie face. Thinness ensures that the two pairs displayed at a vertex are all such pairs. The original chamber-transitive action is transitive on the new faces, hence its image is chamber-transitive. K(c) identifies the full regular automorphism image. Its old `(v,e)` panel preimage is the order-two subgroup `<a>`, so the kernel is trivial.

Only now are `(a,b,ca)` identified as the actual new panel involutions. The second step is `cb`, whose orbits give precisely the original face shadows. The separate injectivity argument for those shadows is essential and present: two distinct old faces with one shadow force the old vertex four-cycle, hence `[a,b]=1`; together with `[a,c]=1` this makes `a` central, contradicting the just-proved faithfulness. Thus biduality is a canonical incidence isomorphism fixing the retained objects, not just an equality of orders or orbit lengths. Every use of a finite four-cycle is a local exhaustion argument inside a connected degree-two graph; no finite ambient geometry is inferred.
