# Exact first-excess classification: 5d−10 ordered markings

10 October 2026. Private phase17 proof, pending independent review.
No frozen phase16 file is changed. A marking is ordered: an isomorphism
must send the generator with index i to the generator with index i.
Nondegenerate means that every adjacent product has order at least
four; labels two are excluded.

**Theorem.** For every d>=3, exactly 5d−10 ordered marked isomorphism
classes of nondegenerate string C-group two-groups have order 2^(2d).
Of these, d−2 have type {4,...,4}. The other 4d−8 have exactly one
entry 8 and all other entries 4. The construction, presentation and
parameter classification below hold uniformly in d; no finite census
or experimental extrapolation is a proof input.

We retain the proved forest canonical-quotient theorem and the central
join construction from [phase15](phase15_graded_support.md) and
[phase16](phase16_central_join.md). In particular the quotient by
gamma3 of any group under consideration is the marked class-two
path group C_d of order 2^(2d−1). Its edge commutators are independent
central involutions and its marked parabolics satisfy IP.

## 1. Central covers and their complete parameters

In a group G from the theorem let K=gamma3(G). The canonical quotient
gives |K|=2, so K=<z> is central. Thus G has class three. Put

    c_i=[r_i,r_(i+1)],
    c_i^2=z^(alpha_i),                    1<=i<=d−1,
    [c_i,r_(i+2)]=z^(beta_i),             1<=i<=d−2,       (1)

with alpha_i,beta_i in F2. Our convention is [x,y]=x^(-1)y^(-1)xy.
At least one parameter is nonzero. Indeed gamma3 is generated by
the commutators of edge commutators with markers, and the following
table lists all of them:

    [c_i,r_j] = z^(alpha_i)   if j=i or i+1;
              = z^(beta_(i−1)) if j=i−1;
              = z^(beta_i)     if j=i+2;
              = 1              otherwise.              (2)

For the first line, either endpoint involution inverts c_i; since
c_i^2 lies in K, its inverse square is the same element. The far
cases follow from the string commutations. The remaining left entry
equals the right entry of the neighboring edge by the class-three
Hall–Witt identity, since r_(i−1) and r_(i+1) commute. Any possible
inverse disappears in the central group K of exponent two.

Here is a presentation determined by a parameter vector. Take
generators r_1,...,r_d,z, impose r_i^2=z^2=1, centrality of z,
the string nonedge commutations, c_i^2=z^(alpha_i), and all of (2).
Call this group P(alpha,beta). Each c_i commutes with every generator
modulo <z>, so the quotient by <z> is generated by involutions with
central involutory edge commutators. Collection gives

    |P(alpha,beta)| <= 2 * 2^(d+d−1) = 2^(2d).           (3)

The relations in fact make the c_i commute with one another: the
map g |-> [c_i,g] is a homomorphism into the central group <z>, and
therefore kills every commutator. Thus the presentation has class at
most three. Every G from the theorem is its marked quotient.
Section 3 constructs a group of the full order for *every nonzero*
parameter vector. Consequently (3) is sharp, the presentation is
complete, and distinct vectors give distinct ordered marked groups.
There is no choice in identifying their kernel involutions.

## 2. Two explicit elementary covers

An **edge seed** is the dihedral group of order sixteen, generated by
two involutions a,b whose product has order eight. Its gamma3 is the
central group <(ab)^4> of order two. Its quotient is the rank-two
canonical group D8. Its only nonzero parameter is its edge alpha.

A **triple seed** is UT4(F2), with the three consecutive elementary
transvections 1+E12, 1+E23, 1+E34. It has order 2^6. These generators
also generate the transvections in positions 13,24,14 by successive
commutators, so generate the full group. Its adjacent products have
order four and its sole nontrivial weight-three commutator is
1+E14. Thus gamma3 has order two and the sole nonzero parameter is
its triple beta.

These seed markings have actual IP. For the dihedral pair this is
immediate. In UT4(F2), the parabolic on a subset of the three markers
consists exactly of the matrices whose potentially nonzero (i,j)
entries have all markers i,i+1,...,j−1 selected. Matrix multiplication
preserves these patterns, and elementary commutators generate every
allowed entry. Taking their intersections proves IP. This also shows
that the kernel involution belongs to a seed parabolic precisely when
the entire seed support is selected.

Extend any seed on its edge or consecutive triple to the whole path
using the frozen central join. Write the resulting group as E_T,
where T is the seed's support. It has order 2^(2d), a central kernel
K_T=<z_T> of order two, and a marked quotient

    pi_T:E_T -> C_d.

Its parameter vector has a single nonzero coordinate, at T: the
central cross-edge coordinates create no weight-three commutators.
The exact central-join parabolic formula also gives

    K_T <= (E_T)_S   if and only if   T subset S.          (4)

The canonical quotient maps every such parabolic onto (C_d)_S.
These facts are valid whether the seed touches the end of the path
or lies in its interior.

## 3. A central sum realizes every nonzero vector

Choose a nonempty set T of active supports, each an edge or a
consecutive triple. Form the fiber product of their elementary covers
over the *same ordered marked* C_d:

    F = { (g_T) : pi_T(g_T) is independent of T }.

It has central kernel Z=product_T K_T and quotient C_d. Mark F by the
diagonal lifts of each r_i. They generate F: their images generate
C_d, and the parameter word belonging to T evaluates to z_T in that
one component and to 1 in every other component. Thus all independent
kernel coordinates are also generated.

Let Z_0 be the kernel of the sum map Z=F2^T -> F2, and put

    G_T = F/Z_0.                                         (5)

Then |G_T|=2^(2d), all its markers are involutions, and the string
relations hold. Its kernel over C_d is a central involution z.
Each active parameter word evaluates to z and all other parameter
words evaluate to 1. Thus (5) realizes exactly the chosen nonzero
vector. It is the full-order quotient required after (3), proving
uniqueness without a central-extension classification theorem.

For later use we need the parabolics, not merely the group order.
In F let F_S be the subgroup generated by the markers in S. Its
image is (C_d)_S, and

    F_S intersect Z = product_(T subset S) K_T.           (6)

For the upper inclusion, project to any E_T. If T is not contained
in S, (4) says the T coordinate of a kernel element must be trivial.
For the lower inclusion, the active parameter word supported on T
produces its isolated kernel coordinate inside F_S whenever T is
contained in S. This proves (6), with no assertion that a general
subgroup of a fiber product is the whole fiber product.

Taking images under (5), the kernel involution lies in (G_T)_S
exactly when at least one active support is contained in S. In
particular

    |(G_T)_S| = 2^( |S| + e(S) + epsilon(S) ),           (7)

where epsilon(S)=1 iff an active support is contained in S and e(S)
is the number of path edges wholly in S.

## 4. The exact intersection-property criterion

For the groups (5), actual IP holds if and only if the active support
family has a least member under inclusion.

Suppose first that J is a least active support. Then the final kernel
lies in a parabolic G_S iff J subset S. Consider any A,B. Both
parabolics map onto their canonical C_d parabolics, whose intersection
is (C_d)_(A intersect B). If at least one of G_A,G_B avoids the kernel,
their intersection maps injectively into this quotient intersection.
It already contains G_(A intersect B), which maps onto the same
group, so equality holds. If both contain the kernel, J lies in
A intersect B. Then both their intersection and G_(A intersect B)
have at most twice the order of the quotient intersection, and the
latter already has exactly that order. Again equality follows.

Conversely, if no least active support exists, choose two distinct
inclusion-minimal active supports A,B. Neither contains the other,
and no active support is contained in A intersect B. Their two
parabolics both contain z by (7), while G_(A intersect B) does not.
Thus IP fails, with an explicit kernel witness. This proves the
criterion and also describes why arbitrary central sums can fail IP.

There are only two possibilities for a least active support:

* J is a triple. The only active support is that triple.
* J is an edge. That edge is active, and the only further possible
  active supports are the one or two consecutive triples containing it.
  Each of those is an independent optional choice.

This proves the complete classification. It also proves necessity
without invoking a separate support-bound or component-count theorem:
every actual group has the presentation in section 1, hence is one
of the central sums, and section 4 characterizes precisely its IP.

## 5. Count and concrete invariants

There are d−2 triple choices. They have alpha=0 and precisely one
nonzero beta, giving the all-four type. For an edge at either end
there are two choices for its one neighboring beta. Each of the d−3
interior edges has two independently optional neighboring betas,
giving four choices. For d>=3 the total is

    (d−2) + 2+2 + 4(d−3) = 5d−10.                       (8)

The edge case has product order eight on its distinguished edge,
and order four elsewhere. The beta choices are distinguished by the
actual marked commutator values in (1). No division by path reversal
or abstract-group automorphisms is being made. If d=2 is considered
separately, there is just one first-excess group, D16; formula (8)
is deliberately stated only for d>=3.

The preceding argument additionally classifies *all* nontrivial
involutory string-marked central covers of C_d with the specified
quotient marking and with central kernel of order two equal to gamma3: there
are 2^(2d−3)−1 vectors, each giving the complete presentation above.
Most fail IP. The count (8) selects precisely the principal support
families from this larger explicitly constructed parameter space.
This is not a classification of unrestricted central covers, where
the chosen lifts could have order four or acquire nonedge twists.

**Reversal corollary.** If ordered markings are additionally identified
under path reversal, the count is (5d−9)/2 for odd d and (5d−8)/2 for
even d. Indeed reversal sends the triple starting at i to the triple
starting at d−1−i and sends edge j to edge d−j. For odd d there is
exactly one fixed marking, the central triple case. For even d>=4
there is no fixed triple, and the central edge gives two fixed markings:
its two neighboring beta values must be equal. Completeness of the
parameter presentation proves that equality with the reversed vector
is also sufficient for self-duality. Burnside's two-element orbit
count gives the displayed formulas. This still does not identify
markings by arbitrary abstract-group isomorphisms.

## 6. Evidence, source overlap and scope

The proof uses the previously proved canonical quotient and the
central-join theorem, plus elementary dihedral groups, unitriangular
matrices, commutator collection and central fiber products. It uses
no classification of finite two-groups, no NQ result as a proof input,
and no extrapolation from ranks three through five.

The separate checker phase17_k1_check.g constructs the displayed
presentations for all nonzero vectors in ranks three, four and five,
checks their orders and every parabolic order, and tests every IP
intersection against the support criterion. These are bounded
diagnostics; the uniform proof is sections 1–5.

The rank-three special case is consistent with the earlier
Hou–Feng–Leemans classification: the paragraph following Theorem 1.3
in [“Existence of regular 3-polytopes of order 2^n”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08947)
(published as “2-groups behaving as automorphism groups of regular
3-polytopes”) treats the n<10
cases, including the unique type {4,4} example and two examples of
each of the dual types {4,8} and {8,4} at order64. This is overlap,
not a claimed new rank-three census. The source's main Theorem1.3
is stated for n>=10; we do not extend that theorem by citation.

At rank five, the reversal count is eight. This matches the eight
rank-five rows in Gomi–Loyola–de Las Peñas, [“String C-groups of order
1024”](https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/download/62475/46803), Table 6,
printed page17: two all-four rows, two end-eight rows, and four
interior-eight rows. Section4, printed page12, explicitly discards
dual classes. In our ordered convention these contribute 3,4,8
objects, respectively, totaling fifteen. Thus the all-rank proof
recovers the complete rank-five count and type multiplicities in the
2018 census. Individual source row presentations have not been
identified with our parameter presentations; no such row-by-row
identification, or recovery of that paper's rank-three or rank-four
census, is claimed.
Conder's [author census ordered by group size](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~conder/RegularPolytopesWithFewFlags-ByOrder.txt)
also contains these eight rank-five/order1024 classes up to duality.
The 2013 minimum-size paper's full proof was not retrieved in this
bounded source check, so no assertion of absent overlap with that
paper is made.

The new [PrincipalKernelIntersections Lean module](formal/PrincipalKernelIntersections.lean)
kernel-checks the group-theoretic sufficiency step of section4:
exact image intersections and principal membership of the possible
kernel involution imply actual IP. Its [scope record](formal/PrincipalKernelIntersections.scope.md)
states the assumptions explicitly. The extension construction,
parameter completeness and census remain written proofs.

A bounded search for the exact general formula and reads of the
related 2-power-order papers have not established its publication
