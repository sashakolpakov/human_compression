# Published criteria applied to the odd-prime family

This is an attribution adapter to the frozen phase49 proof, dated 10 October 2026. It does not replace its retained construction or exact group calculations. Write P=P(p,α,k), q=p^α, and use phase49’s marked rotation groups R=⟨A,B,C⟩, F=⟨A,B⟩ and V=⟨B,C⟩. All isomorphisms below preserve the displayed generator positions.

## The order of the smallest regular cover

The retained P is atomic, has chiral facets and chiral vertex-figures, and has middle generator b of exact order q. Cunningham–Pellicer Proposition 4.2 therefore identifies X(P)=⟨b^q′⟩ with q/q′ prime. Since q is a power of p, q′=q/p and |X(P)|=p. Lemma 2.6 identifies X(P) with the kernel of the rotation-group map R→P. Consequently |R|=p|P|=4p³q. Proposition 4.2(b), Lemma 2.7 and its dual similarly multiply the facet and vertex rotation orders 2pq by p, yielding |F|=|V|=2p²q. Phase49’s direct coordinate derivation proves the same consequences without invoking those chirality results; that independence of proof does not create a priority claim. [Checked source, pp. 8–9 and 20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.04566v1).

## The cover Q is an instance of the facet-cover criterion

Phase49 constructs T=F⋊⟨t⟩, marked by (A,B,c_T) with c_T=tAB. It proves that this is a directly regular rank-four polytope, with c_T of order two, and that its facet group is exactly the marked F. R is a regular maniplex; its facet is the directly regular polyhedron with this same marking F. Thus there is a marked isomorphism, and hence a covering, from every facet of T onto the corresponding facet of R. Regularity transports the base-facet identification to every flag.

Apply Cunningham–Hubard Proposition 6.5 with P1=T and P2=R. The first input is a polytope; the second is a premaniplex; both are regular and hence admissible for the one-vertex symmetry type graph. The flagwise facet-cover hypothesis is the identification just proved. The resulting mix T⋄R, canonically the same marked object as Q=R⋄T, is polytopal. [2025 primary text, Proposition 6.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19334v1).

The corresponding older route is completely algebraic. In Q≤R×T, the facet generated by (A,A),(B,B) is the diagonal graph of the identity on F. The natural marked epimorphism Q→T is therefore injective on that facet. T has the C+ property, and Q satisfies the required rotation relations as a subgroup of a product. Breda D’Azevedo–Jones–Schulte Lemma 3.2 supplies the C+ property for Q. This is the quotient mechanism behind their Lemma 3.3/Remark 3.4 and Cunningham’s later Proposition 3.10. It uses no claim that the nonpolytopal R has the face-poset properties required by a particular older definition of “prepolytope.” [2011 primary paper, §3](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-2011-033-4), [variance manuscript, Proposition 3.10](https://gabrielcunningham.com/VarianceGroups.pdf).

Both coordinate factors carry compatible mirror automorphisms, so their restriction to the generated mix gives direct regularity. Duality gives Q′. The facet criterion alone determines neither the size of Q nor the degree of Q→R: phase49 must still identify the largest common marked quotient by its W calculation. Once that degree is p and R is nonpolytopal, every proper regular quotient of Q covering P factors through R and has index dividing p. The only proper candidate is R. This is the same prime-index argument used for the published degree-three example. [Cunningham, §4](https://gabrielcunningham.com/WhichChiralPolytopes.pdf).

## The shared mix H is another instance

Phase49 proves that the restrictions Q→R and Q′→R are isomorphisms on each end parabolic. In particular, the two facets are canonically isomorphic as marked groups through the same F. The facet of H=Q⋄Q′ is the graph of this marked isomorphism. Hence the projection H→Q is injective on that facet. Both Q and Q′ are now polytopes, so the published identical-facet mix result applies directly; alternatively apply the same group quotient lemma to H→Q. The 2025 facet-cover proposition applies too. The componentwise mirror proves regularity. The vertex injection is useful for the later interval analysis, but facet injection already suffices for this polytopality step.

Thus H’s polytopality is not a new general preservation theorem. Its order and interval require additional information. With the two conjugation characters of the degree-p kernels distinct, their subdirect product over R cannot be a diagonal line in C_p². The full C_p² kernel, its two invariant axes and the nonadmissibility of the entire kernel yield phase49’s exact finite-interval conclusion. No cited facet-cover criterion on its own classifies those admissible kernels.

## What cannot be inferred from this adapter

It gives no assertion about every minimal cover outside H, the other four atomic families, or arbitrary nonpolytopal monodromy groups. It does not establish historical novelty of the calculations or that the broader conjecture remains open as of this date. It also does not convert any of these geometric arguments into Lean proofs. The complete computations and retained inputs remain charged exactly as in phase49 and its independent phase59 review.
