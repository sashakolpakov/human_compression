# A finite regular hypertope for every even source parameter

This note concerns the second proposed family in Fernandes–Leemans–Ivić Weiss, *Hexagonal extensions of toroidal maps and hypermaps* (2018), §8 and Problem 9.2. It is separate from the frozen fixed-parameter-six construction.

**Theorem.** For every even integer \(s\ge2\), there exists a finite regular rank-four hypertope with the source's ordered toroidal parameters
\[
 ((3,0),(2,0),(1,1),(s,0)).
\]
The construction is explicit, using fixed integral permutation-voltage data and reduction modulo \(m=s/2\). Its four maximal parabolic orders, with omitted types \(0,1,2,3\), are
\[
 36,\qquad12s^2,\qquad48,\qquad108.
\]
In particular this is a family with genuinely varying exact torus parameter. No assertion for all odd \(s\), formula for the full group order, equality with the universal group, complete answer to Problem 9.2, or priority claim is included.

## 1. The literal fixed data

The file phase33_L003_even_voltage.json contains three permutations \(p_x,p_y,p_z\) on a set \(\Omega\) of size \(648\) and three integer vectors \(v_x,v_y,v_z\). They define right actions
\[
 (i,t)g=(p_g(i),t+v_g(i))\quad(i\in\Omega,\ t\in\mathbb Z).
\]
Write these transformations as \(g_x,g_y,g_z\). Double the state set and define
\[
 X=\operatorname{diag}(g_x,g_x^{-1}),\quad
 Y=\operatorname{diag}(g_y,g_y^{-1}),\quad
 Z=\operatorname{diag}(g_z,g_z^{-1}).
\]
Let \(r_0\) swap the two copies, and put
\[
 r_1=r_0X,\qquad r_2=r_0Y,\qquad r_3=r_0Z.
\]
All \(r_i\) are involutions. Let \(\widetilde G\) be their group on \(1296\) copies of \(\mathbb Z\). Reducing the integer coordinate modulo \(m\ge1\) gives a finite permutation group \(G_m\) on \(1296m\) points. Modulus one means simply dropping the voltage coordinate. Every \(G_m\) maps onto \(G_1\) by dropping that coordinate.

Set \(x=r_0r_1=X,\ y=r_0r_2=Y,\ z=r_0r_3=Z\). The companion standard-library checker verifies the following nine integral relations at every one of the \(1296\) states:
\[
 x^6=y^3=z^2=(zx)^3=(zy)^6=(x^{-1}y)^2=1,
\]
\[
 (y^{-1}x^{-2})^3=(zx^3)^2
 =(x^{-1}z(y^{-1}z)^2)(zx(zy)^2)=1. \tag{1}
\]
These are exactly the source presentation with the last torus relation omitted.

The fourth parameter word is
\[
 T=y(zy)^2.
\]
Its underlying permutation has exact order two. The checker verifies that \(A=T^2\) is a pure integer translation. With
\[
 u=y^{-1}z,\qquad B=u^{-1}Au,
\]
the two translation vectors at zero-based states \(6,2\) have rows
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}v_A(6)&v_B(6)\\v_A(2)&v_B(2)\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}, \tag{2}
\]
whose determinant is \(-1\).

Consequently \(A,B\) generate a copy of \(\mathbb Z^2\), and their coordinate reductions generate a copy of \(C_m^2\) for every \(m\). The latter statement is uniform: the inverse of the displayed integer matrix is again integral, so its reduction is invertible over \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\), including composite and even moduli.

Also \(T\) has exact order \(2m\) in \(G_m\). An identity power must be even by the underlying permutation. Its even powers are powers of \(A\), and \(A\) has exact order \(m\) because one translation coordinate is \(-1\).

## 2. Three fixed parabolics and one variable parabolic

For a subset \(I\) of the four types, write \(G_{m,I}\) for the subgroup generated by its marks. Let \(P_{m,i}=G_{m,\{0,1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}}\), and use a tilde for the corresponding integer-action groups.

The checker enumerates \(\widetilde P_0,\widetilde P_2,\widetilde P_3\) exactly over the integers. At every step it compares transformations with the same underlying permutation and rejects different translations. Closure terminates with orders
\[
 |\widetilde P_0|=36,\qquad
 |\widetilde P_2|=48,\qquad
 |\widetilde P_3|=108. \tag{3}
\]
Their underlying-permutation maps are injective, so they inject into every modulus and the maps \(P_{m,i}\to P_{1,i}\) are isomorphisms for \(i\ne1\).

The variable parabolic \(P_{m,1}\) contains \(y,z\), and hence contains \(A,B\). Its underlying-permutation image \(P_{1,1}\) has order \(48\), verified by full closure. Both \(A,B\) lie in the kernel of that projection. Equation (2) therefore gives
\[
 |P_{m,1}|\ge48m^2. \tag{4}
\]

For the reverse inequality use source equation (2.3), with its local rotations
\[
 (u,v)=(y^{-1}z,y^{-1}).
\]
The standard torus translation word is \(v^{-1}u^{-2}=y(zy)^2=T\). In modulus \(m\), its power \(T^{2m}\) is the identity. Together with the triangle relations in (1), this makes the full three-involution parabolic a quotient of the regular toroidal-map group with parameter \((2m,0)\). That full group has order \(12(2m)^2=48m^2\). Combining with (4) proves exact equality and a marked isomorphism:
\[
 P_{m,1}\cong\operatorname{Aut}\{6,3\}_{(2m,0)}
\]
with the local type ordering supplied by the displayed rotations.

The other three local adapters to source equation (2.3) are \((zy,zx)\), \((x^{-1},x^{-1}z)\), and \((x,y)\) for omitted types \(0,2,3\), giving parameters \((1,1),(2,0),(3,0)\). Their torus words are the last three relators of (1). Their full toroidal orders equal (3), so their marked quotient maps also are isomorphisms. This identification uses both the exact presentation and its attained order, not order alone.

The toroidal order formula \(12(a^2+ab+b^2)\) and source equation (2.3)'s lattice interpretation are retained standard inputs. The growing local group is controlled by the unimodular minor (2); it is not assumed injective under \(G_m\to G_1\).

## 3. The exact base geometry

The literal checker enumerates the underlying-permutation group \(G_1\) and obtains order \(1296\). It closes all sixteen marked subgroups and verifies all \(256\) intersection equalities
\[
 G_{1,I}\cap G_{1,J}=G_{1,I\cap J}. \tag{5}
\]
The maximal parabolics have orders \(36,48,48,108\).

Flag transitivity is checked separately. For every type \(i\) and every set \(J\) of two or three other types, it counts the \(P_{1,i}\)-cosets incident with the base flag of types \(J\). It obtains exactly
\[
 \left[\bigcap_{j\in J}P_{1,j}:
       P_{1,i}\cap\bigcap_{j\in J}P_{1,j}\right] \tag{6}
\]
in all sixteen cases. The counting algorithm and its geometric meaning are those proved in §3 of the frozen fixed-parameter note: choose representatives in one finite parabolic and test membership in the finite products \(P_{1,i}P_{1,j}\). Equality in (6), by induction on flag size, moves every flag to its base flag and proves typed coset Helly. Thus (5) is not being used alone to assert that the coset system is a geometry.

## 4. Transport with one exceptional maximal parabolic

Here is the strengthening needed because the fourth residue grows.

**Lemma.** Let a generator-preserving epimorphism \(\pi:G\to\overline G\) map corresponding marked generating sets indexed by a type set \(I\). Suppose the downstairs marked family has full intersection property and typed coset Helly. If \(\pi\) is injective on every maximal marked parabolic except possibly one, then the upstairs marked family also has both properties.

**Proof of intersection property.** A proper subset \(S\subsetneq I\) lies in an injective maximal parabolic whenever it omits a nonexceptional type. The only proper subset not protected this way is \(I\setminus\{e\}\), where \(e\) is the exceptional type. Given two proper subsets \(S,T\), if both are unprotected then they are equal and their intersection equality is tautological. Otherwise choose the protected one, say \(S\). For \(g\in G_S\cap G_T\), downstairs intersection property provides \(c\in G_{S\cap T}\) with the same image. Injectivity on \(G_S\) yields \(g=c\). Cases involving the full type set are immediate.

**Proof of typed Helly.** Empty and singleton typed coset families already have a common point. A family with at least two types includes a nonexceptional type \(k\). Normalize its coset to \(P_k\) and represent each other coset \(P_i g_i\) with \(g_i\in P_k\), using pairwise intersection. Downstairs Helly supplies a common image point in \(\pi(P_k)\), with a unique lift \(h\in P_k\). For each \(i\), the image of \(hg_i^{-1}\) lies in \(\pi(P_k)\cap\pi(P_i)\). Downstairs intersection property makes this the image of the common marked subgroup. Its preimage there is also in \(P_k\), so injectivity on \(P_k\) gives \(hg_i^{-1}\in P_i\). Thus \(h\) is common to all the original cosets. No injectivity on the exceptional parabolic is used. \(\square\)

The subgroup formulation works for arbitrary groups and type sets. A separately reviewed Lean module, OneExceptionalParabolic.lean, proves the abstract marked intersection and typed-Helly transport statements. It does not encode the voltage tables, local toroidal identification or resulting geometry.

Apply the lemma to \(G_m\to G_1\), taking exceptional type \(1\). Section2 proves injectivity on the other three maximal parabolics. Therefore every \(G_m\) has full intersection property and typed coset Helly.

## 5. Geometric conclusion and source scope

Typed Helly makes the standard coset system a flag-transitive geometry. Full marked intersection property makes the chamber stabilizer trivial and panel stabilizers the indicated groups of order two, so the geometry is thin. In every residue, the remaining marked involutions generate its chamber-regular residue group and connect its chamber graph. Hence the geometry is residually connected. Thinness and chamber connectivity also show that a type-preserving automorphism fixing one chamber is trivial, so the group \(G_m\) is its full type-preserving automorphism group.

Section2 identifies its exact four toroidal residue groups and markings. Substituting \(s=2m\) proves the theorem. Distinct \(m\) have different fourth residue orders, so these are infinitely many distinct parameter values, rather than many quotients with one fixed parameter.

The source is [Fernandes–Leemans–Ivić Weiss (2018)](https://ria.ua.pt/bitstream/10773/23862/1/2018DFW.pdf), equations (2.3)–(2.4), §8, Table8 and Problem9.2. This is a positive construction for the complete even-parameter subfamily. It does not determine the universal groups' orders; indeed the frozen earlier result proves that the universal regular object at \(s=6\) is infinite, although a finite quotient with these residues exists. All odd parameters are outside the uniform claim here. The original table supplies small odd examples, but adjoining them does not amount to a proof for every odd parameter.
