## 1. Fix the affine convention before checking any words

Let V be a finite-dimensional vector space over F_p, and let a finite group G act linearly on the left. Use

\[
(v,g)(w,h)=(v+g w,gh),\qquad (v,g)^{-1}=(-g^{-1}v,g^{-1}).
\]

Write pi:V semidirect G -> G for projection. A generator rho_i=(a_i,s_i) is an involution exactly when s_i^2=1 and a_i+s_i a_i=0; exact order two additionally excludes the identity. Two generators commute exactly when their projected generators commute and a_i+s_i a_j=a_j+s_j a_i. A right-action implementation must explicitly translate these formulas; a permutation list and a multiplication table alone do not fix which convention its vector code uses.

For every index set I, H_I means the subgroup generated by the actual affine generators with indices in I. Its projection is P_I=<s_i:i in I>, and its translation kernel is K_I=H_I intersect V. In particular, K_I is computed from H_I; it is not defined as the span of its generating translations a_i.

## 2. The exact intersection criterion, with arbitrary projected subgroups

Let A,B be arbitrary subgroups of V semidirect G. Put P=pi(A), Q=pi(B), K_A=A intersect V and K_B=B intersect V. For each g in P choose an actual lift (a(g),g) in A, and similarly (b(g),g) in B for g in Q. The fibers are exactly

\[
A\cap\pi^{-1}(g)=(a(g)+K_A)\times\{g\},\quad
B\cap\pi^{-1}(g)=(b(g)+K_B)\times\{g\}.
\]

To verify the first formula, two elements with the same projection have quotient (v-a(g),1), and left multiplication by any translation in K_A realizes every vector in that coset. Normality of the kernel shows that K_A is P-invariant; similarly K_B is Q-invariant.

Consequently define

\[
R=\{g\in P\cap Q:a(g)-b(g)\in K_A+K_B\},\qquad T=K_A\cap K_B.
\]

Then R=pi(A intersect B), and the nonempty fiber over each g in R is a coset of T. In particular R is a subgroup, even though it was described by a membership condition, and

\[
|A\cap B|=|R|p^{\dim T}.
\]

The condition is independent of the choices of a(g),b(g). If C is a *known subgroup* of A intersect B, then

\[
A\cap B=C\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
R=\pi(C)\ \text{ and }\ T=C\cap V.
\]

For the reverse implication, every C-fiber is contained in the corresponding intersection fiber, and both are cosets of the same translation space. Thus they are equal. Equal cardinalities of these subspaces are enough only after the relevant containments are established.

Knowing only the orders/dimensions of P,Q,K_A,K_B does not determine R or T. Even with K_A=K_B=0, the fibers can differ: in F_3 semidirect C_2 with inversion action, <(0,s)> and <(1,s)> both project onto C_2 but intersect trivially. This is the smallest model of the additional affine obstruction that must be checked.

## 3. Projected intersection property: a useful conditional simplification

Suppose the projected marked group already has the intersection property, so P_I intersect P_J=P_(I intersect J). The actual marked subgroup H_(I intersect J) is contained in both H_I and H_J and maps onto this projected intersection. Therefore the previous R is automatically P_(I intersect J), and

\[
H_I\cap H_J=H_{I\cap J}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
K_I\cap K_J=K_{I\cap J}.
\]

This is conditional on the relevant projected IP. It applies locally inside a proved rank-three projected C-group, for example. **It cannot be used at the top level merely because each projected end is a C-group.** In the intended nonpolytopal base, the projected top intersection has an extra coset; that coset requires a separate fiber test.

## 4. The index-two excess case

Suppose C=<the common marked generators> lies in A intersect B, with projected subgroup P_C, and the exact projected intersection is

\[
P\cap Q=P_C\sqcup P_Cz.
\]

Here z is an explicitly verified representative outside P_C, and there are no other cosets. The fibers over every c in P_C are compatible because there is a common marked C-lift. Pick actual lifts (a(z),z) in A and (b(z),z) in B. Then

\[
A\cap B=C
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
K_A\cap K_B=K_C\ \text{ and }\ a(z)-b(z)\notin K_A+K_B.
\]

Indeed, for c in P_C take a common lift (d(c),c) from C. Multiplying it by the two z-lifts gives fibers over cz whose difference is c(a(z)-b(z)). Since c preserves K_A+K_B, compatibility over cz is equivalent to compatibility over z. This proves the whole-coset exclusion from one representative, rather than treating one checked word as an unexplained proxy for all elements.

For more than two projected cosets, one must check every P_C-coset representative outside P_C (or supply a theorem proving their equivalence). No fixed number of tested words suffices without that exact projected decomposition.

## 5. Computing the actual kernels, without enumerating the large affine group

Let H=<rho_i:i in I> with finite projected group P. Enumerate P and choose, for each g in P, a word w_g in the projected generators, with w_1 empty. Evaluate the same word in the affine generators to obtain h_g=(v_g,g). For every g and every generator i form

\[
k_{g,i}=v_g+g a_i-v_{g s_i}.
\]

This is the translation vector of h_g rho_i h_(g s_i)^(-1). Schreier rewriting proves that these elements generate ker(H -> P): an arbitrary word with trivial projection telescopes into these generators and their inverses. Because the kernel lies in the additive elementary abelian group V, its group closure is exactly their F_p-linear span. Thus

\[
K_I=\operatorname{span}_{F_p}\{k_{g,i}:g\in P_I,\ i\in I\}.
\]

This proof also gives actual lifts for every projected fiber and proves the kernel space is P_I-invariant. It requires a complete verified enumeration of P_I, correct projected multiplication, correct affine word evaluation, and genuine transversal words. Merely taking an orbit of one displayed relation is insufficient to prove that the resulting space is the *whole* kernel, unless another theorem establishes completeness.

## 6. Passing to an invariant module quotient

Suppose the construction first uses M and then passes to V=M/W, with W a G-invariant subspace. Let tilde-H_I be the marked subgroup in M semidirect G, and let tilde-K_I be its translation kernel. Its image H_I has

\[
K_I=(\widetilde K_I+W)/W,
\quad
\dim K_I=\dim\widetilde K_I-\dim(\widetilde K_I\cap W).
\]

The formula dim(K)=dim(tilde-K)-dim(W) requires W contained in tilde-K. That containment is provable if W is generated by G-conjugates of a genuine projected relator translation and the full marked group projects onto G; it still must be stated and checked.

For an endpoint comparison,

\[
\dim(K_A\cap K_B)=
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde K_A+W)+
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde K_B+W)-
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde K_A+\widetilde K_B+W)-\dim W.
\]

Containment K_C contained in K_A intersect K_B comes from marked subgroup containment, not from a coincidental rank. Fiber compatibility over z becomes

\[
\widetilde a(z)-\widetilde b(z)
\in\widetilde K_A+\widetilde K_B+W.
\]

The quotient can create intersections: the two coordinate lines in F_3^2 meet trivially, but after quotienting by the line <e_1-e_2> their images coincide. Therefore testing the original unquotiented kernel intersection alone is unsound.

Exact row reduction over F_p supplies a finite certificate for every rank and membership statement. A vector-space nonmembership result may alternatively be witnessed by a functional annihilating the comparison space and taking a nonzero value on the tested vector. Dimensions of the two separate end kernels, without their joint span or an equivalent intersection computation, do not certify IP.

## 7. A proof of the recursive string-IP criterion

Let rho_0,...,rho_(n-1) be nonidentity involutions with rho_i rho_j=rho_j rho_i whenever |i-j|>=2. Put S={0,...,n-1}, H_I=<rho_i:i in I>. For n>=2 assume the endpoint subgroups

\[
A=H_{S\setminus\{n-1\}},\quad
B=H_{S\setminus\{0\}}
\]

each have IP in their inherited marking, and A intersect B=M=H_(S without {0,n-1}). Then the whole marked group has IP.

Here is a complete subgroup proof. Set L_j=<rho_0,...,rho_(j-1)> and R_i=<rho_(i+1),...,rho_(n-1)>, allowing empty groups. For i<j, L_j lies in A and R_i lies in B. Intersecting first with M inside the respective endpoint groups and then using IP in M gives

\[
L_j\cap R_i=\langle\rho_{i+1},...,\rho_{j-1}\rangle.
\]

The same reasoning gives L_i intersect R_i=1. The factors L_i and R_i commute by the string relations, so the maximal parabolic P_i=H_(S without {i}) is their internal direct product. Both factors inherit IP from an endpoint group, and an internal direct product with disjoint marked sets has IP componentwise. Thus every P_i has IP.

For i<j and x in P_i intersect P_j, write x=lr=uv with l in L_i, r in R_i, u in L_j, v in R_j. Since L_i is contained in L_j and R_j is contained in R_i,

\[
l^{-1}u=rv^{-1}\in L_j\cap R_i.
\]

The preceding formula gives x in H_(S without {i,j}). The reverse containment is immediate; hence all maximal parabolic intersections are the expected ones.

Finally take arbitrary proper index subsets I,J. Choose i not in I and j not in J. If i=j, both subgroups lie in the C-group P_i. Otherwise x in H_I intersect H_J lies in P_i intersect P_j=H_(S without {i,j}). IP in P_i first puts x in H_(I without {j}); IP in P_j then puts x in H_(I intersect J). The reverse containment always holds. Cases I=S or J=S are immediate. This proves full IP, not merely an endpoint intersection identity.

Applied recursively, it suffices to check exact endpoint intersections for every contiguous interval, starting from exact order-two singletons. For four generators there are three intervals of length two, two of length three, and one full interval of length four. One may omit a length-two computation only after verifying its two generators are distinct nonidentity involutions, which gives their cyclic-subgroup intersection directly. All nonadjacent commutations still need to be verified.

For the proposed affine construction, each such endpoint intersection is to be proved using Sections 2–6. Only intervals with proved projected IP permit the simplification of Section 3. The full four-generator interval needs the additional excess-coset test of Section 4 when its projected base has that defect.

## 8. Other obligations distinct from IP

The review must separately verify exact adjacent-product orders (the Schlaefli type), finiteness, generation of the claimed projected base, the actual full translation-kernel dimension, and exact marked homomorphisms. For an element (v,g) with g of exact order m,

\[
(v,g)^m=(v+gv+\cdots+g^{m-1}v,1).
\]

In an elementary abelian p-kernel its order is m when this vector vanishes and mp otherwise. A relation proving an upper bound alone does not establish the asserted type.

An all-family pullback needs a proof that the subgroup generated by the paired marked generators is the full claimed fiber product, and corresponding statements for the parabolics used in IP. A whole fiber product can be larger than its marked generated subgroup. One sufficient route is to show that the generated subgroup maps onto the fixed cover and contains the entire kernel of the changing base -> fixed base map. A displayed marked relation may provide that kernel, but its normal generation and its behavior in the fixed cover must both be proved. Each lifted intersection and adjacent order remains a separate obligation; no uniform statement follows merely from one finite base calculation.

For the eventual minimal-cover inference, let a finite regular marked cover H -> G have odd kernel and let G fail IP. Among normal subgroups of that kernel whose marked quotients are regular covers, choose an inclusion-maximal one. There are finitely many, and the identity subgroup is available. Its quotient is a minimal regular cover over G: any further regular marked quotient would correspond to a larger admissible normal subgroup. Its degree is an odd divisor of the original degree and is greater than one because G is not regular. Therefore it differs from a known degree-two minimal cover of the same G. This establishes existence of a second minimal cover conditionally; it does **not** identify the odd minimal degree or prove that the original large affine cover is itself minimal.

The geometry/marked-group correspondence and the source facts certifying the changing bases, their defect, and the existing degree-two cover remain explicit inputs to be checked in the frozen package. They are not proved by the affine ranks alone.
