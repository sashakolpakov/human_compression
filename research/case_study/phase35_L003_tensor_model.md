# An integral tensor model with every exact Fibonacci parameter

This note concerns the presentation in Fernandes–Leemans–Ivić Weiss, *Hexagonal extensions of toroidal maps and hypermaps* (2018), §8 and Problem 9.2. Write $P_{\mathrm{omit}}$ for its **rotational** presentation with the last torus relation omitted. This notation does not denote the source's parameter-nine group.

**Theorem.** There are three explicit matrices $x,y,z\in\mathrm{GL}_8(\mathbb Z)$ satisfying all nine defining relations of $P_{\mathrm{omit}}$, and an integral involution $r_0$ simultaneously inverting them. Put $r_1=r_0x$, $r_2=r_0y$, and $r_3=r_0z$.

For every integer $s\ge3$, reducing these matrices modulo the Fibonacci number $F_s$ gives a finite group generated by four nonidentity involutions in which the source word $T=y(zy)^2$ has **exact order $s$**. For every $s\ge4$, its maximal marked subgroups, with omitted types $0,1,2,3$, are marked-isomorphic to the source's four prescribed toroidal groups and have orders
\[
36,\qquad12s^2,\qquad48,\qquad108.
\]

The theorem does not assert the intersection property or typed coset Helly for the ambient marking. Thus it supplies finite groups with every indicated exact parameter and, for $s\ge4$, every indicated local marked group; it does not by itself supply a hypertope for each parameter or resolve Problem 9.2. It also does not identify $P_{\mathrm{omit}}$ with its tensor image.

## 1. Literal matrices and conventions

Let
\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
R=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad
S=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad
N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For a triple $A=(A_1,A_2,A_3)$ and a permutation $\pi\in S_3$, write $[A;\pi]$ for the following transformation on row tensors:
\[
v_1\otimes v_2\otimes v_3\longmapsto
w_1\otimes w_2\otimes w_3,
\qquad w_{\pi(i)}=v_iA_i.
\tag{1}
\]
Let $c$ be the cycle $1\mapsto3\mapsto2\mapsto1$ and $f=(1\ 3)$. Define
\[
x=-[(D,R,R);c],\qquad y=[(I,I,I);c],\qquad
z=[(S,Q,S);1],\qquad r_0=[(I,I,I);f].
\tag{2}
\]
All factors have determinant $\pm1$, so all these matrices are invertible over $\mathbb Z$ and over every quotient ring. The leading minus sign in $x$ is a global scalar on the eight-dimensional tensor product.

These definitions are the complete construction. Their discovery used coordinates extracted from a finite parameter-five image, but no extraction data, identification of that finite group, or computer-algebra presentation equivalence is needed to check (2).

## 2. Exact integral identities

Ordinary multiplication of the literal eight-dimensional matrices proves
\[
x^6=y^3=z^2=(zx)^3=(zy)^6=(x^{-1}y)^2=I,
\]
\[
(y^{-1}x^{-2})^3=(zx^3)^2
=(x^{-1}z(y^{-1}z)^2)(zx(zy)^2)=I.
\tag{3}
\]
These are precisely the source's nine fixed rotational relations. The final parameter relation is $T^s=I$, where
\[
T=y(zy)^2.
\]
Also
\[
r_0^2=I,\qquad r_0xr_0=x^{-1},\qquad
r_0yr_0=y^{-1},\qquad r_0zr_0=z^{-1}.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently the four $r_i$ are involutions. The important translation identities are
\[
T=N^{-1}\otimes I\otimes N,\qquad
B:=y^{-1}Ty=I\otimes N\otimes N^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
They commute.

The companion standard-library program verifies every entry of (3)–(5) over the integers. In particular it verifies all $9\cdot64=576$ entries of the nine relators, without reduction modulo a prime or use of numerical approximations. It also checks that the four integral involutions are nonidentity. The identities themselves are finite exact calculations, not empirical evidence for an unbounded assertion.

There is no accidental disappearance of a mark in a quotient ring $\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$, $M\ge2$: the marks $r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3$ have nonidentity factor permutations, respectively $f,cf,cf,f$ under the convention (1) (composition read as the resulting input-to-output map). A nonidentity factor permutation cannot act trivially, by the factor-algebra argument below. Only that each listed permutation is nonidentity is needed.

## 3. Tensor cancellation over composite rings

Let $A$ be a nonzero commutative ring with identity. Inside $\operatorname{End}_A((A^2)^{\otimes3})$ let $\mathcal E_i$ be the full matrix algebra $M_2(A)$ acting on factor $i$ and as the identity on the other two factors. The embeddings are injective, and the three subalgebras are pairwise distinct: for example the elementary matrix that kills one basis vector and sends the other to it acts on different tensor coordinates in the different factors.

**Tensor lemma.** If $U_i\in\mathrm{GL}_2(A)$ and
\[
U_1\otimes U_2\otimes U_3=I,
\]
then each $U_i$ is a scalar unit. More generally, equality of two invertible pure tensors implies that their corresponding factors differ by scalar units. A pure tensor cannot equal a transformation with a nonidentity factor permutation.

Indeed, conjugation by the pure tensor induces conjugation by $U_i$ on $\mathcal E_i$. If the tensor is the identity, each $U_i$ commutes with every element of $M_2(A)$. Commuting with the matrix units forces $U_i=u_iI$, and invertibility forces $u_i\in A^\times$. The equality case follows after multiplication by the inverse tensor. Finally a pure tensor preserves each $\mathcal E_i$ individually under conjugation, whereas a transformation with factor permutation $\pi$ sends these distinct subalgebras according to $\pi$.

This proof uses neither a field assumption nor division by the dimension. In particular it applies to every composite Fibonacci modulus.

## 4. Every exact parameter

Use $F_0=0$, $F_1=1$, and $F_{n+1}=F_n+F_{n-1}$. Direct induction gives
\[
N^k=\begin{pmatrix}F_{k-1}&F_k\\F_k&F_{k+1}\end{pmatrix}
\qquad(k\ge1).
\tag{6}
\]
Fix $s\ge3$ and work over $A_s=\mathbb Z/F_s\mathbb Z$. Since $F_{s+1}-F_{s-1}=F_s$, (6) gives
\[
N^s=F_{s-1}I\quad\text{in }M_2(A_s).
\tag{7}
\]
This scalar is a unit: $N$ is invertible, or equivalently consecutive Fibonacci numbers are coprime. Equation (5) therefore makes $T^s=I$; the scalar from the first factor cancels the scalar from the third.

Conversely, if $T^k=I$, the tensor lemma applied to $(N^{-k},I,N^k)$ implies that $N^k$ is scalar. Its off-diagonal entry is $F_k$, so $F_s\mid F_k$. For $1\le k<s$ one has $0<F_k<F_s$; hence no such $k$ is possible. Thus $T$ has exact order $s$. It follows as well that the image of $N$ in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(A_s)$, meaning invertible matrices modulo scalar units, has exact order $s$.

This includes $s=3$, when $F_s=2$. It is an all-integer proof, independent of the checker's finite sample $3\le s\le25$. Over $\mathbb Z$ the same argument using $F_k\ne0$ also proves that the integral tensor image is infinite.

## 5. Three protected local groups

For the group generated by the four integral marks write $P_i=\langle r_j:j\ne i\rangle$. Exact integer closure in the checker gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
i&0&2&3\\\hline
|P_i|&36&48&108\\
\max\{|g_{ab}|:g\in P_i\}&1&2&1\\
|P_i\bmod3|&36&48&108\\
|P_i\bmod2|&36&12&108.
\end{array}
\tag{8}
\]
Closure is by literal integer matrices: the program starts with the identity, repeatedly multiplies by the three generators, and terminates with precisely the stated finite set. Thus (8) also certifies the upper bounds over $\mathbb Z$.

If $M\ge5$, every difference between corresponding entries of two matrices in one of these sets has absolute value at most four. Congruence modulo $M$ therefore implies equality. The explicit modulus-three check handles $M=3$. Now $F_4=3$ and $F_s\ge5$ for $s\ge5$, so all three groups embed unchanged in every reduction with $s\ge4$.

The local rotational adapters to source equation (2.3) are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{omitted type}&\text{local rotations}&\text{toroidal parameter}\\\hline
0&(zy,zx)&(1,1)\\
2&(x^{-1},x^{-1}z)&(2,0)\\
3&(x,y)&(3,0).
\end{array}
\tag{9}
\]
Their defining torus relations occur in (3). The source's standard full toroidal group order is $12(a^2+ab+b^2)$. Accordingly these local presentations surject onto the displayed subgroups with the same order, proving marked isomorphisms. This uses the actual local presentation and its achieved order, rather than identification from an order alone.

The modulus-two failure in (8) is real. The $s=3$ reduction has the correct order of $T$ but loses the required order-48 local group; it is not being claimed as a parameter-three hypertope.

## 6. The growing local group

Fix $s\ge4$, put $M=F_s>2$, and reduce all matrices modulo $M$. Here the exceptional group is
\[
P_1=\langle r_0,r_2,r_3\rangle=\langle r_0,y,z\rangle.
\]
By (5), for integers $a,b$ its translation subgroup contains
\[
T^aB^b=N^{-a}\otimes N^b\otimes N^{a-b}.
\tag{10}
\]
The tensor lemma and the exact projective order from §4 show that (10) is the identity only if $s\mid a$ and $s\mid b$. Conversely those two divisibilities make it the identity. Thus
\[
\langle T,B\rangle\cong C_s\times C_s.
\tag{11}
\]

The pure factor permutations $\langle r_0,y\rangle$ form $S_3$. They normalize (11), because they permute the three exponents in (10), whose sum is zero. By the final part of the tensor lemma their intersection with (11) is trivial. Hence
\[
J:=\langle T,B,r_0,y\rangle\cong(C_s\times C_s)\rtimes S_3,
\qquad |J|=6s^2.
\tag{12}
\]
This description permits a nonfaithful action of $S_3$ on the translation group; faithfulness of that action is not required for the order calculation.

We have $z\notin J$. Indeed, the factor permutation of $z$ is trivial, so equality with an element of $J$ would force that element's permutation part to be trivial. The pure tensor equality would then make its first factor $S$ a scalar unit times a power of $N$. Every integer power of the symmetric matrix $N$ is symmetric, including negative powers. But
\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
is not symmetric modulo $M>2$. Multiplication by a scalar unit does not change symmetry, giving the contradiction. Therefore the two disjoint cosets $J$ and $Jz$ give
\[
|P_1|\ge12s^2.
\tag{13}
\]
No normality of $J$ is needed here.

For the opposite bound use the local rotations
\[
(u,v)=(y^{-1}z,y^{-1}).
\]
The source's torus word is $v^{-1}u^{-2}=y(zy)^2=T$. Relations (3) and $T^s=I$ make $P_1$ a marked quotient of the standard toroidal-map group with parameter $(s,0)$, of order $12s^2$. Equation (13) proves equality and a marked isomorphism. This completes the theorem.

Run the original checker from its directory:

It requires only Python's standard library and refuses to overwrite a report. It verifies the literal integral identities, exact protected local closures and reductions, and the 23 stated Fibonacci samples. It does not test global subgroup intersections or coset incidence. Sections 3–6 prove the uniform modulus and growing-local-group claims; they do not infer them from the finite samples.

The matrices are a compact representation of the unfilled presentation and its finite quotients. They do not prove that this representation is faithful, nor that the finite quotients are the universal groups listed in the source. Establishing the ambient intersection property and typed coset Helly remains a separate obligation before any all-parameter hypertope conclusion. No current-status or novelty assertion is made here.
