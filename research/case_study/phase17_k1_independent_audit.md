# Independent completeness audit of the first-excess classification

10 October 2026. Written mathematical review of
[phase17_k1_classification.md](phase17_k1_classification.md). The
associated JSON binds the exact reviewed proof. No author files were
changed by this reviewer.

**Verdict: PASS under the retained forest canonical-quotient and central-join
theorems.** The proof classifies the ordered nondegenerate string
C-group two-group markings of rank d≥3 and order 2^(2d), with count
5d−10. It does not divide by arbitrary unmarked group isomorphisms.

The canonical quotient supplies a kernel γ3 of order two. It is
central, because a normal subgroup of order two is centralized by
the whole group; hence the group has class three. This justifies
all uses of central collection. The endpoint entries of the
commutator table follow because each endpoint involution inverts its
edge commutator. The neighboring entries agree by the class-three
Hall–Witt identity; possible inverse signs disappear in a central
group of order two. The distant entries follow from the original
string relations. Thus the α and β list is complete, rather than
merely a collection of necessary invariants.

The resulting presentation has at most 2^(2d) elements: its central
kernel has order at most two and its quotient is a quotient of the
canonical path group. The defining commutator relations really do
make every edge commutator commute with every other one: commutation
against that edge commutator is a homomorphism into the central
kernel, and therefore kills the derived subgroup. The later
full-order constructions show that no hidden consistency relation
reduces the parameter space or collapses the kernel.

The two elementary seeds are correct. D16 has the required central
γ3 and only its edge-square parameter. UT4(F2), with its three
successive elementary transvections, has order 64 and the sole
three-marker parameter. Its asserted parabolic entry patterns are
closed under multiplication and are generated by the corresponding
elementary commutators, proving actual IP. The central-join theorem
then supplies each single-coordinate cover with exactly the claimed
kernel support and canonical quotient.

The fiber-product argument uses more than its ambient order. A
parameter word for one active support evaluates to that single
kernel coordinate and to the identity in every other cover. Hence
the diagonal markers generate all of the central kernel as well
as the common quotient. They therefore generate the entire fiber
product. After dividing by the kernel of the coordinate-sum map,
every active parameter equals the unique surviving kernel
involution. The full order and the presentation upper bound prove
uniqueness of every ordered parameterized cover.

I checked the parabolic-kernel computation before and after this
central quotient. In the fiber product, a kernel coordinate cannot
occur unless its entire seed support is selected; projecting to
that seed proves the upper inclusion. The isolated parameter words
prove the reverse inclusion. In the quotient, a parabolic contains
the final kernel exactly when some active support is contained in
its marker set. No unjustified assumption that a generated
subgroup equals a full fiber product is used.

The least-support criterion is both necessary and sufficient for
actual IP. If there is a least active support, two parabolics that
both contain the kernel have that support in their common marker
set. Their common quotient image bounds their intersection by
twice the canonical intersection order, attained by the expected
parabolic. If either parabolic avoids the kernel, the same comparison
is injective and again exact. Conversely, two distinct minimal active
supports give two parabolics containing the kernel but a common
marker parabolic avoiding it. This is an actual intersection witness,
not an argument that presumes IP survives a quotient.

Only a single active triple, or an active edge with independently
optional containing triples, can have a least support. This gives
(d−2)+4+4(d−3)=5d−10 and the stated types. Distinct parameter vectors
cannot be identified by a marked isomorphism: it must fix the
unique nonidentity element of γ3, and preserves the displayed
commutator words. Reversal acts on the parameters as stated; its
one fixed marking for odd d and two for even d give the two
Burnside counts. The d=2 case is explicitly outside the main formula.

One scope ambiguity was identified during review and corrected by
the author before the final hash: the ancillary classification of
central covers now explicitly requires a kernel of order two equal
to γ3. Without that order restriction, larger elementary-abelian
central kernels would not be covered by the binary-vector
parameterization. The principal theorem was unaffected.

This review did not rerun the finite presentation census or compile
the Lean module. It makes no independent verification claim for
the source-overlap tables or publication priority. Its mathematical
scope is the complete written classification and counting argument;
the previously reviewed canonical quotient and central attachment
remain explicit dependencies.
