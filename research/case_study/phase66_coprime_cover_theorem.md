# An integral obstruction to coprime covers with injective ends

Private author manuscript, 10 October 2026. The main result concerns a specified construction route and a specified kernel class. It does not assert that the source polytopes have unique minimal regular covers. The finite certificate is an exact integral identity, not a sample of field characteristics. No priority claim or Lean formalization is made.

## 1. General local-complement criterion

Let G be a finite 2-group generated by involutions r0,r1,r2,r3 satisfying the string commutations. Put

    A=⟨r0,r1,r2⟩,  B=⟨r1,r2,r3⟩,  C=⟨r1,r2⟩,  I=A∩B.

Assume the two ends A,B are string C-groups on these marks. Suppose

    I=C⟨z⟩,   [I:C]=2,   [z,C]=1.                (1)

For a right G-module V, write v·g for the action. The affine product convention is

    (v,g)(w,h)=(v·h+w,gh).

Thus translation-conjugating the standard lift of g by t_w=(w,1) gives t_w^−1(0,g)t_w=(w(1−g),g).

**Proposition 1.** For a finite abelian G-module V of odd order, a vector w satisfying

    w·r1=w·r2=w,
    w(1−r3)(1−r0)=0,                            (2)
    w(z−1)≠0                                   (3)

gives a finite regular polytope cover of the marked maniplex G, with odd abelian covering kernel and injective restrictions on both ends. Conversely, every cover with odd abelian kernel and injective restrictions on both ends arises in this way after identifying its split extension and conjugating by its kernel.

Proof. Take the subgroup H of V⋊G generated by

    h0=(0,r0), h1=(0,r1), h2=(0,r2),
    h3=(w(1−r3),r3).

All four are involutions. The facet is the standard A; the vertex is t_w^−1 B t_w because w is C-fixed. The h1,h3 commutation follows from w·r1=w and [r1,r3]=1. The h0,h3 commutation is exactly the middle equality in (2). The remaining string commutation is inherited from A. The two end projections are isomorphisms, and their intersection projects to

    {g∈I : w·g=w}.

By (1) and C-fixity, this subgroup is C precisely when (3) holds. Recursive string-C recognition now gives the intersection property. The projection H→G is onto, with kernel a subgroup of V; hence it is finite, abelian and odd. All the claimed local orders and injectivity are inherited from the end isomorphisms.

For the converse, let K be the odd abelian kernel of a finite cover H→G, injective on each end. The extension splits, and its local complements over A,B are conjugate to the standard ones by elements of K. After one global translation, make the facet standard. The vertex is then t_w^−1 B t_w for some w∈K. The shared marked r1,r2 force w to be C-fixed, the r0,r3 commutation forces (2), and the cover's IP forces (3).

Here splitness and conjugacy can be proved by averaging, so no cohomology vanishing theorem is hidden in the argument. Temporarily use the left-action convention for this verification. If f(g,h)∈K is the normalized two-cocycle of a section, its cocycle identity implies, on summing the last argument over G,

    f(g,h)=g·a(h)−a(gh)+a(g),
    a(g)=|G|^−1 ∑_x f(g,x).

Multiplication by |G| is invertible on K. Adjusting the section by −a gives a homomorphic section. Similarly, a one-cocycle c of any subgroup D≤G satisfies c(g)=b−g·b, where b=|D|^−1∑_x c(x). Thus all local complements are translation conjugates. Rewriting this result in the right-action convention gives exactly the form used above. ∎

## 2. A universal integral certificate

Let P=Z[C\G] be the integral right permutation module on the right cosets Cg, with distinguished basis vector e_C. Define

    y=e_C(1−r3)(1−r0),      d=e_C(z−1),
    M=⟨y·g : g∈G⟩_Z ≤ P.                       (4)

**Proposition 2.** If 2^a d∈M for some a≥0, then no cover in Proposition 1 exists. More generally, over a finite field F_p of odd characteristic, such a cover exists with elementary abelian p-kernel if and only if the image of d is nonzero in P_p/(y F_pG), where P_p=F_p[C\G].

Proof. Any C-fixed vector w in any right G-module determines a unique module map P→V by e_C·g↦w·g. It sends y to w(1−r3)(1−r0). If the latter vanishes, it annihilates M. The assumed integral identity gives 2^a w(z−1)=0. For odd V this forces w(z−1)=0, contradicting (3).

For the field statement, if d vanishes in y F_pG the same argument excludes a vector. Otherwise take V=P_p/(y F_pG) and w the image of e_C. This module is finite, w is C-fixed, its y-relation holds, and its d-image is nonzero. Proposition 1 supplies the cover. This is a genuine necessary-and-sufficient module criterion, independent of a choice of irreducible modules or of a finite field sample. ∎

The concrete certificate below takes a=1 and consists of an explicit equality

    2d = ∑_(n,u) n (y·u),                        (5)

with 55 integer/word pairs. The words use indices 1,2,3,4 for r0,r1,r2,r3 respectively and act on the right in their written order. They are listed without interpretation changes in `phase66_coprime_cover_coefficients.json` and embedded literally in the standalone GAP checker. This is a finite lattice certificate of (5), not a numerical rank estimate.

## 3. The source family and its fixed marked quotient

Take the source family Γ2 from Cunningham–Pellicer, *Tight chiral polytopes*, Table 2 and Theorem 4.8 of arXiv:2009.04566v1. Its parameters are β≥5, q=2^β, h=q/4, and independent signs ε1,ε2∈{−1,1}. The rotation presentation is

    a^8=b^q=c^8=1,
    (ab)^2=(bc)^2=(abc)^2=1,
    b a²=a² b^(1+ε1h),  c² b=b^(1+ε2h)c².        (6)

The source's exact presentation, tight order 64q, marked orders and IP are retained inputs. The phase58 theorem, bound explicitly in this package, establishes the following facts about its smallest regular maniplex cover. We record the facts needed here; phase58's construction of the unique minimal quotient within its naive facet/cofacet interval is not assumed to classify all covers.

In the rotation mix R≤P×P put

    Arot=(a,a^−1), Brot=(b,a²b), Crot=(c,c),
    Z=[Crot²,Brot], κ=(1,b^(q/2)).

The mirror μ swaps the coordinates. R has order 128q, the full reflection extension G=R⋊⟨μ⟩ has order 256q, and its marked reflections are

    r0=μ, r1=r0 Arot, r2=r1 Brot, r3=r2 Crot.

Each end is a regular rank-three string C-group. If C=⟨r1,r2⟩, then |C|=2q, I=G_{012}∩G_{123}=C⟨Z⟩ has order 4q, and Z centralizes C. One can also read the latter directly from Z^μ=Z^−1, Z^Arot=Z^−1 and [Z,Brot]=1. Thus (1) holds with z=Z.

For the uniform reduction, we need the exact small presentation also proved in phase58. Start with the power and rotation square relations of (6), and put

    w1=b a²(a² b^(1+ε1h))^−1,
    w2=c² b(b^(1+ε2h)c²)^−1.

Replacing the last two relations of (6) by

    w1=w2,  w1²=1,  [w1,a]=[w1,b]=[w1,c]=1       (7)

presents R. Indeed its central kernel over (6) has size at most two, and the coordinate mix realizes w1=w2=κ≠1 and has order 2|P|. Substituting a=r0r1,b=r1r2,c=r2r3 and retaining the four involution relations and nonadjacent commutations presents G. The rotation subgroup has the upper bound supplied by (7), and the actual reflection extension attains twice that bound. These are exact presentations, not truncated p-quotients.

**Lemma 3.** For every parameter in (6), N=⟨Brot^4⟩ is normal in G, has order q/4, is contained in C, and the marked quotient G/N is the same group of order 1024, independent of β and the signs. Its action on the 128 cosets of C/N identifies canonically with G's action on C\G.

Proof. Put d1=ε1h and u=a². Since d1²≡0 modulo q, the source relations give b^u=b^(1+d1). Using (ab)²=1 gives

    a b² a^−1=b^(−2−d1)a^−4.

Squaring and using a^8=1 yields

    a b⁴ a^−1=b^(−4−6d1−2d1²)
               =b^(−4−2d1)=b^(−4+q/2).         (8)

The exponent k=−1+q/8 is an involutory unit modulo q/4, so a and a^−1 act by the same k on ⟨b⁴⟩. Applying the same calculation to the reversed presentation (c^−1,b^−1,a^−1), which swaps ε1 and ε2, gives the same action by c and c^−1. In the mix,

    Brot⁴=(b⁴,b^(4+q/2)).

Thus Arot and Crot act on ⟨Brot⁴⟩ by k, while Brot centralizes it. Coordinate swapping acts by m=1+q/8. Both k² and m² are 1 modulo q/4, and mk=−1 modulo q/4. In reflection notation the exponents are

    r0 : 1+q/8,   r1 : −1,   r2 : −1,   r3 : 1+q/8.       (9)

This proves normality, and the exact marked order of Brot gives |N|=q/4. Since Brot=r1r2, N≤C. Hence |G/N|=(256q)/(q/4)=1024 and |C/N|=8.

Finally add b⁴=1 to (7). The b^q relation becomes redundant, and h is divisible by four, so both b^(1+εih) become simply b. All remaining defining words are independent of β,ε1,ε2. This is one literal marked presentation for every quotient G/N. The order 1024 was proved before identifying the presentations. As N is normal and lies in C, C\G and (C/N)\(G/N) identify by matching each marked word. The word Z=[(r2r3)²,r1r2] identifies as well. ∎

## 4. Exact obstruction and its limits

**Theorem 4.** For every β≥5 and every pair of signs in (6), the smallest regular maniplex G has no finite regular polytopal cover with odd abelian kernel whose restrictions to both maximal standard parabolics are injective.

Proof. On the fixed group G/N from Lemma 3, the 55-term integer equality (5) holds in its 128-point coset module. It is verified component by component by the bound certificate, using integer arithmetic. Lemma 3 transports this same marked word equality to the coset module of every G in the family. Proposition 2 now excludes the displacement required in Proposition 1 for any odd abelian kernel. ∎

This is a uniform theorem with one fixed finite algebra certificate. The all-parameter conclusion follows from the proved presentation reduction; it is not inferred from eight parameter samples. For independent cross-checking, the GAP checker rebuilds (6) for β=5,6 and all four sign pairs, verifies normality and the exponents (9), identifies the coset action kernel as N, and checks the literal same identity (5) in every case.

A separate diagnostic in the base case β=5, ε1=ε2=+1 forms the 128-by-384 integer constraint matrix for

    w(r1−1)=w(r2−1)=w(1−r3)(1−r0)=0.

Its Smith diagonal has 120 entries 1, one entry 2, and seven zero entries. Its rational left nullspace has dimension seven and is annihilated by Z−1. These computations agree with the obstruction but are not the theorem's only evidence: (5) is the explicit universal-module certificate and works in every odd abelian module, without an unproved lifting assumption.

The theorem does not exclude covers with nonabelian odd kernel, covers with noninjective ends, mixed even/odd kernel, or new even-kernel covers. It does not show that the source polytope has a unique minimal regular cover. The general multiple-minimal-cover problem is not settled here. The phase58 double cover and its degree-four census remain separate results with their own bounds. No new source-compression count, literature-priority claim, or formal Lean component is attributed to this package.
