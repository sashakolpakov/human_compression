## General mathematics

Proposition 1 is valid for arbitrary finite odd kernel groups. The convention `(k,g)(l,h)=(k^h l,gh)`, representing `gk`, is consistent with right conjugation. If `w` centralizes `C`, then the fourth lift is `w⁻¹r3w=r3u` with `u=(w^r3)⁻¹w`. Its outer string relation is exactly `u^r0=u`. The other nonadjacent relations and the involution relations are preserved. Projection gives the exact intersection

```text
A ∩ B^w = {i ∈ A ∩ B : i^w = i}.
```

For `A∩B=C ⊔ Cz`, this equals `C` if and only if `w^z≠w`. Together with the retained rank-three end recognition hypotheses, the classical recursive string-C criterion gives the required rank-four intersection property. Taking the subgroup generated by the two ends preserves an odd kernel and surjectivity. It is not necessary to claim that this subgroup contains all of the ambient kernel group.

Conversely, in a finite extension of the 2-group `G` by an odd group `K`, a Sylow 2-subgroup maps isomorphically onto `G`. Each lifted end, which is injective over its base end, is a Sylow 2-subgroup in that end's full preimage. Sylow conjugacy there lets its conjugator be chosen in `K`. Normalizing the first end fixes its three marked lifts, and the second end is `B^w`; uniqueness of the two common marked lifts forces `w` to centralize `C`. The string relation and IP then give all three displayed conditions. No abelian hypothesis, odd-order solvability theorem, or Schur–Zassenhaus theorem is hidden in this argument.

Proposition 2 also passes in both directions. The assignment `t_(Cg) ↦ w^g` is well-defined and maps the original cell to the noncommutative expression

```text
(w^(r3r0))⁻¹ w^r0 w⁻¹ w^r3 = u^r0 u⁻¹.
```

No factors have been commuted. Translating the relation by `G` gives the whole presentation. Conversely a finite odd quotient with surviving defect need not be equivariant, but replacing its normal kernel `L` by the intersection of its finitely many `G`-translates gives a normal `G`-invariant subgroup `L0⊆L`. The diagonal embedding into the product of the odd quotients proves that `U/L0` is still finite and odd. The distinguished defect still survives, and `t_C` is fixed by `C`, giving the required equivariant construction. Thus the universal finite-odd-quotient criterion is an actual equivalence, not merely a necessary linear test.

The assertion about abelianization is accurate up to the immaterial overall sign of a defining relation: the abelianized cell is the negative of `e_C(1−r3)(1−r0)`, and these generate the same relation submodule. The earlier integral certificate controls the abelianized defect. The present group-word identity controls the defect before abelianization.
