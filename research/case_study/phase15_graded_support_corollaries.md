# Prime-order markings and excess-order consequences

10 October 2026. Separate proposed corollaries of the frozen
[graded-support proof](phase15_graded_support.md). No frozen proof or
certificate is modified here. These statements require independent
review before promotion.

## 1. The all-prime version

Let p be any prime. Let G be a finite p-group with a marking by elements
r_v of order p, indexed by a finite forest with d vertices and m edges.
Assume marked subgroup IP, commuting nonedges, and noncommuting edges.
Then G/γ₃(G) is the vertex/edge class-two cocycle group over F_p, with
order p^(d+m) and the induced marked IP.

Here the terminology “C-group” is reserved for involutory markings;
for odd p this statement is about marked order-p generating systems.

Proof: irredundancy follows from IP. The abelianization is generated by
elements of order p, so Φ(G)=G′; Burnside gives G/G′≅C_p^d and
G′∩<r_v>=1. Every layer of the ordinary lower-central Lie ring is an
F_p-vector space, generated in degree one. If an edge relation is
nonzero, choose the same extremal participating edge as in the forest
proof. Divide its nonzero coefficient in F_p to express its cross
bracket as a linear combination of brackets on the other side.
The Lie support lemma and the finite descending factorization lemma
then apply without change. The original IP kills that edge commutator,
a contradiction. Thus the m edge images are independent in G′/γ₃(G).

For the explicit quotient model use V=F_p^d, Z=F_p^m and

    (v,z)(w,t)=(v+w,z+t+β(v,w)),
    β(v,w)_e=v_head(e) w_tail(e).

The marked vertex elements have order p; each oriented edge supplies
one independent central commutator of order p. Nonedges commute.
The class-two presentation has the collected upper bound p^(d+m),
attained by this model, and the source quotient has exactly that order.
The support description of every marked subgroup, and hence IP, is
identical to the argument over F₂.

## 2. Ordered products supported on a forest

Under either prime-order marking hypothesis above, let D_e be arbitrary
subgroups such that

    D_e≤G_{ends(e)}∩G′.

Order the edges by repeatedly selecting an edge incident with a leaf
of the remaining forest and deleting that leaf. In this order, the map

    ∏_e D_e → G,   (h₁,…,h_m)↦h₁⋯h_m              (1)

is injective. No commutativity of the D_e is assumed.

To prove this, suppose two products are equal. Let v be the leaf of
the first edge e={v,w}. Every factor after the first belongs to the
parabolic on the remaining vertices. Therefore h₁'⁻¹h₁ lies both in
D_e and in that parabolic. Actual IP places it in <r_w>, while its
membership in G′ makes it trivial. Cancel h₁=h₁' and repeat on the
remaining forest. This proves injectivity one factor at a time.

Consequently, if all D_e lie in a subgroup N, then

    ∏_e |D_e| ≤ |N|.                                (2)

This conclusion uses the proved support argument. It is not inferred
from any triple-product property or mere pairwise trivial intersections.

## 3. Nilpotency class and edge orders

Write |G|=p^(d+m+k). The all-prime theorem gives |γ₃(G)|=p^k.
If the nilpotency class is c≥3, the strict lower-central chain from
γ₃(G) down to 1 has c−2 nontrivial factors. Each has order at least p.
Thus

    c≤k+2.                                         (3)

If γ₃(G)=1, the same bound follows directly, including the abelian
case. No classification of the groups attaining this bound is asserted.

For an involutory forest marking, write the order of the adjacent
product on edge e as 2^s_e, with s_e≥2. Its commutator is the square
of that product, and its image in G′/γ₃ has order two. Hence

    D_e=<(r_u r_v)^4>≤γ₃(G),   |D_e|=2^(s_e−2).

Apply (2) with N=γ₃(G) to obtain the additive bound

    Σ_e (s_e−2)≤k.                                 (4)

In particular every adjacent product has order at most 2^(k+2).
For a path with d vertices, |G|=2^(2d−1+k), so both the class bound
and this adjacent-order bound have the form requested by the common
quotient's excess parameter k.

For completeness, the all-prime analogue applies to edge commutators:
if |[r_u,r_v]|=p^t_e, their p-th powers lie in γ₃(G), so

    Σ_e (t_e−1)≤k.

The injectivity proof is the same, with
D_e=<[r_u,r_v]^p>.

## 4. Scope and known overlap

For a path, inequality (4) is algebraically equivalent to

    |G|≥2∏_e 2^s_e,

the standard type-dependent flag bound recalled as equation (4) on
page 3 of Hou–Schulte arXiv:2605.20691v1. The numerical path lower bound
is therefore not claimed as new. The present argument records its
ordered-support mechanism and its compatibility with the canonical
γ₃ excess parameter. No priority assessment is made for the forest
or odd-prime formulations.

These are analytic corollaries, with no finite catalogue or numerical
experiment as a proof input. The full group/Lie argument is not claimed
to be formalized in Lean.
