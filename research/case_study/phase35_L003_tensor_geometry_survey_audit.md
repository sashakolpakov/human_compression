## Mathematical checks

The exceptional subgroup is generated by pure factor permutations and the literal matrix $z$. Its conjugation action on the three embedded copies of $N$ has one common sign choice: it either permutes the copies of $N$ or permutes their complements $I-N$. The identities $S^{-1}NS=Q^{-1}NQ=I-N$ are exact. The three full matrix-factor algebras remain distinct over a composite coefficient ring. Their pairwise intersections contain only scalar matrices, whereas $N$ and $I-N$ are nonscalar. Thus removing the actual factor permutation cannot conceal a different permutation in the normalizer action. If a bounded word lies in the exceptional group, either its factors or those of its product with $z$ commute with $N$.

The commutator bound is applied to literal integer representatives of all bounded words, so a nonzero entry of absolute value at most four cannot disappear modulo any integer $n\ge5$. Sufficiency is separately checked: every accepted case has factors $\delta_iN^{k_i}$ with exponent sum zero and scalar-sign product one. Such a tensor belongs integrally to the rank-two translation subgroup after restoring its pure permutation. This establishes an exact membership equivalence on the finite product set, rather than merely a necessary condition or an unproved description of a quadratic ring's units.

The transport to full subgroup IP is exhaustive. Every proper marked subgroup other than the exceptional maximal one lies in a protected finite parabolic. Two protected elements and their equality are controlled by the matrix-entry bound. The only additional intersection decision is membership in the exceptional group of a protected element; the bounded-word equivalence supplies it. The exceptional subgroup's self-intersection and all meets with the ambient group are tautologies.

Typed Helly is proved independently of IP. For every new type and every set of at least two remaining types, a protected anchor always exists. The checker partitions that anchor into the explicit cosets $(P_i\cap P_{j_0})g$. Eligibility is exactly membership in each required parabolic product. If an exceptional factor occurs, the other factor is protected, so this condition reduces to exceptional membership of a product of two protected elements. All remaining comparisons are equality tests within the bounded product set. The number of eligible cosets is compared with the number supplied by the full intersection of the base parabolics. Equality in all sixteen cases, followed by induction on flag size, is precisely the common-point condition needed for arbitrary flags. There is no ambient group enumeration, quotient-IP assumption, or inference of Helly from subgroup IP.

The final coset-geometry adapter also checks: Helly gives flag completion and flag transitivity; the marked intersection property gives a trivial chamber stabilizer and two-element panels; generated residue groups give chamber connectivity. Thinness and chamber-gallery connectivity make a chamber-fixing type-preserving automorphism trivial. Hence the marked group is the full type-preserving automorphism group of a regular hypertope.

The uniform geometry argument applies to every modulus $n\ge5$, with modulus three checked separately. The retained Fibonacci theorem supplies every exact parameter $s\ge4$ and its exact local marked groups. The previously certified parameter-two and parameter-three geometries are separate retained finite inputs.
