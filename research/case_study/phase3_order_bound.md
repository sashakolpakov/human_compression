# Sharp 2-power hypertope orders: structural lower bounds

9 October 2026. Target: Hou–Feng–Leemans, [*Existence of regular 3-hypertopes with 2^n chambers*, Conjecture 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.09034), also checked in the final manuscript [archived locally](https://dipot.ulb.ac.be/dspace/bitstream/2013/287227/5/Hy2g.pdf). For a regular rank-three hypertope with automorphism group of order 2^n and pair orders `2^s,2^t,2^l`, with s,t>=2,l>=1, the conjecture asserts `n>=s+t+l` (stated for n>=10).

The construction side was recovered uniformly in [phase two](compression_phase2.md). The full lower bound is **not proved here**. The following are uniform structural results, not extrapolations from a finite census:

* the bound holds if any pair order is at most four;
* the bound holds if the derived subgroup has nilpotency class at most two, in particular if G has nilpotency class at most five;
* the bound holds if the derived subgroup has an abelian subgroup of index two;
* the bound holds if any two of its three distinguished cyclic square-rotation subgroups permute, in particular if one of them is normal in the derived subgroup.
* the structural reduction plus an exhaustive SmallGroups certificate proves the bound for every group order through 2^11; this finite consequence is [documented separately](phase3_cyclic_tpp_exploration.md).

The central reduction identifies the remaining obstruction with a special **cyclic subgroup triple product property** problem. This links the geometry to the group-theoretic approach to matrix multiplication, while explaining why a naive product-cardinality argument would be invalid.

## 1. Exact hypotheses supplied by a regular hypertope

Write G=<a,b,c>, with distinguished involutions. Put

    P0=<b,c>, P1=<a,c>, P2=<a,b>.

The C-group intersection property gives `P_i intersect P_j` equal to the distinguished involution subgroup of order two. Flag transitivity of the standard coset geometry supplies Tits' typed Helly identity

    (P_i P_j) intersect P_k
      = (P_i intersect P_k)(P_j intersect P_k)           (1)

for distinct i,j,k. These are equalities of sets; a product on the right need not be a subgroup. Every pair P_i,P_j generates G.

The generators are independent. Burnside's basis theorem for finite 2-groups therefore gives `G/Φ(G)=C2^3`. Since G is generated by involutions, its abelianization has exponent two, and hence

    Φ(G)=G',       |G: G'|=8.                           (2)

No assertion of flag transitivity is inferred from IP alone.

## 2. The minimum-pair-order reduction

Let x,y be two of the three rotation orders and let z be the remaining one. The corresponding two maximal parabolics have product of size 2xy, because their intersection has order two.

If z=2, this already proves `|G|>=2xy=xyz`. If z>2, their product cannot equal G. Indeed equality would make it a subgroup, so (1) would make the four-element set `<u><v>` a subgroup, where u,v are the generators of the remaining dihedral parabolic. A four-element group containing distinct involutions u,v is a Klein four group, forcing uv to have order two, contrary to z>2.

Thus `|G|>2xy`. Both quantities are powers of two, giving `|G|>=4xy`. When z=4 this is exactly the required bound.

Consequently any counterexample must have **all three rotation orders at least eight**. This deduction holds in every group order; it does not use the source's n<=10 computational verification.

## 3. The Frattini subgroup contains a cyclic TPP triple

Let P=G'=Φ(G), and define

    A=<(ab)^2>, B=<(bc)^2>, C=<(ac)^2>.

Their orders are `2^(s-1),2^(t-1),2^(l-1)`. In fact each is exactly the intersection of its dihedral parabolic with P: the two distinguished involutions have independent images in G/P, so the parity kernel in that dihedral group is its even-rotation subgroup.

The groups A,B,C intersect pairwise trivially by IP. Further, (1) gives

    AB intersect C <= (P2 P0) intersect P1
                   = <a><c>.

The cyclic group C intersects the four-element set `{1,a,c,ac}` only in 1: a,c are reflections and ac is an odd power of the rotation. Therefore

    AB intersect C = {1}.                              (3)

Together with A intersect B=1, this is the subgroup triple product property: `xyz=1`, with x in A, y in B, z in C, implies x=y=z=1. The condition is symmetric under permuting A,B,C, by cyclically rotating the equation and taking inverses.

By (2), the desired hypertope inequality is now exactly

    |A||B||C| <= |P|.                                  (4)

TPP does not assert that the multiplication map A x B x C -> P is injective. Collisions compare six factors rather than the three in (3). This distinction is essential: arbitrary subgroup TPP triples in 2-groups can violate (4), and cyclic subgroup triples can violate it in groups that are not 2-groups. For example the three order-two subgroups of S3 have TPP and product of orders eight, exceeding six. The connection with TPP uses the standard subgroup formulation of [Hedtke–Murthy, *Search and test algorithms for Triple Product Property triples*](https://arxiv.org/html/1104.5097).

## 4. Cyclic TPP triples in every finite class-two group

**Theorem.** If P is a finite group of nilpotency class at most two and A,B,C are cyclic subgroups satisfying TPP, then `|A||B||C|<=|P|`.

**Proof.** Induct on |P|; equivalently choose a counterexample of smallest order within this class.

First suppose a nontrivial central subgroup N of P is contained in A. The quotient triple `(A/N, BN/N, CN/N)` has TPP: a relation `abc in N` can be absorbed into its A-factor and then tested in P. Also B and C intersect N trivially. Thus its product-of-orders divided by its ambient order is exactly `|A||B||C|/|P|`. The quotient is smaller and still has class at most two, a contradiction. Applying this with `N=A intersect Z(P)`, and likewise to B,C, shows that in a minimal counterexample

    A intersect Z(P)=B intersect Z(P)=C intersect Z(P)=1.       (5)

If H=<A,B> is proper in P, induction inside H gives

    |A||B||C intersect H| <= |H|.

The injection of C-cosets into P/H gives `|C:C intersect H|<=|P:H|`. Multiplication proves the desired bound in P. Hence <A,B>=P, and similarly for either other pair.

Write A=<a>, B=<b>, and let z=[a,b], of order h. As P has class at most two, `P'=<z>` and every element has a normal form `a^i b^j z^k`. The order h divides both ord(a) and ord(b), while a^h and b^h are central. Condition (5) therefore forces `|A|=|B|=h`.

A normal form is central only if its exponents i,j are multiples of h: commute it with b and a respectively to obtain z^i=1 and z^j=1. It follows both that `Z(P)=<z>` and that the h³ normal forms with 0<=i,j,k<h are distinct. Thus `|P|=h³` and `P/Z(P)` has exponent h.

Finally C injects into P/Z(P) by (5). Being cyclic, it has order at most h. Consequently `|A||B||C|<=h³=|P|`, contradicting the choice of P. ∎

The proof uses cyclicity essentially when controlling C by the exponent of P/Z(P). It does not prove the false analogous theorem for arbitrary abelian subgroups.

**Hypertope consequence.** The conjectural order bound holds whenever G' has class at most two. In particular, if G has nilpotency class at most five then `γ3(G') <= γ6(G)=1`, so this hypothesis holds. This covers all metabelian G as a special case, with no restriction on the sizes of their three pair orders.

## 5. An abelian subgroup of index two also suffices

Here cyclicity is unnecessary.

**Lemma.** Let P be a finite 2-group with an abelian subgroup N of index two. Every subgroup TPP triple S,T,U satisfies `|S||T||U|<=|P|`.

**Proof.** Put S0=S intersect N, T0=T intersect N, U0=U intersect N. The product H=S0 T0 U0 is a subgroup of the abelian N, and TPP makes this product direct. Hence `|H|=|S0||T0||U0|`. Let k be the number of S,T,U not contained in N, so `|S||T||U|=2^k|H|`.

For k<=1, the result follows from H<=N. For k=2, assume S,T are the two outside subgroups, and choose x in S\N, y in T\N. If xy belonged to H, write xy=s0 t0 u0. Then `(s0^-1 x)(y t0^-1)u0^-1=1` would violate TPP. Thus H and xyH are disjoint inside N. It follows that `2|H|<=|N|` and `|S||T||U|=4|H|<=|P|`.

For k=3, choose x,y,z outside N in the three subgroups. All elements outside N induce the same conjugation automorphism on N, because N is abelian. That automorphism preserves S0,T0,U0, since each has one chosen normalizing element outside N. Therefore H is normal in P. The cosets xH,yH,zH are distinct: equality of two would, as above, produce a forbidden TPP relation after absorbing the three H-factors. All three lie outside N/H, so `|N:H|>=3`. This index is a power of two, hence at least four, giving `|P:H|>=8`. Thus `|S||T||U|=8|H|<=|P|`. ∎

This is the 2-group specialization of an established abelian-index bound; compare [Murthy, *A Note on the Triple Product Property for Finite Groups with Abelian Normal Subgroups of Prime Index*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16730). The direct proof above is included so the present conclusion does not depend on stronger claims in other TPP preprints.

Applying the lemma to P=G' proves another uniform class of the hypertope conjecture, including cases where the nilpotency class is large.

## 6. Permuting rotation subgroups and the remaining obstruction

If any two of A,B,C permute, say AB=BA, then AB is a subgroup of order |A||B| and has trivial intersection with C by TPP. The product set (AB)C has size |A||B||C| and lies in P, proving (4). In particular this applies if any one of A,B,C is normal in P.

A counterexample to the hypertope conjecture must therefore have all of the following features:

* all three pair orders are at least eight;
* G has nilpotency class at least six, and G' has class at least three;
* G' has no abelian subgroup of index two;
* no two distinguished cyclic square-rotation subgroups permute.

There is a further useful reduction for the broader cyclic-TPP problem in finite 2-groups. If it has a counterexample, a smallest one has all three cyclic subgroups disjoint from the center and any two of them generate the whole group, by the first two steps of the theorem above. In particular that auxiliary group is two-generated. This is a reduction of a possible auxiliary counterexample, not a claim that every hypertope group itself has this form.

What remains is a proof of (4) for the special higher-class cyclic triples arising from three distinguished involutions, or a construction violating it together with a valid ambient involutory marking and typed Helly property. A failure of the broader cyclic-TPP inequality alone would not yet be a counterexample to the hypertope conjecture. No such failure or ambient counterexample has been established here.

## 7. Exact checks of the indispensable hypotheses

The reproducible [GAP script](phase3_order_bound_check.g) and its [completed transcript](phase3_order_bound_check.log) establish the following finite counterexamples to tempting stronger statements. They are checks of hypotheses, not evidence substituted for the uniform proofs above.

| Exact example | Verified properties | Stronger statement it rules out |
|---|---|---|
| The three reflection subgroups of S3 | Cyclic TPP; product of orders 8, ambient order 6 | A cyclic-TPP inequality for arbitrary finite groups |
| The extraspecial plus group `SmallGroup(32,49)` | Class two; three noncyclic order-four TPP subgroups, product 64 | Removing cyclicity from the class-two theorem |
| The generalized dihedral group of C4², with the three indicated affine reflections | Order 32, all pair orders 4, full C-group IP, failure of typed Helly | Deriving the hypertope lower bound from IP alone |

For the last example the script checks every standard-parabolic intersection, then exhibits the failed Tits identity: its two sides have sizes four and eight. Its three square-rotation subgroups fail TPP. Thus the passage from geometry to (3) uses a genuine additional hypothesis. The script also checks that none of the 56 cyclic triples in the extraspecial example whose order product exceeds 32 has TPP.

The class-two proof received internal agent review; this is not external peer review or a claim of priority. The full conjecture remains open in this work, specifically beyond the structural exclusions in Section 6.

The subsequent [cyclic-TPP certificate](phase3_cyclic_tpp_exploration.md) proves the auxiliary inequality for every 2-group of order at most256. Since |G′|=|G|/8, it certifies the hypertope conjecture through order2^11. Its smallest-counterexample analysis also gives ratio exactly two, all three cyclic orders at least four, and a precise extremal bicyclic case when one has order four. These additional reductions do not remove the general higher-class obstruction.
