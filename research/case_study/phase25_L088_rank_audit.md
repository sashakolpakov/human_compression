## Written proof

I reviewed the entire replacement proof and its exact GAP implementation. The odd-endpoint doubling criterion is valid for a C-group marking under the stated endpoint commutation and odd-product hypotheses. Its three cases exhaust marked subgroups, and the only new mixed intersection is controlled by the specified meet `P ∩ P^a = R`. Generation follows from the explicit odd-order recovery formula. The lemma does not assume that the changed marking is a string marking or that subgroup intersection implies flag transitivity.

The central twist recovers both the central involution and the old generator from the odd adjacent product. It preserves the specified parabolic but makes no unjustified assertion about all other intersections. The reuse of two seed endpoint meets in the four doubling constructions is valid.

The typed coset-Helly criterion is a separate geometric assertion. The right-hand orbit is contained in the common-neighbor set, so equality of their finite cardinalities is actual equality of sets. Induction after normalizing an existing partial flag proves that every flag has a common coset representative and extends to a chamber. The subgroup intersection property supplies the Borel and panel stabilizers; flag transitivity identifies every residue with the appropriate coset residue. The residue-generation and chamber-connectivity arguments then prove residual connectedness, thinness and the full type-preserving automorphism claim. No general implication from subgroup intersection alone to typed Helly is used.

The literal seed is explicitly identified with the retained TomLib J1 model, rather than merely with a group of order 175560. The source presentation has a completed Todd–Coxeter index of 1463 over the subgroup generated by its last three markers. That subgroup is a quotient of the spherical Coxeter group `[3,5]` of order 120. This gives the presentation order upper bound 175560; the literal seed provides the matching quotient and hence the order sandwich. The correctness of the completed Todd–Coxeter computation and the spherical Coxeter bound are retained inputs.

## Primary source and exact scope

I read all ten pages of Hartley–Leemans, *On the thin regular geometries of rank four for the Janko group J1*, Innovations in Incidence Geometry 1 (2005), 181–190, using the [primary publisher PDF](https://msp.org/iig/2005/1-1/iig-v1-n1-p08-s.pdf). I independently rendered and visually inspected PDF pages 9 and 10, in addition to reading the Section 5 definitions.

Those definitions give Γ3 by replacing the last marker with its endpoint conjugate, so Γ3 has two A5 maximal parabolics. The printed figure labelled Γ3 instead has two C2×A5 parabolics. The definition of Γ4 gives two C2×A5 parabolics, while the printed Γ4 figure has two A5 parabolics. Thus the two figure labels are transposed relative to the definitions. Their distinct parabolic-order multisets rule out an explanation by type orientation. The other four figure descriptions agree with the reconstructed markings. This records a literal source discrepancy; it does not claim an unlocated erratum or priority for noticing it.

The package rederives Theorems 3.2, 4.2, 5.2, 5.4, 6.2 and 7.2, their generation steps, mutual distinction, and the two stated failure examples. It supports **principal-construction coverage**. The introductory assertion that these six objects exhaust all thin rank-four J1 geometries, and the exclusion of all larger ranks, remain retained published classification statements. Neither follows from these six witnesses or from the earlier RWPRI census, whose rank-four records have Borel order two. The optional seed extraction used only the previous ambient group model; the standalone recognition does not read a geometry list.

This review retains GAP's exact finite-group and coset algorithms, TomLib's named J1 model and the spherical Coxeter order. It does not claim a Lean formalization, an independent complete thin-geometry census, a new open-conjecture resolution, or truth of every literal sentence in the source.
