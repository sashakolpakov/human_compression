## 1. The complete principal contract

Hou, Feng and Leemans [HFL] study regular polyhedra whose automorphism
groups have order $2^n p$, where $p$ is an odd prime. This application
recovers their principal type restriction, both families of cubic
constructions, their general odd-factor construction, and the exact
existence range for type $\{6,2^s\}$. We use their arXiv:2001.02945v2
edition, dated 14 July 2024, comprising seventeen pages. The correction
in Section 6 concerns an auxiliary presentation in that edition; no
claim about the later journal display is made.

We work with marked rank-three string C-groups. The distinguished
generators $a,b,c$ are involutions, $ac=ca$, and
$\langle a,b\rangle\cap\langle b,c\rangle=\langle b\rangle$.
This last equality is the complete rank-three intersection test. We
retain the standard correspondence with regular abstract polyhedra,
under which the group acts regularly on flags and its order is the
number of flags. Nondegeneracy means both adjacent product orders are
at least three. Conjugation is $x^g=g^{-1}xg$.

**Theorem 1 (type restriction; [HFL, Theorems 1.2 and 3.1]).** If a
group of order $2^n p$ is generated by three elements of order at most two with commuting
endpoints, then $p$ divides at least one adjacent product order.
Consequently, up to duality, a nondegenerate regular polyhedron of this
order has type $\{2^s p,2^t\}$ or $\{2^s p,2^t p\}$.

**Theorem 2 (odd-factor extension; [HFL, Theorem 4.1]).** For positive
odd integers $\ell_1,\ell_2$, integers $s,t\geq2$ and
$n\geq s+t+1$, the explicit presentation in Section 3 is a solvable
string C-group of type $\{2^s\ell_1,2^t\ell_2\}$ and order
$2^n\ell_1\ell_2$. In particular it gives the source’s Corollary 4.2
by taking $\ell_1=p$, $\ell_2=1$.

**Theorem 3 (the exact six-sided range; [HFL, Theorem 1.3]).** For
$n\geq7$, a nondegenerate regular polyhedron of order $3\cdot2^n$
and type $\{6,2^s\}$ exists if and only if

$$2\leq s\leq n-2,\qquad s\ne n-3.\tag{1}$$

The constructions identify the source’s actual presentations, with the
single sign correction specified in Section 6.

**Theorem 4 (the cubic families; [HFL, Theorems 4.3 and 5.1]).** The
three literal presentations of type $\{6,4\}$ give solvable groups of
orders $96m^3,192m^3,384m^3$, for every integer $m\geq1$. The source’s
three type-$\{6,6\}$ presentations give solvable groups of orders
$192m^3,384m^3,768m^3$, for every $m\geq1$. In particular, type
$\{6,4\}$ and type $\{6,6\}$ both occur at order $3\cdot2^n$
for every $n\geq5$.

The shared ingredients are the supported-kernel and square-group
results of [UA], and the complete affine-lattice criterion of [AL].
The existing exact $\{6,6\}$ calculation [C66] is reused. Burnside’s
$p^a q^b$ solvability theorem and Lucchini’s cyclic-core bound are
retained classical inputs, as in the source. One bounded exclusion
retains the complete GAP SmallGroups catalogue of order 384. These
inputs, rather than experimental extrapolation, are the precise
boundaries of the recovery.

## 2. A dihedral quotient gives the type restriction

**Lemma 5.** If a finite group $G$ of order $2^n p$ is generated by
involutions, then $G/O_2(G)\cong D_{2p}$, where $O_2(G)$ is its largest
normal two-subgroup.

*Proof.* Put $\overline G=G/O_2(G)$. It still has an odd part of order
$p$, is generated by involutions or identities, and has no nontrivial
normal two-subgroup. Burnside’s theorem makes it solvable. A minimal
normal subgroup is therefore elementary abelian, and must have order
$p$. Write it as $P$.

Its centralizer $C=C_{\overline G}(P)$ is normal. If $S$ is a Sylow
two-subgroup of $C$, then $C=P\times S$: $P$ is central in $C$ and the
orders multiply to $|C|$. Thus $S$ is the unique Sylow two-subgroup of
$C$, hence characteristic there and normal in $\overline G$. It is
trivial. Therefore $C=P$ and
$\overline G/P\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(P)$ is cyclic.
Because it is generated by elements of order at most two, this quotient
has order at most two. It cannot be trivial: the nontrivial group of
odd order $P$ cannot be generated by involutions. Its order is two and
its action on $P$ is the unique nontrivial involution of
$\operatorname{Aut}(C_p)$, namely inversion. A generating involution
outside $P$ supplies a complement. Hence $\overline G=D_{2p}$.
$\square$

Suppose neither adjacent product has order divisible by $p$. Their
images in $D_{2p}$ have order at most two, and the endpoint product also
has order at most two. Three involutions or identities with all pair
products of order at most two commute pairwise. They would generate an
abelian two-group, not $D_{2p}$. This contradiction proves Theorem 1.
The allowed type forms follow from Lagrange’s theorem. The exponents
$s,t$ in this type restriction are not silently restricted to those of
the narrower construction in Theorem 2.

## 3. Two independent edge periods

Write $h=2^s\ell_1$, $k=2^t\ell_2$ and $d=n-s-t\geq1$. Put

$$R=\{a^2,b^2,c^2,(ab)^h,(bc)^k,(ac)^2,
[(ab)^4,c],[a,(bc)^4]\}.$$

The exact source presentation is

$$G=\begin{cases}
\langle a,b,c\mid R,[(ab)^2,c]^{2^{(d-1)/2}}\rangle,&d\text{ odd},\\
\langle a,b,c\mid R,[(ab)^2,(bc)^2]^{2^{(d-2)/2}}\rangle,&d\text{ even}.
\end{cases}\tag{2}$$

The square-group results UA.Affine and UA.Square identify the quotient
obtained by adding $(ab)^4=(bc)^4=1$. It is the marked affine square
group modulo the lattice

$$\Lambda=\begin{cases}
2^{(d+1)/2}\mathbb Z^2,&d\text{ odd},\\
2^{d/2}\langle(1,1),(1,-1)\rangle,&d\text{ even}.
\end{cases}\tag{3}$$

Call it $B$. It has order $2^{d+4}$, type $\{4,4\}$, IP, and every
Boolean deletion. The least scale in (3) is two, including $d=1,2$;
there is no unverified small seed. These precise presentations and their
normal forms are part of the retained umbrella, not inferred from their
orders.

Apply UA.SK to the adjacent pairs, using the dihedral quotients
$D_{2h}\to D_8$ and $D_{2k}\to D_8$. The resulting compatible product
$H$ has type $\{h,k\}$, IP and order

$$ |H|=2^{d+4}(h/4)(k/4)=2^n\ell_1\ell_2.\tag{4}$$

The relators in (2) hold in it. For the final relator, the base coordinate
is killed by (3), and every attached coordinate is trivial: in either
dihedral factor, one of the commutator entries is trivial or the relevant
square is the identity. The fourth-power commutators also vanish in
every coordinate. Thus the presented group maps onto $H$.

For the reverse bound, $A=\langle(ab)^4\rangle$ is normal in $G$ and
has order at most $h/4$; its normality follows from inversion by $a,b$
and the displayed commutation with $c$. In $G/A$, the cyclic subgroup
on $(bc)^4$ is normal, of order at most $k/4$. Killing it gives exactly
$B$ by the presentation. These two finite cyclic layers bound $|G|$ by
the right side of (4). The surjection attaining the bound is therefore
an isomorphism of marked groups. This proves Theorem 2, including its
literal presentation. Solvability follows from the abelian-by-dihedral
square group and the two cyclic layers.

## 4. One affine group for the three {6,4} families

Define integral affine transformations on $\mathbb R^3$ by

$$a(x,y,z)=(y,x,z),\quad b(x,y,z)=(1-x,z,y),\quad
c(x,y,z)=(x,y,-z).\tag{5}$$

They satisfy the complete source relators

$$U=\langle a,b,c\mid a^2,b^2,c^2,(ab)^6,(bc)^4,(ac)^2,
[a,(bc)^2]^2\rangle.\tag{6}$$

Their linear parts are signed permutation matrices. On the signed basis
$(e_1,e_2,e_3,-e_1,-e_2,-e_3)$ they act as

$$ (1\ 2)(4\ 5),\quad(1\ 4)(2\ 3)(5\ 6),\quad(3\ 6).\tag{7}$$

They generate the full signed permutation group $Q$, of order 48:
the coordinate permutations generate $S_3$, and conjugates of the last
sign change generate all coordinate sign changes. The exact Schreier
transition calculation of AL.Lemma 1 gives translation kernel

$$L=2\mathbb Z^3+\mathbb Z(1,1,1),\qquad[\mathbb Z^3:L]=4.\tag{8}$$

For example the transitions include vectors generating $2\mathbb Z^3$
and an all-odd vector; every transition has three coordinates of the
same parity. These two containments prove (8). A separate exact
Reidemeister–Schreier/Tietze computation for the kernel of the marked
map (6) to (7) returns three generators with the three pairwise
commutator relations. Its actual presentation is free abelian of rank
three. AL.Lemma 2 therefore identifies the full presentation (6) with
the affine group (5). The computation proves a statement about the
abstract kernel, not just about its abelianization.

Here are the source’s three triples of translation words. We keep them
because matching their actual values is necessary to recover the source
presentations.

$$\begin{array}{lll}
x_1=(b c^{ba})^2,&y_1=(c^b b^a)^2,&z_1=(c b^{ab})^2,\\
x_2=(a^b b^{ac})^2,&y_2=(b^{acb}a)^2,&z_2=(a^{bcb}b^{ab})^2,\\
x_3=(bac)^6,&y_3=(cba)^6,&z_3=((cba)^b)^6.
\end{array}\tag{9}$$

Their translation vectors are, in the order $x_i,y_i,z_i$,

$$\begin{aligned}
i=1:&\quad (2,0,0),\ (0,-2,0),\ (0,0,-2),\\
i=2:&\quad (2,0,2),\ (2,-2,0),\ (-2,-2,0),\\
i=3:&\quad (2,-2,2),\ (2,-2,-2),\ (-2,-2,-2).
\end{aligned}$$

The invariant period lattices and their indices are

| Triple | Lattice $\Lambda_i$ | $[L:\Lambda_i]$ |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | $2\mathbb Z^3$ | 2 |
| 2 | $2D_3$ | 4 |
| 3 | $4\mathbb Z^3+2\mathbb Z(1,1,1)$ | 8 |

Here $D_3=\{(u,v,w)\in\mathbb Z^3:u+v+w\text{ is even}\}$. Their
basis determinants have absolute values $8,16,32$, respectively, and
all three lattices are invariant under signed permutations. Imposing
$x_i^m=y_i^m=z_i^m=1$ gives exactly $U/(m\Lambda_i)$, by AL.Theorem 3.
Its order is $96m^3,192m^3$ or $384m^3$.

The adjacent affine subgroups have orders twelve and eight, and inject
into their linear images. The latter meet only in $1$ and the linear
part of $b$. This is checked on the finite point group and implies IP
in every period quotient. The adjacent orders remain six and four.
The point group $C_2^3\rtimes S_3$ is solvable, so every quotient is
solvable. This proves the full source Theorem 4.3. Taking $m=2^j$ gives
exponents $5+3j,6+3j,7+3j$, proving its entire Corollary 4.4 without
three separate atlas identifications.

The matrix convention is explicit. The affine products in (5) act on
the rightmost factor first. GAP uses right point actions; a point
permutation corresponds to the inverse column-action matrix. Since
the three generating matrices are involutions, their displayed
permutations agree with (7), and the kernel calculation is unchanged.

## 5. All extensions except one seed

Set $x=bc$. The abstract group (6) has a retraction onto $D_8$ obtained
by killing $a$. The three triples (9) then become

$$ (x^{-2},x^2,x^{-2}),\qquad(1,1,1),\qquad
(x^6,x^{-6},x^6).\tag{10}$$

Consequently the retraction descends to $U/(m\Lambda_1)$ and
$U/(m\Lambda_3)$ when $m$ is even, and to $U/(m\Lambda_2)$ for every
$m$. This is the precise reason the first and third minimal seeds
cannot be used in this extension. The other pair deletions also hold,
but only the displayed retraction is needed.

For $s\geq2$, replace the second dihedral group by $D_{2^{s+1}}$ in
the compatible product of AL.Theorem 4. It multiplies the seed order by
$2^{s-2}$, preserves the first adjacent group, and gives type
$\{6,2^s\}$. Write $d=n-s$. With $m=2^j$, the three families cover

$$d=3+3j\ (j\geq1),\qquad d=4+3j\ (j\geq0),\qquad
 d=5+3j\ (j\geq1).\tag{11}$$

These are precisely all $d\geq4$ except $d=5$.

The source’s Lemma 4.6 supplies the latter case. Let

$$ w=bacba(cb)^2a,\qquad
V=\langle a,b,c\mid a^2,b^2,c^2,(ab)^6,(bc)^4,(ac)^2,w^2\rangle.
\tag{12}$$

An exact finite presentation computation gives $|V|=384$, the adjacent
orders six and four, and all sixty-four subset intersections. This is
an explicit checked seed, not an imported atlas entry. Killing $a$
defines the required retraction: $w$ becomes $(bc)^3b$, a reflection
in every dihedral group. Therefore

$$V_s=\langle a,b,c\mid a^2,b^2,c^2,(ab)^6,(bc)^{2^s},(ac)^2,
[a,(bc)^4],w^2\rangle \tag{13}$$

is exactly its compatible product with $D_{2^{s+1}}$, by the exact
presentation variant of AL.Theorem 4. It has order
$384\cdot2^{s-2}=3\cdot2^{s+5}$ and type $\{6,2^s\}$ for every
$s\geq2$. This is the entire source Lemma 4.6, with $n=s+5$.
The new seed and the third family of (9) are not interchangeable merely
because both have order 384 at their first parameter: only the former
has the required deletion at that parameter.

## 6. Exact period presentations and a corrected sign

For completeness we identify the source’s Lemma 4.5 presentations,
rather than merely produce the same types and orders. Put

$$R_s=\{a^2,b^2,c^2,(ab)^6,x^{2^s},(ac)^2,
[a,x^2]^2,[a,x^4]\}.$$

For $d=n-s\geq4$, $d\ne5$, use the following extra relations:

$$\begin{array}{c|c|l}
d\bmod3&M&\text{period relations}\\\hline
0&2^{(d-3)/3}&x_1^M=x^{-2M},\quad y_1^M=x^{2M},\quad z_1^M=x^{-2M},\\
1&2^{(d-4)/3}&x_2^M=y_2^M=z_2^M=1,\\
2&2^{(d-5)/3}&x_3^M=x^{6M},\quad y_3^M=x^{-6M},\quad z_3^M=x^{6M}.
\end{array}\tag{14}$$

The first exponent is negative in (14). It is positive in the literal
v2 Lemma 4.5, physical page 12. All other entries agree with that
source. In the first and third branches $M$ is even; in the middle
branch $M\geq1$.

Here is a presentation proof valid for all parameters. Start with the
presentation $R_s$ alone. The cyclic subgroup
$C=\langle x^4\rangle$ is normal, has order at most $2^{s-2}$, and
its quotient is (6). The affine group (6) retracts to $D_8$, so the
presentation maps onto its compatible product with $D_{2^{s+1}}$.
The kernel bound and the exact infinite-base version of AL.Theorem 4
identify the two groups. In this product, each extra relation (14),
using (10), is precisely $(m v,1)=1$ for one of the three source
translation vectors $v$. Normal closure kills exactly
$m\Lambda_i\times1$. The lattice lies in the retraction kernel by
the parity condition already checked. Hence the quotient is exactly
the finite compatible product of Section 5. This proves its order,
marking and IP with no unproved extension consistency.

The sign correction is forced by an elementary identity, independent
of the affine calculation. For any involutions $a,b,c$, the words (9)
satisfy

$$x_1^a=y_1^{-1}.\tag{15}$$

Indeed $x_1=(b a b c b a)^2$, $y_1=(b c b a b a)^2$, and conjugation by $a$
reverses the latter word. In the first branch the literal source imposes
both $x_1^M=x^{2M}$ and $y_1^M=x^{2M}$. Since $M$ is even and
$[a,x^4]=1$, conjugating the first equation by $a$ and comparing with
the inverse of the second yields

$$x^{4M}=1.\tag{16}$$

For $n=10,s=4$, one has $M=2$. Thus the literal presentation cannot
have the advertised rotation order sixteen. GAP computes order 1536
and rotation order eight for that presentation. With the corrected
first sign, its order is 3072, its rotation order is sixteen, and all
intersections hold. Three Lean declarations prove (15), the general
period-sign implication, and (16) directly from the literal conjugation
words. They do not rely on this numerical example.

This is a correction to an auxiliary construction in the bound edition,
not a failure of Theorem 3. The corrected family proves the required
existence statement for every parameter. It is not reported as a solved
open conjecture.

## 7. The excluded excess and the full existence theorem

The rank-three product bound gives
$2\cdot6\cdot2^s\leq3\cdot2^n$, so $s\leq n-2$; nondegeneracy
gives $s\geq2$. If $s=n-2$, the standard tight construction for two
even adjacent orders follows from UA.SK applied to the rank-three
Boolean group. It has exactly $3\cdot2^n$ elements. Sections 5 and 6
supply every $s\leq n-4$. It remains to exclude $s=n-3$.

We retain Lucchini’s cyclic-core theorem [L98, Proposition, p.241]: for a
proper cyclic subgroup $A$ of index $t$ in a finite group $G$,
$[G:\operatorname{core}_G(A)]\leq t^2-t$. Dividing by $t$ gives
$[A:\operatorname{core}_G(A)]<t=[G:A]$. The complete original paper was
read and is bound as a retained primary; its proof is not rederived here. In a hypothetical group of
order $3\cdot2^n$ and type $\{6,2^{n-3}\}$, the second rotation
subgroup $A=\langle bc\rangle$ has index twenty-four. For $n\geq8$,
its order is at least thirty-two, so its core is nontrivial. Its unique
subgroup $N$ of order two is characteristic in the cyclic core and
therefore normal in $G$.

The quotient $G/N$ preserves the first dihedral subgroup injectively:
that subgroup meets $\langle b,c\rangle$ in $\langle b\rangle$,
whereas $N$ lies in the rotation subgroup. Because
$N\leq\langle b,c\rangle$, the adjacent intersection in the quotient
is still exactly the image of $\langle b\rangle$. To see this
directly, if an element of the first subgroup maps into the second
one, it already lies in that second subgroup, since its full preimage
is unchanged by adjoining $N$. Thus the quotient is a string C-group
of order $3\cdot2^{n-1}$ and type $\{6,2^{n-4}\}$. Descent reaches
$n=7$.

For that base, an independent exhaustive calculation uses the retained
complete SmallGroups catalogue of groups of order 384. For every group
$G$ it first tests whether its exponent is divisible by sixteen. For
each remaining group, it takes one representative $c$ from every
conjugacy class of involutions. It enumerates every involution $b$ with
$|bc|=16$, modulo the conjugation action of $C_G(c)$. For each such
pair it takes every involution $a\in C_G(c)$ with $|ab|=6$, and tests
whether $\langle a,b,c\rangle=G$.

Every possible marking is included: simultaneous conjugation can first
place $c$ at its representative and then move $b$ within its centralizer
orbit; the exhaustive centralizer search still contains the corresponding
$a$. The complete computation visits 20,169 catalogue groups, including
1,178 whose exponent passes the first test, and 4,436 reduced adjacent
pairs. There is no generating triple, even before imposing IP. Thus
no required C-group exists at order 384. This is a catalogue-based
computer-assisted proof, not an unexplained statement that a software
package returned no example. The algorithm and counts are replayable.
The induction proves the exclusion for all $n\geq7$, completing
Theorem 3 without a residual list of unspecified small cases.

## 8. The reused {6,6} calculation

The already proved component [C66] uses

$$a(x,y,z)=(y,x,z),\quad b(x,y,z)=(1-x,z,y),\quad
c(x,y,z)=(-y,-x,z).\tag{17}$$

Its point quotient is the same signed permutation group of order 48;
its translation kernel is $2D_3$, of index sixteen in $\mathbb Z^3$.
The retained arithmetic checker records raw affine differences. Its
translation-lattice conclusion uses the invariant-lattice version of
AL.Lemma 1: $2D_3$ is signed-permutation invariant, contains every raw
difference, and is generated by exhibited raw differences. No general
claim that arbitrary raw differences alone generate a kernel is used.
An exact index48 kernel presentation identifies the complete universal
group

$$\langle a,b,c\mid a^2,b^2,c^2,(ab)^6,(bc)^6,(ac)^2,(cbab)^3\rangle
\tag{18}$$

with this affine group. The actual source period words give the lattices
$4\mathbb Z^3$, $4D_3$ and
$8\mathbb Z^3+4\mathbb Z(1,1,1)$, of indices four, eight and sixteen
in $2D_3$. The cited component contains their word-by-word evaluations;
those are part of this application’s dependency, not omitted obligations.
AL.Theorem 3 gives the orders $192m^3,384m^3,768m^3$.

Both adjacent parabolics have order twelve and faithful linear image.
Their linear intersection has four elements. Two lift to the common
identity and middle involution; the other two differ by translation
$(0,2,2)$. None of the three period lattices, or their positive
multiples, contains that vector, since they lie in $4\mathbb Z^3$.
This is the complete all-parameter IP check. The quotients are solvable
by the same point-group argument as before. It proves the exact
source Theorem 5.1, with its presented groups, rather than another
family sharing their numerical parameters.

Taking $m=2^j$ covers all exponents $n\geq6$ in order $3\cdot2^n$.
For $n=5$, take the tetrahedral marking
$(12),(23),(34)$ in $S_4$, and attach independent central involutions
$u,v$ to the first and third markers. The resulting marking in
$S_4\times\langle u,v\rangle$ has adjacent products of order six.
Their cubes recover $u,v$, so it generates all 96 elements. Its two
parabolic kernels are respectively $\langle u\rangle$ and
$\langle v\rangle$; the kernel criterion preserves the tetrahedral
intersection. This proves the full source Corollary 5.2. The proof and
its attribution are reused from [C66].

## 9. Scope, attribution and verification

All four principal results remain attributed to Hou–Feng–Leemans. The
new contribution here is the explicit second affine model, the shared
period-lattice proof, the exact presentation identification of the
extensions, and the independently reproduced nonexistence base. The
source’s cyclic-core descent is retained with its hypotheses and
quotient step made explicit. The mixed-prime type argument is a short
reworking of its solvable-group argument. Neither reuse nor a shorter
presentation is a claim to the discovery of these established results.

The new common paper [AL] and the retained umbrella [UA] carry genuine
proof obligations. The cubic point-kernel computations and the order384
catalogue search are exact finite computations with declared external
algorithms. The three new Lean declarations prove the literal sign
obstruction; they do not establish the entire source contract in Lean.
The previously checked kernel and supported-extension interfaces remain
available, with their own stated scope. All programs, retained input
statements and umbrella costs must be included in later matched
measurements. No numerical rate is asserted by counting paper titles
or pages alone.

The source’s broader Problem 1.1, the characterization of all groups of
order $2^n$ or $2^n p$ arising from regular or chiral polytopes, is not
solved here. Its final question about type (2) for primes at least five
has already been answered in the literature, as recorded in the
project’s question audit and the recovered equal-label construction
[EL]. That existing answer earns no new question credit here. Introductory
survey statements, the small-order table as an independently complete
census, and ancillary uniqueness remarks about the two order384 seeds
are outside this principal contract.
