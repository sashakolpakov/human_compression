# Independent audit of the PSL(4,q) rank-five exclusion

10 October 2026. Internal mathematical review by the `rank_stability`
agent, independent of the proof's author. **Result: PASS within the
scope below.** This is a written-proof review and exact algebra audit,
not external peer review, a Lean formalization, or a priority claim.

The reviewed final proof is
[phase7_psl4_zero_product.md](phase7_psl4_zero_product.md), SHA-256
`41cc7490a558a1d3b4d3c972ec9617a3494de9991beaff6af0b23ace62e86af1`.
Its algebraic cases are unchanged from the private working draft that
received the initial review. This audit also examined the preceding
reductions in [phase7_psl4.md](phase7_psl4.md), the form obstruction in
[psl4_theory.md](psl4_theory.md), and the previously reviewed completely
reducible endpoint argument in [phase3_psl4.md](phase3_psl4.md), §6.

The resulting claim is that PSL(4,q), for every odd prime power q, has no
rank-five string C-group representation. This review does **not** exclude
rank six: its rank-five parabolics need not generate PSL(4,q). Thus it
does not settle the full maximum-rank-four conjecture.

## 1. Coverage and projective signs

The earlier reductions leave nonzero N,M with
N²=M²=NM=MN=0, where the left endpoint generators negate N and fix M,
and the right endpoints fix N and negate M. Every endpoint preserves
both images and both kernels. Proportional N,M are impossible in odd
characteristic. Reversing the string leaves precisely the rank pairs
(1,1), (1,2), and (2,2).

All reasoning takes place over an algebraic closure. Normalizing lifts
to square to I and using a square root of −1 is legitimate there.
Distant commutators are ±I: a scalar commutator between two normalized
involutions squares to I. If two such matrices preserve a common line,
restriction to that line rules out a negative commutator. This justifies
every use of positive cross signs in the rank-one cases; signs are not
silently discarded in rank (2,2).

## 2. Rank (1,1)

Distinct image lines and independent defining forms give exactly
N=E₁₃ and M=E₂₄. The centralizer template in the proof is complete.
Inversion by T₀ fixes each image line, hence fixes each associated
eigenvalue of A individually while inverting it. The semisimple part
therefore squares to I; the same reasoning applies to B. The splitting
group generated by the two outer lifts and the two semisimple parts is
elementary abelian of exponent two. Its invariant complement separates
the two nilpotent chains. The endpoints acting on the opposite chain
are scalar, so the separated-support lemma applies without a hidden
semisimplicity assumption on the remaining two-dimensional pairs.

For a common image line and independent forms, the source quotient has
the two different weights stated in the proof. The middle lift acts by
a single translation on the outer joint weights. A nonzero translation,
together with the three already present weights, forces four distinct
one-dimensional weights. The plane L+T₂L is then invariant under the
entire splitting group and both nilpotents. For the zero translation,
either L or the two-dimensional common kernel is invariant. This
covers also coinciding choices for the remaining fourth weight.

Equal kernels with distinct images follow by inverse transpose. Equal
images and equal kernels make the maps proportional. These alternatives
exhaust all rank-one image/kernel configurations.

## 3. Rank (1,2)

Identifying the quotient with the common two-dimensional level through
M gives the rank-one two-dimensional matrix C. Reversal of C implies
trace zero, and hence C²=0. The two commuting outer level involutions
can therefore be put in the stated Z, Z normal form. Choosing an
invariant complement preserves the intrinsic maps from V/W to W and
does not assume that arbitrary initial extension blocks were zero.

The common centralizer has a single eigenvalue, so each semisimple
endpoint part is scalar. All four joint outer weight lines are distinct.
The four possible translation permutations of the middle involution
give exactly the four cases listed: three preserve a proper plane,
and the fourth is a similarity of the displayed nondegenerate
alternating form. Its paired monomial coefficients multiply to one
because the middle lift squares to I. No characteristic-three exception
occurs.

## 4. Rank (2,2): simultaneous splitting

Here both maps have image and kernel W. The quotient-to-W ratio C is
invertible and conjugate to −C, so trace zero makes it diagonalizable
with opposite nonzero eigenvalues in odd characteristic. This proves
the λZ normalization. The outer level lifts are off-diagonal
involutions, and their commutator sign leaves precisely X and iJ for
the second normalized lift.

The crucial eigenvalue argument is valid on the invariant W before any
extension splitting: inversion by the first outer lift gives b=a⁻¹,
and conjugation up to sign by the other gives b=±a. Thus a²=±1, and
the semisimple endpoint part is scalar times an involution. The
semisimple parts of A and B commute linearly: the possible negative
commutator is already excluded by their commuting invertible diagonal
restrictions to W.

After normalizing all four splitting generators to square to I, their
pairwise commutators are ±I. Every word can be collected as a sign times
a product in which each of the four generators occurs at most once.
Their group consequently has order at most 32 and is a finite 2-group.
Maschke applies in **every** odd characteristic, including three. An
invariant complement to W removes all four extension blocks
simultaneously. Identifying that complement with W through N retains
the stated N,M forms because those quotient-to-W maps are intrinsic.
This resolves the potentially consequential splitting issue.

In the commuting-outer branch, the middle lift either preserves W or
has the two off-diagonal inverse blocks displayed in the proof. Its
projective commutation with X makes the upper block self-adjoint for
I or Z respectively, giving the required alternating similarity form.

In the anticommuting-outer branch, multiplication by outer lifts removes
both middle commutator signs, since the two-dimensional commutator
pairing is nondegenerate. The square remains scalar and can be
normalized. The exact common centralizer is the four-parameter
template in the proof. Its involution equations give either a diagonal
similarity or d=−a and multiplier −1. Multiplication back by the outer
lifts preserves the similarity conclusion. Both branches cover
vanishing off-diagonal parameters as well as nonzero ones.

## 5. Integral checks and the final obstruction

The author's final checker was rerun independently with a separate
output path: all **54** exact checks passed. The independent checker
[phase7_psl4_zero_product_audit_check.py](phase7_psl4_zero_product_audit_check.py)
computes integer Smith normal forms for the five centralizer systems.
Their nonzero factors are, respectively:

- 10 copies of 1;
- 9 copies of 1;
- 10 copies of 1;
- 8 copies of 1 and 2 copies of 2;
- 8 copies of 1 and 4 copies of 2.

It also checks that each claimed template commutes identically and that
its parameter-coordinate matrix has only unit Smith factors. Thus the
templates have the full kernel dimension and remain injective over
every field of odd characteristic. These are integral certificates of
the centralizer descriptions, not just rational dimension calculations.

The matrix identity checks and the written splitting arguments have
no additional exceptional odd prime. The form contradiction is also
valid for q=3: a common nonzero form, even degenerate and even initially
preserved up to scalar, would pull back to a similarity character of
perfect SL₄(q). That character is trivial. The elementary matrices
I+Eᵢⱼ and I−Eᵢⱼ then force the form to be zero in odd characteristic.
Proper invariant subspaces likewise contradict the natural absolute
irreducibility of SL₄(q).
