# An obstruction at label eleven

Author manuscript, 10 October 2026. This manuscript, the universal-group
certificates in phase214, and their independent review concern Conjecture3.7
of Li and Zhang, *Regular 3-polytopes of type {n,n}*, arXiv:2505.09505v1.
They do not presuppose that this conjecture has the same formulation in the
2026 journal edition. That edition has been identified, but its complete
text and possible changes to the conjecture have not been checked.

The proof below is computer assisted. Its finite permutation-group
calculations and coset deductions are reproducible. The general quotient
argument is proved in Lean. The large group calculations, the presentation
identification, and the entire geometric conclusion are not Lean theorems.

## 1. Statement

Write U_d for the group generated by r_0,...,r_(d-1), with the following
relations, and no additional relations:

\[
r_i^2=1,\qquad (r_ir_j)^2=1\ (|i-j|>1),\qquad
(r_ir_{i+1})^{11}=1,
\]
\[
\bigl((r_{i+1}r_{i+2})^k(r_ir_{i+1})^k\bigr)^2=1
\quad(0\leq i\leq d-3,\ 1\leq k\leq10).                 \tag{1}
\]

The source conjecture asserts that these local relations, with eleven
replaced by any n≥3, can hold in the distinguished generators of a regular
d-polytope of exact type {n,...,n}. Merely finding a quotient that fails the
intersection property would not refute this existence assertion. We must
exclude every quotient retaining the required adjacent order.

**Theorem.** No regular d-polytope of exact type {11,...,11} satisfies (1)
when d≥5. In particular, the stated all-label conjecture is false.

We prove the rank-five assertion by identifying U_5 and giving an
intersection witness that survives every possible quotient with a
nontrivial adjacent product. The assertion in higher ranks then follows
from the intersection property of the subgroup on five consecutive
distinguished generators.

## 2. A literal matrix model

Let F=F₂[θ]/(θ⁵+θ²+1), and put α=θ⁵=θ²+1 and β=θ³.
On V=F⁵, with basis e_0,...,e_4, define

\[
 Q(x)=\sum_{i=0}^4x_i^2+\alpha\sum_{i=0}^3x_ix_{i+1},
 \qquad B(x,y)=Q(x+y)+Q(x)+Q(y).
\]

The distinguished matrices are the actual quadratic symmetries

\[
 R_i(x)=x+B(x,e_i)e_i.
                                                               \tag{2}
\]

Since Q(e_i)=1, each R_i preserves Q and is an involution. Nonadjacent
matrices commute. For an adjacent pair, the supporting plane is
nondegenerate, and the restriction of the product has matrix

\[
 H_\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\\\alpha&1+\alpha^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It has exact order eleven. The independent arithmetic checker verifies
this by multiplication in the specified binary field; because eleven is
prime, H≠I and H¹¹=I suffice. The orthogonal splitting by the supporting
plane shows that the order on the whole space is the same.

For completeness, the reason for the extra relations is not restricted
to this example. If e,f,h have quadratic value one, with B(e,h)=0 and
B(e,f)=B(f,h), then z=e+h is singular and orthogonal to their span.
For u in z-perp set

\[
 T_u(x)=x+B(x,z)u+\bigl(B(x,u)+Q(u)B(x,z)\bigr)z.
\]

Direct expansion gives T_uT_v=T_(u+v). These translations form an
elementary abelian group, and σ_uσ_(u+z)=T_u when Q(u)=1.
They are normalized by every relevant quadratic isometry fixing z.
Putting A=σ_eσ_f and C=σ_fσ_h, the product AC is a translation,
so C^kA^k is also a translation for every integer k. Its square is one.
This proves the relations in (1) on the whole ambient space, including
coordinates outside the three-vector span. The retained
CharacteristicTwoTranslations.lean proves this argument with the actual
quadratic form. Both the new binary-field checker and GAP also verify
all thirty extra relations of U_5 literally.

The radical of B is the line spanned by

\[
 z_0=e_0+e_2+e_4,\qquad Q(z_0)=1.
\]

All the R_i fix z_0. The quotient V/⟨z_0⟩ has basis e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3,
where e_4 is replaced by e_0+e_2. Thus the induced matrix is obtained by
adding the fifth row to the first and third rows and retaining the first
four columns. This is an explicit map, checked on every basis vector.

Let S denote the permutation group generated by these induced matrices
on the 33,825 projective points of the four-dimensional quotient.
Our convention is important: matrices act on columns, whereas GAP acts
on row vectors. The homomorphism to the latter action uses inverse
transpose. Each distinguished generator and the reflection used below
is involutory, so their inverse transposes are their transposes, exactly
as in the checker. Transpose alone is not being asserted to be a
homomorphism on arbitrary products.

The exact permutation computations give

\[
 |S|=1,124,799,322,521,600,
 \qquad S\text{ is simple}.                                  \tag{3}
\]

The group is noncommutative: two adjacent involutions have product of
order eleven. In the usual classical-group notation it is Sp₄(32).
This identification is consistent with the preserved alternating form
and the order 32⁴(32²−1)(32⁴−1); the argument below needs the concrete
permutation group and its verified simplicity, rather than a separate
classification of its subgroups.

This action is faithful on the ambient group of Q-isometries. Indeed,
an isometry fixing every projective point of V/⟨z_0⟩ induces a scalar
λ there. Preservation of its nondegenerate alternating form implies
λ²=1, hence λ=1. The isometry therefore has the form
x↦x+ℓ(x)z_0. Preservation of Q gives ℓ(x)²=0 for every x, and hence
ℓ=0. In particular, equality of two of our projective permutations
implies equality of their actual five-dimensional matrices.

## 3. An intersection witness, including its sign

Put u=e_1+βe_2 and define

\[
 J(x)=x+\frac{B(x,u)}{Q(u)}u.                              \tag{4}
\]

The denominator is nonzero. We shall prove that J belongs to both end
subgroups ⟨R_0,R_1,R_2,R_3⟩ and ⟨R_1,R_2,R_3,R_4⟩, but not to the
middle subgroup D=⟨R_1,R_2,R_3⟩. We need a little more: in both end
subgroups, J can be represented by a word of odd length. Recording this
sign prevents an overlooked central factor from invalidating the
argument about universal groups.

On either four-dimensional end space, the restricted quadratic form is
the same constant-coefficient form. Its symmetry group generated by
the four marked matrices acts on an orbit of 1,025 singular projective
points. The computed permutation image has order

\[
 2,147,481,600.                                           \tag{5}
\]

Adjoin two new points, and make every marked generator interchange
them. The resulting signed image has the same order (5). The target
permutation for (4), together with interchange of the two new points,
belongs to this signed image, for both embeddings of u in the end
space. These are explicit finite subgroup-membership checks, not an
assumption about intersections or a consequence drawn merely from
comparing orders.

To pass from permutation membership to matrix membership, the
certificate gives a projective frame in the singular orbit. In the
binary field notation that identifies θ with integer2, its first four
vectors are

\[
 (1,0,1,0),\ (1,0,1,5),\ (1,0,16,5),\ (1,31,16,5).
\]

They form a basis. The fifth vector (1,20,11,22) has coefficients
(14,4,4,15) in that basis, all nonzero. All five vectors are singular.
Any linear transformation fixing their projective points is scalar:
the first four make it diagonal, and the fifth equates its four
diagonal entries. A scalar preserving Q must have square one, and
therefore is the identity in characteristic two. Thus the action on
this singular orbit is faithful on every Q-isometry, including the
target reflection. The independently checked frame proves that the
signed membership tests really give the asserted matrices, with odd
word parity.

Each four-dimensional end space is nondegenerate. Its marked
symmetries and the reflection (4) fix its orthogonal complement
pointwise. Agreement on the end space consequently gives agreement
on all of V. This establishes both signed end memberships for J.

Nonmembership in D has a smaller certificate. The three-space
W=⟨e_1,e_2,e_3⟩ has singular radical ⟨e_1+e_3⟩. On the quotient
W/⟨e_1+e_3⟩, the image of D is a dihedral group of order22. The image
of R_1J has order33. If J belonged to D, this product would belong to
a group of order22, which is impossible. The independent bit-field
program enumerates all22 elements of the quotient and separately
verifies the orders33,3,11 needed to establish that the product has
exact order33. No large-group intersection computation is used for
this nonmembership assertion.

Let ε generate C₂. In C₂×S we therefore have the explicit witness

\[
 w=(\varepsilon,J)\in
 \langle(\varepsilon,R_0),\ldots,(\varepsilon,R_3)\rangle
 \cap
 \langle(\varepsilon,R_1),\ldots,(\varepsilon,R_4)\rangle,
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
whose projection J to S is outside the projection of the middle
subgroup. Here the matrices in (6) denote their faithful permutation
images. Faithfulness proved above justifies this change of notation.

## 4. The universal group

Every defining relator in (1) has even length, so assigning the sign
ε to each generator is compatible with the presentation. Combining
this sign with the matrix representation gives a homomorphism

\[
 U_5\longrightarrow C_2\times S,
 \qquad r_i\longmapsto(\varepsilon,R_i).
\]

The signed permutation image, obtained by adjoining two interchanged
points to the 33,825-point action, has computed order

\[
 2,249,598,645,043,200=2|S|.                               \tag{7}
\]

It is consequently all of C₂×S. There is also a conceptual check:
its projections to both factors are surjective, and a noncommutative
simple group has no nontrivial homomorphism to C₂. Thus a subdirect
product cannot be the graph of a binary character of S.

The independent upper certificates in phase214 prove successively

\[
 [U_3:\langle r_0,r_1\rangle]\leq1024,
\]
\[
 [U_4:\langle r_0,r_1,r_2\rangle]\leq95,325,
 \qquad
 [U_5:\langle r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3\rangle]\leq1,047,552.
\]

The first two involutions generate a group of order at most22.
Each succeeding parabolic is an image of the preceding universal
presentation. Multiplication therefore gives

\[
 |U_5|\leq22\cdot1024\cdot95,325\cdot1,047,552
       =2,249,598,645,043,200.                              \tag{8}
\]

These are genuine upper bounds. The checker introduces coset
representatives along a spanning tree, then accepts another transition
only when a literal relator, all of whose other transitions have been
proved, forces it. It does not infer an upper bound simply because a
permutation table satisfies the relators. The soundness proof and
complete portable replay are in phase214_universal_bounds.md.

The lower bound (7) and upper bound (8) agree. They identify U_5,
with its distinguished generators, with C₂×S. In particular the
matrix obstruction has now been placed in the universal group, rather
than in just one possibly unrepresentative quotient.

## 5. Why no quotient repairs the intersection

We record the short group argument, since it is the step needed to
refute an existence conjecture.

Let S be any noncommutative simple group and let
f:C₂×S→H be a homomorphism. Suppose that

\[
 f(\varepsilon,a)f(\varepsilon,b)\ne1                     \tag{9}
\]

for some a,b in S. The restriction f_S(s)=f(1,s) is nontrivial,
because the product in (9) equals f(1,ab). Simplicity makes f_S
injective. If x=(c,s) lies in ker(f), then for every t in S the
commutator [x,(1,t)]=(1,[s,t]) also lies in the kernel. Injectivity
of f_S gives [s,t]=1 for all t. A noncommutative simple group is
centerless, so s=1. Therefore

\[
 f(x)=f(y)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_S=y_S.              \tag{10}
\]

Apply this to the signed witness w in (6). Its image is in both
end parabolics. If f(w) were in the image of the middle parabolic,
it would equal f(d) for some middle element d. Equation(10) would
put J in the projection of that middle subgroup, contradicting §3.
The intersection property therefore fails in the image of f.

Any proposed polytope in rank five gives such a quotient map f.
Its adjacent product has exact order eleven and hence satisfies
(9). Thus every possible quotient retaining that order is excluded.
This proves the rank-five theorem. In rank d>5, the subgroup on
the first five distinguished generators of a string C-group is
itself a string C-group. It would have type {11,11,11,11} and
satisfy (1), contradicting the rank-five conclusion. The theorem
follows in all ranks d≥5.

The nine general declarations in SimpleQuotientObstruction.lean
include centerlessness, injectivity on the simple factor, (10),
and preservation of an explicit intersection witness. They also
prove that a binary character trivial on an index-three subgroup
is trivial; this supplies an alternative sign argument, but is
not needed once the signed membership tests in §3 have passed.
The formal statements retain their actual hypotheses. They do
not introduce the finite group orders or simplicity as axioms.

## 6. Attribution and verification boundary

The source question and its relators are due to Li and Zhang.
The quadratic symmetry construction belongs to the existing
characteristic-two orthogonal-group literature, including
Brooksbank, Ferrara and Leemans. The local translation identity
and its formal proof are retained from our earlier work; they
are not counted as new again. The string C-group correspondence
and preservation of that property by distinguished parabolics
are standard inputs from regular-polytope theory.

The new work here is the choice and verification of the
label-eleven obstruction, the signed witness, the independently
certified universal-group identification, and the argument that
all quotients retaining the adjacent order inherit the failure.
The upper-certificate component is credited separately to
phase214. The root and independent reviewer perform separate
replays; this does not create multiple conjecture answers.

GAP remains trusted for the large permutation-group order,
simplicity and signed membership calculations. The small field
arithmetic has an independent standard-library Python replay.
The universal upper deductions have a separate C++ checker with
a written soundness proof. Lean checks the general quotient
argument and the retained local power identity. Neither the
large calculations nor the entire counterexample have an
end-to-end Lean certificate.

This answers the mathematical statement of arXivv1 Conjecture3.7
negatively. It does not establish historical priority, continuing
openness in the 2026 published edition, or a compression ratio.
The previously proved positive label families remain valid.
