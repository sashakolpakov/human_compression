# Even labels from an orientable odd-label marking

10 October 2026. Frozen additive author proof; independent mathematical admission is pending. This question-solving note extends the finite-existence track; it is not a compression module or a claim about universal presentations.

## 1. A marked parity fiber theorem

**Theorem.** Let E=⟨e_0,…,e_(d−1)⟩ and G=⟨g_0,…,g_(d−1)⟩ be finite string C-groups, and suppose:

1. E is a2-group.
2. Every adjacent product g_i g_(i+1) has odd order.
3. There are homomorphisms ε:E→F2 and δ:G→F2 taking every distinguished generator to1.

Then the marked mix

\[
H=\langle(e_i,g_i):0\le i<d\rangle\le E\times G
\]

is a string C-group. If J is any subset of the marker path and c(J) is the number of its nonempty consecutive components, then

\[
|H_J|=|E_J|\,|G_J|/2^{c(J)}.                    \tag{1}
\]

More precisely, writing x=∏_C x_C and y=∏_C y_C for the unique component decompositions in E_J and G_J,

\[
H_J=\{(x,y)\in E_J\times G_J:
          \varepsilon(x_C)=\delta(y_C)\text{ for every component }C\}. \tag{2}
\]

The parity maps in hypothesis3 are essential; they are not inferred merely from the markers being involutions.

**Connected intervals.** Fix a nonempty interval C of markers. The projection of H_C onto each factor E_C and G_C is surjective. Put

\[
K=\{x\in E_C:(x,1)\in H_C\},\qquad
L=\{y\in G_C:(1,y)\in H_C\}.
\]

These are normal subgroups of their respective factors. Choosing a companion y for each x gives a well-defined isomorphism E_C/K≅G_C/L; its graph is precisely H_C. This is the elementary subgroup argument usually called Goursat's lemma.

The common quotient is a finite2-group because it is a quotient of E_C. In this quotient the adjacent images of g_i have product of both odd order and2-power order, hence product1. They are involutions or identities, so their images are equal. Connectivity of C shows that the entire common quotient is generated by a single involution, and thus has order at most2. On the other hand H_C lies in the proper parity fiber ε(x)=δ(y), and both parity restrictions are onto. Thus the common quotient is exactly F2, and H_C is the entire parity fiber. This includes singleton intervals.

**Arbitrary supports.** A standard parabolic of a string C-group is the internal direct product of the parabolics on the consecutive components of its support. The components commute by the string relations. To prove the product is direct, intersect one component subgroup with the subgroup on all other components and apply IP; induction gives uniqueness. The same argument applies to H as a subgroup of E×G, without yet presuming IP for H: its component factors commute, and their projections into the direct component decompositions in E and G make their product direct. The interval result proves (2) and (1).

**All intersections.** Let (x,y) lie in H_J∩H_K and put L=J∩K. The IP of E and G places x in E_L and y in G_L. For each component D of L, set

\[
z_D=\varepsilon(x_D)+\delta(y_D)\in\mathbb F_2.
\]

Construct a bipartite graph whose two vertex sets are the components of J and of K, and whose edges are their nonempty intersections. Each edge is exactly one component D of L. Formula(2) says that the sum of the incident z_D is zero at every vertex.

This graph is a forest. Indeed, among any remaining intervals choose one with smallest right endpoint. Every neighboring interval contains that endpoint, because it intersects the chosen interval and cannot end earlier. Intervals in the other family are disjoint, so there is at most one neighbor. Thus every nonempty subgraph has a vertex of degree at most one. Repeatedly removing leaves, the zero sum at a leaf forces its edge value z_D to vanish, and then all edge values vanish. Formula(2) for L now gives (x,y)∈H_L. The reverse containment is immediate. This proves full IP, and the string and involution conditions are inherited from the two factors. ∎

This theorem proves the precise parabolic fiber; a bare assertion that mixing preserves IP would not suffice.

## 2. Orientable odd-label symmetry groups

The characteristic-two construction must first be placed in a nondegenerate even-dimensional ambient quadratic space. A symmetry marking acting only on an odd-dimensional quotient can lose its parity character, so it cannot be used without this step.

For a desired rank d and a permitted odd label n=q+1(q≥4) or n=q−1(q≥8), choose an even D≥d. Use the same constant-coefficient BFL quadratic form on F_q^D and retain its first d distinguished symmetries. The published full-IP theorem makes this parabolic a string C-group on those d markers. The ambient polar matrix is nonsingular: its tridiagonal determinant satisfies det_D=α²det_(D−2), det_0=1, hence det_D=α^D≠0. Every marker is an ambient symmetry of rank1. The previous local-power lemma applies on this full ambient space, so every required power relation still holds.

The classical Dickson homomorphism on the isometry group of a nondegenerate even-dimensional quadratic space in characteristic two is

\[
\delta(g)=\operatorname{rank}(g-I)\pmod2.
\]

It sends every symmetry to1. Its restriction supplies the homomorphism needed in the parity fiber theorem, simultaneously on every parabolic. We retain this classical fact explicitly; it is not the determinant (which is always1 here). A primary reference is Gabriele Nebe and Richard Parker, *Equivariant quadratic forms in characteristic 2*, Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society 55 (2023), 668–679, §3, paragraph immediately before Lemma 3.1, DOI [10.1112/blms.12748](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12748), citing Taylor, *The Geometry of the Classical Groups*, Theorem 11.43. The published HTML §2.1 definition of nondegeneracy and the complete relevant §3 paragraph were freshly read; they specify precisely a nonsingular polarization and the rank formula above. The original Taylor proof has not been retrieved. The [official Magma handbook](https://magma.maths.usyd.edu.au/magma/handbook/text/319), `DicksonInvariant`, independently states the same scope, but no Magma implementation is used as proof.

For n=3, use S_(d+1) with adjacent transpositions and the ordinary sign homomorphism instead.

## 3. Doubling and quadrupling

Take E=C2^d with its standard coordinate generators. It is a string C-group, ε is the sum of coordinates, and all adjacent products have order2. It satisfies the extra consecutive-triple power relation for every integer k, because the entire group is elementary abelian.

Alternatively, take E=UT_(d+1)(F2) with the simple transvections from the frozen label-four note. Its parity homomorphism is the sum of the first superdiagonal entries. Matrix multiplication adds that superdiagonal, so this is a homomorphism taking every generator to1. Its adjacent products have order4, and the previously proved local relations hold for all integer k, by their period4.

Apply the parity fiber theorem to either E and the orientable odd-label group G above. The adjacent product in H has order lcm(2,n)=2n or lcm(4,n)=4n, respectively. Every additional power word vanishes in both coordinates, hence in H. The standard string-C-group/polytope correspondence therefore proves:

**Corollary.** In every rank d≥3 there are finite regular polytopes satisfying the exact Li–Zhang relations for labels n,2n,4n whenever

\[
n=3,\qquad n=2^e+1\ (e\ge2),\qquad n=2^e-1\ (e\ge3).
\]

The separate n=4 construction remains available. This adds, for example, labels6,10,12,14,18,20,28,30,34,36,60,68,… to the finite-existence range. It does not cover arbitrary labels: pure2-powers≥8 and odd labels such as11 or13 remain outside this argument. It gives no universal-presentation identification for the new labels, and it is not yet a whole-claim Lean theorem. Historical novelty is unverified; Goursat's lemma, Dickson parity, the underlying BFL groups and general mixing are credited inputs.
