## Zhang's 2024 paper

Wei-Juan Zhang, *Abelian covers of regular hypertopes*, Journal of Algebra **650** (2024), 123–144, DOI [10.1016/j.jalgebra.2024.03.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2024.03.017). The [publisher preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021869324001534) identifies the author, title and DOI. The volume and pages are also recorded in the bibliography of the author-hosted N005 preprint. The exact online-publication day was not verified from accessible primary metadata in this pass; the year is not inferred from the DOI alone.

The publisher abstract, introduction, section snippets and acknowledgements were read. They describe an abelian group-covering method, illustrated by rank-five through rank-seven families, with orders growing linearly in type parameters. Section 5 names Theorems 4.1 and 4.4 as its construction inputs. The introduction explicitly separates the C-group intersection property from the flag-transitivity needed for a regular hypertope.

**The statements and proofs of Theorems 4.1 and 4.4 were not obtained.** Direct publisher full-text/PDF and Elsevier XML endpoints failed; exact-title, DOI, author/institution and PDF searches found no accessible primary full text. ResearchGate offered only a full-text request. No request was sent. An inaccessible theorem cannot be treated as either equivalent to, or weaker than, our cover criterion.

This is directly relevant prior art for the claim that abelian coverings produce regular hypertopes. It prevents any broad claim that the general idea is new. A precise comparison still requires the source's covering definition, kernel hypotheses, parabolic-intersection argument, and flag-transitivity proof. In particular, the words “cover” and “abelian” do not identify its group construction with a regular graph cover whose higher elements are connected shadow components.

## A precise comparison boundary for the affine-code example

Our frozen phase30 seed uses thirty nonzero binary edge columns in a group of order (2^{13}). The obvious attempt to make these thirty columns the distinguished involutions of a rank-thirty regular input hypertope cannot work: a C-group marking is independent, while thirty vectors in a thirteen-dimensional binary vector space are dependent. Equivalently, repeated use of the intersection property would require at least (2^{30}) distinct subset products.

Consequently, the **direct** use of N005 Corollary 4.13 with this quotient-column marking has an unsatisfied input hypothesis. This elementary obstruction neither rules out another realization of that construction nor proves novelty of the affine-code theorem. The unsubdivided cube before the code quotient and the quotient after imposing the code are different input systems.

The project's current cover theory explicitly separates typed coset Helly, which controls flag completion, from common-shadow monodromy intersections, which control residual connectivity. N005 supplies established coset-Helly and split-extension context; whether Zhang's inaccessible theorems subsume a particular abelian special case remains **undetermined**. The phase31 characteristic surface covers need not split their lifted automorphism-group extension, even though the later affine extension is defined as a semidirect product. No comparison based solely on the latter split extension settles the former.
