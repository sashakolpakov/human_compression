# Finite regular hypertopes at the fixed parameter \(s=6\)

This is a separate geometric supplement to the frozen infinitude certificate. It constructs infinitely many finite regular rank-four hypertopes with the **same** four residue parameters
\[
 ((3,0),(2,0),(1,1),(6,0)).
\]
It does not vary the last torus parameter, identify the universal group, or resolve the parameterized question in the source's Problem 9.2. In particular, the quotient modulus below is not the source parameter \(s\).

## 1. Literal construction and claim

Use the three integer-voltage permutations \(g_x,g_y,g_z\) in phase32_L003_voltage.json on
\[
 \Omega\times\mathbb Z,\qquad |\Omega|=216,\qquad
 (i,t)g=(p_g(i),t+v_g(i)).
\]
All actions and products are on the right. On two copies of this set, let \(r_0\) interchange the copies without changing \((i,t)\). Put
\[
 X=\operatorname{diag}(g_x,g_x^{-1}),\quad
 Y=\operatorname{diag}(g_y,g_y^{-1}),\quad
 Z=\operatorname{diag}(g_z,g_z^{-1}),\qquad
 r_1=r_0X,\quad r_2=r_0Y,\quad r_3=r_0Z.
\]
Thus all four \(r_i\) are involutions and \(r_0r_1=X\), \(r_0r_2=Y\), \(r_0r_3=Z\). Let \(\widetilde H=\langle r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3\rangle\).

For an integer \(m\ge2\), reduce the integer coordinate modulo \(m\), and let \(Q_m\) be the resulting permutation group on \(432m\) points, with its four indicated generators. For \(I\subseteq\{0,1,2,3\}\), write \(\widetilde H_I\) and \((Q_m)_I\) for the subgroups generated by the corresponding marks.

**Theorem.** For every even \(m\ge2\), the standard coset geometry of \(Q_m\) is a finite regular hypertope. Its six pair orders, in the order \(01,02,03,12,13,23\), are
\[
 (6,3,2,2,3,6).
\]
Its four rank-three residues are the exact toroidal maps specified by the fixed source parameters above. Its maximal parabolic orders, with omitted types \(0,1,2,3\), are
\[
 36,\quad432,\quad48,\quad108.
\]
The group \(Q_2\) has order \(27648\). The orders \(|Q_m|\) are unbounded, so the construction includes infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic finite hypertopes. The same marking on \(\widetilde H\) is an infinite regular hypertope.

No formula for \(|Q_m|\) for general \(m\) is asserted here. Literal coordinate reduction has kernel consisting of translations whose coordinates are divisible by \(m\); it must not be identified with \(mL\) for an abstract translation lattice \(L\) without a separate lattice argument.

It verifies all ten source rotational relators, after substituting \(x=X,y=Y,z=Z\), on both copies: \(4320\) relator/state equalities. It verifies that \(y(zy)^2\) has exact order six. It also verifies that

It stores a transformation for every encountered underlying permutation, and rejects any collision with different integer translations. ## 3. Flag transitivity is checked separately

Let \(P_i=(Q_2)_{\{0,1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}}\). Fix a type \(i\) and a set \(J\) of other types, and set \(T_J=\bigcap_{j\in J}P_j\). Consider the type-\(i\) right cosets incident with every member of the base flag \(\{P_j:j\in J\}\).

The cosets meeting \(P_j\) have representatives in \(P_j\). For any fixed \(j_0\in J\), the distinct such representatives are the right cosets of \(P_i\cap P_{j_0}\) in \(P_{j_0}\). A representative \(a\) also gives a coset meeting \(P_j\) exactly when \(a\in P_iP_j\). The checker constructs these finite product sets and counts the common incident cosets.

There is always an orbit of common incident cosets under \(T_J\), with size
\[
 [T_J:T_J\cap P_i].
\]
All sixteen nonautomatic cases, namely every \(i\) and every \(J\) of size two or three, have exactly this cardinality. The cases \(|J|\le1\) are automatic.

Here is why these counts suffice. Induct on the number of elements in a flag. An existing flag can be moved to the corresponding base flag by the induction hypothesis. The equality just checked says that every incident next element is in the orbit of the base next element under the stabilizer \(T_J\). Hence every flag is carried to a base flag by the right action. All flags extend to a chamber, and the group is flag-transitive. Equivalently, every pairwise-intersecting typed family of parabolic cosets has a common point. This argument does not infer flag transitivity merely from the subgroup intersection property.

## 4. The quotient transport lemma

The following elementary form of the quotient criterion includes the flag condition.

**Lemma.** Let \(G\) and \(\overline G\) be groups with corresponding involutory markings and a surjective generator-preserving homomorphism \(\pi:G\to\overline G\). Suppose \(\pi\) is injective on every maximal marked parabolic of \(G\). If the marked subgroups of \(\overline G\) satisfy the full intersection property and its standard coset system has typed coset Helly, then both statements hold in \(G\).

**Proof of intersection property.** Only proper subsets \(I,J\) need consideration. For \(h\in G_I\cap G_J\), the downstairs intersection property provides \(c\in G_{I\cap J}\) with \(\pi(c)=\pi(h)\). Both \(h,c\) lie in \(G_I\), which lies in an injective maximal parabolic. Thus \(h=c\).

**Proof of typed Helly.** Start with a pairwise-intersecting typed family of right cosets. Normalize one of its members to \(P_0\). Every other member can then be written \(P_i g_i\) with \(g_i\in P_0\). Downstairs Helly gives a common point \(\bar h\in\pi(P_0)\), and injectivity on \(P_0\) supplies its unique lift \(h\in P_0\).

For each \(i\), the element \(\pi(hg_i^{-1})\) lies in both \(\pi(P_0)\) and \(\pi(P_i)\). The downstairs intersection property identifies this intersection as the image of the subgroup generated by all marks except \(0,i\). Choose an element \(c\) of this common subgroup with the same image. Since \(hg_i^{-1},c\in P_0\), injectivity gives \(hg_i^{-1}=c\in P_i\). Hence \(h\) belongs to every original coset. The argument works for arbitrary groups; neither finiteness nor a central kernel is required. \(\square\)

Apply the lemma first to \(\widetilde H\to Q_2\). Its maximal parabolics are the exact finite groups enumerated in §2 and map injectively. Next, for every even \(m\), reduction of the integer coordinate gives a surjection \(Q_m\to Q_2\). Each maximal parabolic has the same finite order as the corresponding parabolic of \(\widetilde H\), so this map is injective on it. This proves full intersection property and typed Helly for every asserted marking.

For completeness, these two conditions give the hypertope properties directly. Typed Helly makes the coset system a flag-transitive geometry. Intersection property makes the full chamber stabilizer trivial and every panel stabilizer a group of order two, giving thinness. In every residue, its stabilizer is generated by the remaining marked involutions; successive multiplication by these involutions connects its chamber graph. Thus every residue of rank at least two is connected. Finally, a type-preserving automorphism fixing a chamber fixes every adjacent chamber by thinness, and hence all chambers by connectivity. Therefore the displayed chamber-regular group is the full type-preserving automorphism group.

One can see the order from the triangular-lattice index \(a^2+ab+b^2\) and the twelve flags per translation fundamental cell.

## 6. Infinitely many examples, with the parameter fixed

The word \(w\) from §2 is a pure integer translation with one coordinate equal to \(-1\). Its image in \(Q_m\) has exact order \(m\): every coordinate translation has order dividing \(m\), while the displayed coordinate has order \(m\). Therefore \(|Q_m|\ge m\). The even moduli produce unbounded group orders and hence infinitely many isomorphism classes of finite regular hypertopes with these same residues. The same word proves that \(\widetilde H\) is infinite.

The parameter word \(y(zy)^2\) has exact order six in every one of these examples, because it belongs to an injectively preserved finite parabolic. Thus none of these moduli is being used to manufacture a toroidal parameter \(s>6\).
