# Excluding rank five for PSL(4,q) in odd characteristic

10 October 2026. Written proof with two independent internal mathematical
reviews and exact algebra checks; not external peer review. Publication
priority has not been established. The prior reductions are in
[phase7_psl4.md](phase7_psl4.md).

**Theorem.** For every odd prime power q, PSL(4,q) has no string C-group
representation of rank five.

The proof below completes the zero-product cases left by that prior note.
It does not exclude rank six and therefore does not by itself settle the
full maximum-rank-four conjecture. A rank-five parabolic in a hypothetical
rank-six representation is a proper subgroup, so no such inference is made.

Use the notation of that note. Its theorem reduces a putative rank-five
PSL(4,q) tuple to nonzero N,M with N²=M²=NM=MN=0. Write

    A=T₀T₁=S_A(I+N),   B=T₃T₄=S_B(I+M)

for the multiplicative Jordan decompositions. T₀,T₁ negate N and fix M;
T₃,T₄ fix N and negate M. The semisimple parts centralize both N and M.
The four endpoint generators preserve the images and kernels of both.
The whole five-generator group acts irreducibly.

We may reverse the string and interchange N,M, so only the rank pairs
(1,1), (1,2), (2,2) require consideration. N and M cannot be proportional:
T₀ negates N and centralizes M, in odd characteristic.

## 1. Both nilpotents have rank one

Write N=v f and M=w g with nonzero vectors v,w and linear forms f,g.
Every image vector is in both kernels. If the image lines are distinct
and the forms independent, set

    N=E₁₃, M=E₂₄, W=ker N∩ker M=⟨e₁,e₂⟩.

Every endpoint preserves each image line. Hence all cross signs are +1:
two invertible matrices preserving a common line cannot anticommute.
In particular T₀,T₄ and A,B commute linearly. The common centralizer of
N,M consists of

    [[D,E],[0,D]],   D diagonal, E arbitrary.

T₀ preserves each image line and inverts A. Its conjugation therefore
fixes the two eigenvalues of A separately while replacing them by their
inverses. Thus both eigenvalues are signs, and S_A²=I. Likewise S_B²=I.
The four involutions T₀,T₄,S_A,S_B commute: inversion of a semisimple
involution is trivial, and all other assertions follow from the cross
commutations. Their group D is an elementary abelian 2-group.

Choose a D-invariant complement Q to W by Maschke. Inside Q, the one-
dimensional kernels of N and M are D-invariant because D normalizes each
map up to sign. This separates V into two invariant length-two chains,
one for N and one for M. On the N-chain, T₃,T₄ are scalar: they commute
with N and act diagonally on its two one-dimensional chain levels. On
the M-chain, T₀,T₁ are scalar for the same reason. Consequently the
separated-support lemma of phase3_psl4.md, §1, applies, a contradiction.

Suppose next that the images coincide, L=im N=im M, while f,g are
independent. Then Z=ker N∩ker M has dimension two. Every endpoint
preserves L, so again all cross signs are positive. In a basis with
N=E₁₃,M=E₁₄, their common centralizer has shape

    [[a,b,c,d],[0,e,f,g],[0,0,a,0],[0,0,0,a]].

Its eigenvalues are a on L and V/Z and e on Z/L. Both these lines in the
invariant filtration are preserved by T₀ and T₄. The same inversion
argument gives S_A²=S_B²=I, so D=⟨T₀,T₄,S_A,S_B⟩ is again an elementary
abelian 2-group. The entire endpoint group is generated by D,I+N,I+M.

Consider the joint T₀,T₄ weights. If L has weight χ, the two source lines
in V/Z have weights χ+(1,0) and χ+(0,1), since N and M respectively
change exactly one sign. The remaining line Z/L has some fourth weight.
Thus at least three distinct weights occur. The middle T₂ permutes the
joint weight spaces by a fixed translation δ in F₂².

If δ≠0, equality of weight multiplicities under this translation forces
all four weights to occur once. D preserves each weight line. The space
L+T₂L is a proper two-dimensional space invariant under D and T₂; it is
invariant under N,M as well because their images lie in L. This contradicts
irreducibility. If δ=0 and the χ-weight has dimension one, L itself is
invariant under T₂. If it has dimension two, that weight space equals Z,
which is invariant under T₂. Both alternatives again contradict
irreducibility.

The case of equal kernels and distinct images is the dual of the preceding
case, by inverse transpose. Equal images and equal kernels would make
N,M proportional and has already been excluded. This exhausts rank (1,1).

## 2. Ranks one and two

Let rank N=1 and rank M=2. Set W=im M=ker M. Since NM=MN=0, N kills W
and has image in W. Identify a complement Q with W through M, so

    M=[[0,I],[0,0]],   N=[[0,C],[0,0]], rank C=1.

T₀ centralizes M and negates N. Its action on W and V/W is the same
involution A₀, and A₀ C A₀⁻¹=−C. Thus tr C=0, and C is a nonzero
square-zero two-dimensional matrix. Similarly T₄ acts on the two levels
as B₀ and −B₀ with B₀ C B₀⁻¹=−C.

All endpoints preserve the line im N, so all cross signs are positive;
in particular A₀ and B₀ commute. Choose C=E₁₂ and A₀=Z=diag(1,−1).
A commuting involution reversing C must be ±Z, so change the sign of T₄
to take B₀=Z. Maschke gives a ⟨T₀,T₄⟩-invariant complement to W.
The resulting exact normal form is

    N=E₁₄, M=E₁₃+E₂₄,
    T₀=diag(1,−1,1,−1), T₄=diag(1,−1,−1,1).

The common centralizer of N,M consists of [[D,E],[0,D]] with D commuting
with the nonzero square-zero C; it has only one eigenvalue. Thus S_A and
S_B are scalar, and inversion forces their scalars to be signs. Up to
irrelevant signs,

    T₁=T₀(I+N), T₃=(I+M)T₄.

The four joint weight lines of T₀,T₄ are distinct. Therefore T₂ is
monomial, and its permutation is one of the four translations of their
weight square. For the identity and (1 2)(3 4), the plane ⟨e₁,e₂⟩ is
invariant under every generator. For (1 3)(2 4), the plane ⟨e₁,e₃⟩ is
invariant. The last case is (1 4)(2 3). All endpoint generators preserve
the alternating form F with

    F₁₄=F₂₃=1, F₄₁=F₃₂=−1,

up to scalar. The paired coefficients of the monomial involution T₂
multiply to one, so T₂ᵀFT₂=−F. This is again impossible for PSL(4,q).

## 3. Both nilpotents have rank two

Here W=im N=ker N=im M=ker M. Identify V/W with W through N and write

    N=[[0,I],[0,0]], M=[[0,C],[0,0]], C invertible.

T₀ acts on the two levels as A₀,−A₀ and T₄ as B₀,B₀. Both A₀,B₀
negate C by conjugation. Thus tr C=0. As C is invertible and the
characteristic is odd, choose C=λZ with λ≠0. Both A₀ and B₀ are
off-diagonal involutions. Choose A₀=X. Since T₀,T₄ commute up to sign,
so do A₀,B₀. Either B₀=X up to sign, or B₀=iJ up to sign, where i²=−1,

    X=[[0,1],[1,0]], J=[[0,1],[−1,0]].

We must justify removing the off-diagonal extension blocks from all
semisimple generators simultaneously. The common centralizer of N,M is

    [[D,E],[0,D]], D diagonal.

For A=T₀T₁, write D=diag(a,b). Inversion by T₀ interchanges the two
eigenvalues and inverts them, so b=a⁻¹. Conjugation by T₄ sends A to a
sign of itself and also interchanges a,b. Hence a²=±1. Consequently S_A
is, up to scalar, an involution whose two diagonal blocks are I or Z.
The same holds for S_B. Also A and B commute linearly: they commute up
to sign globally, but their diagonal restrictions to W commute, ruling
out a negative sign. Their semisimple parts commute as well.

Normalize S_A,S_B to square to I. Each pair among T₀,T₄,S_A,S_B commutes
up to sign, so these four matrices generate a finite 2-group D. Maschke
gives a D-invariant complement Q to W. In this decomposition we have
simultaneously

    T₀=diag(X,−X), T₄=diag(B₀,B₀),
    S_A=diag(Z^a,Z^a), S_B=diag(Z^b,Z^b) up to scalar,

with a,b∈{0,1}. The matrices N,M retain their preceding form because
their maps from V/W to W were fixed intrinsically.

Define, for F=I or Z, the alternating form

    Ω_F=[[0,F],[−F,0]].

Both I+N and I+M preserve each Ω_F. Every element of D preserves each
up to scalar. Therefore all four endpoints preserve both forms.

### 3.1 Commuting outer lifts

If B₀=X, then T₀T₄=diag(I,−I). If T₂ commutes with this product it
preserves W, so the whole group is reducible. Otherwise

    T₂=[[0,D],[D⁻¹,0]].

Projective commutation with T₄ says D commutes or anticommutes with X.
In the first case D=aI+bX and Dᵀ=D; take F=I. In the second D=aZ+bJ
and DᵀZ=ZD; take F=Z. In either case FD=DᵀF, which directly gives
T₂ᵀΩ_F T₂=−Ω_F.

### 3.2 Anticommuting outer lifts

If B₀=iJ, multiply T₂ by a suitable product of T₀,T₄ so the resulting
matrix commutes linearly with both. This is possible because their
projective commutator pairing is nondegenerate on a two-dimensional
space. The resulting square is scalar, so rescale to square to I.
Multiplying by the outer matrices does not affect whether a matrix is
a similarity of Ω_I or Ω_Z.

The common linear centralizer has the exact shape

    Q=[[aI,bJ],[cJ,dI]].

The involution equations are a²−bc=d²−bc=1 and
b(a+d)=c(a+d)=0. If b=c=0, Q preserves Ω_Z up to scalar ad.
Otherwise d=−a, and direct multiplication gives

    QᵀΩ_Z Q=−Ω_Z.

Thus every case again supplies a common nonzero form.

## 4. Conclusion, verification and remaining scope

The nonzero square-zero endpoint maps have ranks one or two. The three
rank pairs just considered exhaust their possibilities, including equal
image or kernel cases. Each gives either a proper invariant subspace or a
nonzero common bilinear form, both impossible for natural PSL(4,q).
Together with Propositions A–C of phase7_psl4.md, this proves the theorem.

The root agent and the `rank_stability` agent independently reviewed all
three cases and reported no gap. In particular they checked the invariant
line sign argument, common-centralizer shapes, finite 2-group splitting,
and every middle-generator branch. These are internal reviews, not
external peer review or a claim of publication novelty.

[phase7_psl4_zero_product_check.py](phase7_psl4_zero_product_check.py)
verifies 54 exact algebra identities; its report is
[phase7_psl4_zero_product_check.json](phase7_psl4_zero_product_check.json).
It checks the canonical nilpotents and centralizer dimensions, the rank
(1,2) alternating form and monomial action, and both rank (2,2) middle
branches. The independent review also checks the integer Smith forms of
the centralizer systems: their nonzero factors are only 1 or 2, so their
ranks do not drop in any odd characteristic. Matrix identities supplement
rather than replace the written proof of normal-form coverage.

The existing rank bound is at most six, so the remaining maximum-rank
question is the exclusion or construction of rank six. That question is
not resolved by this note. No finite failed search is used as a universal
nonexistence argument.
