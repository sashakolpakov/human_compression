This is an author work queue, not an admission ledger. A bibliography entry, a recovered count, or an abstract theorem restated in a later source does not earn a new principal-source credit. The ordering favours a complete, accessible principal contract and small *additional* mathematical cost over a large nominal number of papers.

These twelve are candidates, not twelve new completed intakes. L059 and the neighbouring F010/L126 contracts are assigned separately to phase177 and are intentionally not duplicated here. L068 is the only new source on this screen for which this author has now read the complete primary body and fixed a complete principal construction contract. L092 has substantial prior component work and a genuine body, but its proposed *complete* contract needs a fresh unified audit.

Additional accessible but expensive candidates are L091 (the full Higman–Sims census **and** the two Hoffman–Singleton constructions), L071 (algorithms **and** large alternating/Co3 censuses), and L046 (all O'Nan map-classification clauses). They are not cheap algorithm-only admissions. L095 requires the exact effect of its L084 erratum before a final universal-polytope contract.

Several old files named after catalogue IDs contain another paper or HTML. In particular, the local `catalogue_L097.pdf` is the J2 paper, not the J3 paper; `catalogue_L123.pdf` contains the pregeometry paper, not the Sz(8) census; and several nominal L085/L116 PDFs are HTML. The current registry's negative access status is therefore substantive. File names alone are not evidence of full-text intake.

The intended sawtooth measurement is: retained U/C/D legs first, then the exact additional derivation and certificate needed for each newly admitted contract. The numerator must use the matched original result scopes. Neither the number of candidates nor the number of source pages establishes a compression ratio. This ordering implements the reusable-leg discipline of `~/gkm`; it does not import that project's optimization claims into mathematics.
