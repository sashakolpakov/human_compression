# Root admission of interval lifting for finite minimal covers

11 October 2026. **Accepted.** Root read both complete frozen author
manuscripts, the complete independent review, all three Lean declarations,
both finite model programs and the source/formal ledgers. Root also checked
the actual Monson–Schulte product construction in the bound primary text,

The key retained construction preserves the whole prescribed cover Q of
an interval: its original parabolic is the graph of Q→G_I. Projection to
the finite regular extension is injective there, so the quotient criterion
applies even when Q→G_I has a kernel. Repeated extension and reversal keep
that parabolic unchanged.

Choose a minimal C-group quotient M of the resulting finite cover over G.
Every further quotient over G would still be a quotient of the original
finite cover, so this is global minimality. Restriction gives Q→M_I→G_I.
The middle group is C; local minimality therefore forces Q→M_I to be an
isomorphism. Distinct prescribed local covers give distinct marked global
covers. The retained normal flag-kernel correspondence supplies the
unrooted regular-polytope statement as well.

The accepted rank-four theorem applies to any four consecutive monodromy
marks by extreme independence of the flag action. A non-C interval has
local minimal covers of distinct prime-power degrees. The interval theorem
preserves these local covers inside finite global minimal covers. No
global prime-power degree is inferred: the extension may introduce other
primes. This proves the stated all-rank conditional result, not the full
higher-rank finite-cover conjecture.

The broader least-rank reduction includes the necessary rank-three padding
argument. It never equates a monodromy parabolic acting on many sections
with the monodromy of one polytope. The separate divisibility theorem uses
commuting lifted factors with trivial intersection, constructs an explicit
surjection from their kernel to the base intersection, and applies
Lagrange. Its order-64 central-product example is expressly non-polytopal.
There is no uniqueness claim for its order-128 cover: adjacent commutation
in a base need not lift.
