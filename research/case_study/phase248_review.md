Leemans and Qu, *String C-groups of order 4p^m*, as recovered in the frozen
phase236 manuscript, with the explicit correction to the marking in the
bound primary edition. The accepted contract consists of Theorems 1.1–1.3
and the exact construction of Theorem 5.1. It is one principal-source
contract, subject to the project’s existing source registry; it is not an
additional contract for each theorem. The replacement proves no open

## 2. Structural and abelian arguments

The normal-Sylow proof covers the exceptional possible action when
$p=3$ and there are four Sylow subgroups. The action kernel lies in a
Sylow normalizer of order $3^m$. An image of order four would already make
that Sylow subgroup normal. The remaining image is the order-twelve
subgroup $A_4$ of $S_4$, which cannot be generated by involutions. Thus the
exception is impossible. The endpoint involutions give the actual
complement $C_2^2$, rather than an unspecified abstract complement.

The divisibility of both adjacent labels, and the impossibility that both
are odd, follow with the stated nondegeneracy hypothesis. The inverted-orbit
argument proves that two particular conjugates generate the odd kernel.
It only says that the chosen involution inverts those generators; it does
not use the false assertion that inversion is an automorphism of every
nonabelian group. A cyclic kernel would force the two adjacent rotation
subgroups to share its subgroup of order $p$, contradicting the intersection
property. This proves exactly the source’s two-generator conclusion.

For an abelian kernel, the four character projectors are legitimate since
multiplication by four is invertible on a finite group of odd order. The
inverting complement element eliminates two characters, and projection
of the generating orbit makes each remaining character component cyclic.
Both must occur, since the kernel is not cyclic. This is a sufficient
argument for the two-component conclusion; the number of generators alone
would not suffice.

The product bound $2k_1k_2\leq |G|$ excludes the complement element
$r_0r_2$ and then forces the exponent ordering $a\leq b$. For the resulting
models, the intersections with the odd kernel are respectively
$\langle y\rangle$ and $\langle xy\rangle$. Their intersection is trivial
precisely with the proved ordering. The common adjacent subgroup then
injects into $\langle r_2\rangle$ in the complement and contains $r_1$,
giving the required intersection of order two. The rank-three lemma
supplies all remaining subset intersections. Generation and marked
uniqueness are proved, not inferred merely from matching group orders.

For the tightness converse I checked the retained primary theorem directly:
Cunningham–Pellicer, *Classification of tight regular polyhedra*,
arXiv:1408.6703v2, Theorem 1.1 on physical page 2. Its odd/even cases require
the even parameter to divide twice the odd one and give uniqueness. Its
additional odd/even exceptions have even parameter four. Here that
parameter is $2p^a$ with $p$ odd and $a\geq1$, so the exceptions cannot
occur. Together with the retained correspondence between marked string
C-groups and regular polyhedra, this yields the exact converse used in
Theorem B. The retained theorem has not been rederived. Its primary text
was read remotely; there is no local PDF hash or whole-source cost claim.
[Primary theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.6703v2).

The source correction is necessary and correctly delimited. On primary
page 2, the displayed action fixes $x$ and inverts $y$ under $r_0$, but
sets $r_1=xy r_0$. Consequently $r_1^2=x^2\ne1$. The appropriate character
case on page 6 uses $xy r_2$ and requires $|y|\leq|x|$. The replacement
makes both repairs explicit: it uses $xy r_2$ and assigns the larger
exponent to $x$. It does not claim to prove the impossible literal formula,
or that the journal edition contains the same defect.

## 3. The all-parameter cyclotomic construction

The construction covers every odd prime $p$ and every $m\geq3$. Its main
point is not the examples but the exact lattice calculation in
$O=\mathbb Z[\zeta_p]$. With $\pi=\zeta_p-1$, the Eisenstein polynomial
gives an integral domain and the stated integral basis. Cancellation by
$\pi^j$ proves that successive powers of the ideal have quotients of
order $p$. The norm-one argument makes each cyclotomic ratio a unit,
justifying $pO=\pi^{p-1}O$ as equality of ideals.

For $1\leq r\leq p-1$, the displayed mixed-modulus lattice is included in
$\pi^rO$ and has its exact index $p^r$; equality follows. Multiplying by
$p^{e-1}$ proves the full coordinate statement for every
$n=(e-1)(p-1)+r$, including $e=1$ and components of modulus one. Therefore
the statement determines every source generator’s order, not only the
order of the entire group. The boundary $r=p-1$ and the smallest
$m=3$ are included.

The pair multiplication has the conjugation convention needed for
$[s_i,\beta]=s_{i+1}$. Multiplication by $\zeta_p$ is nontrivial when
$n=m-1\geq2$, so the kernel is nonabelian. The cyclotomic norm sum makes
every element outside the additive subgroup have order exactly $p$.
Complex conjugation and additive negation are well-defined independent
commuting involutory automorphisms. The displayed marking generates the
whole semidirect product and has exact adjacent orders $p$ and $2p$.

The final intersection argument genuinely uses primality: the common
involution has index $p$ in the first dihedral subgroup. If that subgroup
lay in the other, the marking would generate a group of order at most
$4p$, contrary to its proved order $4p^m$. The prime-index lemma therefore
applies. This is the source’s short intersection argument in a reusable
form, not a newly discovered old conclusion.

The presentation bridge proves more than the existence of a group with
the right numerical order. Mapping $s_i$ to $\pi^{i-1}$ respects every
literal source relator. In the abstract source presentation the commuting
subgroup on the $s_i$ has order at most $p^{m-1}$ and is normal: conjugation
by $\beta$ maps it into itself, and injectivity on a finite subgroup turns
inclusion into equality. The cyclic quotient has order at most $p$.
Thus the presentation has order at most $p^m$; its surjection onto the
constructed group of order $p^m$ is an isomorphism. This also fixes the
named generators and the two automorphisms. Formula (7.2) and the binomial
expansion supply the source’s auxiliary formulae, including the extra
commutators $s_p,s_{p+1}$.

The lower central series calculation gives class $m-1$ for all these
parameters. The explanation of the Hughes-subgroup boundary is correct:
for $m-1\leq p-1$ the additive group is elementary abelian, and the
whole constructed kernel has exponent $p$. Its Hughes subgroup is then
trivial. The source’s motivating conditional classification is not
silently imposed as a hypothesis that would exclude these parameters.
The presentation, rather than that broader classification, is the object
identified by the replacement.

## 4. Computational and formal evidence

The fresh frozen-author replay passes seven cyclotomic examples and four
abelian examples, with all sixty-four subset-intersection equations for
each marking. It rejects the corrupted cyclotomic constant coefficient,
the literal faulty source marking, and altered source-generator orders.
The driver checks the expected GAP failure text as well as absence of the
success marker; a GAP process returning zero after an error is not
mistaken for acceptance. Python optimization leaves validation enabled.

My independent presentation program uses
$(p,m)=(3,3),(3,6),(5,3),(5,5),(11,3)$. GAP computes the order of each
finitely presented group, then an isomorphic permutation group. The two
maps specified only on $(s_1,\beta)$ extend to bijective automorphisms;
the resulting semidirect product has the claimed marking, exact type and
all sixty-four intersections. This is an independently constructed finite
check, relying on GAP’s group algorithms, not a Lean certificate of the
presentation order. The $p=5,m=3$ and $p=11,m=3$ cases explicitly retain
the listed generators whose orders are one.

A separate false-weakening control uses $C_2^3$: with $C=1$ and two
coordinate planes $H,J$, one has $[H:C]=4$, $H\not\leq J$, but
$|H\cap J|=2$. It confirms why “prime” cannot be dropped from the
intersection criterion. It is not a counterexample to any source theorem.

All five Lean declarations were read and freshly compiled. They verify
the prime-index criterion and its generated-group form, two elementary
reflection identities, and the square of the defective marking. The
reported axioms are subsets of `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and
`Quot.sound`; no extra axioms, proof holes or native decision shortcut
occur. The dependency revisions are pinned. As with the author runner,
cached Mathlib objects are trusted; this is not a from-source rebuild of
all dependencies. Sylow normality, the character classification,
cyclotomic arithmetic, the all-parameter source bridge, and the retained
tight classification are not fully formalized.

## 5. Attribution, manuscript quality and admission

The source’s principal results remain attributed to Hou–Feng–Leemans–Qu.
The Sylow and inverted-orbit arguments are source arguments made explicit;
the character projectors are classical. The cyclotomic presentation and
automorphism proof is the replacement’s mathematical contribution to this
application. No priority claim for that organization is made. The retained
Cunningham–Pellicer theorem is charged as a retained input, not counted as
a recovered additional paper.

The application is a complete eight-page mathematical paper for the
specified principal contract. Its proofs do not depend on numerical
experiments for the unbounded parameters. The existing common lemmas and
cyclotomic layer could serve later applications, but one application does
not establish a multi-paper compression rate or a 20–25-page volume. The
author’s 41,386-byte required-proof-artifact boundary includes manuscript,
programs, Lean and dependency manifest. It is an accounting boundary,
not a matched measurement against the source or a charge for all retained
mathematics. All further reuse measurements must retain those costs and
identify the umbrella assigned to the topic.

All eight rendered pages are readable. One minor typesetting issue is
visible on the first page: the introductory source-title emphasis leaves
literal asterisks around the title. It does not affect the mathematics;
the frozen rendering is preserved. No other correction is required for
this admission.

**Verdict:** accept the complete corrected principal L014 contract, with
the stated retained inputs and formal limits. Accept all twenty-five

principal-source admission if L014 has not already been admitted.
Award no whole-paper coverage, no open-question result, no priority and
