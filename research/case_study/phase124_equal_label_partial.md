# Equal-label polytopes: two uniform subcases and an obstruction to deletion

10 October 2026. Frozen author note; root has read the uniform proofs, with separate hash-bound review and complete formalization pending. This is a partial attack on Li–Zhang, *Regular 3-polytopes of type {n,n}*, arXiv:2505.09505v1, Conjecture 3.7 (PDF p.11). The current arXiv record, checked today, still lists only the 14 May 2025 version. A bounded exact-title/conjecture search found no later resolution; that does not establish current openness or priority.

The conjecture asks for a regular d-polytope, for every d,n≥3, whose distinguished involutions satisfy

\[
r_i^2=1,\quad [r_i,r_j]=1\ (|i-j|>1),\quad (r_ir_{i+1})^n=1,
\]

and

\[
\big((r_{i+1}r_{i+2})^k(r_ir_{i+1})^k\big)^2=1
\quad (0\le i\le d-3,\ 1\le k<n),                 \tag{1}
\]

with exact Schläfli type {n,…,n}. The source literal wording is existence of a quotient with these relations. A stronger interpretation says the entire universally presented group has IP, or specifies its order. These are different assertions. Here we prove finite existence for n=3 and n=4 in every rank; we do not claim the conjecture for n≥5 or identify the universal n=4 presentation with the matrix group below. Classical permutation groups, elementary matrices and the standard string-C-group/polytope correspondence are retained background, not proposed new theories.

## 1. Uniform binary upper-unitriangular construction

**Theorem.** For every d≥3, the group

\[
G=\operatorname{UT}_{d+1}(\mathbb F_2)
\]

with markers r_i=I+E_(i,i+1), 0≤i<d, is a string C-group of exact type {4,…,4} satisfying every relation (1). It has order 2^{d(d+1)/2}. Consequently it supplies a finite regular d-polytope in the n=4 subcase of the source conjecture.

Here UT means the upper triangular matrices with all diagonal entries one. Products and group commutators use ordinary matrix multiplication; [x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy.

**Generation.** For a<b<c, elementary multiplication gives

\[
[I+E_{ab},I+E_{bc}]=I+E_{ac}.                    \tag{2}
\]

Inducting on c−a produces every I+E_(a,c) from the consecutive markers between a and c. These elementary matrices generate UT: clear the entries of an upper-unitriangular matrix successively by adding appropriate later rows to earlier rows, or induct on its size by first clearing the final column. Thus the markers generate all of G. There are d(d+1)/2 freely chosen entries above the diagonal, proving the order.

**Every parabolic and its intersections.** View the markers as the edges of the path on vertices {0,…,d}. For a subset J of markers, let its connected vertex components be the intervals obtained by retaining exactly those path edges. The subgroup G_J is exactly the product of the upper-unitriangular groups on those intervals, embedded as block-diagonal matrices.

One containment follows because each retained marker lies in its corresponding block. For the other, apply (2) inside each interval and use the generation argument there. Equivalently, an upper-unitriangular matrix g lies in G_J if and only if

\[
g_{ab}=0\quad\text{whenever some edge of }\{a,a+1,\ldots,b-1\}
\text{ is absent from }J.                       \tag{3}
\]

The coordinates permitted by both J and K are exactly those permitted by J∩K: an entire edge interval is contained in both sets precisely when it is contained in their intersection. Formula (3) therefore proves

\[
G_J\cap G_K=G_{J\cap K}
\]

for all subsets, including disconnected sets and the empty set. In particular this is the full intersection property, not just a dimension comparison or a test of the two endpoint parabolics.

**Orders and additional relators.** Each marker is a nonidentity involution. Distant markers commute. For adjacent markers,

\[
(r_i r_{i+1})^2=I+E_{i,i+2}\ne I,
\]

so their product has exact order four. Fix a consecutive triple and put A=r_i r_(i+1), B=r_(i+1)r_(i+2). For k=1, BA is the conjugate by r_(i+1) of the product of the commuting outer markers, so (BA)^2=1. For k=3, B^3 A^3=B^{-1}A^{-1}=(AB)^{-1}, and AB=r_i r_(i+2) is an involution. For k=2,

\[
A^2=I+E_{i,i+2},\qquad B^2=I+E_{i+1,i+3}.
\]

These are commuting involutions: both cross-products of the two matrix units are zero. Hence (B^2 A^2)^2=1. These are all k with 1≤k<4. The exact string C-group correspondence now yields the asserted finite regular polytope. ∎

This model happens to give the source rank-three order 2^4·4=64 at d=3. The proof above does not need the source rank-three theorem or a computational identification of its presentation.

## 2. The ternary case

**Proposition.** For every d≥3, adjacent transpositions r_i=(i,i+1) in S_(d+1) provide a finite regular d-polytope satisfying the source relations for n=3, of order (d+1)!.

**Proof.** Adjacent products have order three, and distant markers commute. The subgroup generated by a subset J is the direct product of symmetric groups on the intervals of the path cut out by J. A permutation belongs to both such subgroups if and only if it preserves the intersections of the two interval partitions. These intersections are precisely the components cut out by J∩K; within each intersection either subgroup permits arbitrary permutations. Thus G_J∩G_K=G_(J∩K).

For a consecutive triple, k=1 is again (BA)^2=1 by outer commutation. For k=2, the order-three relations give B^2 A^2=(AB)^{-1}, also an involution. This proves every required relation, full IP and exact type. ∎

Here the universal Coxeter group [3,…,3] is already S_(d+1), by the classical type-A Coxeter presentation, and the added relators are redundant. Thus, if that classical presentation theorem is retained, the stronger universal-presentation interpretation also holds for n=3. No analogous universal identification is inferred for n=4.

## 3. Why Boolean deletion does not attack the remaining parameters

**Proposition.** Let a,b,c be involutions with |bc|=n≥3 and suppose

\[
\big((bc)^2(ab)^2\big)^2=1.
\]

If a homomorphism fixes b,c and sends a to1, then n divides4. Consequently an exact-type realization of Conjecture3.7 can have all Boolean deletions only when n=4.

**Proof.** Apply the homomorphism to the displayed relation. Since b²=1, its image is ((bc)^2)^2=(bc)^4=1. The order of bc divides every exponent annihilating bc, so n|4. The only divisor of4 at least3 is4. ∎

This is stronger than the familiar odd-bond obstruction: it excludes deletion-based SK applications for every n≥5, including even n. It does not disprove the conjecture; S_(d+1) at n=3 already illustrates existence without these deletions. A method for the remaining n must replace the deletion hypothesis, not conceal it in a local seed.
