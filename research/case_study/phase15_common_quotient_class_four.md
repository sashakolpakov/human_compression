# The canonical common quotient through nilpotency class four

10 October 2026. Private proposed proof, internally reviewed. Earlier
frozen artifacts are unchanged. This extends the all-rank class-three
support theorem to class at most four. It does not prove the unrestricted
common-quotient claim or repair the source's specified Frattini-intersection
kernel. Publication priority is not assessed here.

**Theorem.** Let G be a finite two-group with a nondegenerate string
C-group marking r₁,…,r_d, d≥2. If G has nilpotency class at most four,
then its canonical quotient G/γ₃(G), with the induced marking, is
isomorphic to the path-cocycle group G(C_d). In particular it has order
2^(2d−1), type {4,…,4}, and the subgroup intersection property.

The source target is Hou–Schulte, *String C-groups of 2-power order
project onto a common string C-group*, arXiv:2605.20691v1, Theorems 1.1–1.2.
The distinction between the main existence claim and the erroneous
specified-kernel conclusions is recorded in
[phase12_path_cocycle.md](phase12_path_cocycle.md). The present theorem
extends [the class-three repair](phase13_common_quotient_class_three.md).

## 1. Actual supports and notation

For I⊆{1,…,d}, put G_I=<r_i:i∈I>. The given intersection property is

    G_I∩G_J=G_(I∩J).

Irredundancy and Burnside's basis theorem give |G:G′|=2^d: the marked
involutions are a basis of the elementary abelian quotient G/Φ(G), and
Φ(G)=G′ because the abelianization is generated by involutions. In
particular

    G′∩<r_i>=1.                                      (1)

Thus an element of G′ supported on two marked vertex sets whose
intersection has at most one vertex is trivial. Every use of this test
below occurs in G itself, never in a lower-central quotient.

Use [x,y]=x⁻¹y⁻¹xy and right conjugation. Define

    c_i=[r_i,r_(i+1)],       z_i=c_i²       (1≤i≤d−1),
    t_i=[c_i,r_(i+2)]                      (1≤i≤d−2),
    p_i=c_i⁴                              (1≤i≤d−1),
    s_i=t_i²,               q_i=[c_i,c_(i+1)] (1≤i≤d−2),
    h_i=[t_i,r_(i+3)]                      (1≤i≤d−3).

Undefined symbols in boundary formulas mean the identity. Their supports
are respectively the edge {i,i+1} for c_i,z_i,p_i, the triple
{i,i+1,i+2} for t_i,s_i,q_i, and the four consecutive vertices
{i,i+1,i+2,i+3} for h_i.

## 2. Exact collection in class four

The subgroup γ₄ is central elementary abelian. Indeed it is generated
by weight-four commutators ending in a marked involution. If u∈γ₃,
then [u,r_j] is central, and r_j²=1 forces [u,r_j]²=1. Also γ₃ is
abelian because [γ₃,γ₃]≤γ₆=1. The elementary class-three collection
modulo γ₄ gives

    γ₃=<z_i,t_i,γ₄>,     γ₃²≤γ₄,     exp(γ₃)≤4.       (2)

For clarity, the ingredients of this collection do not require quotient
IP. The c_i normally generate G′; an endpoint inverts c_i, giving
[c_i,r_i]=[c_i,r_(i+1)]=c_i⁻². Distant generators commute with c_i.
The weight-three Jacobi identity at three consecutive vertices gives
[c_i,r_(i−1)]≡t_(i−1) modulo γ₄. The only other possible commutator
is [c_i,r_(i+2)]=t_i. These prove the generation statement in (2).
Each z_i²=c_i⁴ lies in γ₄, by commuting z_i with an endpoint;
each t_i² lies in γ₄, by commuting t_i with its final involution.
This proves the remaining assertions of (2).

All p_i,s_i,q_i,h_i belong to γ₄ and have order at most two. The
following table is exact in G:

| Element | Position of r_j | Commutator with r_j |
|---|---|---|
| z_i | j=i−1 | s_(i−1) |
| z_i | j=i or i+1 | p_i |
| z_i | j=i+2 | s_i |
| z_i | other j | 1 |
| t_i | j=i−1 | h_(i−1) |
| t_i | j=i or i+2 | s_i |
| t_i | j=i+1 | q_i s_i |
| t_i | j=i+3 | h_i |
| t_i | other j | 1 |

Here is a direct derivation of its less immediate entries. Since
[G′,γ₃]≤γ₅=1, squaring c_i before taking a commutator squares the
corresponding weight-three commutator. This proves the z_i row from
the preceding class-three calculation; any γ₄ correction disappears
on squaring. For a local triple a,b,c with [a,c]=1, put x=[a,b],
y=[b,c], and t=[x,c]. Conjugation by c sends t to t⁻¹. Conjugation
by a does the same, since x^a=x⁻¹ and a commutes with c, while
[x,t]=1 in class four. Conjugation by b gives

    t^b=[x⁻¹,c y⁻¹]=[x,y] t⁻¹,

because [x,y] is central and conjugating t by y has weight at least
five. Thus [t,a]=[t,c]=t² and [t,b]=[x,y]t², proving the three
interior entries. At the extra vertex on the left, put X=c_i,
a=r_(i−1), and d=r_(i+2). Since [a,d]=1, the commutator interchange
identity gives [[X,d],a]=[[X,a],d] modulo γ₅. The class-three Jacobi
identity gives [X,a]≡t_(i−1) modulo γ₄; its γ₄ correction is central
and drops out. Thus the leftmost entry is h_(i−1), with any sign
irrelevant in the elementary abelian γ₄. The rightmost
entry is the definition of h_i. All farther entries follow from
literal distant commutation of the marked generators.

Since γ₄=[γ₃,G] is central, (2) and this table give

    γ₄=<p_i,s_i,q_i,h_i>.                            (3)

No independence of these generators is asserted or needed. Their exact
supports, rather than a universal dimension count, are what matters.

## 3. Consequences of a hypothetical edge relation

Suppose ε_i∈{0,1}, not all zero, satisfy

    g=c₁^ε₁⋯c_(d−1)^ε_(d−1) ∈ γ₃.                  (4)

Call i selected when ε_i=1. The subgroup G′ has class at most two,
and its commutator subgroup lies in the elementary abelian γ₄. Therefore
g⁴=∏p_i^ε_i. By (2), g⁴=1. Peeling the first selected edge against
the suffix of later edges, using (1) and actual IP, forces its p_i to
be trivial. Repeat to obtain

    i selected ⇒ p_i=1.                             (5)

For every pair of valid vertex indices j,k,

    1=[g,r_j,r_k]=∏[c_i,r_j,r_k]^ε_i.               (6)

The first equality uses g∈γ₃ and class at most four. The product formula
is exact: [G′,γ₃]=1 makes the first commutator distribute over the
ordered product in (4), and γ₃ is abelian for the second step. Its
individual factors are given by the table in section 2 and the
class-three expression for [c_i,r_j] modulo γ₄. Signs disappear in
the elementary abelian target γ₄.

Taking k=j and using (5), equation (6) reduces to

    s_(j−2)^ε_(j−2) s_j^ε_(j+1)=1.                  (7)

The two factors are supported on {j−2,j−1,j} and {j,j+1,j+2}.
Their common support is the single vertex j. Equation (1) and actual
IP force both factors to be trivial, including at the boundaries.
Consequently

    i selected ⇒ s_(i−1)=s_i=1.                     (8)

For a selected i, five more instances of (6), after (5)–(8), give

    h_(i−1)=1,                                     (9)
    q_(i−1)=h_(i−3)^ε_(i−3),
    h_(i−2)=q_(i−3)^ε_(i−3),                        (10)
    q_i=h_(i+1)^ε_(i+3),
    h_i=q_(i+2)^ε_(i+3).                            (11)

The pairs (j,k) used, respectively, are

    (i−1,i+2), (i−1,i), (i−1,i−2),
    (i+2,i+1), (i+2,i+3).

A displayed equation is needed only when its left-hand symbol is defined;
then its vertex pair is valid. Undefined terms on the right are identity,
with ε outside {1,…,d−1} taken as zero. These formulas retain the mixed
weight-four corrections. In particular, (10)–(11) do not generally say
that the displayed q_i or h_i is trivial.

## 4. Split γ₄ across every selected edge

Fix a selected i, and cut the vertices into

    L={1,…,i},     R={i+1,…,d}.

The generators in (3) whose supports cross this cut are exactly

    p_i; s_(i−1),s_i; q_(i−1),q_i;
    h_(i−2),h_(i−1),h_i,

when defined. The p and s terms and the middle h vanish by (5), (8),
(9). Equations (10) put the remaining left-hand q and h inside G_L:
their replacements end at vertex i or earlier. Equations (11) put the
two remaining right-hand q and h inside G_R: their replacements start
at vertex i+1 or later. Every other generator of γ₄ was already entirely
on one side. Centrality therefore gives

    γ₄=Z_L Z_R,   Z_L≤G_L∩G′,   Z_R≤G_R∩G′.        (12)

This splitting is an equality of actual subgroups of G. It holds at
every selected cut, and does not assume anything about IP in G/γ₄.

## 5. Kill the selected edge squares

By (2), g²∈γ₄. The class-two product formula in G′, whose commutators
belong to γ₄, consequently gives

    ∏z_i^ε_i ∈ γ₄.                                 (13)

Let i be the first selected edge still present in this product. Split
γ₄ by (12) at that edge. All later z terms lie in G_R, so (13) writes
z_i=u v with u∈Z_L and v∈G_R. Then

    u=z_i v⁻¹ ∈ G_L∩G_({i}∪R)=<r_i>.

As u∈G′, equation (1) gives u=1. It follows that
z_i∈G_R∩G_{i,i+1}=<r_(i+1)>, and (1) gives z_i=1. Repeat, using
the already established splitting at each selected cut, to conclude

    i selected ⇒ z_i=1.                             (14)

This is a statement inside G. Squaring (4) merely modulo γ₄ without
the splitting argument would not justify it.

## 6. Push the two crossing weight-three terms to the sides

Let k be the first selected edge in the original relation (4), and
use the cut L={1,…,k}, R={k+1,…,d}. Only z_k,t_(k−1),t_k among the
generators z_i,t_i of γ₃ cross this cut. The first is trivial by (14).

If t_(k−1) is defined, commute (4) with r_(k−1). There are no earlier
selected edges, so the class-three commutator formula modulo γ₄ gives
t_(k−1)∈γ₄. Write it as u v using (12). Its support is
{k−1,k,k+1}, and hence

    v=t_(k−1)u⁻¹ ∈ G_R∩G_(L∪{k+1})=<r_(k+1)>.

Both factors in (12) lie in G′, so v=1 by (1). Thus t_(k−1) belongs
to G_L. It need not be declared trivial.

If t_k is defined, commute (4) with r_(k+2). The endpoint terms of
selected edges are trivial by (14), giving

    t_k t_(k+2)^ε_(k+3) ∈ γ₄.                       (15)

The second factor is entirely in G_R. Equation (12) therefore writes
t_k=u v with u∈Z_L and v∈G_R. Since t_k is supported on
{k,k+1,k+2},

    u=t_k v⁻¹ ∈ G_L∩G_({k}∪R)=<r_k>.

Equation (1) forces u=1, so t_k belongs to G_R. Again no unnecessary
vanishing assertion is made.

All the generators in (2), including the split γ₄, have now been assigned
to one side of the cut. The group γ₃ is abelian, so

    γ₃=H_L H_R,   H_L≤G_L∩G′,   H_R≤G_R∩G′.        (16)

## 7. The edge relation is impossible

Every selected edge after k is wholly in R. Relation (4) and (16)
therefore give c_k=u v with u∈H_L and v∈G_R. Just as before,
u∈G_L∩G_({k}∪R)=<r_k>, so u=1. Then
c_k∈G_R∩G_{k,k+1}=<r_(k+1)>, forcing c_k=1.
This contradicts nondegeneracy: c_k=(r_k r_(k+1))² and the adjacent
product has two-power order at least four.

Thus the images of all c_i are independent in G′/γ₃. They generate
that quotient and are involutions there, so |G′:γ₃|=2^(d−1).
Together with |G:G′|=2^d, this gives |G:γ₃|=2^(2d−1).
The class-two path presentation maps onto G/γ₃, and its independent
path-cocycle model has exactly this order. Hence the map is a marked
isomorphism. The target's proved IP supplies quotient IP only at this
final identification, avoiding any circular quotient assumption.

## 8. Scope and verification boundary

The argument uses standard elementary commutator identities, Burnside's
basis theorem, and the actual marked IP. It uses no classification of
finite groups, subgroup catalogue, or assumption about lower-central
quotients preserving IP. The weight-four table is proved locally in
section 2; finite checks of universal nilpotent quotients are separate
diagnostics, not a finite-to-all-ranks extrapolation.

Classes greater than four are not settled: γ₄ ceases to be central, and
the clean support transfer in (10)–(12) gains higher commutator corrections.
The source's unrestricted common-quotient existence claim remains outside
the present theorem's scope. This is also independent of the earlier
counterexamples to its specified Frattini-intersection kernel.

## 9. Exact diagnostics and review

The standalone [GAP/NQ checker](phase15_common_quotient_check.g) constructs
the universal class-four nilpotent quotients of the path involution
presentation in ranks 2 through 6. It checks 120 entries of the z/t
table, 350 double-commutator entries, 20 edge-commutator entries, 62
fourth-power identities, 1,724 distributed double commutators, and 400
instances of (9)–(11) modulo precisely the selected p/s terms. These
are exact finite diagnostics of the uniform collection proof, not
substitutes for its argument in arbitrary rank.

In rank three the universal class-four group has order 4,096. The
checker enumerates all 207 normal subgroups, finds 174 kernels inside
the derived subgroup, and tests all 64 marked subgroup-intersection
pairs for each of the 167 resulting nondegenerate markings: 10,688
comparisons. Exactly 114 markings satisfy IP; none loses an edge in
the class-two quotient. Nine of the 167 markings do lose an edge,
and all nine fail IP. The finite census depends on GAP 4.15.1 and NQ
2.5.11; the written theorem does not depend on their implementation
or on this census. Exact output and input hashes are recorded in the
[check report](phase15_common_quotient_check.json).

The complete written proof received two independent internal reviews.
The separate [independent review](phase15_common_quotient_independent_audit.md)
also derives the local table and checks universal quotients independently.
Neither these reviews nor the diagnostics establish publication priority,
and no Lean formalization of this theorem is claimed.
