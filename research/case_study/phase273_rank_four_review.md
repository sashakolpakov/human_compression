Let G be a finite group generated by four distinct nonidentity involutions
s_0,s_1,s_2,s_3, with commuting nonadjacent marks. Assume
s_0∉G123 and s_3∉G012. For **every prime p>4|G|**, the proof constructs a
finite marked string C-group T mapping onto G with p-group kernel. It
follows that every finite rank-four polytope with non-C monodromy has
infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic **finite minimal regular polytopal
covers**. For each such prime, one can choose a minimal cover whose group
order divided by |Mon(P)| is a nontrivial power of p.

The last ratio is the degree over the smallest reflexible cover, or the
marked monodromy group. It need not equal the number of flags of the regular
cover divided by the number of flags of P. The latter includes the size of
a flag stabilizer in Mon(P).

The theorem is restricted to rank four. It neither establishes finite
covers with this kernel property in every rank nor duplicates the separate
phase216 theorem allowing infinite covers. It gives no whole-manuscript
replacement credit and no compression ratio. A literature search cannot
establish priority by absence; this review makes no priority claim.

## 1. Source question and prior results

I checked the complete relevant question paragraphs in the bound primary
texts. Cunningham–Pellicer, *Open problems on k-orbit polytopes*, §3.1,
PDF pages 7–8, defines coverings through flag adjacency and stabilizer
containment. Problem 5 on page 8 asks for a polytope with non-C monodromy
and a unique minimal regular polytopal cover, or a proof of nonexistence.
There is no finiteness restriction in that question. The consulted title
page is dated 1 July 2018; the local historical filename does not establish
an arXiv edition.

Cunningham, *Chiral polytopes whose smallest regular cover is a polytope*,
§4, PDF pages 6–7, states the multiplicity expectation and discusses a
particular chiral family. Its definition of minimality agrees with the
one used here. The new argument settles the finite rank-four strengthening
for every applicable polytope, including these finite examples; this is
not a license to label the unrestricted finite higher-rank question solved.

Finite regular-cover existence is an older result of Monson–Schulte;
infinitely many finite minimal covers of the Tomotope are also older work,
due to Monson–Pellicer–Williams. The author source ledger distinguishes
both from the new uniform p-kernel theorem. The 2026 premaniplex paper is
listed as literature screening only and supplies no step in this proof.
Exact primary hashes and edition qualifications are retained in
`phase256_sources.json`. The fresh replay verified all five primary bytes.

## 2. The raw surface and normal group cover

Every pair of distinct commuting involutions generates a four-element
Klein group. Thus the three types of raw squares have embedded boundaries,
one for each left coset of G02, G03 or G13. The vertex link is the path
2–0–3–1. In particular the complex is a genuine compact surface with
boundary even when the adjacent middle label is two. Its boundary circles
are exactly the cosets of G12. The counts g vertices, 2g edges and 3g/4
squares give χ=−g/4 and dim H1(X;Fp)=1+g/4.

The path presentation really identifies π1(X) with the kernel K of the
right-angled string group W→G. Attaching only one square per coset does
not discard a relator: its rotations and reverse orientation give all
other base points of that same commuting relation. The mod-p commutator
subgroup [K,K]K^p is characteristic in K, hence normal in W. Consequently
the first connected homology cover is a Cayley complex of the finite
marked group H, rather than merely an unmarked covering graph. Its degree
and order are respectively p^(1+g/4) and g p^(1+g/4). Surjection to G
preserves distinctness, nonidentity and extreme independence.

## 3. The first cover repairs both ends

For S=X01∪X12, the selected relative collapses are valid in the stated
order. Every 02 square has an unselected color-2 edge; two selected such
edges would put s_0 in G12. A color-2 edge belongs to only one raw face.
Every 13 square has an unselected color-1 edge, since two selected edges
would put s_3 in G012; these edges also have only one incident raw face.
Finally each 03 square has an unselected color-0 edge. The 02 face formerly
incident with that edge has already gone. Distinct 03 squares do not
compete for it. These are elementary collapses fixing S, and the resulting
one-dimensional extension cannot kill a class in H1(S). The dual argument
is equally valid.

The lifted intersection argument uses both portions of Mayer–Vietoris.
Equality of lifted endpoints makes the difference of two paths zero in
H1(X), hence in H1(E∪F). Its image in reduced H0(E∩F) identifies the common
projected endpoint's component. Subtracting a path inside that component
then gives two closed classes in E and F. Because H1(E∩F)=0, the map
H1(E)⊕H1(F)→H1(E∪F) is injective; both classes vanish separately. This
removes a possible common deck kernel, not only an erroneous projected
intersection. The intersections here are unions of single color-1 or
color-2 edges. Thus both rank-three ends of H satisfy the intersection
property by the standard recursive string criterion.

## 4. Exterior graphs and actual surface complements

The finite graph obtained by contracting all left A-cosets is connected
and vertex-transitive. It has at least two vertices. A spanning-tree leaf
is a noncut vertex of any finite connected graph, so transitivity implies
there are no cut vertices. Paths after deleting A lift through connected
remaining A-cosets. This proves the complement of a single end is connected
and is valid in every finite intermediate group used later.

I checked every face and edge case in Lemmas 5 and 9. The comparison with
topological complements is not a general assertion about arbitrary
subcomplexes. Here it follows from the explicit conditions: every outside
face has nonempty connected outside vertices along its boundary; every
outside edge has an outside endpoint; and at an outside vertex every
incident face and edge is outside, with connected link. These conditions
identify the outside face-adjacency components, the topological complement
and the induced outside-vertex graph.

For the top pair, a 02 square outside its own end meets the other end in
at most one 2-edge; 13 is dual. In a 03 square, the selected vertices occupy
at most one row and one column. Each 12 disk is selected wholly or has no
selected vertex. Outside edges of colors 0 and 3 cannot join two selected
vertices in the opposite end, by extreme independence. Colors 1 and 2
are selected whenever an endpoint is selected. Thus no isolated open edge
or face component is omitted by the graph argument.

## 5. Boundary classes are transverse to both end fibers

For x∉A, there are at least two middle boundary circles outside A:
([G:A]−1)[A:C]≥2. Both factors matter. A is proper, and s_0∉C forces
[A:C]≥2. If a boundary class u_x were the image of an A-cycle, an equality
of cellular chains would force coefficients on adjacent outside faces to
agree up to sign. An edge on a different exterior boundary forces zero
on its incident face. Connected outside-face adjacency propagates zero
to every outside face, contradicting the nonzero coefficient on xC.
This proves the asserted equivalence over every field, including
characteristic two, without an orientability assumption.

The same argument applies to B. The chosen lift of an end meets a fiber
either in no points or in an affine coset of the image of its first
homology. This is exactly the endpoint description of an abelian homology
cover. Deck translations use one global vector space V; arbitrary local
choices of origins merely alter affine constants. There is no hidden
conjugation of the transverse direction between different fibers.

## 6. The uniform large-prime argument

A group cannot be the union of two proper subgroups, so an exterior base
vertex h exists. A complete middle circle based at any exterior vertex
avoids both ends. Its class u lies outside both end homology images, and
walking around it realizes all translations k u for k∈Fp.

For a prescribed path of length L, translating the entire lift by k u
violates each possible end condition at each visited vertex for at most
one scalar. There are at most 2(L+1) forbidden scalars. This remains true
when a base vertex belongs to both ends, when the path repeats vertices,
and when an endpoint is visited several times. The strict inequality is
p>2(L+1), not a non-strict bound.

A simple path has length at most g−1. Fundamental loops for a spanning
tree have length at most 2g−1, so p>4g handles all of them. For a loop of
class v based at h, first translating by k u_h, safely lifting the loop,
and translating back by −k u_h joins z to z+v in the complement. The
fundamental loop classes generate V, including after the square relations
are imposed. Hence the entire fiber over h is connected there. Every
other exterior fiber reaches it.

The remaining vertices are not silently discarded. Over A\B, color 3
escapes both ends; over B\A, color 0 does so. Over A∩B, a color-0 step
stays in the current unchosen A-component and moves the base vertex to
A\B, after which color 3 escapes. Thus every vertex outside the two chosen
end components joins the exterior region. This proves the quantified
connectivity statement without enumerating the first cover.

## 7. Nonorientability and the local complements

For these colored surfaces, neighboring vertex orientation conventions
are reversed along every edge. Thus the orientation character is walk
length modulo two. If the base graph is nonbipartite, one spanning-tree
fundamental loop is odd and has the same length bound as above. Safe
middle-circle translations add even lengths. Repeating the resulting
odd path p times, with a freshly permitted translate each time, closes
it because p v=0. Its length remains odd because p is odd. This explicitly
puts an orientation-reversing loop in the exterior complement. A zero
class v causes no exception. Capping the even boundary circles does not
change orientability.

For each local pair, the complement outside the entire end is connected;
any additional allowed vertex inside that end exits by the extreme mark.
The author then checks the necessary cell comparison, instead of assuming
that graph connectivity suffices. The first cover's end intersection
property is essential here: a nonselected 12 disk meets G01 in at most
one color-1 edge. Removing that edge's two adjacent vertices from a
polygon of length at least four leaves a connected path. The square and
outside-edge cases are valid as written, including middle label two.
The connected local complements contain the orientation-reversing loop
from the top complement whenever the ambient surface is nonorientable.

## 8. The second cover and minimality

The retained surface criterion has the correct coefficient restriction.
For a connected closed orientable surface with connected complement,
H2(Y)→H2(Y,S) supplies the single coefficient and hence H1(S)→H1(Y) is
injective. In odd characteristic, a nonorientable complementary component
forces the relative coefficient to equal its negative, so it vanishes.
The proof has established this nonorientability for all three complements;
connectivity alone would not have sufficed.

In the top intersection every component is a filled middle disk. The local
intersections are single edges. Their H1 is zero. The lifted intersection
argument therefore proves all three required equalities in the second
cover. It proves local end intersections as well as the top intersection;
then the recursive criterion gives the complete string intersection
property. The second characteristic homology subgroup is normal in
W(q_H), so it again defines a finite marked group cover. The two kernels
are elementary abelian p-groups separately; their composite is a p-group,
with no assertion that the composite is elementary abelian.

For a polytope, the flag action satisfies the extreme independence
hypotheses. If its flags are W/J, a regular marked cover with normal kernel
N covers it exactly when N⊆J, equivalently N⊆core_W(J). Normality also
removes any choice of base flag. Every regular quotient under the finite
constructed T which still covers P therefore maps onto Mon(P). Choosing
a minimal such quotient is legitimate in a finite subgroup lattice and
gives global minimality: any further regular cover below it is still a
quotient of T. An infinite regular polytope cannot lie below a finite one
under a surjective flag covering.

The quotient's kernel over Mon(P) is a p-group and is nontrivial if
Mon(P) is non-C. Orders belonging to two different primes cannot agree.
Consequently the covers are pairwise nonisomorphic even as unrooted
regular polytopes. The optional kernel-product assertion follows because
the common quotient's kernel has order dividing two coprime prime powers;
it is not needed for this conclusion.
