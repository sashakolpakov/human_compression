# Independent full-proof audit of the Hermitian-unital conjectural statement

**Decision: accept the complete written proof of the published Conjecture1.** For every prime power q>2, the literal Van Nypelseer construction Γ(U_H(q)) is a firm, residually connected, flag-transitive rank-three geometry. The proof also establishes the q=2 case and the stronger assertion that PGU(3,q) acts regularly on chambers. No mathematical correction to the retained argument is needed.

This upgrades the independent-review status of the existing conjecture record. It does not create an additional conjecture answer, establish historical priority, or show that the problem remained open immediately before this project's proof. Full formalization is still incomplete. The new all-field norm-shell transport certificate below verifies an actual missing general part of the proof; it must not be mistaken for a Lean proof of the entire incidence-geometric theorem.

## 1. The exact source and reviewed proof

The primary is D. Leemans, *A Generalization of a Construction due to Van Nypelseer*, Beiträge zur Algebra und Geometrie46(2005),561–574, `sources/older_L092_gwdg.pdf`, SHA256 `f76a72aeec658ace5d821519a686c3041e455d3a1c5c4276988bd711ba830403`. I read Construction3 on printed p565 and §4.6 on p569, including direct visual inspection of both PDF pages. Conjecture1 states:

> Γ(U_H(q)) is a firm, residually connected and flag-transitive geometry for all q>2.

The bound proof is `hermitian_unital_proof.md`, SHA256 `173c11ff6cb45bcea3dd9351b887cf92610244aed1b9f7285a8897ce59313528`. I read every proof step, including its norm-shell identification, special-unitary calculation, original incidence conditions, chamber stabilizer and q=2 claim. Earlier finite tests and the phase199 review of Figure2 were useful supporting material, but neither was treated as a prior full-proof review of this conjecture.

The source uses the classical unital as a 2-(q³+1,q+1,1) design. Its blocks are projective secants, not tangent lines and not selected group-orbit fragments. Construction3 has points Q, blocks L and original flags (p,M), with Q∉L, Q∈M\{p}, and M∩L={p} as the three incidence conditions. In this design the last condition is exactly p∈L≠M. Thus the chambers used in the proof match the source literally.

The conjecture does not prescribe PSU as the acting group. This matters when 3 divides q+1: the written proof uses PGU, whose chamber orbit is larger. The discrepancies already established in the printed Figure2 do not change the conjecture's definition or require the proof to reproduce the inconsistent entries. The assertion (IP)₂ in the preceding experimental paragraph is not part of the displayed conjecture and is not needed for this review's acceptance.

## 2. Geometry, firmness and the easy connected residues

Every incident pair extends to a chamber. A point–block pair Q∉L has one completion for each p∈L, with M=Qp, giving q+1. A point–flag pair has q²−1 possible other blocks through p; a block–flag pair has q possible points of M\{p}. The Steiner property makes the missing third incidence automatic in both latter cases. All three numbers are at least two even at q=2. Every singleton has incident objects of the other types, so this checks the entire geometry and firmness assertions.

The original-flag residue is exactly K_(q,q²−1). In the original-point residue at Q, each flag is determined by its other point p, and a block avoids Q. If two such points lie on different Q-lines, their joining block avoids Q. If they lie on the same Q-line, choosing an auxiliary point outside that line produces a two-block path avoiding Q. Such an auxiliary point exists since q³+1>q+1. Every block vertex is attached. This proves that residue's connectivity without relying on an automorphism-group classification.

For whole-geometry connectivity, choose p outside the joining block QR. At most two of the q² blocks through p contain Q or R. Since q²>2, a remaining block joins Q to R by the anti-incidence relation. All block and flag objects have point neighbors. This checks the empty residue as well as the three object-residue types required in rank three.

## 3. The difficult block residue and SU2

A secant two-space W is nondegenerate. A degenerate Hermitian two-space in a nondegenerate three-space has a one-dimensional radical and an anisotropic one-dimensional quotient, so it has only the radical as an isotropic projective point; it cannot contain the q+1 points of a secant. Hence V=W⊥Kz, and norm surjectivity lets us choose h(z,z)=1. Every outside projective isotropic point has exactly one representative v+z, where v∈W and h(v,v)=−1.

Two distinct such representatives determine a projective secant meeting W in the one-space generated by their difference. A common flag in the block residue exists exactly when this difference is isotropic. This proves both directions of the incidence-to-shell bridge. Every flag vertex has q outside neighbors, so connectivity of the shell's isotropic-difference graph implies connectivity of the entire bipartite residue.

In a hyperbolic basis the Hermitian Gram matrix is J=(0 1;1 0). The determinant-one unitary equation gives diagonal entries fixed by conjugation and off-diagonal entries anti-fixed. Choosing a nonzero trace-zero δ identifies anti-fixed elements with δF_q; δ² is fixed and nonzero. For D=diag(δ,1), D⁻¹AD has all entries in F_q and determinant one. Conversely every SL(2,q) matrix conjugates back to a unitary matrix of this form. This verifies the claimed SU(2,q) identification in odd and even characteristic.

The original freeness argument is valid: fixing a vector of nonzero norm preserves its orthogonal decomposition, acts trivially on its span and by a scalar on the other line; determinant one forces that scalar to be one. The norm-fiber count gives

\[
2(q+1)+(q-2)(q+1)^2=q(q^2-1).
\]

This is also the order of SL2: there are q²−1 choices of its first column and q second columns of determinant one. Thus the free action is transitive. These ingredients are finite-field trace/norm facts and elementary Hermitian linear algebra, not a retained classification of unitary groups.

Upper and lower elementary matrices generate SL2 by Gaussian elimination, including diagonal factors. Their corresponding unitary roots move a shell vector by an isotropic coordinate-axis vector. The difference is nonzero for a nonidentity root because a vector of norm−1 cannot have either coordinate zero. Consequently every generating move stays within a graph component, and transitivity joins every pair of shell vertices. This proves the remaining residue connected and completes the residual-connectivity argument. The appended transport supplement spells out the elementary factorization and gives a stronger, count-free proof of the regular shell action.

## 4. The whole chamber action and the q=2 case

Any two distinct isotropic one-spaces have nonzero Hermitian pairing. Otherwise their two-space would be totally isotropic and contained in its orthogonal complement of dimension one, which is impossible. Representatives may therefore be rescaled to a hyperbolic pair; the orthogonal complement is a nondegenerate line and can be normalized to norm one. Mapping one such basis to another proves transitivity on ordered pairs of distinct unital points.

For a fixed pair p=⟨e1⟩, Q=⟨e3⟩, the projective lines through p other than M=pQ are exactly L_t=⟨e1,e2+t e3⟩. The case t=0 is tangent. For t≠0 the displayed trace equation has q solutions: multiplication by t^q is an invertible F_q-linear map, and the trace has fibers of size q. Thus the alternative secants are indexed by K*. The diagonal unitary matrices D_a=diag(a,1,a^(−q)) act on this parameter by t↦ta^(−q), regularly on K*.

A unitary element preserving p and Q also preserves their orthogonal complement, hence is diagonal in this basis. Its middle entry has norm one. Dividing by that central scalar yields exactly D_a. The projective ordered-pair stabilizer is therefore the displayed torus, not merely a subgroup of it. Its free action on the alternative secants proves triviality of every chamber stabilizer. The chamber action is transitive by the ordered-pair and fiber arguments. Since every flag extends, it is flag-transitive. The chamber count q³(q³+1)(q²−1) follows directly; regularity was proved before comparing it with a familiar group-order formula.

Every argument remains valid at q=2. The trace-zero space is F2, the two root families generate SL(2,2), the nonzero norm shell has six vectors, and all panel sizes remain at least two. PGU(3,2) has the verified regular action on216 chambers. No q>2 inequality was silently used in extending the theorem to this case.

## 6. Attribution, unresolved novelty and disposition

The construction, classical unital parameters, Hermitian linear algebra, trace/norm facts and elementary SL2 generation are retained mathematics. The proof's contribution is their explicit combination to establish the precise published conjectural statement, together with a regular chamber action and reproducible verification. The new transport formula is a proof interface and formal certificate; no historical claim of first discovering that linear-algebra identity is made.

The earlier priority search was incomplete. This review does not convert weak negative citation evidence into a novelty claim and does not assert that the conjecture was still open in2026. It does establish that the bound proof answers the source's mathematical statement. The existing conjecture record should therefore be displayed as **independently reviewed complete written proof; partial Lean verification; priority/current-open status unverified**.
