# Phase 21: linear diagonal rank-three source and priority boundaries

Audit date: 2026-10-10. **Publication priority is unverified.** This is a bounded search and selected primary-source reading, not an exhaustive novelty determination. The mathematical claim under review is the explicit construction for $L_2(q)$, the index-two diagonal extension of $\operatorname{PSL}_4(q)$, for odd $q\ge5$.

## 1. What is already published

[Markovskaya–Nuzhin (2025), Theorem 2, printed p.248](https://journal.imm.uran.ru/sites/default/files/content/31_4/TrIMMUrORAN_2025_4_p247_L.pdf), already gives the rank-three conclusion for full $\operatorname{PGL}_4(q)$ when $q\equiv3\pmod4$. This is N011, retained without rederivation credit. The source's $n=4$ construction, read on pp.256–258, begins under that congruence assumption and uses different triples in characteristic three and other characteristics. It first obtains the subgroup of matrices with determinant $\pm1$, then uses scalar determinants to obtain the full projective group.

That intermediate argument is relevant to possible overlap beyond the displayed theorem. If the same generation argument applies at $q\equiv1\pmod4$, its projective image has index two over the socle for $q\equiv5\pmod8$, and is the socle for $q\equiv1\pmod8$. This determinant calculation is elementary. This audit has not independently verified every source matrix identity after extending its parameter range, so it does not certify that extension as a newly proved source corollary. It also does not label the $q\equiv5\pmod8$ part of the present family as verified novel work.

## 2. The linear group and its projective image must be distinguished

[Markovskaya's other 2025 paper](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=jsfu&option_lang=eng&paperid=1290&what=fullt), *On Generating Sets of Matrix Groups over Finite Fields*, treats $\operatorname{GL}^{\pm1}_n(q)$ and some central quotients. Its four-dimensional exclusion in §2.3 concerns involutions in the matrix group. The new endpoint instead squares to a nonsquare scalar and is an involution only projectively.

The quotient discussion on printed p.768 requires a further distinction. Its assertion that the centers of $\operatorname{GL}^{\pm1}_n(q)$ and $\operatorname{SL}_n(q)$ agree for every even $n$ is false as stated: for $n=6,q=13$, the scalar $2I_6$ has determinant $2^6=-1$, so belongs to the first center and not the second. In general their scalar conditions are $\lambda^n\in\{1,-1\}$ and $\lambda^n=1$, respectively. This is a precise correction to that center assertion, not a finding about all other results of the paper. Its Corollary 1 only addresses even $n\ge6$, so is not a theorem for our four-dimensional target in any event. The new proof identifies its projective group directly through determinants modulo fourth powers.

Reading scope for this second paper: introduction, preliminary lemmas, §2.3 and the final quotient corollaries; not every larger-dimensional construction proof.

## 3. Retained automorphism theorem and broader question

The [Guralnick–Zieve author manuscript](https://websites.umich.edu/~zieve/papers/gz.pdf), Appendix A, printed p.42 immediately before Lemma A.5, explicitly recalls $\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{PSL}_2(q))=\operatorname{P\Gamma L}_2(q)$. This is the retained input for the Goursat trace-field step, not a result rederived in this project. Only that appendix passage and surrounding scope were read for this task; the polynomial classification is unrelated to the present proof.

The [2026 Kourovka Notebook, Problem 21.101](https://alglog.org/21tkt.pdf), remains the broader almost-simple rank-three classification question identified in phase20. The present diagonal family is a proposed contribution to that problem, not its full resolution. Field and graph extensions remain outside this construction. No infinity marker or whole-problem closure is inferred.
