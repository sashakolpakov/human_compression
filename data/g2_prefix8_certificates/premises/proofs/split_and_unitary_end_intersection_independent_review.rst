The split-end equality is justified
------------------------------------

SL3(11) has trivial centre. An odd-order witness lies in the index-two
linear kernel, while a Klein four has a nonidentity involution in that
kernel. The odd Singer-normalizer consequence excludes every proper
kernel containing both. Thus an end is either SL3(11) or its full
specified graph extension. No centre quotient or splitting assumption
is concealed in this step.

To exclude the first possibility, use the common eigenbasis of the two
distinct commuting half-turns. For the third half-turn, C5's row-vector
formula y=-I+2 ell*u/(u*ell) has the correct matrix dimensions and
projection direction. A nontrivial order-19 element is irreducible in
dimension three over F11. A zero coordinate of u would give a common
invariant coordinate hyperplane; a zero coordinate of ell would give a
common invariant coordinate line. All coordinates are therefore nonzero.

The diagonal matrix C with entries ell_i/u_i is consequently nonsingular.
For B(v,w)=v*C*w^T, the identities B(v,u)=v*ell and B(u,u)=u*ell show
that y is the orthogonal half-turn about u. The other two diagonal sign
matrices preserve B. Their group lies in SO(B), whose determinant-one
elements in odd dimension three have eigenvalue 1 by reciprocal pairing.
That contradicts the irreducible order-19 witness. This supplies the
missing common form from irreducibility; it does not assume that three
arbitrary linear half-turns preserve a form. Hence the end is the full
natural split-line stabilizer.
