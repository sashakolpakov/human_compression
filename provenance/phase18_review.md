# Phase-eighteen review and publication scope

10 October 2026. **PASS for the stated internally reviewed additions.**
This is a publication-preparation record, not external review or a claim
that the repository is already hosted publicly. The accompanying JSON
binds the original-file manifest, living registry and this note.

The class-at-most-three theorem classifies every ordered nondegenerate
involutory path marking of a finite two-group in that class, for all
ranks d>=3 and all excesses. The active edge and intrinsic triple words
form a genuine kernel basis, and a central quotient construction realizes
every parameter choice with actual IP. A two-state transfer computes the
complete excess polynomial, and its value at one is Fibonacci F_(4d-5).
The full written proof has separate author, survey and root records.

Root freshly ran the frozen GAP/NQ checker and independent all-subspace
Python checker. The first tested 243 actual groups, 5,128 parabolic
orders and 123,072 actual subgroup meets. It constructed all rank-two
kernel quotients at ranks three and four and only the 81 predicted
accepted ones at rank five. The second enumerated all 29,602 universal
kernel subspaces at ranks three through five, making 4,320,898
intersections before rejecting failed candidates. These are distinct
bounded diagnostics; neither replaces the arbitrary-rank proof.

The separate leading-coefficient theorem proves, at every fixed k>=1,
a_k(d)=(5^k/k!)d^k+O_k(d^(k-1)) for the unrestricted ordered-marking
count. Higher-class markings contribute an eventual polynomial of
degree at most k-1. The proof uses the frozen canonical-core bijection
and the five possible interior excess-one components. An independent
rank-agent review checks the whole argument. The survey technical
review explicitly discloses its contribution to the normal-component
argument and is not counted as wholly independent.

A further short form-space argument gives two necessary configurations
for irreducible rank-three projective involution triples in dimension
four that preserve no bilinear similarity form. It proves that both
direct folds of the frozen rank-four templates generate proper
subgroups in every permitted field. Two written reviews check the
argument and actual template matrices. The published automatic-IP
criterion is retained with its precise hypotheses; no uniform
rank-three existence or nonexistence claim is made.
