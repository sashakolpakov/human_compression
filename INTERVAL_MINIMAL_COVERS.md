# Extending finite minimal covers to higher ranks

**Every finite minimal marked C-group cover of a consecutive interval of
generators occurs as that interval in a finite minimal cover of the whole
finite string group.** Consequently, infinitely many local minimal covers
give infinitely many global minimal covers.

Combined with the [rank-four theorem](FINITE_RANK_FOUR_COVERS.md), this gives:

> A finite polytope of any rank has infinitely many finite minimal regular
> covers if a subgroup generated by four consecutive monodromy marks is
> not a string C-group.

The [complete three-page paper](research/case_study/phase277_interval_covers.pdf)
has a [source text](research/case_study/phase277_interval_covers.md),
[independent review](research/case_study/phase279_interval_review.md) and
[root review](research/case_study/phase280_root_review.md). The frozen author
archive retains its earlier review-status wording; these reviews accept
its exact statements.

## Proof and attribution

Monson–Schulte's finite-cover construction extends a prescribed C-cover Q
while preserving Q exactly as a parabolic. In its product construction,
that parabolic is the graph of the map from Q to the target interval.
Extending one end at a time, and reversing the marking when needed,
retains Q inside a finite cover of the entire group.

Now take a minimal regular quotient over the target. Its interval
parabolic is a C-group quotient of Q still covering the same interval.
Minimality of Q forces that quotient to be Q itself. Different local
covers therefore yield different global covers. This last descent argument
and its application to the rank-four theorem are the contribution here;
the underlying finite extension remains credited to Monson and Schulte.

The extension may introduce other primes. Although the rank-four theorem
provides local covers with prime-power kernels, no such assertion is made
about the resulting global kernels. The theorem also does not say that
every global minimal cover has minimal interval parabolics.

## A necessary distinction in the remaining problem

The [separate two-page obstruction](research/case_study/phase277_coprime_obstruction.pdf)
proves that a finite C-cover's kernel order is divisible by the order of
any intersection between separated parabolics of the base group. An
explicit order-64 marked group therefore requires an even kernel in every
finite C-cover, despite having C-group ends.

This group cannot be a polytope's monodromy group: its subgroups on
disjoint sets of ranks intersect nontrivially, which the actual flag
action forbids. Thus an arbitrary-rank extension of the prime-kernel
argument needs more than independence of the generators. The finite
polytope problem whose obstruction first occurs on a longer interval
remains unresolved here.
